# Scenario stress test: Talarico 2029-2037 against the Project 2050 base case

Filed Oct 20, 2026 to frameworks/ (role: reasoning, not current policy). Source: sources/scenario-talarico-2029-2037.md, a converted copy of Espen's Claude doc "Talarico 2029–2037: A Scenario Model" (Oct 19, 2026). Only that model's "Real-world grounding" table is sourced; everything from 2027 on is scenario, and its numbers are internal to the model, not forecasts. Nothing in this file changes a Decided item or a chosen design. Where a finding suggests a change, it is filed as a named option in the domain file that owns it, or listed below as a proposal for that file's next touch.

Figures are tagged **scenario** (the model's own number), **sourced**, or **estimate** (mine). Ledger references are to index/2029-trifecta-master-ledger.md: general-fund claims about $10.97T against a $6.37T base, a gap of about $4.60T, about −$0.08T after the accepted revenue and envelope borrowing; 172 floor days against 163 under path F.

**Status.** Espen adopted three proposals: **DEM-1F** (DEM-1 after R1, by spring 2030), **registration at the point of benefit delivery** (DEM-1E, part of DEM-1's chosen design) and the **standing-disbursement drafting rule** (a governing design rule, with its statute as EXE-18). LAB-9D is chosen for national service (Decision Brief 2). Every other proposal is filed as an option in its domain file; the table under "Proposals for files not edited today" gives each one's status and ID. The stress test's "RTS-3C" is filed as **RTS-3E** (3C was taken), and the Talarico price path is **REV-C1F**.

## Bottom line

1. **The scenario's governing pattern is the base case's biggest exposure: "transfers and rules delivered fast and well; capacity and construction delivered late and short."** The base case is heavy on transfers (FAM-1, HLT-1, CLI-2, EDU-3) and on conditional grants to states for anything that must be built (HOU-3, HOU-6, FAM-2's supply grants, CLI-5). In the model that instrument "fails precisely in the states that most need the thing." Filed: **HOU-9** (federal builder of last resort), with **CLI-10** (public power as builder) as its climate twin.
2. **Sequencing: deliver before reforming the rules.** The model's counterfactual has leading with voting rights in early 2029 cost about forty House seats in 2030 instead of thirteen (scenario). D1 only requires DEM-1 by mid-2030. Adopted: **DEM-1F**, enact after R1 is signed and benefits are announced, and by spring 2030.
3. **A credible threat is worth more unused.** The untested majority-rule carve-out held at 53 votes is what produced a 67-vote Social Security law in the model. Filed: **SS-10** (a bipartisan path on the real Warren-Moreno framework), with D8's baseline as the fallback. The same logic supports D2's stated goal and argues for keeping some majority-precedent power in reserve rather than spending it all in the first month.
4. **The talking filibuster is slower than path C assumes.** The model's contested bills under a talking filibuster took 19 to 31 days each, about 24 on average (scenario), against path C's 15. At 24 days per top fight, path C needs about 267-272 floor days against 163 (estimate). And the minority's top fights are the bills it is *proud* to block (immigration, the Equality Act), not the ones it is embarrassed to block (voting rights). Path C's top-fight list should be re-ordered and its per-fight cost raised.
5. **Every program creates beneficiaries who do not vote in midterms.** The model lost the House in 2034 and 2038 and nearly in 2030 on the same mechanism: 71% of presidential-year turnout in high-propensity districts against 44% in districts with the highest child-allowance uptake (scenario). Felt-by-Nov-2030 delivery is necessary but not sufficient; registration at the point of benefit delivery is the policy lever.
6. **Training is not a jobs policy.** The model's entry-level labor market "improved and never recovered," because the constraint was a demand hole inside firms. Filed: **AI-4E** (an entry-level hiring credit), **AI-4F** (a transitional employment guarantee, second trifecta) and **REV-E2** (public equity, the scenario's late answer to labor's falling share).
7. **The fiscal comparison flatters the base case.** The scenario accepts a structural gap of $1.2-1.5T over ten years (about 0.4% of GDP) and counts $0.6-0.9T of dynamic feedback (scenario). D4-B counts no dynamic revenue and pays for permanent programs at maturity (from FY2040); its ten-year general-fund deficit (about $1.2T) is time-limited investment borrowed inside an envelope that tapers to zero by 2040, not a structural gap. The base case is the stricter plan; the scenario is a reference point for what a looser rule buys.

## Findings, mapped to the base case

Each finding gives what the model found, what the base case does, and what is filed or proposed.

### 1. Construction and capacity: the instrument problem

- **Model:** $95B of housing production money and a social housing authority delivered about 1.9M units by 2050 against a 4.5M shortfall; rents flat in 23 states and not in 27. The binding constraints were preemption limits, municipal permitting, construction labor competing with transmission, and materials. Child-care waitlists cleared in 2035, three years after the cap; clean interconnections rose 40% in 2031 alone once permitting and credits lined up (scenario).
- **Base case:** HOU-3 (state revolving funds plus direct loans), HOU-6 (zoning conditions on new money), HOU-8 (a social housing pilot); FAM-2 phases the child-care cap behind supply grants (design rule 5); CLI-5 ties the transmission credit to plan-selected lines.
- **Filed:** **HOU-9** in domain-housing.md, a federal housing construction corporation on federal and corporation-controlled land, with a workforce drawn from LAB-8 and LAB-9, triggered in low-vacancy metros where state channels miss targets. **CLI-10** in domain-climate.md (TVA, Bonneville and WAPA borrowing; a federal transmission corporation). **Write for this Court:** the model's *Arizona v. HUD* (2038) upheld federal preemption only for federally funded and constructed housing and struck eminent domain for housing on public-use grounds, so HOU-9 relies on federal land and voluntary acquisition.
- **Confirmed in the base case:** FAM-2's phasing (the scenario's cap outran supply by three years; the base case's 2032 cap date is the same lesson applied in advance) and CAP-8's 2029 fast track (the scenario's capacity law came in 2031 and is called "the least-credited achievement of the period"; the base case is two years ahead).
- **Proposed (not filed):** an instructor line for community-college technical programs (the scenario's fourth reason training underdelivered: people able to teach industrial controls earn far more doing them). Natural home: LAB-8, at its next touch; EDU-4's pipeline logic (pay, not slots, is the binding constraint) applies.

### 2. Sequencing: delivery before democracy reform

- **Model:** "Leading with voting rights in February 2029 spends the honeymoon on a process fight with nothing in anyone's bank account." In the model the first checks went out five months after the April 2029 bill, and the voting-rights law passed in March 2030 after 19 days, still in time for the 2031 maps. "The argument that wins later is 'they are blocking what works,' not 'we changed the rules.'"
- **Base case:** D2's rules vote is in week one of 2029; D1 requires DEM-1 (10 floor days) in force by mid-2030 for the 2031 redistricting.
- **Adopted: DEM-1F** (domain-democracy.md): DEM-1's floor time comes after R1 is signed (about May-June 2029), and it is enacted by spring 2030. **The tension:** registration provisions take time to land; the model's Texas Valley turnout recovered only in 2032, "exactly two years late, which is when they were always going to land." Earlier enactment helps 2030 turnout; later enactment protects the honeymoon. Pairing (design rule 4) is the bridge: announce DEM-1 with R1's benefits. D1's deadline is unchanged.
- **Related:** D2's rules vote in week one of 2029 is itself a process fight before any benefit. Under the scenario's logic it could follow R1 too, since R1 needs no rules change; D2 keeps it in week one, and it must precede DEM-1's floor time.

### 3. The credible threat and the 67-vote asset

- **Model:** a partisan Social Security bill failed 53-47 in March 2029; with OASI depletion approaching the 2032 election, Finance negotiated on the Warren-Moreno framework with the majority-rule carve-out "sitting at 53 as leverage"; signed at 67-33 in January 2030. "A 54-vote Social Security law is a repeal target for forty years. A 67-vote one is settled."
- **Real-world anchor (sourced):** Sens. Warren and Moreno published a joint op-ed on June 23, 2026 proposing to lift the payroll-tax cap and said they are working on legislation together.
- **Filed:** **SS-10** in domain-social-security.md (options SS-10A to 10C). Cost to the base case: it drops SS-2 and adds a top-earner bend-point change and a donut. Needs an SSA score and Espen's decision on D8.
- **The same pattern elsewhere in the model:** energy and permitting 67-33 (2029), the federal workforce law 61-39, a global partnership law 64-36, national service 68-32, and 74 judgeships 71-29, all with bipartisan votes (scenario). **Implications for the base case:**
  - **LAB-9 (national service)** sits in R2 at $0.12T, paid for at maturity under D4-B. The model passed it as a bipartisan floor bill. Chosen as **LAB-9D** (Decision Brief 2): a bipartisan authorization at LAB-9B's scale with the R2 money as the fallback.
  - **CRT-P1's judgeships** passed the Senate by unanimous consent in 2024 before a veto (staging 4.1). Phased across presidential terms, they are a plausible 60-vote bill split from term limits and ethics, which would move them off the contested floor list (filed as packaging option **CRT-P4**).
  - **Under path F**, every bill that moves at 60 still costs floor days but does not carry F's 4.5-day minimum fight.

### 4. The talking filibuster as a worked example of path C

- **Model:** debate continues only while 41 senators are physically present; when they cannot produce them on a live quorum call, cloture ripens at a simple majority. Contested bills: voting rights 19 days, WHPA 26, the work and opportunity bill 31, immigration 23, statehood 19, the Equality Act 24, climate displacement 29 (scenario; mean about 24). "Bills they are embarrassed to block die; bills they are proud to block get harder."
- **Base case:** path C assumes the minority picks six top fights at about 15 days each (DEM-1, DEM-5, LAB-1, RTS-1, HLT-3, SS-1), giving 213 days.
- **Sensitivity (estimate, on the current ledger):**

| Per-fight cost | Base top-fight list | Re-ordered list (IMM-1 and RTS-3 in place of HLT-3 and SS-1) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 15 days (path C as modeled) | 211 | 212 |
| 20 days | 241 | 242 |
| 25 days (about the scenario's mean) | 271 | 272 |

- **Read:** the total is insensitive to *which* bills are fought and very sensitive to *how long* each fight lasts. At the scenario's pace path C is about 100 days over the first Congress's budget, so under C the first-Congress agenda does not fit without SS-10 (moving Social Security to a negotiated bill saves about 17 days at 25 per fight, estimate) and the merges the ledger already names. The sensitivity is in ledger-paths.py and informed D2, which chose F.
- **Content effect:** the Equality Act passed only in scoped form, with athletics carved out to a joint Education-HHS rulemaking under a five-year sunset, "the cost of avoiding a four-week public argument the minority wins." That is a third answer to the RTS-3A/3B choice, filed as option **RTS-3E**; athletics is Espen's call (A, B or E).

### 5. The beneficiary-voter asymmetry

- **Model:** Democratic turnout in 2030 ran 71% of 2028 volume in the highest-propensity districts and 44% in the districts with the highest child-allowance uptake; the displacement benefit (2034) and the employment guarantee (2038) repeated the pattern with young, recently unemployed recipients. High-propensity voters respond to corruption, democracy and abortion; low-propensity voters to checks and prices. Special-election overperformance shrinks from about +9 to about +4 under a party's own president but does not reverse (composition, not motivation).
- **Base case:** the ledger tracks whether each row is felt by November 2030 (27 of 54 in-window rows), which measures delivery, not turnout.
- **Adopted (Espen, Oct 20); filed as DEM-1E, part of DEM-1's chosen design:**
  - **Registration at the point of benefit delivery.** The Freedom to Vote floor in DEM-1 includes automatic voter registration; DEM-1's drafting should name the new delivery points (the IRS portal for FAM-1's monthly payments, SSA for the Climate Dividend, state agencies running EDU-3 and FAM-2) as registration agencies.
  - **Design for universality.** The model's finding that "a universal child benefit cannot be racialized" supports EDU-3A (universal meals) over EDU-3B and FAM-1's broad phase-out over a targeted credit. Both are already chosen.
  - **Legibility at the point of payment.** Every check names the program and the law (design rule 3 already requires this).
- **Not solvable by policy alone:** the model never solved it. It is a party-building problem the project should name rather than claim to fix.

### 6. Instrument durability: "a check becomes furniture"

- **Model:** the child allowance and the carbon dividend both survived hostile Congresses because taking away a payment people already receive is the hardest thing in American politics; Treasury's standing authority to pay the child allowance without an appropriation (a 2029 drafting choice) is why a 2035 shutdown failed in seventeen days.
- **Base case:** FAM-1 is a refundable credit (paid under permanent law); CLI-2's dividend is paid from the fee with a permanent appropriation in the durability toolkit (domain-climate.md); EDU-3 is an entitlement.
- **Adopted drafting rule (Espen, Oct 20; now a governing design rule, statute at EXE-18):** every recurring payment carries **standing disbursement authority** that does not lapse in a shutdown, including FAM-1's monthly advance payments (which need express advance-payment authority), the dividend, and EDU-3's reimbursements. The climate durability review's companion proposals (an automatic continuing resolution at prior-year levels and repeal or long suspension of the debt limit) are filed in domain-executive-power.md as EXE-18B (a pointer to CAP-4's automatic CR) and EXE-18C (the debt limit).

### 7. Entry-level work and labor's share

- **Model:** apprenticeship starts rose from 190,000 (2028) to 340,000 (2031), but net employment in AI-affected clusters fell about 2.9M and labor's share fell from 58% to 51% (scenario). The four reasons: wrong population, wrong timing, wrong constraint (demand inside firms), and the instructor bottleneck.
- **Filed:** **AI-4E** (payroll credit for hiring workers under 27 into first career-track jobs; about $0.21T and 340,000 placements a year in the model), **AI-4F** (a transitional employment guarantee, about $62B a year in the model, a second-trifecta question that overlaps LAB-9), **AI-5B** (statutory limits on federal AI surveillance and enforcement use, which in the model was introduced only in 2039 and never passed), and **REV-E2** (public equity whose returns grow with capital income).
- **The question the model leaves:** its 2040 Republican nominee runs "on the question no Democrat has answered in twelve years: what a person is *for*, in an economy where the work is done." The base case has no answer beyond LAB-9 and AI-4. That is a framework question for radical-means-moderate-ends.md, not a domain row.

### 8. Redistricting: what survives the Court

- **Model:** *Oklahoma v. United States* (2032, 6-3, Roberts writing) upheld the mid-decade ban, substantive criteria, VRA compliance, a three-judge federal backstop and a *voluntary* commission safe harbor, and struck a numeric partisan-fairness trigger on *Rucho* manageability grounds; net 12 seats of structural improvement against an expected 20. A hard commission mandate regulates "who inside a state decides," which has no direct Elections Clause precedent.
- **Base case:** DEM-2A mandates independent commissions in every state, with the mid-decade ban and the 2034 exception; no numeric fairness trigger.
- **Filed: option DEM-2E** (domain-democracy.md): substantive criteria plus a three-judge backstop, with commissions as a voluntary safe harbor rather than a mandate, all severable. It trades some reach for a design closer to what the model's Court upheld. **Also from the model:** "redistricting timing is not invertible." The 2031 maps are drawn by legislatures and governors elected in 2030, so the 2030 governorships (Texas above all, where a Democratic governor would hold a veto over 38 House seats) matter as much as the federal law. That is a political note, not a policy row.

### 9. Courts and statutory drafting

- **Model:** three appointments move the Court only from 6-3 to 5-4 Republican appointees, with Roberts as median; major questions and the non-delegation revival survive with five votes. Court expansion refused three times ("I cannot argue the rules bind Vance and not me"). WHPA upheld 5-4 on *Raich* commerce grounds.
- **Base case:** design rule 7 ("write for this Court"); CRT-P1 (term limits, phased judgeships, random assignment, ethics); RTS-1 (the Reproductive Freedom Act).
- **Read:** the model confirms the drafting posture for the whole decade, not just the first years: numbers in statute (CLI-4D's statutory caps), express delegations (CRT-8 templates), conditions on new money only (*NFIB*). RTS-1's record should lead with the commerce power, as the model's WHPA did. Expansion stays out of the base case on reciprocity grounds, which the model's candidate reached independently.

### 10. Climate

- **Model:** −52% below 2005 by 2036 with a $70-to-$190 fee and no statutory caps or sales standard; power −78%, transport only −34%; agriculture reaches only −22% by 2050 unconditioned and −41% with conditioned payments (costing Iowa twice); a 2034 insurance collapse produced the bipartisan adaptation consensus ("not persuasion; the mortgage market"); warming lands at 2.1-2.3°C (scenario).
- **Base case and Oct 20 options:** CLI-1 to CLI-8 chosen; the durability session's options CLI-9 to CLI-13 filed the same day.
- **Filed:** agriculture conditioning inside **CLI-13**; a federal reinsurance backstop as **CLI-7C** (with the warning that the model's $400B facility is off-score contingent exposure; one bad season draws $150-200B); REV-C1F (the steeper fee path). **Cross-check:** the durability session's expanded plan (statutory caps, sales standard, clean load standard) reaches about −64% by 2035, the model about −52% by 2036; the gap is roughly the regulatory backstop, which is why CLI-3 and CLI-4 matter even with a fee.
- **The model's warning about equilibrium:** a rich country can buy tolerable conditions through adaptation long enough that mitigation urgency fades. The base case should not let CLI-7's adaptation spending substitute for the mitigation rows.

### 11. Fiscal design

- **Model:** $13.8T of headline gross spending; $9.2T of on-budget programmatic spending; $10.7T gross revenue including the carbon fee ($3.8T) and mark-to-market ($1.1T); a structural gap of $1.2-1.5T after $0.6-0.9T of dynamic feedback; debt about 4 points of GDP above baseline at exit (scenario).
- **Base case:** D4-B pays for every permanent program at maturity, with no dynamic credit; general-fund claims about $10.97T against a $6.37T base, closed by $3.53T of accepted revenue and $1.15T of time-limited borrowing inside the envelope (about −$0.08T).
- **Read:** "tax rates are the weak lever" matches the base case's design (G11 and G6 over rate increases). The model's soft-spot note on stacking capital-gains parity, mark-to-market and step-up repeal (static scores off by a third either way) supports keeping GX's haircut. Filed in domain-revenue.md as REV-SC1 to SC3 (a $5M bracket, pass-through and deferred-compensation reform, crop insurance reform); capital-gains parity and a larger FTT were considered and not added (overlap and market-effect reasons given there).

### 12. Electoral arithmetic

- **Model:** a D+6.6 popular vote yields 319 electoral votes; a uniform swing off 2024 leaves Ohio, Iowa, Florida and Maine's second district as near misses. "An Obama-2008 popular vote does not buy an Obama-2008 map." DC and Puerto Rico statehood (2033) supply the five electoral votes that make Pennsylvania non-decisive in 2036.
- **Read for D1:** the scenario's 56 Senate seats required an exceptional cycle; planning for 52-54 with 56 as upside is the right posture. The model's statehood timing (second term, after 19 floor days) is the C-path cost of DEM-5 and DEM-6; in the base case they are first-Congress carve-out bills under G.

## What the stress test does not change

D1's seat planning, D3's test, D4's fiscal rule, D5's vehicles, D7's road-fuel exemption and D8's baseline all stand. The scenario is an upside case with a looser fiscal rule; where it does better than the base case (Social Security durability, bipartisan service and judgeships), the base case gains options, and where it does worse (construction, entry-level work, midterm turnout), the base case shares the exposure and now names it.
