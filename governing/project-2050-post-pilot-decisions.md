## 2. Correct the 2030 registration explanation

**Current commitment.** DEM-1E includes marketplace and Medicaid contacts; DEM-1F's rationale says FAM-1 and HLT-3 enrollment are already running before November 2030. Health instead opens the public option in plan year 2031 and automatic enrollment in 2032. Existing health contacts are distinct from that new plan. [DEM, DEM-1E/F; HLT, Felt dates]

**Verified problem and type.** This is a conflicting record with a material delivery inference. A statutory registration duty, a benefit launch, a service contact and a completed registration are separate events. Ex parte renewals or automatic payments may create no fresh interactive contact. Correcting the HLT-3 reference cannot establish how many eligible people will be reached. [TX, §§2–4; DP, §4]

| Alternative | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Retain the current explanation | Leaves the timing contradiction and overstates an early delivery channel. |
| **Use existing contacts while preserving health dates** | Corrects the account without accelerating or narrowing the health design. Requires legal and operational qualification. |
| Accelerate HLT-3 | Changes health policy/delivery assumptions and requires new capacity, cost and timing analysis. Not justified merely to repair DEM-1's rationale. |
| Remove health contacts from DEM-1E | Narrows an adopted registration pathway unnecessarily. The later plan can join on its actual schedule. |

**Approved record correction, prepared for repository review:**

> Before November 2030, existing Medicaid and marketplace contacts could support registration services where legally authorized and operationally ready. FAM-1 and other benefit pathways have their own delivery dependencies. The new HLT-3 public option opens in 2031, with auto-enrollment in 2032; it does not supply the new-plan enrollment channel for 2030. Reach and completion remain to be established.

This language leaves DEM-1E's automatic-registration commitment in force. It does not relabel ordinary application assistance as delivery of that commitment. It also does not claim a turnout gain, electoral result or established causal effect from the scenario that motivated DEM-1F.

**Consequences:** a defensible explanation, less certainty about pre-midterm reach, and no change to health dates or entitlement. The pairing rationale can explain intended benefits without claiming they have already been delivered everywhere.

**Evidence that could change the assessment:** a separately approved health-date change; verified reach by contact type, including renewals completed without a response; exact effective dates and enrollment readiness for other programs; and tested registration disposition rather than portal traffic.

**Approval recorded:** Espen approved preparation of this correction across DEM-1E/F and public explanations, preserving HLT-3 dates. Espen approved the coordinated edits and integration, with DEM-1 floor consideration by March 2029 latest. The unsupported about-zero administrative estimate should be flagged for qualification; no numerical registration cost score is substituted.

## 3. Choose how to develop the 2030 registration service

### Current commitment and verified problem

DEM-1E adopts automatic registration at the named benefit-delivery points; DEM-1A keeps the package together. The worked Medicaid transaction supplies an assisted route and a conditional Oregon automatic route, not a demonstrated national launch. The proposed assisted service requires deliberate action and will miss some people an effective automatic default would reach. Adopting it as the entire 2030 result would be a substantive staging choice. [DEM, DEM-1A/E; TX, §§1–4, 9]

Existing NVRA agency duties include application services and assistance; they are not a blanket new automatic-data-transfer authority. Medicaid safeguards include ordinary identity and eligibility information, so a benefits contact does not make the underlying file freely available for election use. Source-specific permissions still matter. [L-NVRA; L-PRIVACY]

Separate legal purposes need not mean making people type every field twice. DOJ guidance contemplates prefilled registration fields in agency services. Assess lawful prefill and confirmation in the actual system before choosing a more burdensome interface; that guidance is not blanket permission to reuse restricted source data or submit from silence. [L-NVRA, Section 7 guidance]

Oregon's ORS 247.018 supplies a conditional OHP-record route, with federal-law compliance and verified transfer processes. It permits registration without a signature but requires implementing rules preventing ballot counting without the required electronic signature. Its operative date is June 1, 2026. These provisions establish statutory machinery, not a verified production service or permission determination for the transaction proposed here. [L-ORS]

### Alternatives and protections

| Path | Gain | Reach or protection not established / lost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Assisted 2030 floor** | Separate official application, accessible assistance, acceptance/transmission, receipts and correction; health processing remains independent | Requires action and reaches only the relevant contacts/channels. An offered service does not equal completed registration or universal access. Assisted-only adoption loses the adopted automatic default. |
| **Conditional Oregon automatic route plus assisted fallback** | Uses existing state machinery if permission, implementing rules, data quality and operation are demonstrated; assistance handles exceptions | Oregon is not a national operating model. Some people lack qualifying records or a usable signature, never have an interactive renewal, or face unresolved errors. Those gaps need support and reporting. |
| **Broader DEM-1E implementation** | Retains the intended default and multiple benefit pathways, including later programs | Each source, state and election destination has its own authority, capacity and timing. No supported national schedule or price is established. State resistance needs a lawful service/enforcement route. |
| **Delay automatic registration nationally** | Could avoid an unsafe technical cutover | Creates an explicit loss of early reach and may become indefinite without funded scope and a return decision. Requires Espen's approval, not merely a technical gate. |

**Recommendation:** develop an assisted service floor and a conditional Oregon automatic case in parallel, retaining the broader automatic commitment. A lawful automatic service should not be held back merely because an assisted alternative is simpler to describe. Equally, an unverified bulk transfer should not be launched to satisfy an announced date. If evidence supports only assistance for 2030, put the reduced reach and proposed staging before Espen expressly rather than declaring DEM-1E delivered.

The minimum operational specification should preserve: independent health processing; a separate adult election record; lawful qualification and source handling; no inference from a child's or spouse's benefit; confidential decline; accessible/offline assistance; receipt and county disposition; correction before relevant deadlines; and funded support for signature completion where needed. These are design requirements to develop within the legal route, not a certification that the whole set is already authorized. Government-error protections need separate election, immigration and criminal-law review. [TX, §§3–6, 8]

**Consequences:** this approach protects existing assistance while investigating the higher-reach default. It adds real county, notice, training, correction and continuing-service work. Do not infer Medicaid matching eligibility or spare capacity. The CMS follow-up's signed contracts and historical price chain do not supply a registration price; the ONE base term ending June 2029 makes successor authority relevant to 2030. Keep election work, health work and shared federal services under distinct cost owners with supported allocation. The transaction's illustrative dollar examples are not comparative estimates of effectiveness or savings. [CF, §§1–3; TX, §§1, 7]

**Evidence that could change the recommendation:** actual OHA/CMS disclosure decisions and conditions; current rules, permitted data and operating/test records; signature-completion and notice evidence; renewal-contact volumes and unique eligible-person reach; county acceptance/correction capability; current successor contracts; a priced scope and without-proposal case; and lawful national backstop design. A targeted official-source check in this assignment did not locate a later permission or operating record; that is not a finding that none exists. The 2023 LFO fiscal note describes approval uncertainty at that time and cannot answer the current question. [L-LFO; L-ORS; TX, §11]

**Approval recorded:** Espen approved development of the two-path comparison and broader service using the existing records packet. No email or external request is authorized. Retain DEM-1E's default and scope. Do not yet approve an assisted-only national endpoint, a new deadline, a funding amount or a new mandate. Actual external submission would be a separate action; the current drafts remain unsent. [CR]

## 4. Develop restoration and separable Title III protections

**Current commitment.** DEM-1A keeps the voting floor, preclearance, Title III and matching in one bill. Title III aims to restore results-based equal-opportunity protection and permit justified race-conscious remedies. DEM-2A's commissions and mid-decade ban remain chosen; DEM-2E is open. DEM-1's enacted and effective protections before the 2031 mapping process are part of D1's stipulated later scenario, not proof of a future electoral outcome. [G, D1; DEM, DEM-1/2]

**Verified problem and type.** Section 2 already contains results language. The Callais majority ties its districting interpretation to constitutional enforcement limits and circumstances supporting a strong inference of intentional discrimination; it also scrutinizes race-predominant remedial mapping. A repeated statutory formula cannot assure survival. This is a substantive legal/design exposure as well as incomplete drafting. The inference is about the proposed response, not a prediction that every restoration provision will fail. [L-S2; L-CALLAIS; T3]

| Track | Intended protection | Limit / tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Restoration** | Effects-based equal-opportunity protection, supported causation rules and appropriately tailored relief | Requires a contemporary record and text-specific enforcement-power/remedy analysis. Preserve the ambition without promising constitutional success. |
| **Intent and effective enforcement** | Usable circumstantial proof, access to evidence, expert support, express enforcement and timely relief for intentional discrimination | Effects-only injuries may be left unremedied; plaintiffs can face heavier proof and resource burdens. A presumption cannot simply recreate the rejected theory under another label. |
| **Congressional map standards, coordinated with DEM-2** | Neutral statutory standards, disclosed criteria/data and a judicial backstop for congressional maps | Does not replace racial-discrimination protection or establish general state/local mapping authority. Standards and remedies require their own review. Rucho recognizes a congressional legislative role, not automatic approval of this proposed text. [L-RUCHO] |
| **Split or replace the adopted package** | Could make some provisions easier to move independently | Risks stranding the civil-rights protection DEM-1A was designed to carry, or abandoning its intended reach. A different policy choice, not the meaning of severability. |

**Recommendation:** retain restoration and develop the intent/enforcement module alongside it; develop congressional standards once with DEM-2 rather than as a competing map code. Keep DEM-1 as one legislative package while testing which provisions, applications, funding and remedies could operate independently after an injunction. Severability is a drafting strategy, not assurance that courts will preserve every desired piece. Do not adopt DEM-2E through this exercise. [T3, §§2–4; DEM, Options]

The next product should be a compact counsel packet using the specifications already written: operative liability/proof/remedy choices, the powers claimed for each application, present-day evidence and counterevidence, enforcement and jurisdiction questions, and an injunction matrix showing who remains protected and who does not. Include state mapping, candidate-filing and remedial implementation dependencies. A stronger record does not turn uncertain legal compatibility into a guaranteed result.

**Consequences:** parallel development costs more than one unexamined rewrite but avoids making every protection depend on one theory. The fallback's proof burdens and lost coverage must remain visible. DOJ staffing, experts, discovery, state implementation, public data and continuing enforcement remain unpriced; do not assume DEM-11 or CAP already covers them. A judicial stay of a condition D1 relies on calls for a scenario review, not an inferred forecast of House control. [T3, §§4–6; DP, §§6–8]

**Evidence that could change the recommendation:** specialist assessment of actual text and enforcement authority; current discrimination evidence and contrary cases; subsequent controlling doctrine; review of workable proof standards and injunction scenarios; and state-specific remedy timelines. No specialist opinion has been obtained. Another AI report alone would not supply that external check.

**Approval recorded:** Espen authorized parallel drafting and the focused review packet, retaining restoration, DEM-1A and DEM-2A. Defer replacement, reduced protection or a switch to DEM-2E until the tradeoffs can be assessed. Counsel's task would be to explain alternatives and limits; Espen still decides the policy direction.
