## How this doc works

This doc sits above every other doc in Project 2050. It records nine upstream choices that the source agendas each made differently; most of the conflicts in the master ledger trace back to them. Every domain file inherits a decision marked Decided, and a doc that disagrees with one is out of date rather than an alternative.

Each decision lists its options, what each implies, and the decision in force. The options draw on the project's own docs (Radical Means, Instruments and Consequences, the revenue menu, the go-big health tab and Moving America); where the analysis goes beyond them, it says so. The record of what Espen decided on Oct 20 is in the two decision briefs in governing/ (Decision Brief 1: Senate rules and the gap; Decision Brief 2: trims, scope and the fiscal rule).

## Power: D1 and D2

### D1. Which scenario do we plan for?

The source docs assume three different Senates. The war-game set (Floor Time, the Bill Book, Nineteen Months, the Ledger, the Governing Agenda) assumes 56 seats, a 244-seat House and full filibuster reform. The go-big health tab and Beyond Health Care assume 52-54 seats with a filibuster change. Radical Means and the inventory assume Class 2 senators elected in 2026, with the majority's size left open.

| Option | What it means | Cost of choosing it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Narrow: 50-51 seats plus the Vice President | Only reconciliation and a democracy carve-out are safe | Most structural bills drop out of the base plan |
| B. Working: 52-54 seats **(decided)** | Enough to absorb two or three holdouts on a rules change | Some regular-order bills stay contested |
| C. Wave: 56+ seats | Nearly everything passes, as in Nineteen Months | Plans built here fail in any smaller Senate |

**Decided: B, with every item also tagged for whether it survives at 51.** Each domain file designs for one scenario and reports the stress test: ✔ survives (reconciliation, state, executive, House-only); ◐ needs a D2 majority-precedent path; ✖ needs 60, 67 or Republican votes in any scenario. The war-game docs are the upside case, not the plan.

**Scenario stipulations.** The project assumes the following for modeling:

- **White House:** a Democrat holds it from January 2029 to at least January 2037.
- **Senate:** if Democrats reach 52 or more seats after the 2026 elections, they hold it through 2036.
- **House:** possibly lost or narrowed in 2030; a trifecta again from January 2033, on the condition that the electoral and civil-rights package (DEM-1, with DEM-2's redistricting commissions and mid-decade ban and the Callais response) is in force before the 2031 redistricting. DEM-1 is must-pass by mid-2030.
- **Permitting:** a version of the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act of 2026 (Capito-Heinrich-Lee-Whitehouse, Sept 30, 2026; analysis in sources/permitting-aaja-2026-analysis.md) is enacted in the 2026 lame duck or the 2027-28 Congress under Trump. The trifecta's climate floor bill (CLI-3) carries the Clean Energy Standard, written in statute with an express override of the bill's Sec. 1401, and fixes to the permitting law: a cap on its damages, the CWA 401 point-source fix and rail drafting fixes.
- **First-trifecta scope:** regular-order bills stay in 2029-30, subject to the specific exceptions below. Must pass in the first trifecta: HLT-3, CLI-6/CLI-7, IMM-1 and AI-1/AI-4. One exception is the second-term package: House size (DEM-3), the Fair Representation Act (DEM-4) and the presidential election method (DEM-9) move together to 2033 as a 2032 campaign plank, with a one-off 2034 reapportionment and redraw, so DEM-2's mid-decade ban carries an exception for a congressionally mandated redraw. **MOV-31 is a separate second-Congress exception:** consider the FY2032–36 surface transportation authorization in the Congress seated January 2031. Its assumed predecessor expires at end-FY2031; that does not require calendar-2031 enactment, but continuity/extension must be planned. Preserve the separate 2029/2030 transportation reconciliation components. There is no general later queue for other bills. The legacy fixed-duration model reports 172 against an assumed 163 even with MOV excluded; this is a historical crosswalk, superseded analytically by the scenario method below; Calendar C development is authorized, but fit, new merger savings and additional reserve/capacity remain unproven.

These are modeling assumptions, not forecasts.

**DEM-1 timing (DEM-1F, amended October 23).** DEM-1 floor consideration begins no later than March 2029; normally after early R1 signature and its benefits announcement. An R1 delay triggers an explicit recovery decision, not automatic postponement beyond March. Enactment targets spring 2030, within the mid-2030 outer deadline; D2-F precedes consideration. **DEM-1E (adopted):** Title I names the agenda's benefit-delivery points (the FAM-1 portal, the Climate Dividend's non-filer path, the state agencies running EDU-3 and FAM-2, marketplace and Medicaid enrollment, LAB-9) as voter-registration agencies. Both come from the Talarico stress test's delivery-before-process and beneficiary-turnout findings.

### D2. Which Senate rules change do we plan for?

The Governing Agenda sequences a democracy carve-out on day 3, a talking filibuster in month 3 and full elimination in month 14. Radical Means prefers a restored talking filibuster. Instruments and Consequences argues that the talking filibuster burdens the majority, not the minority, and recommends a declining cloture threshold (60, then 57, then 54, then a simple majority, with debate periods between votes). Seven paths are modeled in "Senate Rules Reform: Options" (https://claude.ai/code/artifact/0f226c46-5ce8-4bec-9a54-c5fd7e7a5629).

| Path | Rule | What passes at 52-54 seats | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. Merkley-Daines as written | Faster procedure; final passage stays at 60 | Needs about 7-8 Republicans per contested bill | Not chosen; its Block 1 may pass in the 2026 lame duck |
| B. Cloture at 55 | Threshold lowered, plus A's procedural changes | Needs about 1-3 Republicans per bill | Not chosen |
| C. Talking filibuster with a speech and amendment budget | Blockers must keep 41 present; 10 amendments per side; optional backstop | Everything, after the minority holds a bill about 2-5 weeks | Opening offer, with a 15-session-day backstop |
| D. Uncapped talking filibuster | Blockers must hold the floor, with no limit | Everything in principle; a determined minority can block its top fights indefinitely | Not chosen |
| E. Full elimination | Majority cloture after normal debate | Everything, fastest; weakest reciprocity case | Not chosen; upper bound on speed |
| F. Graduated cloture | 60, then 57, then 54, then a majority, over about 4.5 session days | Everything, after a fixed delay per bill | **Decided** |
| G. Democracy-and-elections carve-out only | Majority cloture for voting rights, statehood and election law; 60 for everything else | The democracy plank; Nineteen Months puts expected enactments at 8.1 of 27 bills against 26.3 with full reform | Written fallback |

**Decided (Espen, Decision Brief 1): F, graduated cloture, with Block 3's minority guarantees (ten guaranteed amendments a side, equal time), adopted by standing-rules resolution and majority precedent in the first week of 2029.** The talking filibuster with a 15-session-day backstop (C) is the opening offer to institutionalist holdouts; the democracy-and-elections carve-out (G) is the written fallback. Why F: its floor cost is predictable, it fits a first Congress that may be the only one with the House, and it keeps the minority's guaranteed amendments as the reciprocity answer. At the Talarico model's observed pace (about 24 days a contested bill), C needs roughly 267-272 floor days against a 163-day budget; F needs about 172. If the Merkley-Daines lame-duck vote passes Block 1 in 2026, the 2029 fight narrows to the final-passage rule.

**Reconciliation.** Reconciliation stays as it is now (R1). Whether to narrow it to deficit reduction from January 2031 (R3, the more reciprocal version; the 2033-36 fiscal bills would then move under F) or from January 2037, when the stipulated Democratic presidency ends (R4), is decided in the same adoption resolution.

**Survival tags.** Every inventory and domain-file row is tagged for survival at 51 (✔, ◐ or ✖; see D1).

## Money: D3, D4 and D5

### D3. What does the $400K pledge cover?

Biden made the $400,000 pledge in 2020 and Harris repeated it in 2024; there has been no prominent party-wide recommitment since. Under a strict reading it rules out Moving America's fuel-tax steps, a self-financed paid leave program and a carbon fee and dividend.

| Option | What it allows | What it costs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Strict pledge: no new tax of any kind on households under $400K | Only top-end and corporate revenue | Moving America's revenue design and self-financed leave fall; the general-fund gap widens |
| B. Pledge on income taxes only | Fuel steps, a carbon fee and dividend, and earmarked contributions that buy a named benefit | Opponents will call it a broken promise |
| C. Drop the pledge, state nothing in its place | The full revenue menu | Cedes the tax-fairness message |
| D. Replace the number with a principle **(decided)** | "More money in middle-class pockets; the uber-wealthy pay their fair share." Any instrument is allowed if the household math comes out ahead | Opponents will point to any tax a middle-income family pays; the answer is the net figure, which the project computes and shows |

**Decided: D, with a test the project applies to itself.** For every income group below the top, the agenda's combined taxes, fees and benefits must leave households better off on net, the distribution check Nineteen Months sketches in its Figure 4. New general-fund revenue must come mostly from the top 1% and corporations. The message stays; the constraint on instruments goes. The household incidence model (index/revenue-household-incidence-model.md) runs this check, and any middle-reaching option is checked there before it is final. On the decided package (scenario Q), every quintile below the top comes out ahead; the fourth quintile nets about +$1,373 a year under retained allocation proxies pending AFA-specific mapping, and 72% of new general-fund revenue comes from the top 1% and corporations (estimates).

### D4. What fiscal rule do we hold ourselves to?

The agenda asks the general fund for about $11.59T over 2030-39 against a $5.99T top-end base (net of the carbon fees' income and payroll tax offset), a $5.60T gap. One procedural fact forces part of the answer: the Byrd rule bars reconciliation provisions that raise deficits after the ten-year window, so a permanent program in reconciliation must be paid for in the out-years or it must sunset. The 2025 tax law used sunsets for that reason. Byrd does let reconciliation add to deficits inside its window, as the unpaid 2021 rescue plan and the roughly $1.5T 2017 tax law did.

| Option | Rule | Effect on the agenda (estimate) | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. Fully paid for over ten years | New spending matched by new revenue and savings across the whole budget, borrowing for nothing | Every dollar of the gap must be cut or raised inside the window | Not chosen |
| D4-A. Paid for over ten years; borrow only for capital | Permanent programs fully offset across the budget by year ten; time-limited capital (housing fund, grid, rail construction) may borrow | Tests the ten-year sum, which leaves no room for time-limited lines beyond capital | Not chosen |
| **D4-B. Fiscal arc** | Permanent programs paid for at maturity; time-limited investments with a written return case borrowed inside an envelope scaled to GDP; guardrails against the real baseline | Ten-year general-fund deficit about $1.2T, almost all envelope borrowing; run rate covered from 2040 | **Decided** |
| D4-C. Deficit allowance | New net borrowing up to about 0.5% of GDP a year (about $2T over the decade), with debt stabilized as a share of GDP by 2045 | Closes the gap on paper without new revenue; the weakest answer to inflation and interest-rate risk, and the easiest for a successor to cite | Not chosen |

The envelope's size had alternatives: 0.25% of GDP (about $0.8T) or 0.75% (about $2.3T); a fixed $0.5T ten-year cap was rejected as too tight for the scale of the federal budget.

**Decided (Espen, Decision Brief 2): D4-B, with these parameters.**

1. *Paid for at maturity:* permanent programs' year-ten run rate is covered, tested in FY2040 (FY2039 net of time-limited lines) and every later year; the ten-year sum is not the test.
2. *Investment envelope:* time-limited investments with a written return case may be borrowed for up to 0.5% of GDP a year through 2034, tapering to zero by 2040 (about $1.6T over 2030-39). The envelope holds the borrowing readings (C21, C30, C31, C32, C37) and CAP-7S's agency rebuild surge (C42), about $1.15T in all. Unused room does not carry forward, and the envelope is available only while test 1 passes.
3. *Guardrails against the real baseline:* total debt below CBO's (adjusted) current-law path every year and by more each decade, and net interest below the baseline's share of GDP. The guardrails are not written on the program's own debt.
4. *Trust funds and scoring:* Social Security, Medicare HI and the Highway Trust Fund are kept whole, read on a 75-year window for the social-insurance funds (the Trustees' standard). No new trust fund is created just to hold a pay-for. No dynamic revenue is counted. Fairness follows D3.
5. *Judgment Fund:* CLI-9's contracts are scored at full expected cost when obligated; AAJA's damages are a disclosed contingent-liability line, bounded by CLI-3's cap.

Espen confirmed the reading: "relative to the real baseline" means CBO's current-law path, with no test on the program's own debt, while permanent programs are still paid for from 2040. Espen's note: a ten-year deficit of $1-2T on a roughly $10T program, mostly time-limited, is acceptable. The Byrd rule stays as it is; paying permanent programs at maturity also meets its out-year bar.

**What is borrowed for, inside the envelope.** Pay-fors are matched to a program only where an existing dedicated fund already does that work; everywhere else the general fund pays and the general fund raises. Borrowing finances only time-limited lines:

- Moving America's FY2032-36 renewal top-ups and the capital lines of its 2029 and 2030 packages (C30, about $0.30T); transit operating grants stay paid for.
- HOU-3C's revolving-fund grants (C21), HOU-5's ten-year public housing repair (C31) and DEM-17's community capital fund (C32).
- Time-limited federal climate capital: climate-bank capitalization (CLI-5E), contracts for difference obligated at enactment (CLI-9), the subsidy cost of public-power and transmission-corporation lending (CLI-10, CLI-11) and CLI-5's adaptation-finance and minerals-offtake lines (C37, accepted in Decision Brief 1 in place of trimming CLI-5 under C29, which is not taken). Tax credits and recurring transfers stay paid for.
- CAP-7S, the agency rebuild surge (C42; see D9). Full multi-year funding is available immediately on enactment, targeting R1 in FY2029. Availability is not an outlay: price FY2029 cash separately and reconcile it with the envelope before treating the fiscal tests as complete. No new envelope amount is adopted by this timing decision.

Two further readings apply. Medicare HI's roughly $0.3T ten-year surplus stays as HI's reserve rather than returning to the general fund; it also hedges the risk that Medicare Advantage savings land low. Paid leave has no general-fund backstop: it carries an automatic step-up toward 0.85% and a repayable Treasury loan (domain-family.md, FAM-3).

**How the gap closes.** Accepted revenue is $3.71T: Package B (C18, C35, C19b, C20), the top-end sprint package C38 (REV-TS1 to TS11), C39-C41 (REV-TS12, REV-HP1, REV-HP3) and C43 (REV-SC1, a 45% bracket above $5M, adopted Oct 21). With $1.15T of envelope borrowing, that leaves about +$0.26T over ten years, after the carbon fees' 25% income and payroll tax offset was booked (CX, Oct 21). Program trims C5, C17, C33, C34 and C36 are declined (they may be revisited later). REV-SC1, held in reserve after Decision Brief 2, was adopted on Oct 21 to close the 2040 shortfall; REV-HP2 is folded into REV-TS1's drafting. The accepted revenue rides R2 with the permanent spending it pays for, because the Byrd rule tests each bill's out-years on its own.

**What the current long-term model shows (index/fiscal-long-term-model.md, scenario DEC).** After the October 24 AFA/one-million-voucher adoption, the working midpoint adds $0.49T of permanent-program claims. With existing financing unchanged, DEC has an FY2040 GF primary deficit of about $53.7B (0.099% of GDP); D4's maturity test fails. The ten-year GF primary deficit is about $2.03T and modeled total debt is about 155.1% of GDP in 2056 against the baseline's 180.2%. The prior small FY2040 surplus and 152.1% debt result belong to the earlier package. D4-B is retained, not relaxed; permanent financing and a supported exact score remain open. The CAP-7S cash path remains an interim proxy.

### D7. One carbon price: how big, and what is its revenue for?

Three docs price carbon three ways. The Bill Book rebates an economy-wide fee as a dividend. The Thriving Economy map uses a border adjustment and methane fees as revenue ($120-240B). Moving America raises gasoline and diesel taxes 3¢ a year for ten years, then adds a fossil premium from FY2040. Stacked, gasoline would be taxed twice.

| Option | Design | Revenue to programs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Full fee and dividend (Bill Book) | Economy-wide fee at about $50 a ton, all rebated | About zero; gasoline hit by both fee and fuel steps |
| B. Fee, gasoline exempt while the fuel steps run **(decided)** | Fee on everything else, about 80% rebated as a dividend, equal per adult (CLI-2A, Oct 21; children are covered by FAM-1), and 20% for transition and low-income help; the border adjustment (REV-C2) and the methane charge (REV-C3) are counted separately and finance the add-ons | The 20% share (about $0.29T) plus REV-C2 (about $0.03T) and REV-C3, the methane charge (about $0.02T) |
| C. No fee | Credits, standards, border adjustment and methane fees only | $0.12-0.24T (Thriving Economy) |

**Decided: B. Gasoline and diesel taxes are set in the surface transportation plan; the fee and dividend governs everything else.** CBO's estimates show that exempting gasoline costs a quarter of a carbon tax's revenue. Exempting it avoids a double hit at the pump, keeps the dividend's constituency, and lets Moving America own transport fuels. Both instruments are built in domains/domain-revenue.md.

**Price path (decided, Espen).** REV-C1, the domestic fee, follows the climate durability session's schedule: **$25 a ton in 2030 rising to about $100 by 2037**, road fuels exempt. It raises about $1.25-1.60T over 2030-39 (mid $1.45T, set from index/climate-carbon-model.md on Oct 21). REV-C2, the border adjustment, nets only about $0.03T because export rebates nearly cancel the import charge. REV-C3 reinstates the IRA's methane charge from 2030, $1,200 a ton of methane rising to $1,500 by 2032, with no intensity threshold and the IRA's compliance exemption made facility by facility (a facility that certifies, on an independent audit and measured reporting, that it meets the leak-prevention standards pays nothing): about $0.02T, since the fee is built to be avoided by compliance (Espen, Oct 21). The general fund books a 25% income and payroll tax offset on all three (CX, about −$0.38T). About 80% of the fee is rebated as the Climate Dividend (CLI-2, about $1.16T, equal per adult) and 20% (about $0.29T) goes to transition and low-income help. The 20% share, REV-C2 and REV-C3 fund the Thriving Economy add-ons (CLI-5), so those do not compete with family benefits for top-end revenue. The 20% carve-out keeps about $0.29T out of the dividend, roughly $110 an adult a year (estimate); the D3 test carries it. The lower starting price keeps the fee's bite small in 2030-31, the years before the midterm (incidence model). Other paths: the $50-plus-5% path is option REV-C1B, not chosen; road-fuel convergence (REV-C1D) is an open option, not adopted.

### D8. Social Security: expand first, or fix solvency first?

The Bill Book's expansion pays for higher benefits by applying the payroll tax above $250,000. The revenue tab shows that change alone closes only about half of the 75-year gap before any benefit increase. The 2026 Trustees put the 75-year gap at 4.42% of taxable payroll and the combined trust funds' depletion in 2034, when 83% of benefits are payable.

| Option | Design | Solvency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Bill Book expansion | Across-the-board increase, CPI-E, tax above $250K | Gap remains, and the increase widens it |
| B. Solvency first, targeted increase **(baseline, decided)** | Tax above $250K with a benefit credit; a Social Security tax on investment income above $400K; a higher minimum benefit and CPI-E | About 64% of the 75-year gap, depletion about 2062 (about 84% and 2081 with SS-5); needs a closer (domain-social-security.md) |
| C. Solvency only | Revenue changes, no benefit changes | Closes the gap; no constituency gain |

**Decided: B is the baseline, as its own regular-order bill in spring 2030.** Section 310(g) keeps it out of reconciliation, so it needs D2's rules change. Passing it before the midterm makes solvency a delivered promise rather than a 2032 crisis.

**Still open within D8: the closer.** Against the 4.42% gap the baseline closes about 64% after a 15% stacking haircut on B2, matching the general fund's haircut on the same income (about 68% before it), and moves depletion from 2034 to about 2062. domain-social-security.md recommends adding SS-5, an investment-income tax on S-corporation and partnership owners (+0.87, SSA's 2026 score moved to 2031), which brings it to about 84% and depletion to about 2081; SS-6 (lower SS-2's threshold, now only about +0.1), SS-7 (a phased one-point rate rise) and SS-8 (equities) are the alternatives. The revenue file's top-rate check also bears on this: SS-1, SS-2 and the 5% Medicare rate stack to about 57% federal on wages and interest above $400K. ## Standing design rules

These rules come from the project's framework docs and apply to every policy, whatever the decisions above. A domain file states how each of its options meets them, or why it doesn't.

1. **Reciprocity.** Every rules or structural reform must be one we would accept while in the minority (Radical Means).
2. **Durable instruments.** Prefer mandatory spending, automatic enrollment and statute over appropriations, opt-in programs and executive action. No sunsets on permanent programs. Use a trust fund only where a permanent benefit has a natural revenue stream that covers much or all of its cost, as Social Security does; otherwise fund it from general revenue under D4.
3. **Legibility.** Name the program, route it through one visible channel, write attribution into the statute. Recurring beats one-time.
4. **Pairing.** No structural reform travels alone; announce each with the material benefit it enables.
5. **Supply with demand.** Supply-side capacity (permitting, housing production, child care slots, grid) moves with or before the transfers that raise demand, to manage the inflation risk Instruments and Consequences names.
6. **Capacity first.** Every delivery item names the agency and staffing it depends on.
7. **Write for this Court.** Use the strongest available power (Elections Clause, taxing and spending), aggressive severability, and the legislative record a challenge will test. Prefer mark-to-market and basis reform to a wealth tax.
8. **One version per policy.** Each policy has one canonical design; alternatives are named options in the same file, never separate docs.
9. **Every number has a basis.** Each figure is tagged as sourced, scaled from a cited source, estimated or proposal, with its budget window (default ten years, 2030-39, in trillions).
10. **Payments do not stop.** Every recurring payment carries standing disbursement authority that does not lapse in a shutdown, including FAM-1's monthly advance payments (with express advance-payment authority), the Climate Dividend and EDU-3's reimbursements. From the Talarico stress test; the statute is EXE-18, with an automatic CR (CAP-4) and the debt limit (EXE-18C) as companion options.
