## Standing design rules

1. **Reciprocity.** Every rules or structural reform must be one we would accept in the minority.
2. **Durable instruments.** Prefer mandatory spending, automatic enrollment and statute over appropriations, opt-in programs and executive action. No sunsets on permanent programs. Use a trust fund only where a permanent benefit has a natural revenue stream that covers much or all of its cost; otherwise fund it from general revenue under D4.
3. **Legibility.** Name the program, route it through one visible channel, and write attribution into the statute. Recurring benefits beat one-time payments.
4. **Pairing.** No structural reform travels alone; each is announced with the material benefit it enables.
5. **Supply with demand.** Supply-side capacity moves with or before the transfers that raise demand.
6. **Capacity first.** Every delivery item names the agency and staffing it depends on.
7. **Write for this Court.** Use the strongest available power, aggressive severability and a strong legislative record. Prefer mark-to-market and basis reform to a wealth tax.
8. **One version per policy.** Alternatives are named options in the same file.
9. **Every number has a basis.** Tag each figure as sourced, scaled, estimated or proposal, with its window (default 2030-39, in trillions).

10. **Payments do not stop.** Every recurring payment carries standing disbursement authority that does not lapse in a shutdown, including FAM-1's monthly advance payments (with express advance-payment authority), the Climate Dividend and EDU-3's reimbursements. *Adopted Oct 20 (Espen), from the Talarico stress test; statute EXE-18, with an automatic CR (CAP-4) and the debt limit (EXE-18C) as companion options.*

## Filing conventions (Oct 19)

- **Folder = role.** governing/, domains/, index/, staging/, frameworks/, sources/, archive/. A file's folder says how much authority it has; nothing sits at the project root.
- **Names** are lowercase kebab-case with no spaces, and start with the domain where there is one (health-, revenue-, climate-, courts-, transportation-, public-safety-, education-, scenario-).
- **domains/domain-<name>.md** is a converted canonical file with IDs. An unconverted canonical source sits in domains/ under its domain and title, and is replaced by domain-<name>.md when converted.
- **archive/** prefixes say why a file is there: ideas- (ideas only), wargame- (the 56-seat war-game set), superseded-.
- **index/** holds generated files only (plus the hand-written revenue audit); regenerate them, never edit them.
- New docs go straight into their role folder, not claude/ or the root. Moves keep a file's content unchanged; only path references in agent-written files are updated. (The two PROJECT 2029 docs carry a short archive note above their unchanged content, at Espen's request.)

Stipulated: the bipartisan Senate permitting deal is enacted in 2026-28 under Trump. Tag every item for survival at 51: ✔ survives (reconciliation, state, executive, House-only); ◐ needs a D2 majority-precedent path; ✖ needs 60, 67 or Republican votes in any scenario.
- D2 Senate rules: DECIDED Oct 20: F (graduated cloture: 60, 57, 54, then a majority, about a day apart) with guaranteed minority amendments and equal time, adopted in week one of 2029; C (talking filibuster with a 15-day backstop) is the opening offer to holdouts and G (democracy carve-out) the written fallback. Options in "Senate Rules Reform: Options" (https://claude.ai/code/artifact/0f226c46-5ce8-4bec-9a54-c5fd7e7a5629). Any middle-reaching option is checked in the incidence model (index/) before it is final.

- D7 Carbon: gasoline and diesel are taxed only through Moving America's fuel-tax steps; an economy-wide fee and dividend covers everything else. The fee (REV-C1) runs $25 a ton in 2030 rising to about $100 by 2037 (about $1.35T), with a border adjustment (REV-C2, about $0.20T); about 80% of the fee is rebated, 20% goes to transition and low-income help. Road-fuel convergence is an open option, not adopted.

After a 15% avoidance haircut on the investment-income piece it closes about 65-72% of the 4.42% gap (86-92% with SS-5); the closer is still to choose (SS-5 recommended).

DESIGN RULES
Reciprocity (only rules we'd accept in the minority). Durable instruments: mandatory spending, auto-enrollment, statute, no sunsets on permanent programs; a trust fund only where a permanent benefit has a natural revenue stream covering much or all of its cost. Legibility. Pairing structural reform with material benefits. Supply with demand. Capacity first. Write for this Court. One version per policy. Every number has a basis. Payments do not stop: every recurring payment carries standing disbursement authority that survives a shutdown.
