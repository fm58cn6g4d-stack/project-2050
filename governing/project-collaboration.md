### Claude's first assignment: test the capacity budget

The approved $300B surge and $300B sustained increase are working envelopes, not demonstrated costs. The old agency shares were explicitly unsourced. Start with **IRS, CMS, HUD and the Department of Education**, because they connect household recovery, revenue, health delivery, rental assistance and the education expansion. Reuse the CMS pilot and component registers. Do not repeat the entire pilot, inspect every federal agency, or write an operating manual.

For each institution, distinguish current recurring spending, temporary/supplemental authority and actual outlays; staffing/contractors and workload where supported; restorative costs versus genuinely additional agenda workload; and money already financed inside HLT, HOU, EDU or another program. ED alone does not administer child care or meals: flag ACF/FNS interfaces without pretending their costs are inside ED's operating budget. Agency benefit payments are not administrative operating costs.

Build a bounded first allocation with an explicit unallocated remainder for other agencies. Do not spend the full envelope on these four. Separate finite rebuild investment from ongoing operations and a plausible FY2029 cash path from immediate legal availability. Test whether IRS receipts are gross or net of enforcement costs, and how the smaller allocation affects the $200B risk allowance. If the source cannot settle it, show the competing interpretations and their fiscal effect; do not count a net revenue score and its costs twice.

Return a readable recommendation, a compact source-backed annual/component table, and a small reproducible adapter if arithmetic warrants one. Suggested owned paths: `sources/capacity-recovery-budget-review.md`, `sources/capacity-recovery-components.csv`, and `index/tools/capacity-review/`. Minimum useful table fields are agency/component, fiscal year, nominal $B, current-law/new/transfer/overlap classification, recurring/temporary, authority/outlay distinction, range or unknown, source URL/table/date, and affected policy IDs. Add fields only when they help the decision. Unsupported values remain unknown; a planning estimate must be labeled as such.

The recommendation should say what to retain, reduce, expand or reserve; what delivery or revenue changes with each choice; and whether the approved envelope needs revision. It may include a proposed canonical patch, but the shared fiscal owner applies a coordinated change to common inputs and outputs. Stop at one reviewable package. Do not silently open a second workstream once the first is waiting for review.

## Parallel work without conflicting versions

1. **Verify and reserve.** Fetch remote `main`; inspect open PRs and the current queue. Record task, source SHA, owner/session, branch, owned paths and intended result. An early draft PR is the visible reservation when available. The queue is an index, not a transactional lock. If two claims overlap, narrow the work or use an independent path; do not overwrite the other branch or wait indefinitely for an unresponsive session. Recheck claims after a long pause.
2. **Isolate work.** Each author uses a separate task branch and checkout/worktree from current `main`. Never change branches, reset files or run a destructive clean in another agent's working directory. Preserve unknown local changes; recover them on a separate branch if needed. A resumed Claude session must inspect its local tree before updating it.
3. **Own the integration points.** While these assignments run, Codex coordinates `index/tools/recovery-budget/`, shared fiscal/incidence inputs, `recovery-adoption.json`, common generated outputs and cross-domain adoption changes. Claude owns its capacity research/adapter paths. Queue, options and governing records are shared: include a proposed update in the PR, then refresh it against the latest main during integration. This is temporary write coordination, not exclusive decision authority.
4. **Exchange artifacts, not recollections.** Use actual files and immutable commits/PRs. A handoff identifies new findings, decisions, assumptions, changed paths, commands/results, remaining gaps and the proposed budget/input delta. Read the cited files before combining findings. Apply the [file-exchange protocol](project-file-exchange.md); don't rely on Library access or screenshots when repository text exists.
5. **Integrate one package at a time.** Review policy and accounting meaning before regenerating the shared model. Fetch again; reconcile both authors' intended semantics, not merely merge conflicts. Run the affected generators once on the combined inputs. Preserve historical inputs/output vintages and distinguish current-law dollars, adopted additions and exploratory cases. A new source estimate is not automatically additive spending.
6. **Check and finish.** The author runs relevant tests; an independent review is valuable for major fiscal/benefit decisions, but do not claim it is a legal or actuarial score. Record available reviews and limits. Open a coherent PR. Merge only with applicable authorization; PR #20's authorization was specific and is already used. Verify remote merge status before marking work integrated. Reassign the next bounded task after results, rather than launch the whole queue.

A useful independent review attempts to break the result: a double-counted baseline, missing household group, inadequate reserve, optimistic take-up or behavioral assumption, or an alternative that does the same job for less. It need not repeat every test or ask a different model to endorse the prose. When agents disagree on a project element, compare the evidence and consequences and make a documented decision under the delegation; return genuine goal conflicts to Espen. Do not settle substantive questions by model majority vote.
