# Espen: Writing Style Guide

Version 1 · October 22, 2026

Working guidance inferred from Espen’s own writing and discussed with them. These are starting observations, not rules Espen has individually endorsed. Their task-specific instructions and subsequent edits take precedence. This document is a reusable reference; it does not itself configure account memory or guarantee automatic use in other chats.

## Core voice

Write in connected paragraphs that develop a thought. State the claim, explain the reasoning or mechanism, and show why it matters. Sentence length can vary; preserve the relationships between ideas rather than forcing every sentence to be short. Use concrete language where possible and technical vocabulary where a distinction requires it.

Be willing to take a position and identify its limits. Distinguish what an argument establishes, what it assumes, and what remains unresolved. Qualifications should locate uncertainty precisely rather than make every claim hesitant. First-person phrases such as “I argue” and “I think” are natural when the genre calls for them.

Connect abstract questions to recognizable situations. Examples should test or advance an argument. In longer work, return to an earlier case with the additional distinctions developed along the way. Explain why a distinction matters before asking readers to retain it.

Keep ethical and political commitments visible while explaining how change might occur. Recommendations should name actors, mechanisms, constraints, and interactions when the evidence supports them. Preserve the distinction between desired outcomes and demonstrated effects.

Personal warmth is candid and specific. Humor is occasional and understated, often a brief aside or an observation about the writer’s own situation. Do not manufacture jokes, intimate disclosures, or personal history to imitate this feature.

## Evidence behind the guidance

- The thesis begins with an everyday grocery-store categorization mistake, develops more consequential cases, and returns to those cases as its framework becomes more precise. Its analysis distinguishes people, human kinds, and concepts; later chapters distinguish mistakes about grounds from injustice within classificatory concepts.
- The SAF memo recommends pooling airline demand, explains why this could reduce uncertainty for producers, and connects that approach to the attractiveness of Port land for production facilities. Its recommendations are sequenced rather than treated as isolated options.
- The SAIS statement illustrates climate communication through a fisherman responding to descriptions of storms and floods. Experience supports the statement’s intellectual interests.
- The public service statement introduces understated humor through the promise never to become a teacher, then builds a concrete account of organizing community activity before reflecting on public service.
- The personal challenge essay is candid about difficulty while maintaining agency, purpose, and attention to shared concerns.
- The conceptual ethics essay carefully limits its claims about concepts’ normative status while defending the normative significance of defining them.

## Drafting and review

1. Establish the actual purpose, audience, factual basis, and length. Choose the closest reference by genre and purpose. For current policy claims, verify facts independently; style samples are not current factual authorities.
2. Build the reasoning before polishing sentences. Give each paragraph a clear job and connect it to the next. Preserve useful nuance while removing accidental repetition and errors.
3. Review for voice: Does the draft explain rather than merely announce? Do examples do analytical work? Do qualifications identify a real limit? Would Espen plausibly use this level of warmth, technical detail, and first-person presence for this audience?
4. Remove generic enthusiasm, inflated self-praise, stock transitions, and strings of slogans. Do not force thesis terminology, chapter-style signposting, or long sentences into a short email. Do not reproduce typos or awkward phrasing as stylistic markers.
5. Keep facts, commitments, and personal disclosures grounded in the task. Never invent an experience or opinion to make a draft sound more personal. Distinguish new proposed language from Espen’s established positions.
