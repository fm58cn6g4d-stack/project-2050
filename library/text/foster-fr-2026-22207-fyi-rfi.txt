DEPARTMENT OF HOUSING AND URBAN DEVELOPMENT

Request for Information To Promote the Success of the Melania
Trump Foster Youth to Independence (FYI) Initiative
AGENCY: Office of the Assistant Secretary for Public and Indian Housing
(PIH), HUD.
ACTION: Request for information.
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
SUMMARY: HUD is issuing this Request for Information (RFI) to solicit
feedback from stakeholders on the Melania Trump Foster Youth to
Independence (FYI) initiative. HUD will use the information gathered
through this RFI to make recommendations to Congress (1) to improve
program operations; (2) to assist youth in navigating the housing
search process and preventing returns to homelessness after the
subsidized rental assistance sunsets; and (3) on how Federal, State,
and local stakeholders can best support participants as they transition
from foster care to successful adulthood.

SUPPLEMENTARY INFORMATION:
I. Background
The Melania Trump Foster Youth to Independence (FYI) was
established in 2019 in response to the advocacy of foster youth and
alumni regarding the need for housing assistance for young people upon
their exit from foster care. According to the Adoption and Foster Care
Analysis and Reporting System, on average 15,000 young people age-out
of foster care each year, and nearly 25 percent of youth responding to
the National Youth in Transition Database report experiencing
homelessness after leaving foster care. Yet, there were no Federal
housing resources solely dedicated to serving this population.
Prior to the establishment of FYI, the primary Federal housing
resource for young people transitioning out of foster care was the
Family Unification Program (FUP). Through public housing agencies
(PHAs), FUP provides housing choice vouchers (HCVs) that can be issued
to either child welfare-involved families or youth transitioning out of
foster care.\1\ FUP participants use their HCVs to rent housing in the
private market and generally pay 30 percent of their adjusted monthly
income towards rent while the voucher pays the remaining portion of the
rent. However, FUP vouchers are only available in a small number of
communities \2\ and have been primarily used to assist child welfare-
involved families, rather than youth transitioning out of foster care.
When FYI was established in 2019, 95 percent of FUP vouchers were being
used to serve child welfare-involved families, and the remaining 5
percent were being used to serve youth transitioning out of foster
care.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\1\ There are two eligible populations for FUP: (1) Families for
whom the lack of adequate housing is a primary factor in the
imminent placement of the family's child or children in out-of-home
care or the delayed discharge of the child or children to the family
from out-of-home care; and (2) youth who have attained at least 18
years of age and not more than 24 years of age and who have left
foster care, or will leave foster care within 180 days, in
accordance with a transition plan described in section 475(5)(H) of
the Social Security Act, and is homeless or is at risk of becoming
homeless at age 16 or older.
\2\ There are approximately 300 PHAs that administer FUP out of
the nearly 2,000 PHAs nationwide that administer the HCV program.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
FYI provides a dedicated source of vouchers for youth transitioning
out of foster care. Since fiscal year (FY) 2020, Congress has
appropriated a total of $165 million for new incremental FYI vouchers.
Further, in FY 2021, Congress codified an on-demand distribution
mechanism for FYI vouchers, meaning that any of the more than 2,000
PHAs administering the HCV program can establish an FYI program and
request FYI vouchers on a rolling basis to serve youth as they are
identified.\3\ This enables HUD to provide young people with FYI
vouchers that are timed with their emancipation from foster care so
that they do not experience homelessness when they leave care.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\3\ Notice PIH 2026-19 ( https://www.hud.gov/sites/default/files/PIH/documents/PIH-2026-19.pdf ) is the most recent notice
implementing this on-demand process.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
To establish an FYI program, a PHA must enter into a partnership
agreement with a public child welfare agency (PCWA). Under the
partnership agreement, the PHA provides the youth with FYI voucher
assistance for 36 months, which may be extended for up to an additional
24 months if the youth is working toward economic self-sufficiency or
meets one of the statutory exceptions to this requirement.\4\ The
[[Page 68535]]
PCWA provides or secures the provision of supportive services for the
youth for 36 months. The combination of time-limited rental assistance
and supportive services is intended to provide youth with a platform to
move toward economic self-sufficiency.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\4\ The Fostering Stable Housing Opportunities (FSHO)
amendments, enacted as section 103 of division Q of the Consolidated
Appropriations Act, 2021 on December 27, 2020 (Pub. L. 116-260),
provide an extension of FYI assistance for up to 24 months for youth
participating in a Family Self-Sufficiency (FSS) program under
section 23 of the U.S. Housing Act of 1937 and for youth who are
unable to enroll in an FSS program who engaged in education,
workforce development, or employment activities for at least 9
months of the 12-month period preceding the extension. FSHO also
provides an extension of assistance for up to 24 months for youth
who meet one of the following exceptions: (1) The youth is a parent
or other household member responsible for the care of a dependent
child under the age of 6 or for the care of an incapacitated person;
(2) The youth is a person who is regularly and actively
participating in a drug addiction or alcohol treatment and
rehabilitation program; or (3) The youth is a person who is
incapable of complying with the requirement to participate in an FSS
program or engage in education, workforce development, or employment
activities, as applicable, due to a documented medical condition.
See HUD's FSHO implementation notice published in the Federal
Register on January 24, 2022 (87 FR 3570).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Since the rollout of FYI, HUD has allocated more than 9,000 FYI
vouchers to assist individual youth. Yet, HUD recognizes that many more
youth can benefit from the program. While FYI has experienced steady
growth with the program being administered by 373 PHAs in 48 states and
the U.S. Virgin Islands as of June 2026 Voucher Management System (VMS)
data, there are still many communities where youth cannot access FYI.
Further, as of June 2026 VMS data, eligible youth are utilizing only 64
percent of the more than 8,000 available FYI vouchers to lease a unit.
HUD has heard anecdotally that the uptake of FYI vouchers has been
limited due to various administrative barriers that communities and
young people face when trying to access FYI. Through this RFI, HUD is
interested in learning what statutory, regulatory, and policy
requirements are preventing communities from participating in FYI; what
barriers young people face in accessing and utilizing an FYI voucher;
and what changes can be made to reduce these barriers. Since FY 2024,
Congress has provided HUD with the authority to establish waivers and
alternative requirements related to the administration of waiting
lists, local preferences, the initial term and extensions of tenant-
based vouchers, and the timing of referral of youth leaving foster
care, in order to reduce barriers to accessing and utilizing an FYI
voucher. HUD implemented this waiver authority through Notice PIH 2025-
08. HUD is interested in learning whether statutory or regulatory
barriers remain and, if so, what statutory changes and/or waiver
authority for the HCV program or FYI program beyond that which has
already been provided would help increase utilization of FYI. HUD is
also interested in learning how PHAs are taking advantage of the
changes that were recently made through Notice PIH 2026-19 to help
expand access to FYI, specifically the elimination of the utilization
threshold and the removal of the cap on the number of FYI vouchers that
can be requested.
HUD has also heard that the lack of dedicated Federal resources for
supportive services can pose a barrier to establishing an FYI program,
particularly in smaller, rural communities where there are few or no
local resources for supportive services. HUD welcomes comments on how
Federal resources can be better targeted or coordinated to reduce these
barriers and support youth as they transition from foster care to
successful adulthood. Additionally, HUD is interested in feedback on
what changes can be made to the program to ensure that it provides a
platform for youth to achieve economic self-sufficiency consistent with
the goals of the Fostering the Future initiative established under
Executive Order 14359, Fostering the Future for American Children and
Families.
II. Purpose of This Request for Information
HUD is interested in promoting and strengthening FYI and supporting
the goals of the Fostering the Future initiative established under
Executive Order 14359, Fostering the Future for American Children and
Families. HUD will share the information received through this RFI with
the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) and separately
consult with HHS on these topics. In accordance with Senate Report 119-
47, HUD will use the information gathered through this RFI and its
consultation with HHS to issue a report to Congress with its
recommendations: (1) to improve program operations; (2) to assist youth
in navigating the housing search process and preventing returns to
homelessness after the subsidized rental assistance sunsets; and (3) on
how Federal, State, and local stakeholders can best support
participants as they transition from foster care to successful
adulthood.
III. Request for Information
Senate Report 119-47 requests that HUD consult with stakeholders on
FUP, which includes FYI. While FUP serves two eligible populations--
families and youth--HUD interprets the Senate Report language as being
specific to the youth population served under FUP. For example, the
Senate Report suggests that HUD consult with stakeholders on statutory
time limits, which only apply to the youth population served under FUP.
Further, the Senate Report asks HUD to use the information gathered
through the stakeholder consultations to make recommendations to
Congress on areas that are specific to youth (i.e., assisting youth in
navigating the housing search process, preventing returns to
homelessness after the subsidized rental assistance ends, and
supporting participants as they transition from foster care to
successful adulthood). Therefore, HUD is issuing this RFI to solicit
feedback on FYI specifically from current and former foster youth,
PHAs, PCWAs, experts on child welfare, and other stakeholders regarding
any potential program challenges due to statutory time limits,
statutory criteria, policy definitions, or other operational aspects of
FYI. While this RFI focuses on FYI, HUD also welcomes comments from
PHAs that do not participate in FYI but administer FUP vouchers issued
to youth, particularly those administering the FUP-FSS demonstration
established under Notice PIH 2016-01 ( https://www.hud.gov/sites/documents/16-01pihn.pdf ). HUD also welcomes comments from PHAs that do
not currently participate in FYI regarding barriers to implementing an
FYI program.
Consistent with Senate Report 119-47, HUD is requesting information
on the topics below but welcomes comments on all aspects of FYI.
Statutory time limits. By statute, a youth may receive FYI
assistance for 36 months, which may be extended for up to an additional
24 months if the youth meets one of the following criteria:
1. The youth is participating in an FSS program under section 23 of
the U.S. Housing Act of 1937;
2. If the youth is unable to enroll in an FSS program, the youth
engaged in education, workforce development, or employment activities
for at least 9 months of the 12-month period preceding the extension;
or
3. The youth meets one of the following exceptions: the youth is a
parent or other household member responsible for the care of a
dependent child under the age of 6 or for the care of an incapacitated
person; the youth is a person who is regularly and actively
participating in a drug addiction or alcohol treatment and
rehabilitation program; or the youth is a person who is incapable of
complying with the requirement to participate in an FSS program or
engage in education, workforce development, or employment activities,
as applicable, due to a documented medical condition.
Questions for consideration:
Is 36 months, with an opportunity for a 24-month
extension, the appropriate amount of time for youth to achieve self-
sufficiency? If not, what would be the appropriate amount of time?
Have youth been able to take advantage of the 24-month
extension? If not, why not?
Are there other types of self-sufficiency activities that
should qualify youth for the 24-month extension in addition to those
already recognized under the law?
[[Page 68536]]
Are there unique challenges for PHAs to administer a time-
limited voucher in comparison to regular vouchers?

1. Is at least 18 years of age and not more than 24 years of age;
2. Has left foster care, or will leave foster care within 180
days,\5\ in accordance with a transition plan described in section
475(5)(H) of the Social Security Act; and

Questions for consideration:
Does the age eligibility requirement pose a barrier to
otherwise eligible youth accessing FYI?
HUD recently extended the referral timeframe from 90 days
to 180 days prior to when a youth will transition out of foster care in
order to allow more time for the youth to find housing with an FYI
voucher prior to leaving foster care. Is 180 days enough time or would
another timeframe be more appropriate?
While not required by statute, HUD has historically
defined ``homeless'' and ``at risk of becoming homeless'' in the FYI
program using the definitions for ``homeless'' and ``at risk of
homelessness'' in the Emergency Solutions Grant (ESG) program and the
Continuum of Care (CoC) program. In the most recent FYI notice,\6\ HUD
no longer uses the ESG and CoC definition of ``at risk of
homelessness'' verbatim but has made several changes to the definition
to expand who is considered ``at risk of becoming homeless'' for FYI
vouchers awarded through the non-competitive process. Specifically, HUD
revised the definition to change the income eligibility requirement
from 30 percent of area median income to 50 percent of area median
income, removed the requirement to not have sufficient resources or
support networks immediately available to prevent moving to an
emergency shelter or another place described in the ESG and CoC
definition of ``homeless,'' and revised several items on the list of
additional conditions. Are these the appropriate definitions to apply
or should HUD use different definitions of ``homeless'' and ``at risk
of becoming homeless'' in the FYI program?
