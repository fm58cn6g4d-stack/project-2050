Introduction
Chairman Amodei, Ranking Member Cuellar, and distinguished Members of the
Subcommittee:
Thank you for the invitation to appear today to discuss the vital work of U.S.
Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) and our Fiscal Year (FY) 2027 President’s Budget
submission. It is an honor and a privilege to lead the 30,215 dedicated men and women who have
answered the call to serve our nation through enforcing our immigration and customs laws. I am
exceedingly proud of our workforce for their dedication to the agency and our mission.
Despite routine villainization, ICE personnel are working around the clock to carry out
President Trump’s commonsense agenda to make Americans safe again, restore order to our
communities, and implement law-and-order policies. In the wake of an unprecedented number of
illegal aliens released into our country under the prior administration, ICE continues to enhance
its role and delegated responsibility to enforce longstanding U.S. immigration law duly enacted
by Congress. However, this increased enforcement activity has been met with increased threats,
assaults, and doxing incidents against ICE employees across the country. In FY 2025 alone,
death threats against ICE personnel increased by more than 8,000 percent from FY 2024. Over
the course of 2025, assaults increased more than 1,400 percent compared to 2024.
Notwithstanding these challenges, ICE continues to implement and execute highly
impactful programs that strengthen our national security, enhance public safety, and restore
integrity to our immigration system. Last summer, Congress passed the Working Families Tax
Cut Act, which included a historic $75 billion for ICE. This bill is fundamentally transforming
the agency’s approach to immigration enforcement. It has enabled ICE to hire 11,000 new
officers and agents and over 1,800 new attorneys. It also enables implementation of a new
detention model, expedites the removal of at least one million illegal aliens annually, and
expands the Homeland Security Task Forces to combat transnational organized crime. As of
March 19, 2026, ICE has obligated $11 billion of Working Families Tax Cut Act funding, and we
will continue to execute the funding to meet the Administration’s priorities and goals, especially
facilitating the deportation of illegal aliens.
While ICE has made progress in executing the Working Families Tax Cut Act over the
last several months, this hearing comes at a critical time for the agency. Members of Congress,
the media, and others incorrectly assert that ICE is somehow unimpeded by the lapse in annual
appropriations. These statements are categorically false. The impacts of the ongoing failure to
fund ICE through the appropriations process have been dire, as numerous ICE personnel and
operations have gone unfunded, including much of ICE’s Homeland Security Investigations
(HSI). While we appreciate the opportunity Congress provided ICE with enactment of the

Working Families Tax Cut Act, ICE cannot rely solely on the funding this bill provides. The law
provides resources for specific programs and activities — namely, to surge or expand ICE’s

programs. As the longest shutdown in history continues, ICE implores the House to pass the
Senate DHS funding bill and work expeditiously to enact a second reconciliation measure by

Additionally, the annual appropriations process is critical for our base operations and our
ability to meet the Administration’s goals. Therefore, the FY 2027 President’s Budget requests
$10 billion in net discretionary funding for the agency. This funding, when combined with the
historic $75 billion from the Working Families Tax Cut Act, will enable ICE to bolster
immigration enforcement and better implement President Trump’s vision for America’s
immigration system.
Today, I am thankful for the opportunity to highlight the work of the dedicated men and
women of ICE, and to discuss the agency’s continued efforts to make the most effective and
efficient use of our resources as we execute ICE’s critical mission.
Enforcement and Removal Operations (ERO)
ERO protects the homeland by arresting and removing aliens who undermine the safety
of U.S. communities and who break federal law. The FY 2027 budget requests $5.4 billion to
sustain ERO’s operations, thus ensuring ICE can continue its robust efforts to focus on threats to
national security and public safety. This budget allows ICE to continue its increased immigration
enforcement operational tempo, maintains partnerships with state and local law enforcement,
supports 41,500 detention beds, promotes best practices in civil detention and facility
compliance, allows the agency to manage aliens on ICE’s non-detained docket, and facilitates the
removal of illegal aliens.
The FY 2027 budget requests approximately $163 million for ICE’s Fugitive Operations,
through which ERO identifies, locates, and arrests removable aliens in the United States, and
$293 million for its Criminal Alien Program, which allows ERO to arrest criminal aliens
incarcerated within federal, state, or local jails and prisons. These two critical funding sources
permit ICE to identify and arrest aliens unlawfully present in the United States. Since January
20, 2025, ICE arrested nearly 457,000 aliens. 1 Among those arrested were more than 281,000
aliens with criminal histories, 8,400 suspected gang members, 1,600 known or suspected
terrorists, and 54,000 special interest aliens, making our country and our communities
dramatically safer in the process. Moreover, Criminal Alien Program funding enables ICE to
continue expanding the number of state and local law enforcement partners through its 287(g)

Additionally, these immigration enforcement efforts led to an increase in the amount of

ICE’s increased enforcement efforts over the last 15 months have led to a need for more
detention bedspace. While the Working Families Tax Cut Act provides additional beds to meet
surge detention requirements, the FY 2027 budget includes $3.7 billion to maintain Custody
Operations. At this funding level, the FY 2027 budget sustains 41,500 detention beds.
Throughout the past year, ICE has explored various options to increase its bedspace with federal,
state, local, and private partners. Currently, ICE is reimagining its detention structure and
acquisition strategy. These efforts aim to meet the growing demand for bedspace, streamline the
detention and removal process, and focus on non-traditional facilities that are built specifically to
support ICE’s needs. We have briefed this Subcommittee on those plans, and ICE looks forward
to continuing to work with you going forward. As a result of the Working Families Tax Cut Act
funding and FY 2027 budget, ICE will ensure a safe and secure environment for aliens in its
custody while working to remove them from the United States in furtherance of interior
immigration enforcement.
Moreover, through its Title 8 authority, ICE removes aliens with final orders of removal
from the United States. The FY 2027 budget includes $748 million so that ERO can coordinate
the safe and secure transportation of illegal aliens who are subject to final orders of removal or
require custodial transfer within the interior of the United States. With this funding, ICE’s
Transportation and Removal Program will use flights, ground transportation, and international
cooperation to successfully and efficiently deport illegal aliens with final orders from the United
States.
Last year, President Trump tasked ICE with effectuating interior enforcement in
accordance with existing law, and we are taking every step possible to fulfill that mandate. Since
January 20, 2025, ICE removed more than 565,000 aliens. 3 To meet one of President Trump’s
key immigration initiatives, ICE developed the Incentivized Voluntary Departure (IVD) program,
which is separate from ICE’s overall removals. Designed to complement DHS’s self-deportation
program, IVD offers eligible aliens — those recently arrested by ICE or in ICE custody — the

Homeland Security Investigations
Because of its unique authorities and footprint, HSI is the federal law enforcement
agency that is best positioned to investigate and dismantle transnational criminal organizations
and narco-terrorist organizations. The FY 2027 budget requests $2.6 billion to maintain HSI
operations at current levels, empowering HSI to continue its critical investigations and
enforcement activities.
Under the Trump Administration, HSI continues to deliver exceptional results. HSI has
led large-scale, intelligence-driven operations nationwide; intercepted dangerous narcotics such
as fentanyl before they reached our communities; and rescued unaccompanied alien children
from abusive and exploitative situations. Specifically, in 2025, HSI:
• Arrested over 500 criminal sponsors for offenses including child sexual exploitation,
forced labor, identity fraud, immigration violations, and aggravated assault.
• Conducted more than 4,800 arrests related to child exploitation and rescued nearly 1,500
child victims from ongoing sexual abuse.
• Dismantled more than 2,400 human smuggling operations, including over 3,100
convictions related to human smuggling, human trafficking, and child exploitation.
• Reached 114 million people nationwide through public awareness campaigns focused on
preventing child trafficking and sponsor fraud.
• Seized more than 2.5 million pounds of narcotics, representing a 53 percent increase from
FY 2024. This total includes more than 20,000 pounds of fentanyl and nearly one million
pounds of precursor chemicals — dangerous substances capable of devastating entire
communities that were intercepted before they could reach American streets.
• In support of the Administration’s efforts to impose maximum pressure on Venezuela and
Iran, HSI export enforcement and sanctions investigations led to the issuance of three
judicial seizure warrants. These warrants resulted in the seizure of three oil tankers that
were involved in illegally transporting Venezuelan- and Iranian-origin oil on behalf of the

Since President Trump took office, HSI has also significantly expanded enforcement
efforts to protect unaccompanied alien children and hold offenders accountable. HSI special
agents have conducted more than 72,000 field engagements since February 2025, including doorto-door interviews, to locate children, verify sponsor legitimacy, and uncover criminal activity

investigations, intelligence gathering, and close coordination with federal, state, local, and
foreign law enforcement partners to identify fraudulent sponsors, dismantle trafficking networks,
and pursuing individuals who prey on vulnerable children. Incredibly, under President Trump’s
leadership, HSI special agents and others within DHS law enforcement and the Department of
Health and Human Services have located more than 145,000 unaccompanied alien children that
the Biden Administration “lost.”
Additionally, HSI has an unparalleled capability to combat transnational criminal
organizations on a global scale. The FY 2027 budget increases funding for international
operations to ensure that HSI can leverage its global partnerships to combat transnational crime
and safeguard national security. Specifically, the FY 2027 budget request includes a $2 million
increase for Capital Security Cost Sharing to help protect our HSI personnel at 81 international
embassies and consulates. The budget also includes an increase of $3 million for International
Cooperative Support Services, which is the cost-sharing service provided by U.S. embassies
overseas. Both efforts are critical to supporting our HSI international operations to detect and
deter national security and public safety threats before they reach the homeland.
Office of the Principal Legal Advisor (OPLA)
The FY 2027 budget reflects a total of $469 million for OPLA, which is the largest legal
program in the Department of Homeland Security and one of the largest law offices in the nation.
Additionally, OPLA serves as the Federal Government’s exclusive representative in removal

5

proceedings before the Department of Justice’s Executive Office for Immigration Review. The
FY 2027 budget includes an additional $8.7 million for the OPLA Principal Legal Advisor
Network. This is a strategic initiative that will help address OPLA’s increased workload and
administrative needs, thereby allowing OPLA’s attorneys to focus on litigating cases before the
Executive Office for Immigration Review and providing legal advice to ICE programs.
For example, OPLA attorneys, paralegals, researchers, and support personnel work under
tremendous caseloads related to removal proceedings and a variety of immigration laws while
also providing counsel to ICE officials. In FY 2025, OPLA represented DHS in nearly 2 million
removal hearings before the Executive Office for Immigration Review. This number includes
master calendar hearings, hearings held for pleadings, scheduling, and other similar matters, as
well as other individual hearings where substantive legal arguments and applications for relief
and protection from removal were presented and decided. In FY 2025, they managed more than
1,900 human rights-violator cases and over 4,000 national security cases nationwide. The
increased attorney staffing enabled by the Working Families Tax Cut Act has been instrumental
in reducing the enormous backlog in immigration court. This enhanced capacity has allowed
legal resources to be strategically allocated to complex cases, particularly those involving fraud
and threats to public safety and national security. Additionally, the expanded hiring has improved
essential client services and strengthened OPLA’s ability to support the Department of Justice in
prosecuting criminal and civil immigration violations in federal court. These improvements — in
concert with the continued support provided by the annual appropriations — directly contribute
to the integrity and efficiency of the immigration system and reinforce the agency’s commitment
to upholding the rule of law.
Mission Support
Our FY 2027 budget requests $1.6 billion for Mission Support activities, programs, and
personnel. While the Working Families Tax Cut Act supported the hiring, outfitting, training and
infrastructure needs of these additional personnel, our FY 2027 annual appropriations are critical
to providing the ongoing support and infrastructure needed to support the workforce.
Mission Support is the backbone of the agency and advances every aspect of ICE’s
mission. Our dedicated Mission Support professionals address the agency’s expansive
operational needs by providing a solid, integrated information technology infrastructure; a
dynamic human capital program encompassing proactive recruitment endeavors and preemployment suitability; the development of acquisition strategies and oversight of procurement
activities and contracts; and sound and prudent stewardship and protection of all ICE resources
and information. The FY 2027 budget will ensure foundational services are fortified and continue
to advance in key areas, such as a $25 million increase for General Services Administration rent.

Mission Support also led the agency’s unprecedented 2025 hiring campaign that resulted
in ICE hiring approximately 11,000 new personnel in less than a year. This effort followed the
receipt of over 250,000 applications. With funding provided under the Working Families Tax Cut
Act, ICE rapidly acquired over 11,000 vehicles and secured leases in over 100 locations to
provide the additional personnel hired by ICE with the resources and facilities needed to perform
ICE’s mission. In addition, ICE executed 7,401 contract actions totaling $9 billion, thus
advancing ICE’s mission and supporting key priorities. Mission Support personnel also executed
more than 200 service agreements with state and local law enforcement agencies under the
287(g) Task Force Model, significantly expanding joint operational capacity.
With the Working Families Tax Cut Act funding and our FY 2027 budget, ICE will
continue to maintain ICE’s IT infrastructure. In FY 2025, ICE has been able to advance its IT
modernization efforts by proactively deploying artificial intelligence tools — including the I-9
Portal, HSI Tipline, and ERO’s Enhanced Leads Identification & Targeting Enforcement system.
These tools improve data analysis, lead identification, and operational decision-making. ICE’s
modernization initiatives ensure technology meets current and future operational requirements,
enabling the agency to more effectively support its personnel and mission. By adapting to
evolving challenges, ICE demonstrates its commitment to leveraging innovative solutions to
protect the nation, disrupt criminal activity, and optimize law enforcement operations.
Included among our dedicated Mission Support personnel is the agency’s Office of
Professional Responsibility (OPR), which is responsible for promoting the agency’s integrity and
accountability through a multi-disciplinary approach of security, inspections, and investigations.
The FY 2027 budget includes $204 million to continue OPR’s critical operations. In FY 2025,
OPR vetted an unprecedented number of individuals in support of ICE’s hiring surge, in
furtherance of the President and the Secretary’s bold agenda to enforce our laws and detain and
remove illegal aliens in a safe and humane manner. Additionally, OPR’s Office of Detention
Oversight conducted more than 100 compliance inspections, 20 detainee death reviews, and
more than 30 Prison Rape Elimination Act (PREA) audits to ensure the agency’s ongoing
compliance with ICE policy and legal requirements. Furthermore, OPR oversees the agency’s
287(g) Jail Enforcement Model, ensuring our law enforcement partners remain compliant with
the terms of their Memoranda of Agreement with ICE.
As evidenced by all these contributions, ICE law enforcement personnel could not have
accomplished their crucial work without the commitment and dedication of Mission Support
personnel.
