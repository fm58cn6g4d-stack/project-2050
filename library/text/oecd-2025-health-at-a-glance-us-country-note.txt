United States
Health at a Glance provides a comprehensive set of indicators on population health and health system
performance across OECD Members, Key Partners and accession candidate countries. These indicators
cover health status, non-medical determinants and risk factors, access to and quality of healthcare, health
spending and health system resources. Analysis draws from the latest comparable official national
statistics and other sources.
This country note shows how the United States compares to other OECD countries across a selection of
key indicators from the report.

How does the United States perform overall?
Health status and risk factors
The United States performs better than the OECD average on 3 out of 10 key indicators measuring health
status and risk factors for health.

Health status
•

In the United States, life expectancy was 78.4 years, 2.7 years below the OECD average.

•

Preventable mortality was 217 per 100 000 in the United States (higher than the OECD average of
145); with treatable mortality at 95 per 100 000 (higher than the OECD average of 77).

•

Suicide rates were 15 per 100 000 population in the United States, compared to the OECD
average of 11 deaths per 100 000 population.

•

3.4% of people in the United States rated their health as bad or very bad (OECD average 8.0%).

Risk factors for health
•

Daily smoking prevalence in the United States, at 8.0%, was lower than the OECD average of
14.8%.

•

Alcohol consumption was similar to the OECD average; at 9.5 litres per capita in the United States
versus 8.5.

•

36% of adults in the United States did not perform sufficient physical activity, higher than the OECD
average of 30%.

•

Self-reported obesity prevalence was 35% in the United States, higher than the OECD average of
19%.

Quality of care
•

94% of eligible children were vaccinated against DTP in the United States, similar to the OECD
average.

•

80% of women in the United States were screened for breast cancer, higher than the OECD
average of 55%.

•

No comparable data are available on antibiotic prescriptions.

•

There were 733 avoidable admissions per 100 000 population in the United States, more than the
OECD average of 473.

•

In the United States, 30-day mortality after AMI was 5.2% (OECD average 6.5%), and 4.5% after
stroke (OECD average 7.7%), based on unlinked data.

Health spending
•

The United States spends $14 885 per capita on health, more than the OECD average of $5 967
(USD PPP).

•

This is equal to 17.2% of GDP, compared to 9.3% on average in the OECD.

•

The United States spends 3.6% of total health spending on prevention in current health
expenditure, similar to the OECD average of 3.4%.

Health workforce
•

There are 2.7 practising doctors per 1 000 population in the United States (OECD average 3.9);
and 12.4 practising nurses (OECD average 9.2).

•

The number of long-term care workers per 100 people aged 65 years and over in the United States
is 4.1, similar to the OECD average of 5.0.

•

There are 99 pharmacists per 100 000 population in the United States, compared to the OECD
average of 86.

Hospitals and equipment
•

The United States has 2.8 hospital beds per 1 000 population, less than the OECD average of 4.2.

Key features of Health at a Glance
Health at a Glance provides a comprehensive set of indicators on population health and health system
performance across OECD Members, Key Partners and accession candidate countries. These
indicators cover health status, non-medical determinants and risk factors, access to and quality of
healthcare, health spending and health system resources. Analysis draws from the latest comparable
official national statistics and other sources.
Alongside indicator-by-indicator analysis, an overview chapter summarises the comparative
performance of countries and major trends. This edition also includes a thematic chapter on gender and
health.
