State Readiness

State Readiness
• Stakeholders shared that most states have made significant progress
to implement on January 1, 2027, but many will need to continue
making updates to information technology (IT) systems after go-live
• The IFR specifies that states can request a time-limited good faith
effort exemption to delay implementation
– Stakeholders shared that states are still awaiting CMS to issue the template for
these requests, and no states have officially requested an exemption

• On October 1, 2026, five Governors sent a letter to the U.S.
Department of Health and Human Services to request delaying work
requirements
– The letter raises that states need more time to develop and test their systems, and
to protect beneficiaries from avoidable coverage disruption

4

Ex Parte Verification
• Stakeholders shared that most states have access to data
sources needed for ex parte verification of household monthly
income and some exclusion categories (e.g., parent of a
dependent under age 14)
• Data gaps will limit states’ ability to use ex parte, so instead
many will rely on self-attestation in 2027
– Community service
– Volunteer hours
– Parents and caregivers of individuals with disabilities

5

Eligibility Staff
• Support new applicants and beneficiaries with understanding
work requirements and documentation needed to demonstrate
compliance, exception, or exclusion status
• Stakeholders raised concerns about eligibility staff capacity to
handle the anticipated increase in volume and complexity of
beneficiary questions
• Stakeholders shared that eligibility workers have not received
adequate training and resources

Medical Frailty

IFR Definition
• In statute, individuals must meet at least one of these medical frailty
criteria:
–
–
–
–

Blindness or disability (as defined at Section 1614 of the Act);
Substance use disorder (SUD) and not in stable recovery;
Disabling mental disorder;
Physical, intellectual, or developmental disability that significantly impairs their
ability to perform one or more activities of daily living (ADLs); or
– Serious or complex medical condition

• The IFR expands on the statutory definition by requiring the
individual’s condition to significantly impair their ability to comply
with work requirements
8

Operationalization
•

The IFR emphasizes the importance of program integrity and requires
states to use data and information that ensure all eligibility determinations
are auditable
– States must share their auditable condition and diagnosis list with CMS for
oversight and monitoring

•

Stakeholders shared states began developing their approaches to verify
medical frailty before the IFR, and are now revising them to assess severity
of a condition, including some published approaches
– CMS’s tiered framework for categorizing conditions based on the extent to which
available data can confirm whether an individual’s condition impairs their ability
to comply with work requirements
– Two-stage approach developed by researchers at Harvard TH Chan School of
Public Health and Brigham and Women’s Hospital
9

Operationalization, cont.
• Stakeholders shared state concerns with how to operationalize
the IFR definition of medical frailty and uncertainty about whether
their implemented approaches will be considered compliant
• Stakeholders shared states need more guidance from CMS on
the medical frailty definition that auditors will use, especially given
upcoming financial penalties in fiscal year 2030

10

Self-Attestation
• In 2027 only, states may accept self-attestation when sources of
documentation are not reasonably available
– Most states plan to allow self-attestation when the state cannot automatically
verify medical frailty

• Self-attestation can facilitate application and enrollment
processes while states update IT systems, but stakeholders
raised concerns about state and beneficiary administrative
burden
– Eligibility worker training on self-attestation will be critical to support
beneficiaries

11

Provider Role in Determinations
• The IFR allows states to accept provider documentation from
practitioners to determine if an individual’s condition qualifies
them as medically frail
• Stakeholders shared provider concerns with:
– Administrative burden with providing documentation
– Qualifications and training to determine whether an individual’s condition
significantly impairs them from complying with work requirements
– Auditing of their medical frailty determinations

12

Beneficiary Outreach

Communicating with Beneficiaries
• The IFR requires states to use clear, consumer-friendly language
• Based on our review of initial notices sent to beneficiaries, they
vary in content, terminology, readability, and accessibility
– Stakeholders identified challenges in readability and the use of confusing
language

• Many states have websites to educate and provide resources on
work requirements
– Some websites include screeners that guide users through questions to
determine if they are subject to work requirements
– Stakeholders noted that beneficiaries may not be aware of these websites or
resources
14

Outreach Challenges
• Beneficiaries often do not know their eligibility pathway, and are not
aware requirements apply to them
• States need to communicate multiple and complex policy changes in
plain language
• Policy and guidance continues to evolve, making it difficult for states
to share accurate information

15

Federal and State Reporting

Federal and State Reporting
• CMS releases a monthly enrollment and eligibility snapshot and has
indicated they will continue to publish these data
– Published data are lagged by several months

• The IFR established reporting requirements for monitoring
– States must report new work requirement-related metrics through performance
indicator (PI) and Medicaid and CHIP Eligibility Processing (EP) data
– Updates to the Transformed Medicaid Statistical Information System (T-MSIS)
have not been released yet

• Stakeholders shared that several states have published, or are
planning to publish, data related to work requirements through
dashboards or other reporting mechanisms
17

Findings on Early State Implementation

Nebraska Implementation
• Nebraska implemented work requirements on May 1, 2026, and
modified their application and online portal
– Accepting self-attestation for most compliance activities (except for income),
exceptions, and exclusions

• Stakeholders shared Nebraska has integrated Medicaid and
Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) eligibility
workers to support the call center and did not hire more staff
• Beneficiary advocates shared that notices were confusing, lacked
detail, and were written at a 14th grade reading level

Updates since release of IFR

New Federal Guidance
• Based on the IFR, states are anticipating guidance or additional
resources on:
– Coordination with exchanges
– Submitting a request for a good faith effort exemption

• CMS continues to provide technical assistance and hold weekly
state calls

Ongoing Litigation
•

Commonwealth of Massachusetts v. Oz et al

•

Taylor et al. v. Kennedy, Jr. et al

– June 29, 2026: Twenty-five states and the District of Columbia filed a lawsuit
challenging aspects of the IFR’s interpretation of work requirements, including medical
frailty
– July 29, 2026: Plaintiff states’ motion for a preliminary injunction was denied
• The court’s decision sets an expedited schedule for a full briefing of merits before
the implementation deadline
• The hearing on the motion for summary judgment was on October 22, 2026
– September 18, 2026: Five Medicaid beneficiaries, six provider organizations and the
City of Columbus filed a lawsuit challenging specific provisions, including the medical
frailty exclusion definition and limitation on self-attestation
– October 20, 2026: Plaintiffs filed a motion for summary judgement
– November 3, 2026: Defendants shall file a cross-motion for summary judgment
