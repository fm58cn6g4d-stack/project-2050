Message from the Inspector General
The U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA)
Office of Inspector General’s (OIG) Annual
Plan details OIG’s plans to accomplish its mission to
promote the economy, efficiency, and effectiveness
of and to prevent and detect fraud, waste, and abuse
in USDA’s programs and operations during fiscal
year (FY) 2026. Our Annual Plan is aligned with the
USDA OIG Strategic Plan—Fiscal Years 2025–2029,
which shares our organizational priorities for the next
5 years.
As we carry out our mission to improve and protect
USDA programs, we will focus on the importance of
enhancing external partnerships and relationships
and continuing to cultivate an internal culture that
embraces continuous improvement.  
Our fiscal year 2026 mandated and priority work is
organized into four categories:
• Fighting Fraud in Benefit Programs;
• National Security in Agriculture;
• Public Safety; and
• Efficient Delivery of USDA Programs, Operations,
and Initiatives.
We leverage multiple tools to perform this work,
including audits, investigations, inspections, reviews,
and data analytics.
Fraud and fraud prevention are high priorities for our
agency. OIG work is intended to strengthen program
integrity and detecting and preventing fraud
regarding USDA’s programs and operations. For
example, we have ongoing evaluations of the
quality and integrity of the Food and Nutrition
Service’s (FNS) Supplemental Nutrition Assistance
Program (SNAP) participant data for selected
States. We are also continuing to assess the
Risk Management Agency’s (RMA) debarment
enforcement process. In line with the President’s
initiatives to deter and detect fraud in Government
programs, we have prioritized investigating allegations
of potential fraud, waste, or misuse involving
FNS feeding programs to include SNAP, the Child and
Adult Care Feeding Program (CACFP), the Special

Supplemental Nutrition Program for Women, Infants,
and Children (WIC), the National School Lunch
Program (NSLP), and the Summer Food Service
Program (SFSP). We also plan to assess fraud risk
mitigation for the Agricultural Marketing Service’s
(AMS) Perishable Agricultural Commodities Act
violations. Our Office of Investigations will also
continue to investigate allegations of feeding
program, crop insurance, and other farm assistancerelated frauds, in coordination with our law
enforcement partners at the Federal, State, and local
levels. In addition, we will continue to use analytics
to better detect and prevent fraud by proactively
analyzing data and enhancing investigative tools.
Our work also addresses the need for national
security in the agricultural sector. We have ongoing
work for this area, including assessing whether the
National Bio and Agro-Defense Facility (NBAF) is
appropriately configured to house animals for highlevel biosafety and animal disease research. In
addition, we are conducting work regarding foreignowned land. OIG is also working to ensure we are
supporting the Secretary’s National Farm Security
Action Plan. Specifically, OIG is named in the plan to
ensure programs like SNAP are being preserved for
truly needy individuals legally in the United States
and safeguarding them from fraud, abuse, and foreign
adversaries while respecting the generosity of the
American taxpayer. In the research security space,
we will evaluate the designation process for research
scientists and evaluate security over unauthorized
transfers of agricultural research and the potential
that funding could be utilized by foreign adversaries.
Additionally, our work also ensures public
safety regarding the Department’s programs
and related resources. We will continue to actively
investigate threats involving agroterrorism and
the safety of meat, poultry, and egg products to
protect the public. Additionally, we will continue
our investigations into Animal Welfare Act
violations, including animal fighting, in line with the
Administration’s priorities. As part of the animal
welfare initiatives, we will sit on the Animal Welfare
Executive Committee, provide trainers at the Animal
Welfare Summit, and participate in the Animal
Welfare Tiger Team. We also have work planned to
3i

support public safety, including reviewing USDA's
processes for eradicating the New World Screwworm.
In addressing our mission goals, our work also
helps USDA strengthen the efficient delivery of
USDA programs, operations, and initiatives. For
example, we are continuing to evaluate the Farm
Service Agency's (FSA) controls of the American Relief
Act (ARA) funds under its Supplemental Disaster Relief
Program. We also will continue to address allegations
of senior management misconduct, whistleblower
reprisal complaints, other statutorily mandated
requirements and investigations, and sensitive
matters.
This Annual Plan demonstrates our commitment
to strengthening stakeholder relationships and
our operational efficiency by advancing a creative,
forward-thinking organization and embracing
continuous improvement.

Introduction
OIG is committed to providing a positive return on
taxpayer investment. In FY 2025, our work resulted
in a monetary impact of more than $1.2 billion,
leading to a return on investment of $11 for every
dollar invested. We plan our work to address issues of
greatest risk and achieve the most significant results.
This Annual Plan presents the FY 2026 work OIG plan
to perform in support of the USDA OIG Strategic
Plan—Fiscal Years 2025–2029.

Resources and
Organization
Pursuant to the Inspector General Act of 1978,
USDA OIG has the authority and responsibility to
oversee the Department’s programs and operations.
USDA has approximately 300 programs that benefit
diverse sectors of the public, which include but
are not limited to: food and nutrition assistance
recipients, farm and ranch operations and other rural
businesses, future-focused research scientists seeking
solutions to agricultural problems, and those affected
by wildland fires and other disasters.

OIG’s Areas of
Responsibility
Audit
The Office of Audit (OA) examines the economy and
efficiency of USDA programs and operations, including
program results, compliance with applicable laws and
regulations, and the accuracy of financial reports.
While most audit work is done by in-house staff, OA
also contracts with independent public accounting
firms for some work and oversees the quality of work
completed by auditors under contract to other USDA
agencies. OIG audits are completed in accordance
with Government Auditing Standards, published by
the Government Accountability Office. In addition,
OA performs inspections conducted in accordance
with CIGIE’s Quality Standards for Inspection and
Evaluation.

Investigations
The Office of Investigations (OI) utilizes specific
law enforcement activities, tools, and techniques,
including use of asset forfeiture, digital forensics,
financial analyses, data analytics, and other innovative
approaches to conduct investigations to detect and
prevent fraud, waste, and abuse in the programs and
operations of USDA. OI’s investigative work may result
in criminal, civil, administrative, and/or monetary
outcomes in resolving allegations and to prevent and
deter future instances of illegal or fraudulent acts
or employee misconduct. The OIG Hotline serves as
a key operational function for reporting potential
violations of laws and regulations relating to USDA
programs.

Compliance and Integrity
The Office of Compliance and Integrity (OCI) facilitates
OIG’s enterprise risk management and continually
performs compliance reviews, inspections, and
evaluations of OIG programs and operations. OCI’s
reviews provide senior management with reasonable
assurance that OIG operations and activities are
in conformance with policy, law, and professional
standards. Additionally, OCI conducts evaluations
and investigations of alleged employee misconduct
to safeguard public trust. OCI also assesses the
effectiveness and efficiency of OIG operations
to identify trends, best practices, and areas for
development.

Counsel
The Office of Counsel (OC) provides legal advice
and representation on issues arising during OIG
work, including audits, investigations, inspections,
data analytics, and reviews, as well as on internal
administrative and management issues. OC also
manages OIG’s ethics, Freedom of Information
Act, and Privacy Act programs, as well as reviewing
proposed legislation, regulations, policies, and
procedures.

Office of Civil Rights
The Office of Civil Rights (OCR) leads OIG’s efforts
to ensure compliance with applicable federal
civil rights and Equal Employment Opportunity
(EEO) requirements. The office is responsible for
administering the Agency’s EEO complaint process
and overseeing Alternative Dispute Resolution
activities. OCR also provides policy guidance, conducts
compliance reporting and workforce analysis. Through
these efforts, OCR advances accountability and equal
opportunity across OIG’s workforce.

Analytics and Innovation

Management

The Office of Analytics and Innovation (OAI) is
modernizing the way OIG does business by using
innovative techniques, providing visibility into data,
delivering decision-ready information, and employing
innovative communication methodologies. OAI, in
collaboration with OA and OI, creates and deploys
advanced analytical tools utilized for potential fraud
detection and performance risk assessments. OAI
develops data-driven reports, dashboards, and
interactive data stories on a wide range of topics for
stakeholders and the public.

The Office of Management enables the effective
operations of OIG mission areas and offices by
providing expert business advice, infrastructure,
technology, and other support to meet customers’
diverse needs. Responsibilities include asset
management, budget formulation and execution,
contracts and acquisitions, human resources
management, workforce development, workplace
safety, facilities and space management, and
information technology.
