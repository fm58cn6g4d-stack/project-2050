Subject: The Melania Trump Foster Youth to Independence Initiative
I.

PURPOSE
This notice provides guidance on the process public housing agencies (PHAs) must
follow to request targeted vouchers under the Melania Trump Foster Youth to
Independence Initiative (FYI). This notice also sets forth the operating requirements for
FYI. Through the FYI Initiative, HUD provides housing choice vouchers (HCVs) for
youth eligible under the Family Unification Program (FUP), subject to funding
availability. Throughout this notice, these vouchers are referred to as FYI vouchers.
This notice supersedes Notice PIH 2023-04, Foster Youth to Independence Initiative, and
makes the following major changes:


Renames the program to The Melania Trump Foster Youth to Independence
Initiative, in accordance with the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026; however,
the acronym FYI used to refer to the program remains unchanged



Revises the definition of “at risk of becoming homeless” for the purpose of
determining eligibility



Amends the process by which PHAs may apply for $38.2 million in new incremental
voucher assistance



Removes the cap on the number of FYI vouchers that a PHA can request each Federal
fiscal year, subject to availability of funding



Clarifies the process by which PHAs may voluntarily return FYI noncompetitive
vouchers and associated funding that is no longer needed to HUD and that HUD will
issue future guidance on HUD-initiated recapture of unutilized FYI noncompetitive
vouchers that are no longer needed



BACKGROUND
The Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026 (Pub. L. 119-75, approved on February 3,
2026), referred to hereafter as the “the 2026 Act,” made available $25 million in new
incremental voucher assistance. The Full-Year Continuing Appropriations and
Extensions Act, 2025 (Pub. L. 119-4, approved on March 15, 2025), referred to hereafter
as “the 2025 Act,” authorized $25 million under the terms and conditions of the
Transportation, Housing and Urban Development, and Related Agencies Appropriations
Act, 2024 (division F of Pub. L. 118-42). The 2026 Act and the 2025 Act both provided
that $25 million shall be available on a noncompetitive basis to PHAs that partner with
public child welfare agencies (PCWAs1) to assist FUP-eligible youth under section 8(x)
of the U.S. Housing Act of 1937 (42 U.S.C. 1437f(x)). HUD is making the funding under
the 2026 Act available through this notice. HUD expects approximately $13.2 million in
carryover from the 2025 Act will also be made available.
The 2026 Act specified that assistance made available for this purpose shall be known
and designated as The Melania Trump Foster Youth to Independence Initiative. As stated
in section I., the acronym “FYI” used to refer to the program remains unchanged. The
2026 Act also directs HUD to review utilization of the FYI vouchers awarded on a
noncompetitive basis under the 2026 Act or any prior appropriations act and that any
unutilized voucher assistance no longer needed based on such review shall be recaptured
and reallocated through the noncompetitive process. HUD will make any funds
recaptured through this process available through this notice or any superseding notice.
Senate Report 118-70 (2024) directs HUD to maximize the availability of FYI vouchers
based on the identified need and referrals for such assistance and directs HUD to ensure
that the utilization thresholds for the FYI program do not inadvertently lead to delays and
preclude identified youth from receiving FYI vouchers. Accordingly, this notice removes
the cap on the number of FYI vouchers that a PHA can request (see section X.V.) and the
utilization threshold requirement for requesting new FYI vouchers (see section VI.B.).

For States that have privatized child welfare, the agency designated to conduct child welfare on behalf of the State may be considered a PCWA.
Federally recognized Tribes operating a foster care program are considered a PCWA under the FYI program and can therefore refer eligible
youth to the PHA for an FYI voucher and/or may serve as the PCWA providing supportive services.

3
Further, the Senate Report directs HUD to encourage PHAs to amend their
Administrative Plans to facilitate requesting FYI vouchers. A PHA may establish an FYI
program at any time. The benefit of proactively entering into a partnership agreement
with a PCWA (via a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) or a letter of intent (LOI))
and making corresponding updates to its Administrative Plan is that the PHA will be able
to immediately request FYI vouchers upon receiving a referral of an eligible youth, rather
than needing to delay the request during these administrative processes. To maximize the
availability of FYI vouchers, HUD encourages PHAs that do not already have an FYI
program to consider taking proactive steps to make this resource available in their
community.
On April 9, 2025, HUD issued Notice PIH 2025-08, which provides new flexibilities to
reduce barriers and increase opportunities for FYI and FUP voucher holders to secure and
maintain housing stability. Through these flexibilities, FYI and FUP participants will
have more time to secure decent and safe housing with their voucher, providing them
with a platform to achieve greater self-sufficiency.
III.

ELIGIBLE PHA APPLICANTS
The PHA must have an existing Annual Contribution Contract (ACC) with HUD for
HCVs. Nonprofit administrators of HCV Mainstream Vouchers, which by statute are
classified as PHAs solely for the purpose of administering HCV Mainstream Vouchers,
are ineligible for FYI. Moving to Work (MTW) agencies must administer FYI vouchers
consistent with the requirements described in this notice. HUD will not evaluate
applications from ineligible applicants. Additional eligibility threshold criteria are
detailed in section VI.

IV.

IMPACT ON AWARDS AND REQUESTS UNDER NOTICE PIH 2023-04
Requests submitted to HUD by PHAs pursuant to Notice PIH 2023-04 prior to the
publication of this notice do not need to be resubmitted. However, requests that were
received prior to the publication of this notice that have not yet been funded will be
reviewed in accordance with the requirements of this notice.

V.

FUNDING SOURCES
The 2026 Act provides that, of the $30 million appropriated for FUP, $25 million shall be
made available for FUP youth on a noncompetitive basis to PHAs that partner with
PCWAs to identify FUP-eligible youth. HUD makes the full $25 million available on a
noncompetitive basis through this notice. Additionally, HUD makes available
approximately $13.2 million from carryover funds from the 2025 Act, for a total of
approximately $38.2 million. As provided for in section 11303(4) of the 2025 Act, HUD
utilized $25 million of carryover FUP funds, including those specified for FYI under the
2024 Act, to address HCV and/or mainstream voucher shortfalls.
Through this notice, HUD also reallocates any recaptured FYI assistance awarded on a
noncompetitive basis and is no longer needed. If additional funding is made available for
this purpose in further appropriation acts under the same terms and conditions, HUD may
continue to make awards under this notice rather than issue a new notice. If an

appropriations act adds additional requirements or establishes new conditions regarding
this funding, HUD will publish a revised notice.
Subject to the following conditions, HUD will accept and process PHA requests for FYI

A. Funding Remains Available
HUD will use available funding as described above for FYI awards under this notice.

expended unless additional funds become available through future appropriations acts
or the recapture of unused assistance (see paragraph B.).
B. Recapture of FYI Assistance Awarded on a Noncompetitive Basis
Through this notice, HUD will also reallocate any recaptured FYI assistance awarded
on a noncompetitive basis.
VI.

PHA ELIGIBILITY THRESHOLD REQUIREMENTS
A PHA requesting assistance under this notice must meet all the following eligibility
requirements:
A. Partnership Agreement
PHAs applying for assistance under this notice must first enter into a partnership
agreement with a PCWA. HUD strongly encourages adding other important partners,
such as State or local governments (including schools), philanthropic and faith-based
organizations, and the Continuum of Care (CoC),2 or a CoC recipient it designates, to
the partnership. PHAs may establish an FYI program at any time and HUD
encourages PHAs to work with their partnering PCWA to make the required
corresponding updates to their Administrative Plans to facilitate requesting FYI
vouchers. For example, a PHA may update their Administrative Plan to reflect
specific policies for partnership structure and agreements, such as defining roles and
responsibilities. PHAs may also collaborate with their partnering PCWA consistent
with the provisions in Notice PIH 2025-08 regarding the timing of referral of youth
leaving foster care. See more details regarding the form partnership agreements may
take in section XII.
B. PHA Eligibility Based on Utilization of Existing FYI and/or FUP Awards

FYI program do not inadvertently lead to delays and preclude identified youth from
receiving FYI vouchers. While the intent of the utilization threshold was to help HUD
target FYI vouchers where they were most needed, HUD found that the utilization
threshold caused some confusion for PHAs and other stakeholders and, in some
instances, posed a barrier to PHAs being able to receive an FYI voucher for an

Nevertheless, HUD generally expects PHAs to utilize their existing FYI and/or FUP

information does not support the need for new FYI vouchers.
C. Received Referral
A request for assistance may not be made until the PHA has received a referral of an
eligible youth from the partnering PCWA. If the PHA receives a referral from another
organization outside the PCWA, the PHA must confirm eligibility with its partnering
PCWA of the youth’s prior qualifying foster care involvement (see section VII.C.).
VII.

PCWA ROLES AND RESPONSIBILITIES
The partnering PCWA must meet all the following requirements:
A. Identify Eligible Youth
The PCWA must have a system for identifying eligible youth within the agency’s
caseload and for reviewing referrals from the PHA or a third party, such as a State,
local governments (including schools), philanthropic, faith-based organizations, CoC,
or a CoC recipient it designates.
B. System of Prioritization
Given the limited nature of this resource, the PCWA may need to implement a system
of prioritization for eligible youth based upon level of need and appropriateness of the
intervention. For youth still involved in the child welfare system, the PCWA should
consider the young person’s permanency goals. The PHA must ensure any
prioritization criteria for referral to FYI comply with Federal fair housing and civil
rights requirements.
C. Written Certification
The PCWA must provide written certification to the PHA that a youth is eligible.

6
D. Supportive Services
The PCWA must either directly provide or secure a commitment from partners for the
provision of supportive services that are required to be offered to youth. PCWAs
should consider creating partnerships with other community organizations that offer
these supportive services.
VIII. REQUIRED SUPPORTIVE SERVICES
Eligibility to receive funding under this notice to administer FYI vouchers requires the
PCWA, as stated above, provides or secures a commitment for the provision of
supportive services for participating youth to assist the youth in achieving selfsufficiency. The services listed in (A) through (E) below must be secured and made
available for a period of 36 months to eligible youth receiving rental assistance through
this notice. HUD encourages full participation in self-sufficiency services as appropriate
for participating youth.
A. Basic life skills information/counseling on money management, use of credit,
housekeeping, proper nutrition/meal preparation, and access to health care (e.g.,
doctors, medication, and mental and behavioral health services)
B. Counseling on compliance with rental lease requirements and with HCV program
participant requirements, including assistance/referrals for assistance on security
deposits, utility hook-up fees, and utility deposits
C. Providing such assurances to owners of rental property as are reasonable and
necessary to assist eligible youth to rent a unit with a voucher
D. Job preparation and attainment counseling (e.g., where to look/how to apply, dress,
grooming, and relationships with supervisory personnel)
E. Educational and career advancement counseling regarding attainment of GED and
attendance/financing of education at a technical school, trade school, or college,
including successful work ethic and attitude models
The PHA or another partnering entity may provide some or all the supportive services if it
has the resources and expertise to do so.
Administrative fee reserves may be used to cover costs for the supportive services listed
above (A-E) with HUD approval. Refer to Notice PIH 2026-12 for information on 2026
Act provisions on administrative fees.

IX.

YOUTH ELIGIBILITY
The population eligible to be assisted with funding under this notice are youth certified
by the PCWA as meeting the following conditions:


Has attained at least 18 years and not more than 24 years of age (have not reached
their 25th birthday);



Left foster care, or will leave foster care within 180 days,3 in accordance with a
transition plan, described in section 475(5)(H) of the Social Security Act; and



Is homeless4 or is at risk of becoming homeless at age 16 or older.

In this notice, HUD changes the definition of “at risk of becoming homeless” to mean:


An individual or family who has an annual income below 50 percent of median
family income for the area, as determined by HUD, and meets one of the following
conditions:
o Is living in the home of another because of economic hardship;
o Has been notified in writing that their right to occupy their current housing or
living situation will be terminated;
o Lives in a hotel or motel and the cost of the hotel or motel stay is not paid by
charitable organizations or by Federal, State, or local government programs for
low-income individuals;
o Lives in a single-room occupancy or efficiency apartment unit in which there
reside more than two persons or lives in a larger housing unit in which there
reside more than 1.5 persons reside per room, as defined by the U.S. Census
Bureau;
o Is exiting a publicly funded institution, or system of care (such as a health-care
facility, a mental health facility, foster care or other youth facility, or correction
program or institution); or
o Otherwise lives in housing that has characteristics associated with instability and
an increased risk of homelessness, as identified in the approved Consolidated Plan
for the relevant jurisdiction.

X.

LENGTH OF ASSISTANCE
As required by statute, an FYI voucher may only be used to provide housing assistance
for youth for a maximum of 36 months. However, youth who first lease a unit with an
FYI voucher after December 27, 2020, the date of enactment of the Fostering Stable
Housing Opportunities (FSHO) amendments, may receive an extension of the 36-month
time limit of assistance for up to an additional 24 months if they meet the requirements of
FSHO. See HUD’s FSHO implementation notice published in the Federal Register on
January 24, 2022 (87 FR 3570).

XI.

ROLE OF THIRD PARTIES
HUD strongly encourages participation of State, local governments (including schools),
philanthropic, faith-based organizations, and the CoC, or a CoC recipient it designates.
These parties may play a critical role in identifying eligible youth in the community at
risk of or experiencing homelessness that are no longer part of the child welfare system.
Further, these parties may provide or leverage supportive services on behalf of the youth.
Youth who are part of the PCWA's active caseload do not have to be added to the CoC's
coordinated entry process, nor do youth identified by other third parties have to be added
to the CoC’s coordinated entry, to be referred for FYI.

The partnership agreement may take the form of an MOU or LOI between the parties. If a
PCWA is authorized under State law to contract out its functions and has done so, both
the official representative of the PCWA and the official representative of the contractor
organization must sign the MOU. At a minimum, the partnership agreement must clearly

A. Define Eligible Youth
Define youth eligible to receive assistance under this notice using the criteria in

B. Identify Supportive Services
List the supportive services provided to eligible youth receiving rental assistance
through this notice. All services in section VIII. must be provided for a period of 36

C. Address PHA Responsibilities
The following PHA responsibilities must be identified:


If the PHA uses the HCV waiting list: The PHA, upon receipt of a referral(s) from
the PCWA of an eligible youth, must compare the name(s) with youth already on
the PHA's HCV waiting list. Any youth on the PHA's HCV waiting list that

closed HCV waiting list, it must reopen the waiting list and place on the waiting

The PHA may reopen the waiting list to accept an FYI eligible youth without
opening the waiting list for other applicants. The requirements at 24 CFR 982.206
for giving public notice when opening and closing the waiting list apply.

separate FYI and/or FUP waiting list. The youth may also be placed on the PHA’s
general HCV waiting list even though they are being placed on the separate FYI
and/or FUP waiting list. This facilitates the PHA’s selection of youth from the
general HCV waiting list upon expiration of the FYI voucher if necessary to
maintain stable housing.

Document that youth are informed of their eligibility for supportive services and
the duration of the availability of those services.

regulations and requirements.

XIII. VALUE OF VOUCHER
The value of the FYI voucher is dependent on the Per Unit Cost (PUC) identified by
HUD for the PHA. If a PHA has concerns regarding the sufficiency of the funding based
on the PUC, the PHA can request higher funding within 12 months of the award’s
effective date based on actual costs. Direct inquiries about PUC increases to PIH’s
Financial Management Division mailbox at PIHConversionActions@hud.gov.

C. Funding Process
Approved requests will be referred to PIH’s Financial Management Division (FMD)

In accordance with Notice PIH 2026-12 and any subsequent Funding Implementation

additional Special Fees may be provided for PHAs administering FYI vouchers.
PHAs may use these funds to assist youth to lease units by conducting FYI initiative
administrative activities, such as FYI landlord recruitment, housing search assistance
activities, and landlord/tenant mediation activities, or other FYI expenses, such as
owner incentives, retention payments, and security deposits. PHAs must follow
requirements under Notice PIH 2022-18.
XV.

MINIMUM AND MAXIMUM REQUEST LIMITS DURING THE FEDERAL
FISCAL YEAR
A PHA’s minimum request size for FYI vouchers may be as small as one voucher. To
ensure that vouchers are available where youth need assistance, HUD previously limited
the number of FYI vouchers that a PHA may request in a Federal fiscal year to 50 FYI
vouchers.6 Senate Report 118-70 directs HUD to maximize the availability of FYI
vouchers based on the identified need and referrals for such assistance. Therefore, HUD
is eliminating the cap of 50 FYI vouchers and the maximum number of FYI vouchers that

An application for vouchers under this notice may not occur until the PHA has received a

must be based on a referral(s) of eligible youth.

XVII. ADDITIONAL PROGRAM REQUIREMENTS
The following program requirements also apply:
A. Youth Failure to Use Voucher/Turnover
Should a youth fail to use the voucher, the PHA must issue the voucher to another
eligible youth if one has been identified.
PHAs must continue to use FYI vouchers awarded under this notice for eligible
youth upon turnover.
B. Reporting
PHAs must maintain a special program code for FYI voucher participants in line 2n
of the Family Report (form HUD-50058), line 2p of the MTW Family Report (form
HUD-50058 MTW), or line 2n of the MTW Expansion Family Report (form HUD50058 MTW Expansion), as applicable. The special program code is “FYI.” PHAs
must also properly record the date the PHA issues the voucher to youth and the date
of admittance to the program. Lines 2b, for the effective date, and 2h must be used to
report the date the PHA initially admitted the youth into the program. PHAs should
also be responding to the homeless at the admission field (line 4c) of the form.
PHAs must also report leasing and expense information for these vouchers in VMS,
form HUD-52681B. The “Foster Youth to Independence – HAP and Leasing” or
“MTW Foster Youth To Independence HAP and Leasing” category, as appropriate,
must be used.
The total number of vouchers leased for all FYI vouchers awarded, including FYI
tenant protection vouchers (TPV) vouchers, should no longer be reported in the FUP
field. This went into effect in March 2022 for the February 2022 data submission.

XVIII. FYI TPV ADMINISTRATION
Awards of FYI TPVs continue to be administered under the requirements of Notice PIH
2019-20. This includes turnover requirements and the requirement to inform HUD when
a youth does not use a voucher or leaves the program. FYI TPVs awarded under Notice
PIH 2019-20 “sunset” when a youth leaves the program. This means that the PHA cannot
reissue FYI TPV assistance issued under that notice to another youth when an initial
youth exits the program. HUD does not have authority to allow the voucher to be used for
a youth other than the one identified in the request.
