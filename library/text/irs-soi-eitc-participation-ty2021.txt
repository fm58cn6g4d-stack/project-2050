Abstract
The Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC), enacted in 1975, offers a refundable tax credit to low
income working families. This paper provides taxpayer and dollar participation estimates for the
EITC covering tax year 2021. The estimates derive from an approach that relies on linking the
2022 Current Population Survey Annual Social and Economic Supplement (CPS ASEC) to IRS
administrative data. This approach, called the Exact Match, uses survey data to identify EITC
eligible taxpayers and IRS administrative data to indicate which eligible taxpayers claimed and
received the credit. Overall in tax year 2021 eligible taxpayers participated in the EITC program
at a rate of 78 percent while dollar participation was 81 percent.

Methodology Overview
The EITC methodology provides two primary sta s cs to gauge EITC par cipa on: (1) the taxpayer
par cipa on rate and (2) the dollar par cipa on rate. The taxpayer par cipa on rate (a percentage) is
the total number of taxpayers who received an EITC payment divided by the total number of eligible
taxpayers. The dollar par cipa on rate (a percentage) is the total amount of eligible EITC dollars paid out
divided by the total EITC dollars for which all taxpayers are eligible. Both par cipa on rates represent
par cipa on condi onal on eligibility.12
The Exact Match methodology for es ma ng the par cipa on rates consists of linking individuals in the
Current Popula on Survey Annual Social and Economic Supplement (CPS ASEC) to their tax return
informa on from IRS administra ve data. The CPS ASEC iden fies the EITC eligible taxpayers and eligible
dollars, while the IRS administra ve data and return informa on indicate which taxpayers claimed and
received the credit and dollars paid. The following sec ons describe the CPS ASEC and IRS administra ve
data and detail the process of producing the par cipa on rates.

The CPS ASEC is a stra fied random sample of 60,000-90,000 household addresses from the civilian,
non-ins tu onalized popula on in the United States and is collected by the Census Bureau. It is a
supplement to the monthly Current Popula on Survey, conducted in February-April of each year, that
contains detailed informa on on demographic characteris cs, annual earnings and incomes,

poverty sta s cs. All income informa on in the CPS ASEC refers to the prior calendar year while

The first step of the par cipa on methodology involves modeling tax units, EITC eligibility, and EITC

Analysts in the Taxpayer Services, Strategies and Solu ons program area at IRS download the public use

1. Change filer from head to spouse adjustment: The IRS tax model designates the head of a
household as the primary filer, which may not always reflect actual filing behavior. This
adjustment moves the modeled tax return informa on from the head to the spouse if the
spouse filed but the head did not. If both the head and the spouse filed, the modeled return
informa on is moved from the head to the spouse when the spouse claimed EITC, and the head
did not. If both the head and spouse filed, and both claimed EITC, the modeled return
informa on is moved from the head to the spouse when the spouse claimed the qualifying child
and the head did not. If both filed and neither claimed EITC, but a qualifying child appears on the
spouse’s return, the modeled return informa on is moved to the spouse.

The CPS ASEC includes supplement weights designed to sum to the total civilian, non-ins tu onalized
