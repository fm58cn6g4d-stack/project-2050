September 30, 2026
The Honorable Brooke L. Rollins
Secretary, U.S. Department of Agriculture
1400 Independence Ave, SW
Washington, DC 20250
Dear Secretary Rollins:
For decades, the Section 502 Single Family Housing Direct Loan Program has helped
low- and very-low-income families in rural communities achieve homeownership when
conventional financing is otherwise out of reach. Congress has consistently provided bipartisan
support for this program because access to safe, affordable housing is essential to the economic
strength of rural communities.
Recent reports indicate that approximately $232 million in Fiscal Year 2026 (FY26)
Section 502 Direct Loan funding could expire by the end of today, after the U.S. Department of
Agriculture (USDA) obligated only a portion of the approximately $1 billion Congress provided
for the program. This would represent a significant departure from the program’s historic
funding needs at a time when rural families continue to face high housing costs, limited housing
supply, and elevated borrowing costs.
The potential expiration of these funds is particularly concerning following changes
USDA made to the Section 502 program earlier this year. On February 10, 2026, USDA issued
Procedural Notice 655, which reduced the maximum allowable loan limit from 80 percent to 60
percent of applicable area limits and made other changes affecting the processing and eligibility
of Section 502 loans. Members of Congress raised concerns that these changes could reduce
access to the program and slow the delivery of assistance to eligible rural families. The House
subsequently passed FY27 Agriculture Appropriations legislation containing language that
would preserve the previous 80 percent loan limit unless USDA completes notice-and-comment
rulemaking.
Congress has also continued to demonstrate strong support for rural housing resources.
Members from both parties have requested robust funding for Section 502 and other USDA rural
housing programs over many Congresses. Allowing hundreds of millions of dollars already
provided by Congress to go unused would undermine those efforts and warrants a clear
explanation. Accordingly, we respectfully request that USDA:
 Use every available lawful authority to obligate or preserve FY26 Section 502 funding
for its congressionally intended purpose as quick as possible;
 Provide an accounting of FY26 Section 502 obligations, unobligated balances, and any
funds that will expire or carry forward into Fiscal Year 2027;
 Explain the factors that contributed to the unusually large unobligated balance, including
the effects, if any, of the FY26 handbook revisions, additional review requirements,
staffing levels, application processing times, and changes to loan packaging;

Provide information on the number of Section 502 applications received, approved,
denied, withdrawn, and pending during FY26, including a state-by-state breakdown;
Identify any steps USDA will take in FY27 to address application backlogs, improve
processing times, and ensure that appropriated resources reach eligible rural families; and
Work with Congress and rural housing stakeholders before making additional significant
administrative changes to the Section 502 program.

These resources were provided by Congress to help families in rural America purchase
homes and strengthen the communities in which they live and work. Regardless of party, we should
share the goal of ensuring that congressionally appropriated funds are administered efficiently and
reach the rural families they were intended to serve. We appreciate your prompt attention to this
matter and request a response outlining USDA’s plans for the Section 502 Direct Loan Program.
