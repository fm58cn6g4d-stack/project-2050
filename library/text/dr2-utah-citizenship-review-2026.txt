STATE OF UTAH​
OFFICE OF THE LIEUTENANT GOVERNOR

DEIDRE M. HENDERSON
LIEUTENANT GOVERNOR
May 27, 2026
In April 2025, my office initiated a comprehensive citizenship review of Utah’s voter registration
rolls. A preliminary update was published in January. Today we are releasing the final
comprehensive report of our review, which involved cross-referencing more than 2 million
records against the Driver License Division and/or the Systematic Alien Verification for
Entitlements (SAVE) program for citizenship verification.
Federal and state laws have historically required individuals to affirm citizenship by checking a
box and signing an affidavit on their voter registration applications. Prior to H.B. 209 (2026
General Session), state law did not require verification of citizenship. However, the review from
my office confirmed that 99.72% of Utah’s registered voters are verifiably U.S. citizens.
During the review, we found 27 confirmed noncitizens, whom clerks have since removed from
the voter rolls. Additionally, there are 5,007 registrants whose citizenship status we were unable
to confirm, many of whom registered to vote years before driver license or Social Security
numbers were required. This week, these individuals will receive notice of the new citizenship
requirements in state law. To be eligible to vote in upcoming state and local races, they will need
to show proof of citizenship to their county clerks, otherwise they will receive a federal-only
ballot. My office also identified 25 registered voters who are probable noncitizens. These
individuals will have 30 days to provide proof of citizenship before being removed from voter
rolls by their respective county clerks.
Through this review, my office was able to identify and fix potential vulnerabilities in our voter
registration processes while also protecting the rights of eligible voters. We are encouraging
county clerks to be vigilant, especially while processing paper registration forms, to ensure that
all applications are properly complete. I remain committed to a transparent and secure election
process for all Utahns.

Utah Voter Registration Citizenship Review
May 27, 2026

On April 17, 2025, Lt. Gov. Deidre Henderson initiated a comprehensive citizenship audit of
Utah’s voter registration rolls. The purpose of the audit was to gain insight into the scope of any
potential vulnerabilities and help identify any changes necessary to ensure that only eligible
citizens are voting in Utah elections. This report summarizes the findings of the review, which
examined the records of all active and inactive voters as of September 2025.
The Office of the Lieutenant Governor (OLG) compared the voter records first against data from
the Utah Driver License Division (DLD), and then if necessary, through the Systematic Alien
Verification for Entitlements (SAVE) program, administered by the Department of Homeland
Security (DHS) and U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services. If our office could not obtain
citizenship verification through these two sources, we performed a manual review of proper
attestation by the voter on the registration form, in accordance with the National Voter
Registration Act (NVRA). In total, our office reviewed 2,069,640 voter records.
Our office began by verifying the driver license and Social Security numbers of all registered
voters. We identified 2.9% who lacked either a driver license, a Social Security number, or both.
We collaborated with the DLD to add or update approximately 43,000 voter registration records
with driver license numbers and/or the last four digits of Social Security numbers (SSN4).
While we found information obtained from the DLD or SAVE to be reliable in confirming
whether an individual is a citizen, it was not reliable in indicating an individual was not a citizen,
without considering other factors. For example, delays in federal agencies updating recently
naturalized citizens’ records, and naturalized citizens failing to update their information with the
DLD could result in the inability of those systems to confirm citizenship.
Throughout the citizenship review, we worked to strike a balance between identifying ineligible
noncitizens while protecting the rights of eligible voters. Improperly removing an eligible voter
is a violation of that individual’s rights and of federal law.

Federal and State Legal Context
The U.S. and Utah Constitutions establish citizenship as a fundamental requirement for voting.
However, the voter registration system operates under a legal framework that creates challenges
for automated verification (Amendment XIV, Section 2, Article IV, Section 2).
The NVRA requires voters to affirm citizenship under penalty of perjury, but does not require
documentary proof of citizenship at the time of registration. Federal courts have upheld this
standard for federal elections. H.B. 209, passed by the Utah legislature in 2026, provides a
statutory process for addressing voters whose citizenship cannot be confirmed.
Under H.B. 209, voters who affirm citizenship on a registration form and whose citizenship
status is confirmed through the DLD or SAVE program will be eligible to vote a standard ballot.
Voters who affirm citizenship on a registration form, but whose citizenship status cannot be
confirmed through DLD or the SAVE program, and who do not provide proof of citizenship, will
be eligible to vote a ballot that includes only federal races. Our office will notify these voters of
their potential federal-only status and they will have the opportunity to provide proof of
citizenship to their county clerk.

Conducting the Review
Our office attempted to verify citizenship through three methods.
1.​ Citizenship data provided from the Driver License Division (DLD) of the Utah
Department of Public Safety
2.​ Information provided by the Systematic Alien Verification for Entitlements (SAVE)
program, administered by the U.S. Department of Homeland Security, U.S. Citizenship
and Immigration Services, Verification Division
3.​ Manual Review of registration records by the Office of the Lieutenant Governor (OLG)
Driver License Division Database matching
Through our collaboration with the DLD, we cross-referenced all voter records with the DLD
database. In conjunction with our review, the DLD conducted its own review of their database
and updated citizenship statuses, as necessary. Driver license data confirmed 96% of all
registered voters as citizens.

o​ Re-verification of driver license citizenship status to identify any that had been
updated by the DLD
o​ Identified information provided on registration forms, but not included in the
voter records
o​ Evaluated discrepancies with information in the DLD record and in the voter

o​ Reviewed citizenship affirmation checkbox and signature
o​ Reviewed methods used during voter registration
o​ Determined whether the individual voted

●​ 5,659 records confirmed as citizens
●​ 5,007 records not confirmed as citizens
●​ 27 records confirmed as noncitizens (already removed from rolls)
●​ 25 records considered likely noncitizens

Next Steps
To comply with federal law and the National Voter Registration Act (NVRA) and state law as
mandated by H.B. 209, our office will take the following steps this week:
●​ Mail notifications to 5,007 voters whose citizenship status is unconfirmed. If they do not
provide documentary proof of citizenship to their county clerks, they will receive a
federal-only ballot beginning with the 2026 November General Election.
●​ Mail notifications to the 25 probable noncitizens per the challenge process outlined in
H.B. 209. If they fail to provide documentary proof of citizenship to their county clerk
within 30 days of the notification, they will be removed from the voter rolls.
●​ Documentary proof of citizenship is limited to:
o​ a Utah driver license number that verifies U.S. citizenship
o​ a Utah state identification card number that verifies U.S. citizenship
o​ a legible copy of an individual's birth certificate that verifies U.S. citizenship
o​ a legible copy of the pages of an individual's United States passport that identifies
the individual and the individual's passport number
o​ an alien registration number that verifies U.S. citizenship
o​ a legible copy of the voter's United States naturalization documents
o​ a Bureau of Indian Affairs card number, tribal treaty card number, or tribal
enrollment number
o​ a legible copy of a certificate of degree of Indian blood or a Bureau of Indian
Affairs affidavit of birth

o​ other documents or methods of proving United States citizenship that are

Recommendations and Improvements
The citizenship audit identified areas for improvement with our processes and systems used for
voter registration.
●​ Vigilance during processing of registration forms - Paper forms are the most common
method for noncitizens to use for registration. Clerks should carefully inspect that the
citizenship box is marked “Yes” and the affidavit is signed. Clerks should suspend and
not process registrations lacking this information. When verifying driver license numbers
during the registration process, clerks should also verify that the date of birth and SSN4
on the driver license match the information provided by the individual on the registration
form. This will help alleviate typos and other errors that may hinder the ability to verify
citizenship.
●​ Legacy Records - Approximately 2,000 of the unconfirmed records resulted from older
voter records that lacked sufficient information for automated checks through DLD or
SAVE. These voter records were imported into the voter registration database more than
20 years ago when driver license numbers and SSN4 were not required. These voters will
receive a letter asking to provide documentary proof of citizenship or be converted to a
federal-only voter. Clerks should look for opportunities to reach out to these voters to
update their records.
●​ Online registration portal - By default, the online voter registration form selects the
“No” box next to the statement confirming U.S. citizenship. An applicant must change it
to “Yes” to send the registration to the clerk for processing. Previously, people were able
to change the citizenship box to “Yes” without providing any proof of citizenship. Now,
after website improvements in 2025, only individuals with a Utah driver license that
confirms citizenship are able to register online.
●​ Updated System - The State of Utah will implement a new voter registration system in
2027. This system will allow clerks to run a check through SAVE at the time of
registration for any individuals without a driver license number that proves citizenship.
●​ Personal responsibility - Thirty-four of the confirmed or probable noncitizens checked
the box “Yes” next to the statement confirming U.S citizenship, even though the
registration form clearly states a person must be a citizen to register and vote. Individuals
who falsely indicate their citizenship status should be held accountable.
