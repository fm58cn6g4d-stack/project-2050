How the model works

This is a light, transparent model: each lever carries a central 2035 estimate per metric, anchored where possible to published modeling, then combined with four interaction rules. Treat outputs as order-of-magnitude planning numbers, not a score.

  • Baseline. Current-policy 2035 net emissions of 4,650 Mt CO2e, about 30% below 2005 (6,635 Mt), consistent with post-OBBBA projections from Rhodium (27–44% below 2005 in 2035) and REPEAT.
  • Restoring repealed credits. REPEAT estimates OBBBA adds ~470 Mt in 2035, ~$280 a year to household energy costs and ~300 GW of lost wind and solar. Restoration in 2029 is assumed to recover ~65% of that by 2035, split across the electricity, household and EV levers.
  • Transmission interaction. REPEAT found that if transmission grows only at its historical ~1% a year, most of the IRA's potential emissions cuts are lost. Here, clean-power levers deliver 60% of their effect with neither the permitting deal nor the transmission credit, 85% with permitting only, 75% with the credit only, and 100% with both.
  • Delivery interaction. Grant and program levers deliver 85% of their effect and spending without the Chapter 9 funds-delivery rules, reflecting 2025–26 cancellations and DOE's slow IIJA execution.
  • EV pairing. Consumer EV credits and freight electrification each get 10% more abatement when both are on (shared charging build-out).
  • Overlap. Combined emissions cuts and combined bill savings are discounted for diminishing returns (scale 2,600 Mt and $1,500 per household), since several levers chase the same tons and the same utility costs.
  • Ranges. Each lever has a confidence grade (H ±20%, M ±40%, L ±65%); ranges combine them as independent errors. Most House contrast bills and new mandates are graded L.
  • Metrics. Bills are the average across 132M households in 2035, against roughly $2,150 a year in energy spending. Federal cost is FY2029–38 (Stage 1 items are counted in the same window for comparability). Jobs are gross job-years supported by investment and program spending at 4 per $1M, averaged over 2029–35, not net employment. Damages avoided cover disaster, infrastructure and health losses; the global climate benefit uses EPA's 2023 social cost of carbon ($190/t).
  • Not modeled. Macroeconomic feedback, energy-price effects of lower demand, state policy responses, and items without a quantifiable 2035 effect (most Tribal sovereignty, data and institutional reforms).

Sources: REPEAT Project OBBBA analysis (July 2025); Rhodium Group projections; EPA GHG Inventory and 2023 methane rule RIA; Brattle; NIBS Natural Hazard Mitigation Saves; FEMA Building Codes Save; Climate Leadership Council; SEEC Institute Thriving Economy Project (2026). Each lever's notes name its specific anchor.
