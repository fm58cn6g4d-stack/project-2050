# Scaling Geothermal and Cutting Its Costs

Oct 17, 2026 · @espen

## Bottom line

Geothermal can plausibly grow from about 4 GW today to roughly 10 GW of new next-generation capacity by 2035, but only if three things hold: drilling costs keep following the curve Fervo has shown, permitting stops taking 6-8 years, and early projects get financed at lower risk premiums. Targets marked \* are proposals in this plan; other figures are cited.

| Metric | Today | 2030 | 2035 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Installed cost ($/kW) | \~$7,000 (Cape Phase I, Fervo's figure) | $4,500-5,500\* | $3,000 (Fervo's long-term goal) |
| Levelized cost ($/MWh) | \~$70-76 (US geothermal, [Energy Intelligence](https://www.energyintel.com/0000017b-a7d7-de4c-a17b-e7d7f1360000)) | $60-70 ([DOE Liftoff](https://www.thinkgeoenergy.com/us-doe-publishes-report-on-commercial-liftoff-of-next-generation-geothermal/)) | $45 ([DOE EGS Shot](https://www.energy.gov/eere/geothermal/articles/introduction-enhanced-geothermal-shottm-technology-pathways)) |
| Next-gen capacity online | 3.5 MW operating; 100 MW starting up | 1 GW or more\* | 10 GW\* (DOE's stretch goal is 40 GW) |

## Where things stand (October 2026)

The technology has just crossed from pilot to first commercial scale, and the cost curve is real but rests mostly on one company's data.

- **Installed base:** the US has about 3.8 GW of geothermal, mostly conventional hydrothermal in the West ([NREL](https://www.nrel.gov/docs/fy20osti/75134.pdf)).
- **Fervo Cape Station (Utah):** initial power is due in Q4 2026 from a 100 MW first phase; 400 MW more is slated for 2028. Fervo has 658 MW of signed power purchase agreements and a $421 million non-recourse financing ([Fervo Q2 results](https://fervoenergy.com/fervo-energy-reports-second-quarter-2026-results/)).
- **Cost today:** Phase I is about $7,000/kW and Phase II is targeted at $5,500/kW, against about $2,157/kW for a new gas plant in 2025 ([IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/fervo-cape-station-geothermal)).
- **Demand:** Google signed a 396 MW power purchase agreement for Cape Station in September, and a non-binding framework for up to 3 GW through 2033 ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/10/06/1145252/fervo-energy-geothermal-power-streamline-plan/)).
- **Policy:** federal tax credits for geothermal survived the 2025 budget law, BLM created a new exploration permitting shortcut in April 2026, and the House passed a bipartisan permitting bill in June. The Senate has not acted.
- **Potential:** DOE's 2023 analysis found US geothermal could reach 90 GW by 2050 ([DOE](https://www.energy.gov/GeoVision)).

## Cost levers

Drilling is the biggest lever because it makes up over 75% of well cost, and it falls fastest when crews drill many similar wells in a row.

1. **Repetition in drilling.** Fervo cut its drilling time from 71 days in 2022 to 21 days in 2024 and its well cost from $9.4 million to $4.8 million over the first four Cape wells, a learning rate of 35% against the 18% it planned ([Fervo](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240212763499/en)). Policy implication: keep rigs and crews continuously employed by securing a steady project pipeline, because gaps reset the learning.
2. **Well productivity.** More megawatts per well matters as much as cheaper wells. DOE's cost path leans on higher flow rates, longer laterals, hotter rock and larger plants ([Power Magazine](https://www.powermag.com/delving-deeper-new-optimism-for-enhanced-geothermal-systems)). Fervo's Phase II well design targets exactly this.
3. **Standard plant blocks.** Cape uses 33 MW "GeoBlocks" and Fervo is moving to standardized 50 MW plants. Repeating one design across sites is how cost falls on the surface side.
4. **Better exploration.** Dry or weak holes are costly. AI-assisted resource mapping (Zanskar and others) and DOE's resource characterization work cut that risk.
5. **Cheaper capital.** Financing cost is a large share of geothermal's levelized cost because nearly all spending is upfront. Once a few plants perform for several years, lenders should accept lower rates.

**Condition public support on shared data.** Pay milestone-based grants, tied to publishing drilling and well-performance data the way DOE's Utah FORGE project does (FORGE cut well cost from $13 million to $5 million per [DOE's Liftoff report](https://www.thinkgeoenergy.com/us-doe-publishes-report-on-commercial-liftoff-of-next-generation-geothermal/)). This spreads learning beyond one company and is my proposal, not an existing program.

## Permitting and federal land

Geothermal permitting on federal land has taken 6-8 years, and an environmental review can be triggered up to six times along the way ([ThinkGeoEnergy](https://www.thinkgeoenergy.com/pro-geothermal-policies-being-pushed-in-the-us-at-federal-state-levels/?amp=1)). DOE's own analysis found that fixing permitting timelines alone could double 2050 geothermal capacity ([DOE GeoVision](https://www.energy.gov/GeoVision)). BLM manages geothermal leasing on about 245 million surface acres, mostly in the West.

Steps in order of priority:

1. **Pass H.R. 5631 in the Senate.** The House passed the Geothermal Energy Advancement Act on June 2, 2026. It creates a BLM geothermal ombudsman and task force, extends oil-and-gas-style NEPA categorical exclusions to geothermal, and reforms royalties and cost recovery ([Lobby News summary](https://www.thelobbynews.com/congress/bills/119/house-bill/5631)). One analyst expects it to move only inside a broader permitting package, probably not before late 2026 ([Capstone](https://capstonedc.com/insights/how-geothermal-developers-will-benefit-from-updated-federal-policies/)).
2. **Put the April 2026 BLM exclusion into statute.** BLM's new categorical exclusion for small-scale exploration is administrative, so a future administration can reverse it ([H.R. 1077 analysis](https://thelobbynews.com/congress/bills/119/house-bill/1077)). The STEAM Act would codify the broader fix.
3. **Staff BLM's geothermal permitting.** Delays come from competitive leasing and staffing shortages as much as from the law. Fund dedicated reviewers and set shot clocks for lease and permit decisions.
4. **Keep lease sales on a fixed calendar.** BLM has two geothermal sales announced for 2026, including an Idaho sale in October. Predictable annual sales let developers plan drilling campaigns.
5. **Align the states.** Western states formed a geothermal coalition in June 2026. California's governor vetoed a bill that would have exempted exploratory wells from state environmental review ([Capstone](https://capstonedc.com/insights/how-geothermal-developers-will-benefit-from-updated-federal-policies/)), so state action there is the open gap.

## Financing and demand

The federal tax credits are secure through 2033, so the near-term job is reducing first-of-a-kind risk, and the harder timing problem is that credits step down just as costs must fall the most. The 2025 budget law kept the full investment and production credits for geothermal projects that begin construction by 2033, then steps them to 75% in 2034, 50% in 2035 and zero after ([Enterprise Community Partners](https://www.enterprisecommunity.org/sites/default/files/2025-08/What-the-One-Big-Beautiful-Bill-Means.pdf)).

- **Keep corporate offtake flowing.** Data-center buyers are the main demand today (Google's 396 MW deal, 658 MW of contracts at Fervo). Standard contracts and shared transmission upgrades for clusters speed these deals up.
- **Add state clean-firm procurement.** DOE's Liftoff report recommends utility commission procurement mandates, cost-sharing and government capital for early projects ([ThinkGeoEnergy](https://www.thinkgeoenergy.com/us-doe-publishes-report-on-commercial-liftoff-of-next-generation-geothermal/)). A target for firm clean power in Western states would create demand beyond tech companies.
- **Share the resource risk.** Lenders worry most about whether a field will perform. A federal or state drilling-risk guarantee, which pays part of the loss if a field underperforms, could lower borrowing costs for the first several projects. This is my proposal and would need design work.
- **Use federal loans selectively.** Back the first plants of each new technology (see the portfolio table below) rather than later copies of a proven design.
- **Revisit the 2033 deadline in 2030.** If costs are not near $4,500-5,500/kW by then, a short extension of the full credit would be worth debating, with the cost trend as the test.

## Grid access and siting

Transmission is already a risk for the flagship project: Fervo warns of possible curtailments on lines serving Cape Station during 2027 because another asset is joining that network ([Q2 call summary](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/fervo-energy-q2-earnings-call-highlights-2026-08-12/)). Geothermal also competes for queue positions with solar and storage, which dominate Western interconnection queues ([CE Advisors](https://ceadvisors.com/first-enhanced-geothermal-system-in-the-u-s-showcases-an-ancient-technology-with-modern-potential-but-obstacles-persist/)).

- **Cluster first.** Build in resource-rich clusters (Cape Station's resource could reach 4.3 GW, per [Deseret News](https://www.deseret.com/environment/2026/10/14/fervos-4-gigawatt-geothermal-plant-has-big-goals)) and plan transmission for the whole cluster, not plant by plant.
- **Give firm clean power a faster queue lane.** Because geothermal runs around the clock, its grid value per megawatt of line is higher than solar's. Regional operators and FERC could reflect that in queue rules and capacity accreditation. This is my proposal.
- **Sequence by region.** Utah, Nevada, Idaho and Oregon first (Quaise plans a 50 MW "superhot" plant in central Oregon). Then Texas, where Sage Geosystems is building a first plant in the ERCOT market, because that tests whether geothermal works outside the traditional hotspots ([Enki AI summary](https://enkiai.com/geothermal/fervo-energy-geothermal-2026-500-mw-project-with-google/)).

## Workforce, supply chain and technology portfolio

Enhanced geothermal borrows the shale playbook, so the fastest way to scale it is to move oil-and-gas rigs, crews and suppliers over rather than build a new industry. Fervo uses shale-style drill bits and horizontal wells, and, per its Q2 results, added a third rig from the drilling contractor Helmerich & Payne for Phase II ([Hart Energy](https://www.hartenergy.com/exclusives/oil-gas-drilling-tech-transfer-boosts-fervos-geothermal-prowess-208181)). It also signed domestic supply agreements with ABB for electrical systems and Vallourec for tubulars ([Blockspace](https://blockspace.media/insight/fervo-google-geothermal-framework-3-gw/)).

- **Workforce:** fund oil-and-gas-to-geothermal cross-training. DOE announced up to $165 million in 2022 for oil-and-gas technology transfer, and the proposed Hot Rock Act adds a cross-training program ([Foley Hoag](https://www.foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/blogs/energy-and-climate-counsel/2026/march/new-daylight-for-geothermal-how-federal-support-and-bipartisan-permitting-reform-are-fueling-a-geothermal-boom/)).
- **Supply chain:** commit to bulk orders of casing, bits and turbines for several projects at once, as DOE did for reactors, to give suppliers a reason to expand.

Different technologies are at very different stages. Fund by stage and set gates:

| Approach | Example | Stage | Plan stance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Enhanced geothermal (EGS) | Fervo, Cape Station, Utah | First commercial plant starting up | Main bet: scale now |
| Pressure geothermal | Sage Geosystems, South Texas, 150 MW by 2028 | Raised $97M in January 2026 for first commercial plant | Back first plant, then reassess |
| Closed-loop | Eavor, Geretsried, Germany (8.2 MWe) | Pilot, start-up slipped from early 2025 | Pilot-stage funding only |
| Superhot rock | Quaise, planned 50 MW in Oregon | Seeking $100M for first plant | Research grants with milestone gates |

## Milestones and metrics

The next 18 months decide whether the plan works, because Cape Station's first year of output and the Senate's permitting vote are the two events that set everything else.

| When | Milestone | Test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Q4 2026 | Cape Phase I (100 MW) delivers first power | Reaches design output with little downtime in year one |
| Late 2026 to 2027 | Senate passes H.R. 5631 or an equivalent | Enacted, with BLM ombudsman funded |
| 2028 | Cape Phase II (400 MW) and Sage's 150 MW plant | Phase II at or below $5,500/kW |
| 2030 | 1 GW or more of next-gen capacity online; review the 2033 credit deadline | $4,500-5,500/kW and $60-70/MWh |
| 2033 | Last year to begin construction at the full tax credit | Pipeline of projects ready to start |
| 2035 | 10 GW of next-gen capacity online\* | $3,000/kW and $45/MWh |

## Risks

- **Evidence rests on one company.** Nearly all the cost data is Fervo's own, and the 2030 and 2035 cost figures are company targets, not results. A weak first year at Cape would raise financing costs for the whole sector.
- **Long-run reservoir performance.** No enhanced geothermal field has run for decades yet. How fast output declines (general technical concern, not researched here) drives real lifetime cost.
- **Local opposition.** Induced seismicity and water use are recurring local concerns for geothermal (general knowledge, not researched here).
- **Grid delays.** Cape already faces possible 2027 curtailments.
- **Policy reversals.** The April 2026 BLM exclusion is administrative, and DOE cancelled funding for two hydrogen hubs this year ([Gasworld](https://www.gasworld.com/story/court-documents-reveal-political-bias-in-rescinded-doe-hydrogen-hub-funding/2255697.article/)), a reminder that federal support can change.
- **Competition.** Geothermal's case is firm, around-the-clock power, not the lowest cost: new gas and solar plus storage are cheaper per MWh, and the [Energy Transitions Commission](https://static.energy-transitions.org/uploads/resources/20260210_ETC-Representatives-Meeting_Role-of-Nuclear-and-geothermal_vFinal.pdf) expects new geothermal and nuclear to stay higher-cost than other clean options in most regions.
