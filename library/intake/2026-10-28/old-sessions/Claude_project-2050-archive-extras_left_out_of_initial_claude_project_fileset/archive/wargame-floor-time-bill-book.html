Freedom to Vote Act + John Lewis Voting Rights Advancement Act

Modeled on S. 2747 (Freedom to Vote) and H.R. 4 (John Lewis VRAA)

Set a national floor for ballot access, end partisan gerrymandering, and restore the enforcement teeth the Supreme Court stripped from the Voting Rights Act in Shelby County and Brnovich.

Key provisions
  • Access floor. Automatic and same-day registration, online registration, a minimum 15 days of early voting, no-excuse mail ballots and drop boxes, and Election Day as a federal holiday.
  • Small-dollar matching. Public matching of small contributions (6:1 up to $200) to rebuild a grassroots donor base against big money.
  • Rights restoration. Restore voting rights to citizens upon completion of any incarceration.
  • Anti-gerrymandering. Ban partisan gerrymanders outright, require neutral map criteria, and give voters a private right of action to challenge maps in federal court.
  • Election protection. Paper-ballot requirement with risk-limiting audits, chain-of-custody rules, and federal protection for election workers against harassment and partisan removal.
  • Preclearance, rebuilt. The John Lewis VRAA writes a new Section 5 coverage formula responding to Shelby County, adds nationwide "practice-based" preclearance for known-discriminatory changes, and reverses Brnovich's narrowing of Section 2.
Path Democracy carve-out (or regular order under full reform) Enacted 100% Durability Contested

Campaign Finance Transparency (DISCLOSE) Act

Modeled on the DISCLOSE Act and Honest Ads Act

End anonymous "dark money" in federal elections by forcing the true sources of political spending into the open.

Key provisions
  • Donor disclosure. Any organization spending on federal elections must disclose donors who give more than $10,000, ending the 501(c)(4) dark-money shield.
  • Beneficial ownership. Require the real people behind political-spending entities to be named, and add "stand by your ad" attribution for organizations.
  • Digital ad transparency. Extend broadcast-style disclosure and a public ad file to online platforms (the Honest Ads Act).
  • Coordination rules. Tighten the line between candidates and nominally independent super PACs to curb functionally coordinated spending.
  • Enforcement. Restore a functioning FEC quorum practice and close reporting loopholes for shell entities.
Path Democracy carve-out (rides the Freedom to Vote family) Enacted 100% Durability Contested

Washington, D.C. Admission Act

Modeled on H.R. 51

Grant the 700,000+ residents of the District full voting representation by admitting the State of Washington, Douglass Commonwealth.

Key provisions
  • Statehood. Admit the residential and commercial District as the 51st state, with one voting House member and two senators.
  • A shrunken federal district. Retain a small constitutional "Capital" — the Mall, White House, Capitol, and principal federal buildings — as the seat of government under Article I.
  • Twenty-third Amendment fix. Trigger an expedited repeal of the 23rd Amendment, which otherwise would award the tiny residual district three electoral votes for a near-empty population.
  • Transition. Convert the existing mayor and council into state officers; assume state functions over courts, National Guard, and budget autonomy.
Note on the voteArticle IV §3 admits new states by simple majority; the only barrier is the filibuster, not the Constitution. It is the single most durable act on the board — a state, once admitted, cannot be un-admitted — which is why it draws the fiercest resistance and stays the closest vote.
Path Regular order under full reform (carve-out eligible) Enacted 83% Durability Entrenched

Government Ethics & Anti-Corruption Act

Modeled on the Protecting Our Democracy Act and TRUST in Congress Act

Close the conflicts of interest that let public officials profit from public office, and harden the guardrails a hostile executive learned to bend.

Key provisions
  • Congressional stock ban. Prohibit members of Congress — and their spouses and dependents — from holding or trading individual stocks; require blind trusts or divestiture.
  • Revolving-door limits. Extend lobbying cooling-off periods, and bar former members from lobbying their old chamber.
  • Executive conflicts. Require presidents and vice presidents to divest or blind-trust financial holdings and disclose tax returns.
  • Inspector-general protection. Bar removal of IGs without cause and advance notice to Congress, and protect their independence.
  • Executive divestiture. Codify the Day-1 divestiture executive order (mandatory blind trusts, closed foreign-payment/LLC loopholes) and attach criminal emoluments penalties.
  • Enforcement machinery. Reorient DOJ, SEC, CFTC, FTC, and CFPB toward anti-corruption, and restructure the FEC with genuinely independent commissioners to break the 3–3 deadlock.
  • Foreign-influence transparency. Modernize FARA and strengthen Hatch Act enforcement against political use of federal office.
Path Full reform required — good-government, not election law, so no carve-out Enacted 99% Durability Contested

Supreme Court Ethics & Accountability Act

Modeled on the Supreme Court Ethics, Recusal, and Transparency Act

Subject the justices to the same enforceable ethics standards that already bind every other federal judge.

Key provisions
  • Binding code of conduct. Adopt an enforceable code for the Supreme Court — currently the only federal court without one.
  • Recusal standards. Set clear recusal rules and require justices to publish written explanations of recusal decisions.
  • Gift and travel disclosure. Impose disclosure of gifts, travel, and hospitality stricter than current practice, with penalties for noncompliance.
  • Complaint mechanism. Create a process to receive and review ethics complaints against justices.
  • Amicus transparency. Require disclosure of funding behind organizations filing briefs and financing litigation.
The frictionThe most contested of the anti-corruption cluster: it regulates the branch that will ultimately rule on its own constitutionality, which is both why it matters and why it is the least durable reform on the board.

Permanent Expanded Child Tax Credit

Makes the 2021 American Rescue Plan CTC permanent

Restore and lock in the monthly child benefit that cut child poverty nearly in half — the fastest-felt, most legible benefit in the whole program.

Key provisions
  • Credit amount. $3,600 per child under 6 and $3,000 per child ages 6–17, indexed to inflation.
  • Full refundability. Remove the earnings threshold so the lowest-income families receive the full credit — the single most important change, and the one Manchin's work-requirement objection blocked in 2021.
  • Monthly delivery. Pay the credit in advance monthly deposits rather than a once-a-year lump sum.
  • High-income phase-out. Retain phase-outs at upper incomes so the benefit concentrates on working and middle-class families.

Universal Pre-K & Child Care Act

Modeled on the Build Back Better care provisions

Make child care affordable and pre-K universal — the highest-return social investment in the package, and the slowest to arrive.

Key provisions

  • Head Start alignment. Coordinate and expand Head Start within the new system.

Public Option & Medicare at 55

Modeled on the Medicare-X and Medicare buy-in proposals

Add a public plan to every marketplace and lower Medicare's door to age 55, insuring millions while bending the cost curve through public competition.

Key provisions
  • Public option. A federal public health plan on ACA exchanges in every state, reimbursing providers at Medicare-plus rates.
  • Medicare at 55. Lower Medicare eligibility to 55, letting the most expensive pre-retirement cohort buy in.

  • Drug pricing. Expand Medicare negotiation to more drugs, faster, and extend the $35 insulin cap and out-of-pocket limits to all payers.
  • Public-health rebuild. Restore and expand NIH and CDC funding; grow community health centers from 32M to 36M patients served.
The fightMedicare-adjacent reimbursement is a direct revenue hit to hospitals and specialists; expect the industry's full lobbying weight against it even as the coverage gains prove nearly impossible to repeal.

Clean Energy Standard & Carbon Dividend Act

Clean electricity standard + transmission reform + carbon fee-and-dividend

Drive the grid to 80% clean by 2035, build the wires to carry it, and put a price on carbon whose revenue goes back to households as a check they can see.

Key provisions
  • Clean electricity standard. 80% clean power by 2035 on a technology-neutral basis (renewables, nuclear, hydro, geothermal, carbon capture), enforced through tradable compliance credits.
  • Transmission & the grid. FERC backstop siting authority for interstate lines, interregional transfer minimums, cost-allocation reform, and interconnection-queue overhaul — the binding constraint on the whole transition.
  • Carbon fee. An economy-wide, rising fee on carbon at the point of extraction/import, with a border carbon adjustment so importers pay the same price.
  • Clean Energy Dividend. Return the majority of net fee revenue to households as an equal, branded, quarterly dividend check; most households in the bottom two-thirds come out ahead.
  • Just transition. Direct the remaining revenue to grid resilience, transmission buildout, and a fund for fossil-fuel communities.
Why the dividend mattersA clean-electricity mandate has no constituency; a dividend check has millions of names on it. Bolting the dividend on is the one move in the program that deliberately buys the bill its own durability — moving it out of the "regulation a future EPA can gut" category and toward the stickiness of a cash transfer.
Path Partial reconciliation (fee & dividend) + regular order (standard) Enacted 100% Durability Contested

PRO Act & Sectoral Bargaining

Modeled on H.R. 20 / S. 567, combined with sectoral-bargaining and portable-benefits frameworks

The most consequential rewrite of American labor law since Taft-Hartley — tilting the field back toward workers who want to organize, and opening bargaining beyond the single worksite.

Key provisions
  • ABC test. Adopt a federal ABC standard for employee vs. independent-contractor status, nationalizing the gig-classification fight California fought over Prop 22.
  • Override right-to-work. Permit fair-share fee agreements, overriding state right-to-work laws.
  • Penalties with teeth. Civil penalties and personal liability for executives for unfair labor practices, replacing today's toothless remedies.
  • Protect the strike. Ban permanent replacement of strikers; expand secondary and intermittent action; prohibit captive-audience meetings and streamline recognition.
  • Sectoral bargaining & portable benefits. Establish sectoral frameworks (restaurant, home care, gig) so wages and standards can be set industry-wide, with benefits that follow the worker between jobs.
  • Executive backstop. Paired with NLRB precedent reversals (captive-audience ban, joint-employer, 21-day elections), an OSHA heat standard, and a wage-theft enforcement surge.
Path Regular order under full reform Enacted 100% Durability Contested

Living Wage & Small-Business Transition Act

$17, then indexed to two-thirds of the median · builds on the Raise the Wage Act with EPI/Low Pay Commission design

Raise the floor to a genuine living wage and keep it there — while giving the small employers and low-wage regions that face the steepest climb a gentler slope, so the tide lifts every boat instead of swamping the smallest.

Key provisions
  • $17, then indexed to the median. Raise the federal minimum to $17, then peg it permanently to two-thirds of the national median hourly wage — the benchmark the UK, most of the EU, and the current expert consensus have converged on. It self-adjusts with the economy and never re-erodes the way the frozen $7.25 did.
  • A longer runway for small employers. Large firms (500+ workers or $1B+ revenue) reach the floor over five years; the smallest businesses phase in over eight — the thinnest-margin firms get the most time to adjust.
  • A regional circuit-breaker. In counties where the scheduled floor would exceed ~60% of the local median — the mostly rural, low-wage markets where the research finds employment effects turn negative — the phase-in automatically extends. Same destination everywhere, a gentler grade where the relative jump is largest, with a hard sunset so it can't calcify into a permanent regional loophole.
  • Small-Business Wage Transition Credit. A temporary, refundable payroll-tax credit offsetting a declining share of the added wage cost for small firms during their phase-in — support for small business without a two-tier wage.
  • A recession off-ramp. If unemployment crosses an automatic trigger the next scheduled step pauses until conditions normalize, and the annual indexed adjustment is capped — so the floor never rises into a downturn. It never falls, either.
  • End the subminimum wages. Phase out the $2.13 tipped wage (over seven years, for restaurant runway), the youth wage, and the 14(c) disability certificate — everyone reaches the same full floor.
Procedural note — a useful splitThe wage mandate is Byrd-barred (the parliamentarian struck the $15 wage from the 2021 rescue plan), so it needs regular order and full filibuster reform. But the Small-Business Transition Credit is fiscal, so it can ride reconciliation — meaning the cushion for small employers can be locked in even before the mandate clears the floor.
Path Regular order (wage) + reconciliation (small-business credit) Enacted 96% Durability Entrenched

Housing Abundance & Supply Act

Modeled on the Neighborhood Homes and YIMBY-incentive proposals — building on the ROAD to Housing Act, enacted July 2026

Supply the capital and administrative capacity the 2026 ROAD Act deliberately left out — so three years of zoning and permitting reform finally convert into built housing.

Key provisions
  • Emergency response. An emergency housing declaration with $85B in rental and construction assistance in the first reconciliation bill.
  • LIHTC expansion. Substantially enlarge the Low-Income Housing Tax Credit, add a middle-income credit, and a Neighborhood Homes rehab credit for distressed markets.
  • Zoning incentives. Condition a Housing Supply Fund and federal transportation/CDBG dollars on local reforms — by-right multifamily, legalized ADUs, reduced parking minimums.
  • Construction & public housing. A $250B mandatory revolving construction-loan fund and the Public Housing Capital Fund raised $3.5B→$9.2B against an $80B repair backlog.
  • Vouchers & workforce. 400,000 new Section 8 vouchers toward an entitlement, plus 450,000 construction apprentices.
  • HUD capacity. Fund the staffing and operations ROAD never appropriated — the ~35 programs and two unfunded pilots it assigned to a department absorbing a 24% staffing cut — so reforms already on the books can actually be executed.
  • Social housing pilot. Seed a mixed-income public development pilot.
Compounding on the ROAD ActThe ROAD Act — enacted July 11, 2026 without the President's signature — was the deregulatory half of housing policy with no money attached: zoning and permitting reform, manufactured-housing rules, small-balance mortgages, an institutional-investor limit — and no HUD funding. This bill is the other half. Capital landing on ground already cleared of permitting friction converts to units far more efficiently than a cold start: roughly 4–7M additional units over a decade versus ~3–4M without ROAD in place. The old federalism catch — federal dollars can incentivize zoning but not command it — is precisely what ROAD spent three years softening.
Path Regular order under full reform (recon-eligible core) Enacted 100% Durability Contested

Antitrust Enforcement & Competition Act

Modeled on CALERA and the American Innovation and Choice Online Act

Rewrite merger law for a concentrated economy and fund the enforcers to use it — the one bill on the board with genuine populist-right reach.

Key provisions
  • Merger standards. Shift the burden to merging parties in large deals, block acquisitions that entrench dominant firms, and lower reporting thresholds.
  • Killer acquisitions. Scrutinize a dominant firm's purchase of nascent competitors, and tighten review of vertical and conglomerate mergers.
  • Platform conduct. Ban self-preferencing by dominant digital platforms and require interoperability.
  • Enforcer capacity. Increase DOJ Antitrust and FTC funding via higher merger-filing fees — the fiscal core that can ride reconciliation.
  • Labor markets. Ban most non-compete clauses and revive monopsony and Robinson-Patman enforcement.

Junk Fees & Consumer Protection Act

Codifies the all-in pricing and junk-fee rulemakings

Kill the hidden fees that ambush people at checkout — low-cost, unusually bipartisan, and felt the moment a customer sees the real price.

Key provisions
  • All-in pricing. Require the advertised price to include all mandatory fees — tickets, hotels, and short-term rentals up front.
  • Ban surprise fees. Cap or prohibit surprise bank-overdraft charges, high credit-card late fees, airline family-seating fees, and "convenience" surcharges.
  • Telecom & cable. Ban undisclosed add-on fees and require clear, itemized billing.
  • Click to cancel. Require cancellation to be as easy as sign-up for subscriptions.
  • Enforcement. Grant the FTC rulemaking and penalty authority and a consumer private right of action.

Comprehensive Immigration Reform

Modeled on the U.S. Citizenship Act framework

Legalize long-settled residents, clear the backlogs, and rebuild an asylum and border system that actually functions.

Key provisions
  • Earned citizenship. A pathway for Dreamers, TPS holders, farmworkers, essential workers, and residents with 10+ years and clean records.
  • DHS restructuring. Separate customs enforcement (retained) from interior immigration enforcement, replacing the latter with a Humanitarian Processing Service; rebuild USCIS capacity (12,000 hires).
  • Asylum overhaul. A 180-day processing timeline with more judges and officers, and expanded alternatives to detention.
  • Clear the backlogs. Recapture unused green cards, exempt categories from caps, and cut family- and employment-based wait times.
  • Labor pathways. Pair mandatory E-Verify with legalization; expand agricultural and care-worker visas; smart border investment over wall construction.
The hardest floor voteThe most contested regular-order bill after statehood — no reconciliation vehicle to hide behind and real defection risk among border-state and populist-lane members. Long-run fiscal effects of legalization are positive.

Billionaire Minimum Income Tax

Modeled on the Billionaire Minimum Income Tax proposal

Ensure the very wealthiest households pay a minimum rate on all their income — including the paper gains that today go untaxed for a lifetime.

Key provisions
  • 25% minimum. A 25% minimum tax on total income — including unrealized capital gains — for households worth more than $100 million.
  • Mark-to-market. Value tradable assets annually; apply a deferral charge and installment rules to illiquid holdings.
  • No double tax. Credit prepaid amounts against future tax on realized gains.
  • Enforcement. Fund IRS high-end enforcement and write valuation rules.
The constitutional exposureIts greatest vulnerability isn't passage — it's the courts. Moore v. United States left the realization question open and several justices signaled interest in striking a tax on unrealized gains. It is the single most legally fragile item in the program: near-certain to pass, one 5–4 opinion from nonexistent. Pairing it with the broader corporate/income raisers (Bill 18) diversifies that risk.

Corporate & High-Income Tax Fairness Act

Modeled on the Biden-era revenue proposals

Rebuild the revenue base on conventional, court-durable ground — so the program's pay-fors don't rest on a single contested theory.

Key provisions
  • Corporate rate. Raise the corporate income tax rate from 21% toward 28%.
  • Top individual rate. Restore the 39.6% top bracket and add a surtax on the highest incomes.
  • Capital gains. Tax capital gains at ordinary rates above a high threshold and limit stepped-up basis at death.
  • Carried interest. Close the carried-interest loophole so fund managers' fees are taxed as ordinary income.
  • Global & buybacks. Strengthen the global minimum tax, curb inversions, and raise the stock-buyback excise.
Why it matters strategicallyUnlike the billionaire tax, none of this depends on taxing unrealized gains, so it carries no Moore exposure. It is the durable revenue floor beneath the whole agenda — the offset that survives even if the Court strikes the flashier one.

Puerto Rico Status Act

Modeled on the Puerto Rico Status Act (H.R. 8393, passed the House in 2022)

Resolve, at last, the status of 3.2 million American citizens with no voting representation — by letting them decide.

Paid Family & Medical Leave

Modeled on the FAMILY Act

End America’s status as the only wealthy country without national paid leave — twelve weeks, as social insurance, for every worker.

Key provisions
  • 12 weeks, universal. Paid family and medical leave for birth, adoption, serious illness, caregiving, and military deployment — covering every worker, including self-employed and gig workers.

War Powers & Iran Accountability Act

The war-powers and Iran-transition framework of the unified agenda

Return the war power to the branch the Constitution gave it — and wind down the inherited conflict under law rather than improvisation.

Key provisions
  • Notification framework. First-ever statutory requirement of congressional notification before military strikes, with defined emergency exceptions.
