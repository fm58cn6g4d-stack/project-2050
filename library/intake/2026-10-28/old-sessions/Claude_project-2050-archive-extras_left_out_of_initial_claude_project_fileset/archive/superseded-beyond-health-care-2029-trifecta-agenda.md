# Beyond Health Care: The 2029 Trifecta Agenda

Oct 12, 2026 · Espen Swanson · [Live doc](https://claude.ai/code/artifact/eb98710c-e896-4076-8878-5d7976ed2d98)

## Bottom line

Beyond the health bill, a 2029 trifecta should spend its scarce floor time on three things: fixing the rules it wins under, delivering benefits people feel before the November 2030 midterms, and rebuilding the state that delivers them. The test for every item is whether it makes the next Democratic majority more likely, not only whether it is good policy.

This doc assumes the same setup as the health bill tab: a 2029 trifecta, 52 to 54 Democratic senators, and a filibuster change. Health care, revenue, passenger rail, public safety and constitutional amendments have their own workstreams and appear here only where they interact.

## The agenda at a glance

Fifteen areas in four tiers. Reconciliation can carry most of the money; none of the rules changes can ride it.

| Area | Tier | Vehicle | Target timing | What makes it last |

| Voting rights and redistricting | 1. Rules | Regular order (filibuster change) | Enacted by mid-2029, in force for 2030 maps | Federal standards for House elections that states cannot unwind |
| Courts | 1. Rules | Regular order | 2029 to 2030 | Term limits and new judgeships change the bench over a decade |
| Executive-power guardrails | 1. Rules | Regular order; some appropriations riders | 2029 | Statutory limits a future president cannot reverse by order |
| DC and Puerto Rico statehood | 1. Rules | Regular order | 2029 | Two to four Senate seats, permanent |

## Tier 1: Rules of the game

These protect the majority itself. They need the filibuster change, so they go first, while the majority is largest and before any special election or retirement narrows it.

**Voting rights and redistricting.** On April 29, 2026, the Court ruled 6 to 3 in [Louisiana v. Callais](https://www.findlaw.com/?p=213981) that a race-conscious Section 2 remedy needs a strong inference of intentional discrimination. A rebuilt Voting Rights Act should therefore stand on two legs.

- **Elections Clause leg (House races):** Congress's power over the time, place and manner of House elections does not depend on proving discrimination. Use it to ban partisan gerrymanders, require independent commissions, and set national floors for registration, early voting and mail ballots.
- **Enforcement leg (all races):** build a detailed congressional record of intentional discrimination to support a new preclearance formula that can survive the current Court.
- **Option to weigh:** multi-member House districts with ranked-choice voting (the Fair Representation Act model). It ends the gerrymander fight but is a large change to defend.
- **Timing:** maps must be set for the 2030 elections, or for the 2032 cycle after the 2030 census at the latest.

**Courts.**

- Supreme Court term limits by statute (18-year active terms, then senior status) and a binding ethics code.
- New district and circuit judgeships, matched to caseload.
- Limits on nationwide injunctions and judge-shopping, written to bind both parties.
- Court expansion stays a fallback, not the opener.

**Executive-power guardrails.**

- Restore civil-service protections and close the reclassification route to mass firings.
- Give the Impoundment Control Act teeth: automatic release of withheld funds and a private right of action.
- Reform the National Emergencies Act so emergencies lapse without a congressional vote.
- Trade: in February 2026 the Court held that IEEPA does not authorize tariffs, and it [left Congress room](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/02/Supreme-Court-Strikes-Down-IEEPA-Tariffs-What-Decision-Means-Tariff-Payers-and-Refund-Claim-Buyers) to authorize them expressly. Write a trade statute that grants tariff power deliberately and time-limits it, including the Section 122 fallback the administration used.
- Protect DOJ and inspector-general independence, and tighten Hatch Act and emoluments enforcement.

**DC and Puerto Rico statehood.** This is the only lever that changes the Senate's structural tilt. DC statehood is a simple-majority bill once the filibuster changes. Puerto Rico needs a binding status vote first.

**Campaign finance and disclosure.** Full disclosure of dark money and small-donor matching funds. Overturning Citizens United belongs to the constitutional-reform workstream.

## Tier 2: Benefits people feel by 2030

These build constituencies. The rule is visibility before the November 2030 midterm: a benefit that starts in 2032 does nothing for the majority that passed it.

**Family benefits.**

- Refundable child tax credit, paid monthly through the IRS, with no earnings test.
- Child care: cap costs at a share of income and fund supply through state grants. Universal pre-K for 3- and 4-year-olds.
- Paid family and medical leave through a social-insurance fund.
- Vehicle: reconciliation. Financing competes with the health bill for the same top-end revenue (see the revenue tab).

**Housing supply.**

- Tie federal transportation and community grants to local zoning reform that allows more homes.
- Create a federal financing vehicle for mixed-income social housing that recycles its capital.
- Repeal the Faircloth cap on new public housing; expand the Low-Income Housing Tax Credit.
- Speed environmental review for infill housing.

**Labor law.**

- The PRO Act: real penalties for illegal firings, faster first contracts, and limits on captive-audience meetings.
- Pilot sectoral wage boards in a few industries.
- Raise and index the federal minimum wage.
- Why it lasts: unions are the coalition's organizing infrastructure, so the payoff compounds.

**Social Security.** The 2026 Trustees project the retirement trust fund [runs dry in late 2032](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-06-25_IF13256_e1a3332f1a9ae29c3698a1f2e5a5a1d598ec6c57.html), and the combined funds in the third quarter of 2034. That falls inside this trifecta's window, so the majority will legislate on Social Security whether it plans to or not.

- Act first and on Democratic terms: apply the payroll tax above $400,000, raise the minimum benefit, and avoid any cut to scheduled benefits.
- Reconciliation cannot touch Social Security, so this needs regular order and the filibuster change.
- Link to the AI-era fiscal workstream: a shrinking wage base makes payroll-only financing weaker over time.

**Climate and energy.**

- Restore the clean-energy credits H.R. 1 cut back, as direct pay where possible.
- Pass permitting and transmission reform so projects actually get built: a federal siting backstop for interregional lines and firm deadlines for review.
- Locate manufacturing and grid projects across many districts so repeal has local opponents.

## Tier 3: State capacity

Every Tier 2 benefit fails if the agencies cannot deliver it, and slow delivery is what voters remember. Treat delivery speed as a policy goal with its own funding and deadlines.

- **Rebuild gutted agencies:** restore staffing and statutory missions at Education, NOAA, CDC, the IRS enforcement and customer-service functions, and foreign assistance. Write minimum staffing and program floors into statute where possible.
- **Standing digital-delivery corps:** a permanent, civil-service technology workforce that builds benefit systems in house rather than through one-off contracts.
- **Automatic enrollment by default:** use data the government already holds (tax returns, Medicaid, SNAP) to enroll eligible people without applications. This also makes benefits harder to quietly shrink.
- **Permitting and procurement reform:** shorter timelines for federal review and simpler contracting, so infrastructure money turns into visible projects before 2030.
- **Statutory protection:** pair the rebuild with the Tier 1 civil-service guardrails, so a future administration cannot empty these agencies again by order.

## Tier 4: Contested but unavoidable

These carry real political risk, but leaving them alone carries more. The design goal for each is a version the coalition can defend in swing districts.

**Immigration.**

- Legal status and a path to citizenship for Dreamers, TPS holders and long-settled residents.
- Enforcement accountability: identification and body-camera rules for agents, limits on enforcement at schools, hospitals and churches, and independent oversight of detention.
- Modernize legal immigration: clear employment-visa and family backlogs.
- Pair it with visible border-processing capacity so the bill is not framed as open borders.

**AI and work.**

- Transition support for displaced workers: wage insurance and portable benefits.
- Protect the tax base as income shifts from wages to capital (see the AI-era fiscal workstream).
- Baseline safety and transparency rules for frontier systems, and limits on algorithmic decisions in hiring, lending and benefits.

**Antitrust and prices.**

- Fund the antitrust agencies and update merger law for platforms.
- Target the prices voters see: groceries, rent-setting software, ticketing, junk fees.
- Make the noncompete ban statutory (it also appears in the health bill).

**Abortion rights.** Codify a federal statutory right to abortion, contraception and IVF. These move as separate bills alongside the health package, with little or no federal cost.

## Sequencing, 2029 to 2030

The calendar runs rules first, money by summer, and delivery before the midterm. Each phase depends on the gate before it. (The live doc draws this as a roadmap.)

| Phase | Dates | Main items | Gate to next phase |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Open strong | Jan to Apr 2029 | Filibuster change; voting rights bill; DC statehood; executive-power limits | Budget resolution adopted |
| 2. Reconciliation I | May to Sep 2029 | Health bill money; child tax credit; child care and leave; clean-energy credits | Reconciliation I signed |
| 3. Structural bills | Oct 2029 to Mar 2030 | PRO Act; court reforms; immigration; housing and permitting | 2030 maps locked |
| 4. Deliver and defend | Apr to Nov 2030 | Social Security deal; agency rebuild; benefits reach homes | Midterm, Nov 5, 2030 |

The filibuster change has to come in the first weeks, while the majority is largest. Reconciliation I carries the money so its benefits start in 2030. Social Security lands last but must pass before the late-2032 depletion, and the Floor Time calendar should budget its days.

## Design rules for durability

1. **Pay benefits automatically.** Mandatory spending, trust funds and auto-enrollment survive changes in administration better than annual appropriations or opt-in programs.
2. **Front-load what voters can see.** Anything that can show results before November 2030 goes first; long-build items start early but are not sold as the payoff.
3. **Spend floor time on rules.** Reconciliation can carry most of the money. Senate floor days are the scarce input, so they go to the structural bills reconciliation cannot carry.
4. **Spread benefits geographically.** Projects and payments in many districts give repeal local opponents.
5. **Run through states with a federal backstop.** State partnerships build allies, but every program needs a federal fallback where a state refuses.
6. **Write for the current Court.** Ground each statute in the strongest available constitutional power (the Elections Clause, taxing and spending), and build the legislative record as if it will be challenged.
7. **Avoid sunsets.** Temporary provisions create cliffs the other party can let expire.

## Open questions

- How much top-end revenue is left for family benefits and housing once the health bill takes its share?
- Does the filibuster change cover all legislation, or only voting rights and statehood?
- Can the redistricting standards take effect for 2030, or only for the post-census 2032 maps?
- Which Social Security package can hold 50 votes plus the Vice President?
- How large a Senate majority is realistic, and which items fall away at 51?
