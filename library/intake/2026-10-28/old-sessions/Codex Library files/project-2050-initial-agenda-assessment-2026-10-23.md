## What the assessment establishes

Project 2050 already has a useful governing and analytical foundation. Its policy IDs, chosen designs, alternatives, financing channels, legislative vehicles and shared rebuilding process make it possible to investigate the program as a connected agenda. The strongest work often identifies its own limits: the Social Security file acknowledges that the adopted baseline does not attain solvency, the CMS register preserves unpriced work, and the incidence report explains that an average gain can conceal households that lose. Those distinctions should be retained as the project becomes more public.

The principal gap is between a specified policy and a demonstrated account of its consequences. Much of the agenda explains what government would change and assigns a budget amount. Far less consistently does it establish how the change would reach people, what existing resources it would use, which constraints would bind, and how we would know whether it served the charter’s purposes. This is particularly important for child poverty, dependable care, practical opportunity and public influence. These goals are central to the charter, but they do not yet have an analytical foundation comparable to the fiscal ledger.

Three conclusions deserve attention before the project makes stronger claims of readiness. First, the current fiscal result is conditional on uncertain revenue and savings, incomplete costs and an unreconciled surge cash path. Second, the legislative plan is not yet a feasible schedule for the whole adopted agenda: the aggregate floor estimate exceeds its budget, the vote simulator excludes important bills from the calendar, and Moving America retains a conflicting 2031 authorization route. Third, the household model is useful for comparing distributions under stated assumptions, but it is neither a poverty model nor evidence that every middle-income household gains. None of these findings requires abandoning the project’s ambitions. They identify the next questions the analysis must answer.

The next stage should therefore retain the two contrasting pilots while addressing a small set of shared constraints and researching defining outcomes alongside them. A government-wide inventory or a new integrated scenario engine would be premature as a prerequisite. A narrow funding bridge, an institutional implementation chain and a carefully bounded household explanation can already produce useful work.

## Method and interpretation

The matrices below cover all twenty converted canonical domains and transportation. To keep seven separate dimensions readable, they are presented as three linked tables with the same domain rows. There is no combined readiness score.

An assessment concerns the material inspected, not every argument that could be made for a policy. A source list is evidence that material has been identified; it is not evidence that the project has applied it correctly. A legal vehicle is a proposed route, not a determination that every provision qualifies. A dollar estimate may support comparison without being a congressional score. A named agency is a useful start without establishing its workload, available capacity or funding.

Four finding types recur:

| Type | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Missing analysis** | A question has not been answered well enough to support the intended claim | No estimate of the adopted package’s residual child poverty |
| **Conflicting record** | Files, passages or generated results disagree about the same design, amount or schedule | REV-SC1 described both as adopted and held in reserve |
| **Design weakness** | A specified mechanism leaves a consequential problem unresolved, even before exact effects are known | AI-attributable displacement is an unresolved gate for obtaining assistance |
| **Specialist review** | A conclusion requires legal, parliamentary, actuarial, fiscal or operational expertise beyond this assessment | Constitutional repair after Callais; a joint Social Security package score |

Some findings belong to more than one type. Absence of analysis is not proof that a policy will fail; a disclosed limitation still constrains what the project can claim.

## Findings that should constrain current claims

### 1. Fiscal consistency is ahead of fiscal validation

The committed fiscal report shows a roughly 0.01%-of-GDP general-fund primary surplus at maturity under its interim cash proxy. It also expressly warns that FY2029 surge outlays are unpriced. The result therefore cannot establish that the accelerated CAP-7S path satisfies D4. Its positive result is further conditional on calibrated annual profiles and revenue/savings estimates. Broader social benefits remain worth investigating, but D4 does not permit counting dynamic revenue to close a gap. [Fiscal report][FISCAL]; [surge report][SURGE].

HLT-6 is the clearest concentration risk. Its midpoint savings support both the general fund and HI; these are distinct dollars but dependent on the same reform’s performance. GAO’s current MA audit documents inadequate corrective planning and the demands of accelerated audits. It does not establish Project 2050’s recoverable savings. Separate statutory payment changes, audit recoveries, implementation costs, behavioral responses and the Part A allocation before revising an estimate. [Health, “Financing by channel”][HLT]; [GAO-26-107946][EXT-GAO].

The Social Security baseline is an explicitly incomplete solvency design. The model’s optional SS-5 case still has a negative actuarial balance, and its provision interactions are simplified. HI’s summary model does not reproduce the Trustees baseline exactly and reports a residual deficit/depletion on its own rates. The project should preserve these disclosures and obtain more complete analysis before claiming that D4’s fund conditions have been met. Delaying depletion is valuable but differs from achieving sustainable solvency. [Dedicated funds, Social Security and HI][FUNDS].

### 2. Legislative simulation does not yet describe a financed, implementable enacted package

The floor-path model estimates 172 days under F against a 163-day budget. The vote simulator’s calendar puts both CLI-7 and SS-1 outside the available floor time, despite their governing timing requirements. This is an internal planning conflict, not a prediction that either bill cannot pass. The calendar function accumulates days as it visits bills; its treatment of a bill that does not fit also deserves review before interpreting later exclusions. Its relationship to appropriations, confirmations and the NDAA needs a clear account. [Floor-path script][PATHS]; [vote report][VOTES]; [vote script, `calendar`][VOTECODE].

The simulator drops provisions to obtain a majority, including revenue provisions, but does not then calculate whether the surviving package remains financed or meets each bill’s out-year test. High modeled passage of “some version” is consequently not evidence of passage of the adopted program. Probabilities derive from estimated behavioral parameters and a dated roster; they should guide comparison and challenge, not be presented as election forecasts. The post-November 3 re-baseline remains a named update trigger. [Vote script, `simulate`][VOTECODE]; [tools roadmap][ROADMAP].

### 3. Distributional averages cannot carry the whole fairness argument

The incidence report’s committed Q result gives Q4 about $1,189 per household per year on the ten-year average, with a $1,568 loss in 2030 and gains in 2034 and 2039. Its table therefore supports a qualified statement that the model’s bottom-four-quintile year-five and year-ten tests pass. It does not show that each household gains or that early losses are harmless. Health coverage is valued at government cost, much of which is not additional disposable income. Pattern keys and annual profiles are estimates rather than household-level responses. [Incidence report, “By year” and “Limits”][INCIDENCE].

There is a specific timing conflict: the revenue file says C19b should take effect in 2031 with the public option, while the incidence report attributes part of the 2030 loss to the cap and proposes delaying it. The annual phase-in inputs need reconciliation before using the early-loss amount to recommend a new policy decision. Separately, the fiscal profiles and incidence profiles are maintained through different inputs. An agreement on ten-year totals does not establish agreement on when families pay or receive benefits. [Revenue, “Vehicle and floor time”][REV]; [incidence phase-ins][IPHASE]; [fiscal profiles][FPROFILES].

### 4. The charter’s evaluation and participation commitments need institutional expression

EDU-10 and SAF-10 already offer practices to preserve: advance expectations, independent evaluation, anti-gaming safeguards, and help before penalties. DEM-18 offers a pilot location for examining recruitment, evidence, authority and response. The bibliography’s GOV-05/06 and COM-02/03 provide relevant legal and comparative starting points. They do not establish that these arrangements solve political incentives to suppress unwelcome findings.

Test the charter’s correction question through both pilots: what is recorded before a decision, who evaluates it, who must publish and respond, what can be changed, and what affected people can demand. Distinguish reasonable error from concealment or failure to correct, and distinguish an inconclusive evaluation from credible evidence of harm. Remedies belong in the design alongside grace for policymakers. This remains an institutional research proposal, not a new automatic adjustment power or a sunset on permanent benefits.

## Validation performed and limits

The review read the approved charter and complete bibliography report, governing documents, all twenty converted domain files, and selected consequential sections of the much longer transportation draft: summary/design, financial architecture, vehicle decisions, transit design, delivery/data provisions, political path, fallback tiers and open checks. This was not a line-by-line legislative audit of transportation’s entire drafted text. Supporting source/audit passages and tool code were inspected where the conclusions above depend on them. Original cited research and every current-law claim were not exhaustively reverified.

The repository was left unchanged in tracked files. The shared runner was invoked without `--write` or `--fetch`. It rebuilt transportation, ledger, floor paths, votes, incidence and fiscal outputs, then stopped because the shell could not locate Node. This was an environment failure, not a failing carbon assertion. Carbon regression checks were subsequently executed in the available JavaScript runtime: 52 emitted checks passed, with no failures; documentation describes 53, so the count itself needs a minor documentation reconciliation. Transportation’s Draft 2 reproduction check passed, and all four surge-boundary tests passed. Dedicated-fund and surge modules ran separately and reproduced their committed Markdown byte-for-byte.

The incidence rebuild changed its ledger headline only; its distributional results were unchanged. The fiscal rebuild changed a calibration note; its displayed numerical paths were unchanged. The vote report reproduced byte-for-byte. The full runner, carbon Python reporting driver, dashboard export and final regenerated consistency report were not completed, so this brief does not claim a complete clean rebuild. No generated file was copied back into `index/`.

For fiscal claims, seek joint behavioral/actuarial estimates rather than converting this initial source review into a precision score. ## Decisions and handoff

No policy decision is required to use this assessment as a research map. The recommendations above remain proposals; adopted designs, spending and governing tests remain in force. The findings do justify raising the calendar and financing qualifications now, before a broader readiness claim.

The first practical step is to turn priority 3 into the bounded CMS-IMP-02 dossier/workbook while priority 2 defines the DEM-1 dossier and reconciles the governing calendar. In parallel, commission the two focused outcome reviews under priority 4 and begin priority 1 with the HLT-6/TS12 source bridge. Priority 5 should begin with the small source/calendar reconciliation before a new simulation or large geographic model.

Bring those saved findings back for the charter’s structured governing-decision review when they can support choices. Earlier escalation is appropriate if they establish a consequential contradiction, harm or failure to meet an adopted condition. That review should consider what to retain as well as what to reopen; none of the present uncertainties silently changes D1–D9.

**Handoff:** initial assessment and priorities brief complete; source branch/commit recorded above; one new report saved; no repository policy/input changes or PR; validation limited as stated; open choices and specialist needs retained; next action is the bounded CMS and DEM-1 pilot work with targeted outcome research.
