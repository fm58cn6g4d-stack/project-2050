## Executive decision memo

### Bottom line

**Some HLT-1/HLT-4 relief can credibly reach households or providers during 2029 without changing those titles' already-adopted R1 vehicle.** The strongest cases are enhanced ACA premium assistance, prospective repeal of Medicaid work/renewal restrictions, correction or reenrollment where Congress supplies an explicit remedy, and rapid funding through existing health-center or other established grant channels. HCBS can begin delivering money and rate/workforce support in 2029, but new service capacity and broad slot growth will lag. Coverage-gap closure is the weak point because the adopted outcome does not yet specify the operating mechanism; it cannot inherit the ACA-credit implementation schedule merely because both sit in HLT-1. This is a component-level implementation finding, **not a proposal to accelerate HLT-1/4 into R1—they are already in R1**. fileciteturn18file0L2-L6

The historical evidence is unusually strong for mid-year Marketplace relief. After the American Rescue Plan was signed on March 11, 2021, CMS made enhanced HealthCare.gov premium assistance available April 1; returning consumers who updated their applications could apply the additional APTC to monthly premiums during the same plan year. That establishes that a January plan-year boundary is not inherently necessary for a change implemented through existing Marketplace infrastructure. It does **not** establish a universal 21-day implementation standard, especially for state-based Marketplaces or materially different statutory rules. citeturn4search0turn4search3turn4search6 State-based Marketplace implementation can require separate system work and funding; CMS separately offered ARP implementation grants to SBMs in 2021. citeturn13search5

For Medicaid, current law makes the 2029 task materially more than preventing a future restriction. The P.L. 119-21 community-engagement requirement is scheduled to operate beginning in 2027, with states verifying compliance and exemptions, issuing notices, and potentially denying or terminating eligibility. citeturn11search0turn3view2 By 2029, repeal therefore has two distinct implementation jobs: **stop new adverse actions** and **repair people already adversely affected**. Existing Medicaid processes provide machinery for reconsideration after some procedural terminations, but they do not by themselves establish a universal remedy for work-requirement terminations. citeturn12search1turn12search2 Congress must decide expressly whether prior terminations receive automatic reinstatement, a reconsideration window, retroactive eligibility, reimbursement of claims, or only a prospective repeal.

For Medicare hearing and vision, the critical finding is more consequential: **summer-2029 R2 authority is substantially less clean for a full January 1, 2030 launch than the existing project record suggests because of Medicare Advantage's statutory calendar.** Medicare Advantage plans must provide Medicare Parts A and B benefits. citeturn6search5 The annual MA rate announcement is due by the first Monday in April, and MA organizations submit next-year bids by the first Monday in June. citeturn9search0turn5search3 For calendar year 2030, those dates are **April 2, 2029** and **June 4, 2029**, respectively. A summer R2 therefore arrives after both ordinary pricing milestones.

More importantly, 42 U.S.C. § 1395w-22(a)(5) contains a specific rule for legislative changes to MA required benefits occurring after the annual rate announcement: if the Chief Actuary finds a significant unpriced change, the ordinary rule can defer that change to the following contract year unless the new law requires otherwise. citeturn8search3 That does not make a summer-R2 January-2030 launch legally impossible. Congress could expressly require 2030 application and create a mid-cycle payment/bid adjustment. But the Project 2050 HLT-2 specification contains no such mechanism today, and the plans' June bids would already have been filed. A summer-R2 route therefore cannot presently be called a **full** January-2030 launch with high confidence.

**Recommendation: conditionally prefer Route C for hearing and vision.** Put January-2030 hearing/vision authority in R1 **only if** the entire narrow HLT-2 launch title—benefit specification, score/financing treatment, MA 2030 instruction, implementation funding authority and text-specific Byrd/jurisdiction review—is ready before R1 is frozen and can be included without threatening February/March R1. The value of Route C is not just “a few more months.” It gets statutory authority in front of the 2030 MA rate/bid cycle and gives CMS considerably more usable post-enactment time. If those prerequisites cannot be met without endangering rapid R1, preserve R1 and use Route B only with an explicit statutory solution to the MA mid-cycle problem; absent that solution, retain the adopted 2031 fallback rather than calling a partial 2030 rollout the chosen benefit. This is an inference from the adopted calendar plus current Medicare law. citeturn8search3turn5search3turn9search0 fileciteturn17file0

### Decision rule

The coordinating session should not choose between B and C on a generic “six months versus ten months” implementation estimate. The decision should turn on four gates:

| Gate | Route C can proceed only if… | If the gate fails |
|---|---|---|
| R1 protection | complete text, committee preparation and score/financing can be added without moving the February-preferred/March-latest R1 | Keep H/V out of R1 |
| Medicare Advantage | legislation can place the new Part B benefits into the CY2030 MA payment/benefit cycle before ordinary rate/bid deadlines | R2 would need an express mid-cycle override/payment mechanism or fall back to 2031 |
| Benefit completeness | services, devices, cost sharing, payment, suppliers and frequency rules are specified for the **full chosen benefit** | Do not substitute exams-only coverage or a smaller allowance |
| Funding/operations | FY2029 implementation authority and a single-cost-owner map for CMS-IMP work exist | Treat Jan. 1, 2030 as unverified, not “zero-cost preparation” |

This preserves the adopted 2031 hearing/vision date as a responsible fallback and avoids turning an operational difficulty into an unapproved benefit reduction. fileciteturn18file0L2-L6

## Full hearing and vision launch specification

### What Congress must actually specify

The current HLT-2 line establishes the political benefit—dental, vision and hearing in traditional Medicare—but does not yet contain enough operational specification to certify January 1, 2030. fileciteturn18file0L2-L6 Historical unenacted bills are useful drafting precedents only. The 2021 Build Back Better materials proposed relatively near-term Medicare hearing/vision dates, which rebuts the idea that a 24-month ledger field is a statutory minimum, but those dates were proposed rather than observed launches. The earlier House report itself is a 2021 proposal, not implementation evidence. citeturn25view1

Before either R1 or R2 text is final, the following choices need to be explicit:

| Launch element | Specification required for the chosen full benefit | Why omission is consequential |
|---|---|---|
| **Hearing services** | evaluation/covered testing beyond already-covered diagnostics; hearing-aid selection; fitting/programming; follow-up; audiologic rehabilitation; repair/replacement rules | Today Medicare covers some diagnostic hearing care but not the ordinary hearing-aid/fitting benefit. citeturn22search1turn22search2 |
| **Hearing devices** | prescription versus OTC devices; unilateral/bilateral coverage; device payment or allowance; replacement interval; lost/damaged devices; batteries/accessories if covered | These determine beneficiary value, supplier population, cost and claims edits. A small allowance is not equivalent to an unspecified full-device benefit. |
| **Vision services** | routine comprehensive exam/refraction frequency and covered professional services | Routine refraction is currently excluded, so this is genuinely new Part B scope. citeturn22search0 |

A normal CMS rulemaking path is also a real schedule dependency. Medicare statute generally requires notice-and-comment for regulations establishing or changing substantive legal standards governing benefit scope, payment, or provider eligibility, ordinarily with at least a 60-day public-comment period, subject to statutory exceptions. citeturn24search1 A summer-2029 law can therefore leave very little margin for full notice-and-comment, clearance, claims implementation, provider guidance and production testing before January 1. Congress can reduce that risk by putting consequential benefit/payment rules directly in statute and, where appropriate, expressly authorizing interim implementation or program instructions. That authority itself must be drafted; a proposed historical start date does not supply it.

### Prioritized unresolved decisions and specialist-review questions

**The first decision is the hearing/vision statutory specification.** Espen needs a single full-benefit rule sheet settling devices/services, frequency/replacement, cost sharing, payment method and eligible provider/supplier categories. Without that, neither CMS nor CBO can price or build the benefit. Exams-only or a smaller device allowance is a separate policy option, not implementation.

**Second is the MA 2030 mechanism.** Legislative/budget specialists should test whether an R1 enactment early enough for the April/June cycle is operationally sufficient and draft the necessary MA effective/payment language. For Route B, the review must answer the harder question: exactly how Congress would require January-2030 MA coverage after the rate announcement and bid filing, how CMS would adjust CY2030 payments/bids, and whether that can be executed without destabilizing plan contracts. No contact with outside reviewers was initiated in this session.

**Third is the R1 go/no-go test.** Budget counsel/scorekeeping review must determine instruction headroom, committee jurisdiction, bill-specific financing and text-specific Byrd treatment. The question is not whether HLT-2 is generally budgetary; it is whether the complete early-authority title can be ready without compromising the February-preferred/March-latest R1.

**Fourth is the marginal score.** The requested score is not “10 percent of dental/vision/hearing.” It is: full hearing plus full vision in CY2030; resulting SMI benefit spending, premiums and state buy-in; FY2029 setup; any MA payment adjustment; state/Medigap interactions; and any administrative cost that is incremental rather than merely shifted one year earlier.

**Fifth is HLT-1 statutory transition language.** The coordinating session needs a provision-by-provision table of the H.R. 1 health provisions being repealed and, for each, the desired effective date and treatment of already-terminated cases. “Repeal” and “make people whole” are different instructions.

**Sixth is the coverage-gap architecture.** Until the adopted closure mechanism is specified, no responsible 2029 launch, systems schedule or cost can be assigned.

**Eighth is CMS-IMP pricing, not a restart of CMS-IMP-02.** The next work is to populate the existing register with quantities, incremental shares, FY2029 BA/obligations/outlays and single ledger owners for CMS-IMP-01/02/03/04/08/11/13, using the completed pilot/source-chain evidence rather than rerunning it. ### What households might actually receive

A Marketplace household is the clearest candidate to feel R1 during **2029**: if Congress makes the restored enhanced credits applicable to 2029 coverage months and the relevant Marketplace updates its system, the household can see a lower monthly premium before the next plan year. The 2021 federal Marketplace precedent establishes that this can be done in-year. citeturn4search0turn4search6

A Medicaid adult could stop facing the restored work or renewal restriction during **2029**, but someone whose coverage was already terminated will not necessarily reappear on the rolls merely because Congress repeals the rule. The bill needs an effective reinstatement/correction route, and the state needs to process it. citeturn11search0turn12search1

A community-health-center patient could see more clinic staffing, appointment capacity or other services in **2029** if R1 funds existing grantees through a rapid award mechanism. A new building or a new physician produced by an expanded residency pipeline is not a realistic equivalent of that near-term relief. citeturn14search0turn14search2

An HCBS recipient or direct-care worker could benefit in **2029** from higher state payments, retention support or another existing-channel intervention, but broad growth in service slots depends on states and an actual workforce. citeturn13search0turn13search1

A Medicare beneficiary gets the full new hearing and vision benefit on **January 1, 2030 only if Congress and CMS solve the entire FFS-plus-MA launch**, not merely if a law is signed in 2029. Route C gives that objective the stronger implementation path. If the required scope, financing, MA treatment and claims/provider work cannot be completed without compromising rapid R1, the honest fallback remains the adopted **2031** launch—not a quietly reduced 2030 benefit.
