## 1. Calendar C: what the current numbers actually establish

The legacy F case is **172 = 117 regular-order bill days + 51 reconciliation days + 4 rules days**. There are 21 regular-order bills. Each reconciliation allowance is 17, comprising 15 bill days and two budget-resolution days. These are planning assumptions, not statutory durations. MOV-31 is already excluded; moving it to the second Congress creates no new first-Congress saving. [R2–R5]

The builder describes 163 as 148 discretionary days plus 15 freed through nomination measures. This assignment did not recover a defensible itemized derivation of the 148-day allowance. The current model therefore establishes a nine-unit shortfall against an assumed agenda budget, rather than proving a nine-day shortage of physically available Senate sittings. Existing mergers, fast tracks and nomination savings are already embedded. They cannot be credited again. [R4–R5]

The model's priority allocation excludes CLI-6 and CLI-7, whose adjusted costs total nine. That is an allocation result, **not an approved decision to abandon either bill**. A path table showing all 21 eligible bills and a capacity simulation fitting only 19 answer different questions. Neither should be described as a validated full-agenda calendar. [R4–R5]

Official Senate activity reports show 192 session days in 2021 and 178 in 2022, with 1,083 hours 11 minutes and 958 hours 32 minutes respectively. Those counts include sittings of differing lengths and all other business. They demonstrate why raw sitting days and agenda-cost units must be kept separate; they do not establish 370 usable Project 2050 days. [P1–P2]

Congressional terms begin January 3; presidential and vice-presidential terms begin January 20. Pre-inauguration congressional preparation is therefore real capacity, but the incoming administration's legal authority is not available early. [P3]

Parallelism means concurrent House work, committee work and preparation, plus floor opportunities for ready measures. It does not allow the Senate to debate two contested bills at once. Existing notice, referral, hearing and committee-meeting requirements still matter. Pre-drafting reduces drafting delay; it does not eliminate legislative steps. [P4]

R1's two modeled budget-resolution days should not be mistaken for a legal ceiling. The Budget Act allows up to 50 Senate debate hours on a budget resolution; reconciliation debate is limited to 20 hours, with separate rules for later stages. Voting, amendments and bicameral resolution still take time. The model's 17-unit package requires procedural review before it can support a promised February completion. [P5–P6]

### Capacity request and contingencies

Define `B` as additional overhead and reserve **not already allowed for** in the 163-unit budget; `Δ` as additional available agenda capacity; `s` as verified new merger savings; `e` as earned savings from complete earlier enactment; and `d` as additional disruption cost. The requirement is:

`163 + Δ ≥ 172 − s − e + B + d`.

Baseline: **Δ must cover at least 9 + B**, before disruption. B is unknown, not zero. This avoids double-counting routine business that may already have been deducted from the original allowance.

| Case | Required treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Separate climate bills; full disaster fallback | Reserve all 172 units and identify at least 9 + B additional units. No bill disappears to make the total look feasible. |
| Supported full-scope climate merger | Replace the two reservations only after estimating combined cost, lost fast-track opportunities and bicameral work. Keep CLI-7/8 fully reserved. |
| Full CLI-7/8 scope enacted earlier | Remove only genuinely redundant work after a provision-level enacted-law comparison. Maximum current modeled release is 4.5; partial coverage earns no automatic 4.5. |
| R1 misses February | Protect March R1 completion and DEM start through an explicit daily/hourly recovery plan. Name any displaced March work. |
| R1 slips to May–June | This damages early affordability/restoration delivery, delays agency preparation and collides with summer R2. The intended early goodwill cannot be assumed. It is a failure-recovery case, not a co-equal preferred calendar. |
| FY2029 reconciliation opportunity unavailable | Obtain a distinct legal/architecture plan. Moving R1 scope into R2 crowds other scope and requires new eligibility and timing decisions; it is not a free 17-unit saving. |
| Climate merger fails | Revert to the funded separate-bill reservations, rather than drop grid or CES provisions late. |

R2 before October 2029 is legally possible in principle. Text-specific instructions, interactions between successive resolutions, allocations and Byrd issues remain specialist work. Social Security's adopted regular-order route should not be moved into reconciliation. [P6–P8]

Leaders should inventory actual legislative hours and member attendance, then consider additional Fridays, selected weekends or shorter state-work periods. Historical schedules show these are choices, not fixed constitutional ceilings. No numeric gain is credited here. More sitting time also needs committee, drafting, scoring, security and staff capacity; it is not costless. [P1–P2, P9]

## 2. CLI-3 plus CLI-6: a real policy comparison

There is a substantive reason to develop combined text: the CES creates demand for clean electricity, while transmission, interconnection-related protections and utility governance affect whether that electricity can reach people affordably. The statutory fixes protect the regulatory and transport components from conflicts with the stipulated permitting-law baseline. This complementarity supports drafting; it does not establish a winning coalition or a shorter floor process. [R3, R6]

### Scope that a full merger must preserve

| Adopted component | Combined-bill treatment | Review question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CLI-3A: 80% clean electricity by 2035; tradable retail-supplier credits; capped alternative compliance payment | Retain standard, schedule, credit rules and cap explicitly | Administration, consumer impacts, compliance resources and interaction with fee/credits; payment receipts remain unsized |
| CLI-3 AAJA §1401 override | Preserve express protection for CES and Clean Air Act obligations | Text-specific interaction with existing permits and remedies |

| Rail fixes | Preserve FRA program/ROW, predominant-control and intercity-project corrections | Coordinate transportation language now; do not wait for MOV-31 in 2031 |
| Carried CLI-4A | Keep statutory endangerment finding and express vehicle/power-sector authority | Air-law drafting and durability; this is already inside CLI-3's six-unit allowance |
| CLI-6A transfer minimums | Keep NERC-study-based minimums and FERC enforcement | Engineering adequacy, regional implementation and enforceability |

This matrix follows chosen CLI-3A/CLI-4A/CLI-6A. It excludes unchosen pipeline restrictions, accelerated CES dates, the clean-load option, congressionally deemed-approved corridors and new data-center disclosure titles. Those may deserve later consideration; inserting them during consolidation would be a separate policy decision. The canonical CLI-6 cost near zero is an estimate, not proof that small-co-op funding or new administration is free. [R3]

The ENR committee advanced the Manchin–Barrasso permitting bill 15–4 in July 2024. That is evidence that a negotiated energy package can obtain bipartisan committee support. It does not show 60 Senate votes, support for this CES or utility-accountability package, or a viable 2029 coalition. The expectation that CES/CAA restoration may attract a different coalition from narrower grid provisions is an inference requiring a title-by-title whip review. No new passage probability is assigned. [P10]

### Jurisdiction and amendments

Use coordinated Senate ENR and EPW drafting, with Commerce participation on rail provisions and other consultations determined by actual text. House Energy and Commerce is a central energy/grid counterpart; transportation and water provisions warrant Transportation and Infrastructure participation. These are anticipated work assignments, not a ruling on formal referral. The official House energy-subcommittee jurisdiction expressly includes grid policy, utility regulation, cybersecurity and FERC. EPW's jurisdiction covers air and water matters. [P11–P12]

Senate Rule XVII generally uses predominant subject matter for referral and provides a route for joint or sequential referral. The exact text and parliamentary advice govern. Committee hearing notice and meeting restrictions can affect simultaneous floor/committee work. A larger bill does not automatically make multi-committee work disappear. [P4]

Keep D2-F's ten amendments per side and equal time. A merger cannot quietly remove the adopted minority guarantees. Agree balanced title coverage and potential substitutes before floor entry; determine whether scope disputes, amendments or House differences erase the expected savings. Do not assume the number of amendments, amendment votes, cloture rounds or conference returns is unchanged merely because two bill numbers become one.

### What the merger can and cannot save

The model charges CLI-3 six units and CLI-6 4.5 under F, totaling **10.5**. If a combined bill costs `c`, new savings are `10.5 − c`; total agenda demand becomes **161.5 + c + B**, before disruption or earned disaster savings.

| Illustrative combined cost c | Saving against separate 10.5 | Total before B | Additional capacity above 163, before B |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 4.5 | 6 | 166 | 3 |
| 6 | 4.5 | 167.5 | 4.5 |
| 8 | 2.5 | 169.5 | 6.5 |
| 10.5 | 0 | 172 | 9 |
| 12 | −1.5 | 173.5 | 10.5 |

**These are sensitivities, not duration estimates.** The existing F model applies a 4.5-unit minimum to an ordinary contested bill. Consequently, the merger alone cannot close the nine-unit gap under that model: even at its minimum, at least three additional units plus B remain. Fitting inside 163 using only this merger would require c ≤ 1.5, inconsistent with that ordinary contested-bill treatment. [R5]

For illustration only, c = 6 with B = 0/10/20 requires additional capacity of 4.5/14.5/24.5. These reserve values are not recommendations or estimates. Even full earlier CLI-7/8 enactment plus c = 6 merely reaches 163 before any additional reserve. It does not demonstrate a resilient calendar.

No confirmed manager estimate, negotiated procedural agreement, title-level whip count or House companion was obtained. A full merger should therefore be described as a potentially useful efficiency, not the balancing item that makes C work.

### Two adjacent merger screens

INT-1 and CAP-8 already use assumed expedited routes of one and two units. If combining them loses that treatment and produces an ordinary contested F bill at 4.5, the combined route costs at least 1.5 more. Preserve their separate quick routes unless there is evidence that a combined route retains the advantage. [R4–R5]

EXE-1 and CAP-8 have institutional connections, but a broad executive-power package could jeopardize the assumed CAP fast track. Their separate allowance is eight; any saving requires a combined cost below eight after added process. Coordinated preparation is justified now; wholesale consolidation is not established. These are bounded screens, not recommendations to redesign additional domains.
