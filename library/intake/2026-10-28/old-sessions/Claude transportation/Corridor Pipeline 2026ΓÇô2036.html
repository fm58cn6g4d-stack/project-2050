Corridor Pipeline 2026–2036

Corridor ID Program · modeled FY2027–2046 · v8, two authorizations

Seventy-one corridors, twenty years

Every corridor on the FRA's March 2026 obligation report, tracked year by year through four policy eras: the BUILD America 250-Plus cycle, a 2029–30 climate and fair-economy reconciliation package, the ARCA authorization, and the FY2036 reauthorization that carries the work to 2046. Four corridors at the bottom are not in the FRA pipeline at all — they are the second generation, entering under the 2036 bill and opening in the 2050s. Each row carries FRA's own scope: route mileage, current and proposed frequencies, and target speed. Three tracks run at once and this grid separates them: the cell color is the Corridor ID planning stage, the amber bar underneath is capital in the ground, and the red edge is new or improved service actually running. They are not the same clock — three corridors are carrying new trains today while their planning sits at Step 1.

What the model says

Corridor names, sponsors and current stages are from FRA's Corridor ID Program obligation status report of March 31, 2026. Scope descriptions are from FRA's FY2022 selection summaries; route mileage, current and proposed frequencies and target speeds, where shown, are from FRA's Corridor ID StoryMap of November 2025, which at the time of writing could be read only in part. Planning stages from FY2027 are modeled from each corridor's March 2026 position, its sponsor's institutional capacity, and the three political phases in the ARCA baseline. Construction years are modeled separately, from whether the corridor has a discrete funded project — a bridge, a siding, a grade separation, a station — that a partnership or CRISI grant can build without waiting on the corridor's own service development plan. The Service column is the first new or improved trains on the corridor; the Full scope column is the corridor as FRA's selection summary describes it. Near-term service dates are taken from sponsors' announced targets and discounted where funding is incomplete.
