## WHAT THIS DOCUMENT IS

Project 2029 is a comprehensive platform and governing blueprint for a Democratic federal trifecta beginning January 20, 2029. It covers:

- A **2026 midterm campaign platform** capable of winning a House majority and strengthening the Senate
- A **2028 presidential campaign platform** capable of winning the Electoral College and a governing mandate
- A **governance blueprint** — specific legislation, executive actions, timelines, vote counts, and fiscal analysis — for the first Democratic term

Unlike previous Democratic planning efforts, this framework insists that campaign and governance be designed together from the start. The lesson of Project 2025 is that power comes to those who plan before they have it. The lesson of the Obama and Biden years is that governing without a pre-built, coordinated execution plan produces wins that are politically inarticulable and easily reversed.

This summary synthesizes 14 full policy chapters, a Master Executive Action Inventory (78 Day 1-30 actions), a Master Governance Calendar (2028-2033), and three internal audit cycles into a single readable document. The full chapters are available for deeper development of any domain.

## PART ONE: THE POLITICAL ANALYSIS

### The Terrain in March 2026

The 2024 election was a multi-factor Democratic collapse. Its core drivers: economic grievance that Republicans channeled into pseudo-populism while Democrats offered process language; a campaign that asked voters to "save democracy" rather than telling them specifically what government would do for their lives; and a coalition that didn't turn out in sufficient numbers because it didn't have enough reason to.

The terrain has shifted since November 2024 in Democrats' favor — but in ways that create both opportunity and trap. Trump's approval is below 40%. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA), signed July 4, 2025, has become the most visible legislative failure in a generation: it cut $1.02 trillion from Medicaid (11.8 million losing coverage), ~$175B from SNAP, and trillions more from student loans and other programs — primarily to extend and expand tax cuts for the wealthy, adding $3.4 trillion to the deficit. Rural hospitals are closing. Medicaid recipients are losing coverage. The OBBBA is what happens when plutocracy gets what it wants in legislative form.

The trap: Democrats could coast on anti-Trump anger in 2026 without building the substantive mandate needed to govern boldly in 2029. Anger wins midterms. It does not win presidencies or governing mandates. This framework is designed to build both.

### The Winning Coalition Architecture

A Democratic majority requires holding the base (Black voters, young voters, college-educated suburbanites, organized labor) while making genuine inroads with persuadable voters (non-college white voters, rural communities, small business owners, Latino men). These are not in conflict if the policy is right. They are in conflict only when the messaging is defensive, abstract, or culturally coded.

The empirical insight that resolves the base-vs-persuasion debate: new data from the 2024 Cooperative Election Study found that Democratic nonvoters were actually *less* progressive than Democrats who voted. A more left-wing appeal would not have brought them back. What was missing was *economic populism with material specificity* — naming the villain, naming the fix, naming the timeline.

### The Anti-Oligarchy Frame — What It Means and Doesn't Mean

This framework adopts "rigged rules" over "class war"; specific villains over abstract categories; "fair share" over "punish the rich." This is not ideological moderation — it is precision. 

**What it means:** Anti-monopoly, anti-capture of government by concentrated private wealth, pro-competitive markets, pro-small business. Small businesses and family farmers are natural allies, not bystanders.

**What it doesn't mean:** Anti-success, anti-aspiration, or anti-capitalism. The frame is "good capitalism vs. rigged capitalism." Democrats defend competitive markets and the opportunity to build something — against the rigged-rules capitalism that crushes competition and concentrates wealth.

**Why it works across the coalition:** 68% of voters (including Republican majorities) support a wealth tax on fortunes over $50M. 78% believe corporations have too much political power. 80%+ support a public health insurance option. These are supermajority positions when asked without partisan framing. The anti-oligarchy agenda is not a left-wing fringe position. It is the majority position on issue after issue.

## PART THREE: THE ELECTORAL PATH TO 2029

### The Senate Math

A Democratic trifecta requires winning the presidency AND both chambers of Congress. The binding constraint is the Senate.

**2026:** Democrats defend 13 seats; Republicans defend 22. But most Republican seats are in deep-red states. Realistic targets: hold Georgia (Ossoff) and Michigan (open), flip North Carolina (Roy Cooper recruiting), and potentially Maine, Ohio, or Nebraska. Realistic outcome: 49-51 Democratic seats entering 2028.

**2028:** With presidential coattails, the primary targets are Wisconsin (open if Johnson retires) and North Carolina (Ted Budd). A Democratic presidential win plus two Senate flips = 51-52 seat majority entering January 2029.

**The narrow majority reality:** This framework is designed for a 51-52 seat majority — not a 58-seat majority that isn't coming. Every policy proposal is tested against what 50-51 senators can actually pass.

### The Filibuster as the Hinge

A 51-vote majority can pass significant legislation through budget reconciliation (one bill per fiscal year). But voting rights, the PRO Act, most anti-corruption legislation, and Supreme Court reform all require 60 votes or a filibuster carve-out.

**The most important strategic recommendation in this entire framework:** Make the filibuster carve-out for voting rights a condition of DSCC support for every Senate candidate in 2026 and 2028. Elect senators who are pre-committed. The time to solve the Manchin problem is before it becomes the Manchin problem.

## PART FOUR: THE GOVERNING PLAN

### The Four Reconciliation Bills — The Legislative Backbone

Without filibuster reform, reconciliation is the primary legislative tool. Democrats can potentially pass four reconciliation bills over a four-year term — one per fiscal year. These four bills must carry the core agenda:

| Bill | When | Core Content |
|------|------|-------------|
| **The Restoration Bill** (FY2029) | Day 60 | Repeal OBBBA Medicaid cuts; Medicare PAYGO override; reverse TCJA for top earners; restore SNAP; Child Tax Credit |
| **The Building Bill** (FY2030) | Fall 2029 | AmeriCare public option; Medicare dental/vision/hearing; IRA clean energy restoration; Civilian Climate Corps; grid investment |
| **The Fairness Bill** (FY2031) | Fall 2031 | Carbon price and dividend; wealth tax; capital gains reform; FTT; long-term care insurance; housing investment; student debt relief |
| **The Reserve Bill** (FY2032) | 2032 | Social Security solvency; anything Years 1-3 couldn't finish |

**The Restoration Bill is non-negotiable and goes first.** Every day it is delayed is a day someone loses Medicaid coverage. Political capital is highest at inauguration and diminishes steadily. Nothing else happens before the first vote on the Restoration Bill.

### What Requires Filibuster Reform

- Voting Rights (John Lewis Voting Rights Advancement Act)
- The PRO Act (labor law reform)
- Dark money disclosure (DISCLOSE Act)
- Most anti-corruption legislation
- Supreme Court reform

**The fallback if the carve-out fails:** Reconciliation maximalism, state-level embedding of reforms, and making the blocking senators' names famous for the 2030 and 2032 cycles.

## PART FIVE: THE POLICY AGENDA — CHAPTER BY CHAPTER

### Healthcare (Chapter 4) — Restore, Expand, Transform

**The damage:** OBBBA cut $1.02 trillion from Medicaid, triggering coverage loss for 11.8 million Americans; ACA enhanced subsidies expired (5 million more uninsured); Medicare faces $500B in automatic cuts triggered by OBBBA's deficit; rural hospitals are closing.

**Phase 1 — Restore (Year 1, via Restoration Bill):** Repeal OBBBA Medicaid work requirements; restore ACA enhanced subsidies permanently; prevent Medicare automatic cuts; rural hospital stabilization fund; restore drug price negotiation.

**Phase 2 — Expand (Year 2, via Building Bill):** Launch AmeriCare — a Medicare public option available to every American, with income-scaled premiums; add dental, vision, and hearing to Medicare; establish long-term care insurance program; enforce mental health parity.

**Phase 3 — Transform (Year 3+):** Administrative simplification — standardized billing, all-payer reference pricing, global budgeting pilots — attacking the 25-34% administrative overhead that makes American healthcare twice as expensive per care dollar as peer nations. Auto-enrollment of the remaining uninsured into AmeriCare. The goal is universal coverage by 2039.

**What's different from restoration:** The administrative simplification agenda is not a rebuild of the broken pre-OBBBA system. It attacks the structural cost driver that made healthcare unaffordable before OBBBA. Mental health infrastructure is built as physical capacity, not just insurance mandates.

### Economic Fairness (Chapter 7) — Antitrust, Worker Power, Housing, Trade

**The structural insight:** Corporate monopoly power is the root cause of most kitchen-table economic grievances. Four companies control two-thirds of the grocery market. RealPage's algorithm added $70/month to the average rent in large buildings. Five companies control 46% of egg-laying hens. Wages stagnate because when one employer dominates a region, workers have nowhere else to go. The economic fairness agenda attacks the structure, not just the symptoms.

**Anti-monopoly:** Robinson-Patman Act revival protecting small grocers; Platform Monopoly Act preventing self-preferencing; algorithmic pricing prohibition (housing, groceries, hotels); RealPage full divestiture; FTC and DOJ Antitrust Division fully funded and fully mandated.

**Worker power:** Federal minimum wage to $17 then $19 (30M+ workers); PRO Act (Year 3 with filibuster carve-out); sectoral bargaining boards in five industries; wage theft criminalized as a felony; non-compete federal ban; gig worker ABC test.

**Housing:** $200B affordable housing trust fund; federal land for housing; zoning preemption tied to federal grants (and stronger: multi-family by-right within 1/2 mile of transit is void exclusionary zoning for federally-funded projects); algorithmic rent price-fixing ban; national tenant protection floor. **And the permitting reform that makes all of it buildable:** NEPA categorical exclusions for clean energy, FERC transmission authority, 18-month infrastructure shot clocks.

**Trade:** Allied trade architecture replacing the tariff disaster; $1,500/household tariff cost reversed; $166B in unconstitutional IEEPA tariffs refunded; Democratic Trade Alliance with labor and environmental standards; targeted China competition strategy.

### Democracy & Anti-Corruption (Chapter 6) — Structural Prerequisites

**Why this must come early:** Without restored civil service merit protections, every rebuilt agency is one spoils-system reversal away from collapse. Without Inspector Generals, every dollar of investment can be looted without detection. Without voting rights, the coalition that wins in 2028 can be suppressed in 2030.

**Restore and reform civil service:** Repeal Schedule Policy/Career — but also genuinely reform hiring (30-day target), pay (market-rate technical bands), and performance management. The goal is a merit-based civil service that is both protected from political purges AND actually competitive for the talent government needs.

**Government 2.0:** The Democratic positive vision of government that works — IRS Direct File (universal, free, permanent); automatic benefits enrollment ($80B+ in unclaimed benefits reached to eligible Americans); unified digital identity (Login.gov); VA modernization (90-day claims standard). This is the Democratic answer to DOGE: not "government as usual" but "government that works."

**Inspector Generals:** All fired IGs reinstated; cause requirement codified in statute; DOGE forensic audit launched; criminal referrals as warranted.

**Democracy reforms:** John Lewis Voting Rights Act (via filibuster carve-out); automatic voter registration; independent redistricting nationwide; DC and Puerto Rico statehood; ranked choice voting in federal primaries; DISCLOSE Act; Small Dollar Democracy matching.

**Drug policy:** Federal marijuana descheduling and expungement; federal harm reduction fund; end the ban on syringe exchange funding. 70%+ public support; direct racial justice measure; long overdue.

### Climate & Energy (Chapter 8) — Restore, Build, Price

**The damage:** OBBBA eliminated EV credits (September 2025), residential solar credits (end of 2025), and will phase out commercial solar and wind. 80% of this investment went to Republican districts. Natural gas prices rose 56% in 2025. The supply chain we were building to compete with China is being ceded.

**Pillar 1 — Restore:** Full IRA clean energy credit restoration via Building Bill reconciliation; extend through 2035; restore domestic manufacturing credits for batteries and solar.

**Pillar 2 — Build:** Civilian Climate Corps (300,000 jobs → 400,000 at peak; $280B over 10 years); grid investment ($150B); federal transmission permitting authority (FERC siting for interstate lines); long-duration storage; offshore wind.

**Pillar 3 — Price:** Carbon fee and dividend via Fairness Bill — $40/ton starting price, rising $15/year; 100% of revenues returned as monthly dividends (~$700-900/year per adult); border carbon adjustment that protects American manufacturers from competitors who don't price carbon. Two-thirds of Americans receive more in dividends than they pay in higher prices. Nearly three-quarters of voters support a carbon border adjustment once they hear a brief explanation.

**The emissions math:** With all three pillars, U.S. greenhouse gas emissions are on a trajectory to 40%+ below 2005 levels by 2033 — the fastest sustained decarbonization in American history.

### Immigration (Chapter 3) — Secure, Fair, Humane, Structural

**The political foundation:** By a 59-29% margin (Fox News, 2025), Americans prefer a path to citizenship over mass deportation. 78% support DREAMers' citizenship. 65% of Americans support pathways for established workers. The Trump administration spent $170B on enforcement that made the system worse. The Democratic position wins when stated with conviction.

**The policy:** Comprehensive reform — E-Verify mandatory for large employers (in exchange for real legal pathways); 200-day asylum adjudication with adequate court capacity; $75B rural hospital stabilization; a 10-13 year earned legalization pathway for 10-12 million established residents; DACA codified; TPS restored; family separation permanently banned; Article I immigration court (independent from DOJ) as the structural fix.

**The structural reform that's new:** Creating a genuinely independent immigration court, removing judges from DOJ control, and introducing inquisitorial elements for asylum cases — because adding judges to a broken adversarial model produces faster bad outcomes, not better ones.

**The Reagan anchor:** Ronald Reagan signed earned legalization into law in 1986 with bipartisan support. Democrats should use this constantly.

### Education (Chapter 12) — Rebuild and Reform

**The damage:** ED workforce cut nearly in half; student loans transferred to Treasury (legally questionable, operationally worse); OBBBA eliminated Grad PLUS loans, restricted IDR to 30-year plans, weaponized PSLF against political opponents; 10.3 million borrowers in forbearance.

**Student debt:** $50,000 cancellation for Pell Grant recipients; $25,000 for other borrowers below $125,000 income; restore 5% IDR; restore PSLF for all public service workers; restore the tax exclusion for forgiven debt.

**Free community college:** Universal tuition-free community college; $10,000 Pell Grant maximum; workforce Pell for short-term credentials; 1 million apprenticeships per year.

**K-12 equity:** Title I increased to $30B/year; federal teacher pay floor ($60K → $75K with matching grants); rural teacher recruitment with loan forgiveness and housing; full IDEA funding (honoring the 50-year-old 40% commitment); school infrastructure bonds prioritizing high-poverty districts.

**Universal pre-K:** Federal-state partnership for all 3- and 4-year-olds; $300-400B over 10 years; child care stabilization fund ($150-200B) to close the gap between what families can afford and what quality care costs.

**What's different from restoration:** Community schools model as structural reform (co-locating services, healthcare, mental health with academic instruction); extended learning time for highest-need students; student mental health infrastructure as separate from parity enforcement.

### Foreign Policy & Defense (Chapter 9) — Rebuild and Reform Simultaneously

**The NATO commitment:** Unconditional, on Day 1, by Presidential Statement. Then codified in statute (requiring 2/3 Senate vote for withdrawal). Then sustained by showing up — Secretaries of State and Defense in European capitals in Week 1.

**Ukraine:** Restore full military assistance immediately; reject any settlement that formalizes territorial conquest; pursue European-led security guarantee backed by U.S. support; support Ukrainian sovereignty as the test case for whether borders mean anything in the 21st century.

**USAID:** 500K-1M projected deaths from program interruption by 2025 alone. Restore to $65B/year by Year 3; reconstitute the institution with genuine reforms — 40% of programming through local organizations within 5 years; independent evaluation authority; Millennium Challenge Corporation model expanded. Rebuilding better, not restoring unchanged.

**China:** Compete on technology and clean energy (the industries of the future); cooperate on climate and pandemic preparedness (shared survival interests); confront on security threats and human rights (clear red lines); coordinate with allies (the force multiplier Trump threw away). Neither full decoupling nor naive engagement.

**Defense reform:** Pentagon audit — 7 consecutive failures; force structure reform toward cyber, space, precision strike, and autonomous systems away from Cold War legacy platforms; acquisition reform; pay and support for service members over weapons procurement for contractors.

### Reproductive Rights (Chapter 11) — Multi-Track Strategy

**The political record:** 12 for 12 on abortion-related ballot measures since Dobbs, including in red states. Kansas rejected an abortion ban by 18 points. Ohio protected reproductive rights by 14 points. The majority position is clear. The political challenge is translating it into legislative action against a hostile Supreme Court.

**The Comstock Act is the most urgent threat:** A 19th-century obscenity law could be used by a future hostile DOJ to ban medication abortion nationwide without new legislation. Comstock repeal is the highest legislative priority in this chapter — protecting access even in states where abortion is legal.

**IVF first:** The IVF Protection Act passes with 60+ Senate votes — 80%+ public support, bipartisan base, exposes the fetal personhood contradiction. It goes first to demonstrate bipartisan viability and frame the broader fight.

**WHPA 2.0:** A statutory right to abortion through viability — deliberately scoped to survive judicial challenge rather than attempting a broader bill that would be immediately enjoined and establish worse precedent.

**The long game:** State constitutional amendments (2026: Nevada and Virginia; 2028: Florida, Georgia, North Carolina); federal constitutional amendment (introduced in Year 1; ratification is a decade-long project that never finishes if it never begins).

### Technology & AI (Chapter 13) — Democratic Governance of the Transformation

**The central insight:** Trump didn't deregulate AI — he captured AI governance for the industry being governed. He stripped states of authority (December 2025 EO preempting state AI laws), revoked Biden's safety testing requirements (Day 1), and made billion-dollar discretionary investments in specific companies with no congressional accountability. This is not less government. It is unaccountable government.

**Restore safety:** Biden AI Safety EO restored; AI Safety Institute reconstituted at NIST; mandatory safety testing for frontier models; independent governance structure insulated from political direction.

**Restore state authority:** Repeal the December 2025 preemption EO; restore BEAD broadband funding to all states; establish federal floors, not ceilings — states may always protect their citizens more strongly.

**Protect workers:** AI Worker Transition Fund ($200B, funded in part by 0.5% levy on AI revenues above $1B); 180-day advance notice for AI-driven mass layoffs; data labor compensation fund for artists, writers, and journalists whose work trained the AI systems.

**Protect people:** Federal data privacy law (right to know, correct, delete, opt out); children's online safety (strongest bipartisan legislative target); targeted Section 230 reform for algorithmic amplification of adjudicated unlawful content; algorithmic transparency requirements.

**Win the China competition:** Allied technology export coordination; National AI Research Cloud ($10B for academic and startup access); expanded NSF AI funding; "trusted AI" certification standard that makes U.S. and allied AI products the global market preference.

### Criminal Justice (Chapter 14) — Public Safety Through Smarter Justice

**The reframe:** The Democratic position is not "less law enforcement." It is better law enforcement, smarter incarceration, genuine rehabilitation, and accountability systems that make the whole enterprise more effective and more legitimate. The current system fails by its own metrics: a 43% federal recidivism rate at $43,000 per prisoner per year is not tough — it is expensive failure.

**The damage from Trump's second term:** Acting DAG Bove's Day 1 memo directing maximum charging in every case — the same policy that produced the 1990s mass incarceration crisis. AG Bondi ended the moratorium on the federal death penalty and has sought to expand capital punishment to drug trafficking cases. Consent decrees were gutted — the primary federal mechanism for police accountability dismantled.

**Sentencing reform:** The First Step Act expansion (passed 87-12 in the Senate) — reducing mandatory minimums, expanding safety valve relief for low-level nonviolent offenders, and eliminating the crack/powder cocaine disparity (18:1 is not justice; it is documented racial disparity with no public safety basis). Individualized sentencing as the standard: prosecutors make charging and sentencing decisions based on the actual case, not the maximum available charge.

**Police accountability:** The George Floyd Justice in Policing Act (Updated) — banning chokeholds, no-knock warrants in drug cases, and qualified immunity in federal civil rights cases; requiring body cameras; creating a national misconduct database so officers fired for abuse cannot simply transfer to a new department. Restore consent decrees as the tool for addressing systemic constitutional violations. This is not anti-police — it is pro-accountability, which makes policing more effective and more legitimate.

**Rehabilitation:** Restore Pell Grant eligibility for incarcerated people; expand vocational training in federal prisons; fund reentry housing (10,000 transitional beds); restore federal voting rights to all U.S. citizens upon release. The single largest predictor of recidivism is housing instability — a $15B investment in reentry housing pays for itself in reduced incarceration costs.

**Clean Slate and second chances:** Automatic federal expungement for simple marijuana possession; Clean Slate Act for low-level, nonviolent federal offenses after 7 crime-free years; Ban the Box for federal contractors. 70 million Americans have criminal records — removing barriers to employment, housing, and education is a fiscal and public safety investment, not a gift.

**The bipartisan opening:** Criminal justice reform has the broadest genuine conservative partnership in the entire framework. Faith communities, business groups, and fiscal conservatives all support evidence-based reform. The First Step Act coalition should be rebuilt and expanded. Democrats should pursue this aggressively rather than cede the "tough on crime vs. soft on crime" frame to Republicans — the correct frame is "smart on crime."

**The fiscal case:** $120 billion over 10 years in new investment, but conservative estimates put the return on evidence-based reentry investment at $5–10 per dollar invested in reduced future incarceration costs, reduced crime victimization, increased workforce participation, and reduced emergency healthcare utilization. The current system spends $43,000 per prisoner per year to produce a 43% recidivism rate. We can do better for less.

## PART EIGHT: THE COMMUNICATION STRATEGY

### The Master Narrative — Three Sentences

1. **The problem:** "The game is rigged — by billionaires who buy politicians, corporations that fix prices, and a Republican Party that took your healthcare to give them a tax cut."
2. **The villain:** "While you were working two jobs, they were writing laws to make sure you'd never get ahead."
3. **The solution:** "We have a specific plan. Here's exactly what it is and when it happens."

### What to Say About the Deficit

> "Republicans added $4 trillion to the debt to give tax cuts to millionaires. Our plan runs a modest deficit while front-loading the most urgent investments — then the wealth tax kicks in and we narrow the gap. That's a trajectory. What Republicans did is a permanent hole with no plan for fixing it."

### What to Never Say

- "Working families" (sounds focus-grouped; say "people who work for a living")
- "Systemic" anything (politically charged; use policy-specific language)
- "Equity" as a standalone term (say "fairness")
- "Latinx" (strongly rejected; say "Latino" or "Hispanic")
- "Tax the rich" (say "fair share" or "two cents on the dollar")
- "Medicare for All" as the near-term ask (say "AmeriCare" or "public option")

### The Simplicity Test

Every policy must survive this test: *Could a busy parent who heard this in a 30-second ad tell their spouse what Democrats are going to do about this issue?* If no — simplify until yes.

### The Messenger Rule

Match messenger to audience. Warren-style economic populism in the Rust Belt. Shapiro-style competence-first in suburban Philadelphia. Neither should play the other's role. The national party maintains message discipline on the core economic populism frame; regional candidates calibrate tone and emphasis.

## PART NINE: THE FIVE THINGS THAT CAN DERAIL EVERYTHING

**1. The Restoration Bill fails (most dangerous scenario)**
Prevention: Pre-negotiate every provision before floor vote; never go to the floor without 50 committed votes; have a fallback version of every contested element.

**2. The filibuster carve-out fails and voting rights cannot pass**
Prevention: Make the carve-out a campaign condition in 2026 and 2028; elect pre-committed senators. If it fails anyway: pursue every budget-adjacent provision via reconciliation; make blocking senators famous.

**3. The wealth tax is struck down by SCOTUS**
Prevention: Build the strongest constitutional record; prepare mark-to-market capital gains as the ready fallback. If struck down: enact the fallback immediately; the revenue gap is bridged and the policy goal is substantially preserved.

**4. The House majority collapses (special election losses)**
Prevention: Front-load all Year 1 legislation before any vacancy creates risk; never assume the majority is safe.

**5. Progressive-moderate coalition fractures on imperfect legislation**
Prevention: The governing philosophy in one sentence: *Pass what you can pass, embed it so it's durable, build the coalition to pass the rest — and never sacrifice a real win for a symbolic position.*

## CONCLUSION: WHAT THIS IS FOR

The Project 2025 authors understood something that Democrats have historically ignored: governing power is not bestowed by winning elections. It is built by planning before the election, preparing to act before the inauguration, and executing with discipline and urgency from the first day.

The Project 2025 agenda was not built to improve American life. It was built to concentrate power — in a single president, in a set of ideologically captured agencies, in the hands of those who already had the most. It has partially succeeded. The institutions it damaged — the civil service, the Inspectors General, the Department of Education, USAID, the voting rights regime, the healthcare safety net — will not repair themselves.

Project 2029 is built for a different purpose: to restore what was damaged, reform what was broken before the damage began, and build something better than what existed before. It is built on the conviction that government can be competent and accountable; that economic fairness is achievable through specific policy; that democracy can be made structurally more durable; and that the American majority — which already agrees with most of what this document proposes — can be organized into a governing coalition that changes the country.

The plan is here. The question is whether the party will use it.
