## PREFATORY NOTE: WHY CLIMATE IS AN ECONOMIC CHAPTER AS MUCH AS AN ENVIRONMENTAL ONE

The most important political reframe for Democrats on climate is one that is already mostly true but not yet fully internalized: **the clean energy agenda is an industrial policy, a jobs agenda, a consumer cost agenda, and a national security agenda that also happens to save the planet.**

The IRA did not fail politically because climate is unpopular. It failed politically because its benefits — 300,000+ new manufacturing jobs, lower energy costs, supply chain independence from China — were never translated into a coherent story that working-class voters could feel and connect to a political party. The OBBBA then stripped those benefits away, and the people who lost them are in many cases in Republican districts.

The chapter proceeds from a core governing reality: the climate crisis is accelerating beyond what incremental action can address, but the political path to dramatic action runs through economics, jobs, and energy costs — not through guilt, sacrifice, or abstract planetary concern. Every provision in this chapter has been designed to answer the question: *who gets a job, who gets lower bills, and who gets protected from the next wildfire, hurricane, or heat wave?*

## PART I: THE DAMAGE INVENTORY — WHAT OBBBA DID TO CLEAN ENERGY

The OBBBA's assault on the IRA's clean energy provisions is the most sweeping reversal of industrial and energy policy in American history. Understanding precisely what was repealed, phased out, and preserved is essential to designing the restoration.

### I.1 What Was Eliminated Entirely

**Residential clean energy (immediate elimination):**
- The **Residential Clean Energy Credit (RCEC)** — 30% credit for homeowners installing solar panels, geothermal heat pumps, and battery storage — was repealed for all equipment completing installation after December 31, 2025. Homeowners who had planned installations for 2026 and beyond have no federal support.
- The **Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit (EEHIC)** — 30% credit for insulation, windows, HVAC upgrades — appears to apply only to property placed in service before January 1, 2026.
- The **Credit for Construction of Energy-Efficient New Homes (CCEENH)** — eliminated for homes acquired after June 30, 2026.
- The **Energy Efficient Commercial Buildings Deduction (EECBD)** — eliminated for property beginning construction after June 30, 2026.

**Electric vehicles (near-immediate elimination):**
- The **Section 30D New Clean Vehicle Credit** — the $7,500 EV consumer tax credit — eliminated for vehicles purchased after September 30, 2025.
- The **Section 45W Commercial Clean Vehicle Credit** — eliminated for vehicles acquired more than 180 days after the bill's signing.
- The **Section 30C EV Charging Station Credit** — eliminated for property placed in service after June 2026.
- The **Alternative Fuel Vehicle Refueling Property Credit** — eliminated after June 2026.

### I.2 What Is Being Phased Out (Solar and Wind)

The OBBBA contains an early termination of the core commercial solar and wind tax credits:
- Solar and wind projects that **begin construction on or after July 4, 2026** (one year after enactment) AND are **not placed in service by December 31, 2027** lose eligibility for the Section 45Y Production Tax Credit and Section 48E Clean Electricity Investment Tax Credit entirely.
- Projects beginning construction in 2026: eligible for 40% of current ITC/PTC. In 2027: 45%. In 2028: 50%. In 2029: 55%. In 2030 and beyond: 60%.
- Wind energy manufacturing production credits (Section 45X) are eliminated for components produced and sold after 2027.
- Critical mineral manufacturing credits (Section 45X) are being phased out beginning 2031 at 25% per year.
- The **Section 45X advanced manufacturing production credit** for batteries, solar components, and other clean energy manufacturing is preserved on its original IRA schedule for most components — this is the most important surviving provision and must be defended vigorously.

### I.3 What Was Preserved (The Partial Victory)

Not everything was eliminated. Several provisions survived, largely because they benefit Republican-leaning states and industries:
- **No retroactive clawback**: tax credits for projects already operational are protected.
- **Projects that began construction before enactment**: generally protected, subject to placed-in-service deadlines.
- **Section 45X advanced manufacturing credits** for batteries, solar modules, and critical minerals: preserved on schedule (with wind component exception).
- **Nuclear production credits**: substantially preserved.
- **Carbon capture and sequestration credits**: preserved and in some cases expanded.
- **Geothermal and hydropower**: phased out on a slower schedule than solar and wind.
- **The New ERA rural electric cooperative clean energy program**: survived intact, reportedly because the program serves rural constituencies that supported Trump.

### I.4 The Investment Disruption

Beyond the specific credit eliminations, the OBBBA created a market disruption that is its own category of damage:
- The introduction of massive uncertainty caused private clean energy investment to freeze or slow in the second half of 2025: "The biggest effects of the Trump administration on clean energy has been to introduce massive chaos."
- Projects that were in development pipeline are being cancelled, restructured, or delayed — the credit elimination affects not just projects that would have begun after the cutoff, but the entire planning horizon.
- The U.S. clean energy manufacturing supply chain — which the IRA was building to compete with China — is now being ceded. If these investments aren't made in the next few years, the U.S. will essentially be ceding these industries to China.
- Natural gas prices increased 56% in 2025 compared to 2024, driven by global market forces, making consumers more vulnerable to fossil fuel price volatility than they would have been with expanded renewable capacity.
- Of the $289 billion invested in clean tech manufacturing since late 2022, districts currently represented by Republicans received $223 billion — almost 80%. The OBBBA's repeal predominantly harmed Republican districts, a political fact that must be communicated clearly.

### I.5 EPA and Regulatory Rollbacks

Separate from the OBBBA, the Trump administration reversed or weakened:
- Clean Power Plan successor rules (power plant carbon standards)
- Methane emission regulations for oil and gas
- Clean car and fuel efficiency standards
- EPA's endangerment finding (the legal basis for regulating greenhouse gases) — attempts to revoke this represent the most fundamental regulatory attack
- NEPA environmental review guidance that accelerated clean energy permitting
- Justice40 commitment directing 40% of climate investments to disadvantaged communities

## PART II: THE GOVERNING PHILOSOPHY

### II.1 The Framework: Climate Realism Meets Economic Populism

The climate chapter proceeds from two convictions held simultaneously and without apology:

**Conviction 1: The physics are not negotiable.** The planet is warming at an accelerating rate. The consequences — more frequent and severe wildfires, hurricanes, floods, droughts, heat waves, and sea-level rise — are already visible and will worsen dramatically in the absence of sustained action. The 2029 term is probably the last realistic window in which the United States can pass legislation that keeps a 2°C temperature pathway alive. This is not alarmism. It is the scientific consensus from NASA, NOAA, the IPCC, the U.S. military, and every major scientific institution in the world.

**Conviction 2: The politics must be won on economics, not sacrifice.** Democratic climate messaging that asks voters to accept higher costs, restricted choices, or economic disruption in exchange for planetary benefit will lose — not because voters don't care about the climate, but because it violates the first rule of political persuasion: don't ask people to pay a visible cost for an invisible benefit. The correct frame is the opposite: clean energy means lower electricity bills, more domestic manufacturing jobs, energy independence from foreign fossil fuel markets, and communities protected from climate disasters. The cost of inaction is not invisible — it is a wildfire destroying a town in California, a hurricane flooding Houston, a drought wiping out a farm in Iowa.

### II.2 The Three-Pillar Architecture

**Pillar 1: RESTORE** — Bring back what OBBBA eliminated. Full restoration of all IRA clean energy tax credits for households, businesses, and manufacturers. This is the non-negotiable floor, achievable via reconciliation, and has bipartisan constituency support because 80% of the investment flowed to Republican districts.

**Pillar 2: BUILD** — Construct the infrastructure the IRA only began. Grid modernization, offshore and onshore clean energy deployment at scale, Civilian Climate Corps, clean manufacturing supply chain independence from China. This turns the restored IRA into a platform for a genuine industrial transformation.

**Pillar 3: PRICE** — Establish a market signal that makes clean energy the default economic choice. A carbon fee and dividend with a border carbon adjustment is the most economically efficient mechanism, has genuine bipartisan support, and is the only tool that reaches every sector of the economy simultaneously. It must be designed so that most households receive more in dividends than they pay in higher energy costs.

## PART IV: THE LEGISLATIVE AGENDA

### IV.1 The Climate Restoration Act of 2029

**Part of the FY2030 Building Bill reconciliation package (see Chapter 5)**

This is the legislative restoration of all OBBBA-eliminated and degraded IRA clean energy provisions. It is entirely reconciliation-eligible because all provisions are revenue (tax credits) or spending. It faces lower political risk than the healthcare provisions because its beneficiaries are in Republican districts.

#### Title I: Residential Clean Energy Restoration

**Sec. 1.1 — Restore Residential Clean Energy Credit**
- Restore the 30% RCEC for solar panels, geothermal heat pumps, wind turbines, and battery storage, effective for equipment placed in service after enactment
- Extend through 2035 (beyond original IRA schedule) to restore investor and consumer confidence disrupted by OBBBA
- Expand eligible technologies: add heat pump water heaters, EV-integrated home charging systems, and community solar subscriptions

**Sec. 1.2 — Restore Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit**
- Restore 30% EEHIC for insulation, windows, doors, HVAC upgrades, and heat pumps
- Raise the annual cap from $3,200 to $4,500 to reflect cost inflation since 2022
- Extend through 2035

**Sec. 1.3 — Restore New Energy Efficient Home Credit and Commercial Building Deduction**
- Restore CCEENH for home builders; restore EECBD for commercial building upgrades
- Extend both through 2035

#### Title II: Clean Transportation Restoration

**Sec. 2.1 — Restore EV Consumer Tax Credit (Section 30D)**
- Restore $7,500 new clean vehicle credit; effective immediately upon enactment
- Expand income eligibility: $200,000 for single filers (up from the original $150,000 IRA threshold); $300,000 for married filing jointly (restoring the original IRA threshold eliminated by OBBBA)
- Extend point-of-sale rebate option (consumers receive credit at dealership, not on tax return) — this makes the credit accessible to lower-income buyers who cannot wait for a tax refund
- Require domestic content: 50% domestic battery content by 2027; 75% by 2030 — maintains supply chain development pressure

**Sec. 2.2 — Restore Commercial Clean Vehicle Credit (Section 45W)**
- Restore credit for commercial EVs (trucking, delivery, fleet vehicles); critical for small trucking businesses
- Extend through 2032

**Sec. 2.3 — Restore EV Charging Infrastructure Credit (Section 30C)**
- Restore credit for EV charging station installation
- Prioritize rural and low-income community installations: enhanced 40% credit for charging in census-designated rural areas or low-income communities

#### Title III: Commercial Clean Electricity Restoration

**Sec. 3.1 — Restore Solar and Wind Production and Investment Tax Credits**
- Restore full Section 45Y PTC and Section 48E ITC for solar and wind projects beginning construction after enactment, with no beginning-of-construction or placed-in-service deadline
- Eliminate the OBBBA's phased-down domestic content percentages that disadvantaged projects in development
- Note: The political case for full restoration is strongest in Republican-district solar and wind markets — Senators from Iowa, Texas, Wyoming, Georgia, and the Carolinas have constituency interest in restoration

**Sec. 3.2 — Restore Wind Energy Manufacturing Credits (Section 45X)**
- Restore Section 45X production credits for wind turbine components through 2032
- This is the domestic manufacturing supply chain provision — eliminating it ceded wind turbine manufacturing to China and Denmark
- Expand 45X to cover offshore wind components and floating offshore wind technology

**Sec. 3.3 — Protect and Extend Critical Mineral and Battery Manufacturing Credits**
- Extend Section 45X critical mineral and battery credits through 2035 (beyond their current phase-out schedule beginning 2031)
- Expand eligible materials to include rare earth permanent magnets, advanced cathode materials, and solid-state battery components
- Add a 10% bonus credit for manufacturing in communities that experienced coal mine or fossil fuel facility closure

**Sec. 3.4 — Clean Hydrogen Production Credit**
- Maintain and strengthen Section 45V clean hydrogen production credit
- Clarify eligibility rules: "green" hydrogen (electrolysis with clean electricity) receives full credit; "blue" hydrogen (natural gas with carbon capture) receives partial credit; eliminate gaming of additionality requirements

**Sec. 3.5 — Offshore Wind Development**
- Fund Bureau of Ocean Energy Management to accelerate permitting pipeline for Atlantic, Gulf, and Pacific offshore wind
- Provide loan guarantees for floating offshore wind technology development (deepwater sites that fixed-bottom cannot reach)
- Restore offshore wind leasing program halted by Trump Day 1 executive order

### IV.2 The Carbon Price and Dividend Act

**Pathway: Reconciliation (carbon fee is a revenue measure; dividend distribution is spending). This is the most consequential and politically complex element of the climate agenda.**

#### The Policy Design

**The core mechanism:** A fee on carbon emissions at the point of production or import — fossil fuel companies pay per ton of CO2-equivalent when coal, oil, or natural gas enters the economy. One hundred percent of net revenues are returned to all Americans as a monthly dividend. A border carbon adjustment ensures American manufacturers are not undercut by competitors from countries without equivalent carbon pricing.

**The fee schedule:**
- Starting price: $40/ton of CO2-equivalent in Year 1 (2031, when enacted)
- Annual increase: $15/ton per year above inflation
- By Year 5 (2035): approximately $100/ton
- By Year 10 (2040): approximately $175/ton

At $40/ton starting price, the fee translates to approximately 36 cents per gallon of gasoline. At $100/ton, approximately 89 cents per gallon. These price signals make clean energy alternatives progressively more competitive with every passing year, without mandating any specific technology.

**The dividend:**
- 100% of net fee revenues distributed equally to all U.S. residents (not just taxpayers)
- Children receive one-half share
- Distribution: monthly direct deposit or check — visibility of the payment is essential; it should feel like a paycheck
- At $40/ton starting price: approximately $700-900/year per adult; $1,400-1,800/family of four
- About two-thirds of Americans will receive more in dividends than they pay in higher energy prices — the policy is progressive because wealthy households consume vastly more energy
- The bottom 20% of earners receive approximately three times their increased costs back in dividends

**The border carbon adjustment:**
- Products imported from countries without equivalent carbon pricing pay a fee equal to the carbon content of their production at the U.S. carbon price
- American exporters to those countries receive a rebate
- This prevents carbon leakage (production moving offshore to avoid the fee) and protects American manufacturers from unfair competition
- The EU's CBAM (Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism) creates a natural ally: coordinate with the EU to develop mutually compatible CBAM frameworks — this becomes a trade policy tool as well as a climate tool
- Countries that adopt equivalent carbon pricing are exempt from the border adjustment — creating powerful incentives for global alignment

#### The Political Case

The carbon fee and dividend has genuine bipartisan structural support that no other climate policy achieves:

- Nearly three-quarters of voters nationwide support a carbon border adjustment once they hear a brief explanation, with solid support across party lines including in states with heavy manufacturing and fossil fuel-based economies.
- During the first 20 years alone, a carbon fee and dividend policy would lead to a 50% reduction of carbon emissions below 1990 levels and the addition of 2.8 million jobs above baseline.
- About two-thirds of Americans will receive more in dividends than they will pay in higher prices.
- Republican Senator Bill Cassidy (Louisiana) introduced the Foreign Pollution Fee Act; Republican Senator Kevin Cramer (North Dakota) co-sponsored the bipartisan PROVE IT Act — the carbon border adjustment has Republican support rooted in manufacturing protection and China accountability
- The Climate Leadership Council — a group of Republican elder statesmen including James Baker and George Schultz — has advocated for carbon fee and dividend for years

**The political framing:**
> "Oil companies pay a fee for the pollution they create. Every dollar comes back to you — every American gets a monthly check. It's not a government program. It's your money, returning to you from the people who've been treating the atmosphere as a free dump for a century. And the border adjustment means Chinese factories can't undercut American manufacturers by polluting more — they pay the same price we do."

#### The Procedural Path

Carbon fee and dividend is reconciliation-eligible because:
- The carbon fee is a tax (revenue provision)
- The dividend distribution is a spending provision (direct payments to individuals)
- The border carbon adjustment is a tariff modification (revenue)
- The CBO can score all three components in conventional budget terms

The political challenge is not procedural — it is intra-caucus. The most conservative Democratic senators from fossil-fuel states (Montana, West Virginia successor, potentially Louisiana or Texas if flipped) will face pressure against any carbon price. The design features that address this:

1. **The dividend makes working-class constituents whole** — their senators can say "most of my constituents get more back than they pay"
2. **The phase-in is gradual** — a $40/ton starting price is manageable for most industries
3. **The border adjustment protects domestic industry** — including fossil fuel-adjacent industries like steel and aluminum that use energy intensively
4. **Free permits for high-risk industries in transition** — coal and natural gas communities can receive enhanced dividend shares or transition assistance in the first years
5. **The revenue is not used for general spending** — 100% back to people is a firewall against "carbon tax to fund government" attacks

### IV.3 The Civilian Climate Corps Authorization Act

**Pathway: Reconciliation (spending provision, fully budgeted)**

#### The Vision

The Civilian Climate Corps is the New Deal's Civilian Conservation Corps for the 21st century — a federal program that employs hundreds of thousands of Americans in climate resilience, conservation, and clean energy work, paying prevailing wages with full benefits, and creating a trained workforce for the industries that will define the next economy.

The CCC (1933-1942) enrolled 3 million workers, planted 3 billion trees, built thousands of parks and public lands infrastructure, and provided employment in the depths of the Depression. The Civilian Climate Corps adapts this model to the dual crises of climate change and economic dislocation — deploying workers to do the resilience and conservation work that must be done and that private markets will not do at sufficient scale.

#### Scale and Scope

**Target enrollment:** 300,000 workers by Year 3; 400,000 at peak
**Wage:** Prevailing wage for the region and skill level; minimum $20/hour; full benefits (healthcare, retirement, education stipend)
**Term:** 12-24 month service terms; renewable; pathway to permanent federal or private sector employment

**Work categories:**
- **Climate resilience infrastructure**: wildfire prevention (forest thinning, controlled burns, firebreaks); flood barrier construction and wetland restoration; urban heat island mitigation (tree planting, green roofs); coastal restoration and seawall maintenance
- **Clean energy installation**: residential solar and weatherization for low-income households; rural broadband and EV charging infrastructure in underserved communities; battery storage installation
- **Conservation and land management**: trail maintenance, invasive species removal, reforestation; public lands restoration; urban parks
- **Community resilience**: disaster preparedness planning and infrastructure; emergency response training; water system protection

**Priority communities:**
- Communities that experienced coal mine, power plant, or fossil fuel facility closures
- Environmental justice communities with disproportionate pollution burden
- Rural areas with high unemployment and limited clean energy access
- Tribal communities facing climate displacement

**Education component:**
- Every CCC member receives a $20,000 education stipend usable at community colleges, vocational programs, or apprenticeship programs
- Partnership with community colleges and union apprenticeship programs for on-the-job credentialing
- Veterans priority: 25% of enrollment target for veterans; CCC service converts to VA healthcare and GI Bill eligibility for education stipend

#### The Political Frame

> "We're putting 300,000 Americans to work protecting their communities from wildfire, flood, and extreme heat. Good jobs, union wages, and benefits. Protecting your home, your farm, your watershed. This is America doing what it does best — putting people to work on the biggest challenge of our time. And at the end of their service, they've got job skills for the industries of the future."

#### Budget

- Year 1: $15B (establishment, recruitment, training infrastructure)
- Year 2: $25B (full ramp-up to 300,000 enrollees)
- Years 3-10: $30B/year
- 10-year total: approximately $270-280B
- Note: This is consistent with the Chapter 2 climate spending allocation; funded within the FY2030 Building Bill reconciliation package

### IV.4 The Grid Modernization and Clean Electricity Act

**Pathway: Reconciliation (spending); Clean Electricity Standard requires 60 votes OR agency rulemaking under Clean Air Act**

#### The Grid Crisis

The United States electricity grid is the binding constraint on the clean energy transition. Renewable energy generation is growing rapidly — but it cannot reach consumers if there is no transmission infrastructure to carry it, and it cannot be reliable if there is no storage and grid intelligence to manage its intermittency.

Specific problems:
- The U.S. has approximately 40,000 miles of high-voltage transmission lines; experts estimate 100,000 additional miles are needed to achieve 80% clean electricity by 2035
- The interconnection queue — applications from clean energy projects waiting to connect to the grid — has grown from 4,000 projects in 2015 to over 15,000 projects in 2025, with average wait times exceeding 5 years
- The average age of the U.S. grid infrastructure is 40+ years; critical components (transformers, substations) have lead times of 1-3 years for replacement
- Extreme weather events are already straining grid reliability: Texas (2021 freeze), California (heat waves), Florida (hurricanes); the grid is not designed for the climate it now operates in

#### Title I: Transmission Infrastructure Investment

**Sec. 1.1 — National Transmission Planning Authority**
- Establish a Federal Transmission Planning Office within FERC with authority to designate nationally significant transmission corridors and streamline permitting across jurisdictional boundaries
- Current problem: a transmission line crossing state lines requires separate approval from each state's utility commission — a single 1,000-mile line can require 10 different permitting processes
- New authority: designate "National Energy Security Corridors" where federal environmental review substitutes for duplicative state reviews (with state consultation, not exclusion)

**Sec. 1.2 — Grid Investment Fund**
- $150B over 10 years in federal loan guarantees and grants for high-voltage transmission infrastructure
- Priority corridors: Midwest wind-to-load (wind energy from Kansas to Chicago and Detroit); Southeast solar-to-load; Western interconnect upgrades; offshore wind-to-load for Atlantic coast
- Co-investment requirement: 50% private or state/local match for grants; 20% match for loan guarantees in rural and underserved areas
- Workforce: all grid construction must meet prevailing wage and domestic content requirements

**Sec. 1.3 — Interconnection Queue Reform**
- Fund FERC to hire 200 additional staff for interconnection review
- Implement cluster interconnection studies (review groups of projects together rather than sequentially)
- Set maximum interconnection review timelines: 18 months for projects under 100 MW; 36 months for larger projects
- Mandate that interconnection costs are socialized across the grid zone rather than assigned entirely to first-in-queue projects (the current system creates a cost lottery that discourages investment)

#### Title II: Grid Resilience and Storage

**Sec. 2.1 — Long-Duration Energy Storage**
- $20B over 10 years for deployment of long-duration energy storage (LDES) technologies: pumped hydro, iron-air batteries, compressed air, hydrogen storage
- LDES is the missing piece: lithium-ion batteries can store electricity for 4-6 hours; LDES stores for 12-100+ hours, enabling reliable clean electricity through nights, cloudy days, and seasonal variation
- Loan guarantee program through DOE for first-of-kind LDES commercial deployments

**Sec. 2.2 — Grid Hardening and Resilience**
- $30B over 10 years for grid hardening: underground transmission in wildfire-prone areas; storm-hardened infrastructure in hurricane zones; substation backup power and cybersecurity
- Priority: communities that have experienced catastrophic grid failure events (Texas, Louisiana, Puerto Rico)
- Puerto Rico: dedicated $5B for reconstruction of Puerto Rico's grid as a modern, clean, distributed, resilient system — the island's colonial-era grid failed catastrophically in Maria (2017) and has never been fully rebuilt

**Sec. 2.3 — Distributed Energy and Virtual Power Plants**
- Fund development of virtual power plant (VPP) networks: aggregate distributed rooftop solar, home batteries, EV batteries, and smart appliances into controllable grid resources
- Mandate FERC rules requiring utilities to compensate distributed resource owners for grid services (currently utilities often receive distributed resources' grid value without payment)
- $10B for community microgrids in rural and island communities to provide resilience from grid outages

#### Title III: Clean Electricity Standard

**Pathway: This is the most ambitious provision and faces the highest procedural barriers. A Clean Electricity Standard is a regulatory mandate, not primarily a budget provision, making reconciliation eligibility contested. Three pathways exist:**

**Pathway A: Agency rulemaking under the Clean Air Act** — The EPA's Clean Power Plan (and its successors) established that EPA has authority to regulate power plant carbon emissions under CAA Section 111. A Democratic administration should issue a new Clean Power Plan on Day 1 via executive action, restoring and strengthening standards. This is immediately effective but reversible by future administrations.

**Pathway B: Legislation via 60-vote regular order** — A Clean Electricity Standard has some bipartisan potential. Several Republican senators from states with high renewable energy penetration (Iowa, Kansas, Wyoming, South Dakota) have constituent interest in stable clean energy markets. The policy design that maximizes bipartisan viability is a Clean Electricity Standard with a compliance flexibility mechanism (trading between utilities) that allows market forces to determine the technology mix.

**Pathway C: Use reconciliation for what it can cover** — Even without a formal CES mandate, the combination of restored IRA tax credits, grid investment, and a carbon price creates the market conditions under which utilities will choose clean electricity without a mandate. The carbon price is the market signal; the tax credits are the investment support; the grid investment is the infrastructure. A formal mandate may not be necessary if the economics make clean energy the obvious choice.

**This framework recommends:** Pursue the Clean Power Plan via EPA rulemaking on Day 1 as the immediate bridge; pursue Clean Electricity Standard legislation via regular order with a 60% clean electricity by 2035 target; accept that the 100% target may need to be achieved through market forces plus the carbon price rather than through a mandate.

**Clean Electricity Standard design (for the legislative attempt):**
- 70% clean electricity by 2035 (achievable given current trajectory plus restored IRA)
- 90% by 2040
- 100% by 2050 (with reliability provisions allowing dispatchable clean fuels — clean hydrogen, advanced geothermal, advanced nuclear — to cover the final 10%)
- Compliance flexibility: utilities can trade "clean electricity certificates"; non-complying utilities pay into a Clean Energy Investment Fund distributed to states for clean energy deployment

### IV.5 The Environmental Justice and Climate Resilience Act

**Purpose:** Ensure that the benefits of clean energy reach the communities most harmed by pollution, and that climate resilience investment prioritizes those most vulnerable to climate disaster.

**Sec. 1 — Justice40 Restoration and Strengthening**
- Restore and codify in statute the Justice40 commitment: 40% of the benefits of federal climate and clean energy investment must flow to disadvantaged communities
- Define "disadvantaged community" using a specific, enforceable screening tool (EPA's EJScreen or equivalent)
- Require agencies to publicly report annually on Justice40 compliance by program

**Sec. 2 — Clean Air and Environmental Health**
- Restore and strengthen EPA air quality standards for fine particulate matter and ozone — Trump-era rollbacks of these standards caused measurable increases in asthma, cardiovascular disease, and premature death, disproportionately in communities of color near industrial facilities
- Fund community air monitoring networks in environmental justice communities: real-time, public-facing data on local air quality
- Establish a Health Harms Fund: $10B over 10 years for communities with documented excess health burdens from industrial pollution; funded through civil penalties from Clean Air Act violators

**Sec. 3 — Climate Disaster Resilience**
- FEMA reform: restructure FEMA around pre-disaster mitigation as well as post-disaster response; current FEMA funding is 90% reactive and 10% preventive, when the reverse would be far more cost-effective
- Invest $30B over 10 years in pre-disaster mitigation: buyout programs for properties in high-risk flood zones; community relocation assistance for communities facing chronic flooding; forest management to reduce wildfire risk; urban heat island mitigation
- Climate-resilient building codes: federal incentive for states to adopt modern building codes that make structures resilient to the specific climate hazards they face (hurricane codes in Florida, wildfire codes in California, freeze codes in Texas)
- Flood insurance reform: restructure the National Flood Insurance Program to reflect actual risk; end subsidized flood insurance for repeatedly-flooded properties; fund buyouts instead

**Sec. 4 — Superfund and Legacy Pollution Cleanup**
- Fully fund and accelerate Superfund cleanup: 1,300+ Superfund sites still await cleanup decades after listing; many are in or near disadvantaged communities
- Fund cleanup of coal ash ponds: 700+ coal ash impoundments contain toxic heavy metals leaching into groundwater near communities
- Fund orphan oil and gas well plugging: 3 million unplugged orphan wells leak methane and contaminate water; a bipartisan cleanup program creates thousands of jobs in fossil fuel communities

## PART VI: THE NUCLEAR QUESTION

Nuclear energy deserves honest treatment rather than avoidance. The Project 2029 climate agenda neither abandons existing nuclear power plants nor commits to large-scale expansion. The position:

**Existing nuclear:** The approximately 90 operating nuclear reactors in the United States provide approximately 20% of U.S. electricity and nearly 50% of U.S. carbon-free electricity. They should be kept operating as long as they are safe and economically viable. The IRA's nuclear production credit (Section 45U) should be maintained to prevent premature closure.

**Advanced nuclear:** Small modular reactors (SMRs) and advanced reactor designs — if they can deliver on cost and timeline promises — represent a potentially important complement to wind and solar for providing clean firm power. The DOE should continue funding advanced nuclear R&D and early commercial deployment. The NRC should be resourced to review advanced reactor designs on reasonable timelines.

**New large light-water reactors:** The Vogtle experience — the first new large U.S. nuclear plant in decades, completed in 2024 at 2-3x projected cost and years behind schedule — demonstrates that new conventional nuclear is extraordinarily expensive and slow. It should not be the primary tool for decarbonization, though other countries' experiences with new construction may yield design and cost lessons over time.

**The honest framing:** Nuclear's role in the 2029-2033 term is to maintain what we have, accelerate the evaluation of advanced designs, and not make ideological commitments in either direction. Climate urgency requires deploying proven, cost-competitive clean energy at maximum speed — which means wind, solar, storage, and grid investment first — while keeping the nuclear option open for the longer run.

## PART VII: THE POLITICAL STRATEGY

### VII.1 The Republican District Coalition

The single most important political fact about the IRA is that 80% of its clean energy manufacturing investment went to Republican districts. This is not a footnote — it is the organizing principle of the political strategy for IRA restoration.

**Target Republican senators for IRA restoration (60-vote path attempt):**
- Iowa (Chuck Grassley): wind energy capital of the U.S.; Section 45X wind component credit elimination directly harms Iowa manufacturers
- Kansas (Jerry Moran): major wind producer; solar growing rapidly
- Wyoming (John Barrasso): wind and coal; carbon capture credits benefit Wyoming
- Texas (John Cornyn): offshore wind off the Gulf Coast; solar; manufacturing
- Georgia (TBD): solar manufacturing facilities built under IRA are in Georgia
- South Carolina (Tim Scott): BMW EV manufacturing; Volvo manufacturing; German suppliers building EV supply chain

The IRA restoration is not a partisan vote for these senators — it is protection of jobs and investment in their states. The framing: "We're not asking you to believe in climate change. We're asking you to believe in jobs and investment in your state."

### VII.2 The Carbon Price Coalition

The carbon fee and dividend has different political terrain — it requires Democratic unity via reconciliation or a bipartisan coalition that includes fiscal conservatives (who see the carbon fee as revenue), China hawks (who want the border adjustment to penalize dirty Chinese manufacturing), and manufacturing state senators (who want the border adjustment to protect domestic industry).

**Pre-legislation strategy:** Commission and release detailed distributional analysis by Senate state showing which states and income groups benefit from the dividend. The analysis will show that in most fossil-fuel-dependent states, the majority of households come out ahead because they are lower-income and consume less energy per dollar of income than wealthy coastal households. This undermines the "carbon tax hurts working people" attack before it lands.

### VII.3 The Weather Disaster Frame

Climate disasters are the fastest-growing political opportunity in the climate agenda. When a hurricane destroys a city, when wildfires burn towns, when floods wipe out crops, voters want government that protects them. The climate resilience investment — FEMA reform, pre-disaster mitigation, community resilience — is the near-term political opportunity that makes the longer-term decarbonization agenda more politically sustainable.

> "We can keep spending $50 billion after every disaster to clean up the damage, or we can spend $10 billion before the disaster to prevent it. We choose prevention. And we make the fossil fuel industry help pay for the damage they've been contributing to for a century."
