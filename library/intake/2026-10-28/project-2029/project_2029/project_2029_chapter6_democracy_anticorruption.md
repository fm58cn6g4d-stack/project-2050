## PART I: THE DAMAGE INVENTORY

### I.1 The Inspector General Massacre

The Inspector General system — created by the Inspector General Act of 1978 in the wake of Watergate, designed to be the permanent, nonpartisan watchdog of every federal agency — has been methodically dismantled.

**The scale:** President Trump has removed more Inspectors General than all other presidents combined. Trump broke historic norms by firing 17 presidentially appointed IGs; Trump's firings, additional resignations, and preexisting vacancies have resulted in over 75% of presidentially appointed inspector general positions sitting vacant.

**The pattern of retaliation:** In several cases, Trump's removal of IGs has been closely tied to the issuance of OIG reports critical of specific administration actions, or has come as a result of an IG fulfilling their duty to relay whistleblower disclosures to Congress. The most stark example: USAID's Inspector General was fired just weeks after Trump's first mass IG purge, after his office released a report warning that the administration's actions had left oversight of humanitarian aid "largely non-operational."

**The replacements:** In most cases, Trump has replaced fired IGs with those whose independence is questionable, who have had close ties to his administration, or who have a history of unethical or potentially illegal conduct.

**The chilling effect on those who remain:** Key trends emerged from IG office staff testimony before the Senate Homeland Security Committee: low morale, concerns about independence, and an inability to carry out responsibilities. One employee stated their "biggest concern is that we have no independence," while another stated that "people are terrified to do their work, speak up, give new ideas, report anything unfavorably on the agency, or disagree with DOGE."

**The workforce collapse:** Inspectors general offices lost 16.6% of their workforce from January 2025 to early 2026, exceeding overall government staffing cuts of 12%.

**The suppression of inconvenient findings:** An unpublished draft SSA IG report showed the watchdog planned to report that in 2025 total wait time for Social Security callers averaged 46 minutes to over two hours — but that information was deleted from the draft after the agency reviewed it. A whistleblower at the SSA alleged that the acting IG instituted an informal policy not to contradict or criticize DOGE.

**The dollar cost:** A Senate report found that the 19 IGs Trump fired had identified billions of dollars more in potential savings to taxpayers than DOGE — the very agency created to replace them. The government fired its most cost-effective watchdogs and replaced them with an unaccountable private operation with access to sensitive data systems and no legal authority.

**The legal violations:** The White House did not provide a rationale for removals as required by law. Senator Grassley wrote: "The law says POTUS has to specifically inform Congress about IG firings and unless the courts say otherwise that's still the law." The Trump administration also blocked funds appropriated by Congress to the Council of the Inspectors General on Integrity and Efficiency, an umbrella office that supports the IG community.

### I.2 The Schedule F / Schedule Policy-Career Spoils System

The civil service merit system — created by the Pendleton Civil Service Reform Act of 1883 specifically to end the political patronage and corruption of the Gilded Age spoils system — has been systematically dismantled and replaced with a modern spoils system.

**What happened:** On January 20, 2025, Trump signed Executive Order 14171 reinstating Schedule F as "Schedule Policy/Career," a classification that strips civil service protections from career federal employees in policy-influencing roles, making them effectively at-will employees who can be fired for "refusing to advance the policy interests of the president."

**The scale:** OPM estimates that roughly 50,000 positions could be affected — although the rule does not limit future reclassifications. The rule took final legal effect on March 9, 2026 — six days before this chapter was written.

**The explicit ideology:** OPM's final rule cites "accountability to the president" as grounds for stripping civil servants of protections, and describes the 1978 Civil Service Reform Act — itself a post-Watergate accountability measure — as an "unconstitutional overcorrection."

**What "at-will" means in practice:** Employees reclassified under Schedule Policy-Career lose the right to advance notice of removal and the right to appeal their firing to the Merit Systems Protection Board. They become functionally dismissible for any reason, including perceived insufficient loyalty to the administration's political agenda.

**The public verdict:** The proposed rule received over 40,000 public comments, 94% of which were opposed. The administration finalized it anyway.

**The Watergate parallel:** This is precisely the system Nixon tried to build — a federal workforce responsive to political direction rather than law and merit. The Pendleton Act, the civil service reforms, the 1978 Act — all were designed to prevent exactly this. The Trump administration is explicitly characterizing those safeguards as unconstitutional obstacles.

- The Foreign Corrupt Practices Act enforcement was formally paused, effectively exempting Trump's foreign business partners and politically connected corporations from anti-bribery enforcement

### I.4 The DOGE Data and Accountability Crisis

DOGE — formally a private advisory body operating within the Executive Office of the President with no statutory authority, no independent oversight, and no legal accountability framework — accessed the most sensitive government data systems in existence:

- Social Security Administration records covering hundreds of millions of Americans
- Treasury payment systems controlling federal disbursements
- Medicare and Medicaid claims data
- IRS tax records
- Personnel records for federal employees including intelligence community staff

A whistleblower at the SSA alleged that DOGE's access to sensitive SSA data violated laws and protocols, and that SSA had moved the sensitive data of hundreds of millions of people to an insecure cloud environment.

No independent forensic audit of what DOGE accessed, copied, transmitted, or modified has been conducted. The IGs who would normally conduct such an audit have been fired or compromised. Congress's ability to investigate has been blocked. The Trump administration directed agencies not to cooperate with the Government Accountability Office, Congress's watchdog, and hid legally required budget data used for oversight.

### I.5 Campaign Finance and Dark Money

The corrosive influence of concentrated private wealth on American politics has reached historic proportions:

- Citizens United v. FEC (2010) opened the floodgates to unlimited corporate and billionaire spending in elections
- Dark money — political spending by nonprofit organizations that don't disclose their donors — has grown from negligible amounts to billions per cycle
- Super PACs allow unlimited coordination-adjacent spending; disclosure rules are riddled with workarounds
- A single individual — Elon Musk — spent an estimated $250 million or more on the 2024 presidential election, making him among the largest political spenders in American history, and was then given authority over federal data systems and effectively made a shadow cabinet member
- The crypto and tech industry poured hundreds of millions into 2024 elections to install sympathetic regulators
- Agricultural, pharmaceutical, fossil fuel, and financial lobbies routinely translate political spending into favorable regulatory treatment

This is what "oligarchy" looks like in concrete, documented form. It is not a rhetorical charge — it is a measurable, structural fact.

### I.6 Voting Rights

- The Voting Rights Act of 1965, gutted by the Supreme Court in Shelby County v. Holder (2013) and further damaged in Brnovich v. Democratic National Committee (2021), no longer provides effective preclearance protection against discriminatory voting rules
- Since Shelby County, jurisdictions previously covered by preclearance have enacted voter ID laws, polling place closures, voter roll purges, and early voting restrictions at rates far exceeding non-covered jurisdictions
- The Trump administration has used executive authority to encourage state-level voting restrictions and to contest the integrity of elections it lost
- Gerrymandering — partisan manipulation of district lines — has produced House maps in key states (Wisconsin, North Carolina, Ohio, Georgia) where the minority party wins a majority of seats, fundamentally distorting representation
- Money in politics means that wealthy donors have multiple vectors of influence; ordinary citizens have one: their vote. Suppressing that vote is therefore the anti-democratic complement to the campaign finance system that magnifies wealth.

## PART II: THE GOVERNING PHILOSOPHY

### II.1 The Core Commitment

Democracy reform is not "good government" incrementalism. It is the fight to ensure that the system that is supposed to translate public will into policy actually does so — rather than being captured by those with the money and power to rig it.

The Project 2029 commitment:

**Every American's vote should count equally.** Not diluted by gerrymandering, suppressed by ID requirements designed to exclude, or overridden by the Electoral College distortion of one-person-one-vote.

**Every public official should be accountable.** Not shielded by fired watchdogs, protected by dark money, or immunized from oversight by a politicized DOJ.

**The government workforce should serve the public, not the party.** A federal employee hired because they are expert should not be fired because they are insufficiently loyal.

**Private wealth should not determine public policy.** The ability to spend billions on elections should not translate into the ability to write regulations, gut agencies, or access sensitive public data.

### II.2 Why This Must Come First

The sequencing in Chapter 5 places voting rights and government accountability early in the legislative calendar for substantive reasons, not just rhetorical ones:

- Without restored civil service protections, every agency rebuilt in Chapters 3 and 4 is vulnerable to the next political appointee
- Without an independent Inspector General system, every dollar of public investment can be looted without detection
- Without voting rights protection, the electoral coalition built to win in 2028 can be legally suppressed in 2030 and 2032
- Without campaign finance reform, every legislative win can be purchased away by the industries it regulates

The sequence is: win power → protect the mechanism for holding power democratically → use it for everything else.

## PART IV: THE LEGISLATIVE AGENDA

### IV.1 Title I — Inspector General Restoration and Independence Act

**Purpose:** Restore the IG system to full independence; make political removal effectively impossible; fund and staff it to pre-DOGE levels and beyond.

**Sec. 1.1 — Restoration of Fired IGs**
- Statutory directive to reinstate, with back pay, all IGs fired without cause in the January 2025 purge and subsequent firings, except those found by independent review to have committed actual misconduct
- Establish an Independent IG Review Board — composed of retired federal judges and former IGs from both parties — to review the record of every Trump-era IG firing and replacement, and to recommend restoration or formal removal proceedings where warranted

**Sec. 1.2 — Cause Requirement for IG Removal (Codified)**
- Amend the Inspector General Act of 1978 to require that an IG may only be removed for cause — specifically: (a) commission of a felony; (b) gross neglect of duty; (c) malfeasance in office; (d) knowing violation of law or regulation
- Removal must be preceded by: 30 days written notice to Congress with specific cause stated; a hearing before the Senate committee of jurisdiction; and a majority committee vote finding cause
- Any removal without following this procedure is void ab initio; the IG retains their position and pay
- These provisions supersede any presidential Article II claim of removal authority for this category of officer; the statutory cause requirement is constitutionally grounded in Congress's authority to create and structure offices

**Sec. 1.3 — Restore and Expand IG Funding**
- Restore all IG offices to their pre-January 2025 staffing and funding levels within 90 days of enactment
- Establish a permanent IG Minimum Staffing Act: no IG office may be reduced below 75% of its prior-year staffing without a 60-day Congressional notification and a formal finding of mission-compatibility
- Restore Council of the Inspectors General on Integrity and Efficiency funding and authority; prohibit White House impoundment of CIGIE appropriations

**Sec. 1.4 — DOGE Accountability**
- Direct all IG offices with relevant jurisdiction to conduct a comprehensive forensic audit of DOGE's access to, modification of, and transmission of federal data systems
- Audit must cover: SSA, Treasury, IRS, CMS, OPM, and any other systems accessed
- Results reported to Congress within 180 days; criminal referrals to DOJ as appropriate
- IG offices given full document and system access for this purpose; agency non-cooperation is a federal crime

**Sec. 1.5 — Whistleblower Enhancement**
- Strengthen the Whistleblower Protection Act: expand covered disclosures; increase damages for retaliation; extend protection to intelligence community whistleblowers who use proper channels
- Create an Office of Whistleblower Protection independent of agency IGs, reporting directly to Congress
- Prohibit non-disclosure agreements (NDAs) that prevent federal employees or contractors from reporting potential violations of law to Congress or IGs

### IV.2 Title II — Civil Service Restoration and Protection Act
*(The "Saving the Merit System" Act)*

**Purpose:** Restore merit-based civil service protections; permanently prohibit Schedule F-style reclassification; codify in statute what should never have been at executive discretion.

**Sec. 2.1 — Repeal Schedule Policy-Career**
- Immediately repeal Schedule Policy/Career (formerly Schedule F) in its entirety; rescind OPM's February 2026 final rule
- Restore to competitive service all employees reclassified under Schedule Policy/Career since January 20, 2025; restore their due process and appeal rights retroactively
- Direct MSPB to expedite all pending appeals from employees who were removed following reclassification

**Sec. 2.2 — Permanent Prohibition on Spoils Reclassification**
- Add to the Civil Service Reform Act of 1978 a categorical prohibition: no president, agency head, or OPM director may reclassify a position from the competitive service to the excepted service on the basis that it is "policy-influencing," "policy-determining," or similar characterization, unless the position is specifically designated as a political appointment by Congress in statute
- The number of political appointee positions is hereby capped at the 2020 level (approximately 4,000 Schedule C and PAS positions); any expansion requires a specific Act of Congress

**Sec. 2.3 — MSPB and Due Process Restoration**
- Restore full Merit Systems Protection Board jurisdiction over all personnel actions
- Hire and confirm sufficient MSPB judges to eliminate the backlog of appeals created by 2025-2026 mass firings
- Prohibit agencies from using reductions-in-force as a pretext for removing specific employees; require RIF plans to be reviewed by OPM and IG for pattern of political targeting

**Sec. 2.4 — Federal Employee Rights Restoration**
- Restore collective bargaining rights for federal employees reduced by Trump-era executive orders
- Restore official time for union representatives (time used for representational activities)
- Restore telework rights to pre-2025 levels pending proper agency-by-agency review
- Restore probationary employee rights: probationary employees fired en masse must receive individualized written justification and have access to MSPB appeal

**Sec. 2.5 — Hatch Act Integrity**
- Strengthen the Hatch Act's prohibitions on political coercion of federal employees
- Create an independent Hatch Act Enforcement office with authority to impose civil penalties and refer criminal violations; remove enforcement authority from the OSC political appointee and vest it in a nonpartisan Board

### IV.3 Title III — DOJ and Law Enforcement Independence Act

**Purpose:** Codify DOJ independence from White House political interference; restore FCPA enforcement; protect the FBI's nonpartisan character.

**Sec. 3.1 — DOJ Independence Standards (Codified)**
- Codify in statute the longstanding Justice Department norm prohibiting direct White House communication with DOJ about specific pending cases, with narrow national security exception
- Prohibit the President, Vice President, and White House staff from directing or recommending specific prosecutorial decisions or case dispositions
- Violations are a federal crime; penalties: up to 5 years imprisonment; removal from office for executive branch officials

**Sec. 3.2 — Restore FCPA Enforcement**
- Rescind all Trump-era guidance pausing or limiting Foreign Corrupt Practices Act enforcement
- Direct DOJ to publish, within 60 days, all matters in which FCPA enforcement was paused, suspended, or dropped after January 20, 2025, with explanation
- Restore and expand DOJ's FCPA Unit; fund 100 additional enforcement attorneys
- Mandate that any future pause of FCPA enforcement for a specific matter requires written authorization from the Attorney General and immediate notification to Congress

**Sec. 3.3 — Attorney General Confirmation and Conduct Requirements**
- Require the Attorney General to testify before the Senate Judiciary Committee quarterly
- Create an Office of Attorney General Accountability, staffed by career officials, to track and publicly report on all cases where prosecutorial decisions deviate from career staff recommendations, with written justification

**Sec. 3.4 — Commission on DOJ Political Interference**
- Establish a bipartisan commission — modeled on the 9/11 Commission — to document DOJ politicization from 2025-2028; issue a public report; make criminal referrals where warranted

### IV.4 Title IV — Presidential Accountability Act

**Purpose:** Close the gaping accountability loopholes that allowed Trump and family to profit from the presidency with minimal legal consequence.

**Sec. 4.1 — Financial Disclosure and Blind Trust Requirements (By Statute)**
- Require all presidential and vice-presidential candidates to publicly disclose 10 years of tax returns within 30 days of accepting a major party nomination; require sitting president to disclose annually
- Require the President, Vice President, and Cabinet members to place all financial assets in independently managed blind trusts within 90 days of taking office; "blind" means no communication with trustees about portfolio composition
- Trustees must be approved by the Office of Government Ethics; OGE has authority to impose compliance and levy civil penalties
- Prohibit the President, Vice President, and their immediate family members from entering into new commercial contracts with foreign governments or state-owned enterprises while in office

**Sec. 4.2 — Emoluments Enforcement**
- Create a private right of action for Congress to enforce the Emoluments Clause; explicitly authorize Congressional standing to sue in federal court
- Direct DOJ to publish within 90 days all instances of potential Emoluments Clause violations identified by career staff from 2017-2021 and 2025-2029

**Sec. 4.3 — Anti-Nepotism Strengthening**
- Expand and codify anti-nepotism prohibitions: prohibit appointment of a president's immediate family member to any position, paid or unpaid, in the executive branch
- Close the "advisory" loophole used to bring Jared Kushner and others into the White House structure without formal appointment

**Sec. 4.4 — Pardon Reform**
- Prohibit a president from pardoning themselves, their family members, or any individual or entity in which they have a financial interest
- Require written public justification for any presidential pardon of a federal criminal conviction; DOJ OIG to audit all pardons within 180 days of grant

### IV.6 Title VI — The Anti-Corruption and Ethics Reform Package

**Sec. 6.1 — Congressional Stock Trading Ban**
- Ban members of Congress, their spouses, and dependent children from trading individual stocks, bonds, commodities, or derivatives
- Required divestiture within 180 days of taking office; assets must be placed in index funds, mutual funds, or blind trusts
- Violations: mandatory disgorgement of profits; civil penalty of 3x the profit; public disclosure; referral to ethics committee for additional sanction
- This provision has strong bipartisan public support (>70%); pursue as standalone bill eligible for 60-vote passage

**Sec. 6.2 — Revolving Door Strengthening**
- Extend "cooling off" period for senior executive branch officials lobbying their former agencies from 2 years to 5 years
- Extend to Members of Congress from 1 year to 3 years for House members; 2 years for Senators; 5 years for Chairmen and Ranking Members of relevant committees
- Prohibit revolving door in specific high-risk sectors: former financial regulators may not work for regulated financial institutions for 5 years; former defense officials may not work for defense contractors for 5 years

**Sec. 6.3 — Lobbying Transparency**
- Require quarterly rather than semi-annual lobbying disclosure reports
- Require disclosure of "lobbying-adjacent" activities: coalition coordination, PR campaigns, and "grassroots" organizing funded by corporate interests
- Require disclosure of foreign government lobbying arrangements within 10 days (currently up to 90 days)
- Create an online, searchable, machine-readable database of all lobbying disclosures

**Sec. 6.4 — The STOCK Act 2.0**
- Close existing STOCK Act loopholes: blind trust exemption, family member trading exemption, 45-day filing delay
- Reduce disclosure deadline from 45 days to 48 hours for all trades
- Grant SEC enforcement authority over STOCK Act violations (currently self-policing)
- Public dashboard of all Congressional trades updated weekly

### IV.7 Title VII — Campaign Finance Reform

This is the hardest legislative terrain — Citizens United (2010) is Supreme Court precedent that cannot be overridden by statute without a constitutional amendment. But significant statutory action is possible within it.

**Sec. 7.1 — DISCLOSE Act 2.0**
- Require full public disclosure of all donors to organizations spending $10,000 or more on federal election-related communications, including 501(c)(4) dark money groups
- Apply to all spending within 120 days of a federal election
- Require real-time online disclosure (within 24 hours) for expenditures over $10,000
- The DISCLOSE Act has passed the House multiple times; its key obstacle is the 60-vote threshold in the Senate — pursue via voting rights filibuster carve-out or bipartisan negotiation

**Sec. 7.2 — Small Dollar Democracy Matching Program**
- Create a federal 6:1 matching program for small donations to federal candidates (donations up to $200 matched at 6:1 by federal funds)
- Funded by closure of carried interest loophole (which is itself long-overdue tax reform)
- Effect: amplifies the political voice of small donors; reduces candidates' dependence on major donors; makes small-dollar fundraising more competitive with big-dollar bundling

**Sec. 7.3 — Super PAC Coordination Prohibition**
- Strengthen legal prohibitions on coordination between campaigns and Super PACs
- Create FEC enforcement unit specifically for coordination; restore FEC to full 6-member bipartisan composition with mandatory vacancy replacement rules
- Require Super PACs to wait 60 days (up from 0) before running ads featuring or mentioning a candidate who has not yet declared

**Sec. 7.4 — Foreign Money in Elections**
- Strengthen enforcement and penalties for foreign election spending
- Extend foreign money prohibitions explicitly to "straw donor" arrangements where foreign funds are laundered through domestic entities
- Require social media platforms to verify the citizenship/residency of all political ad purchasers

**Sec. 7.5 — The Constitutional Amendment Strategy**
- Formally introduce and begin building ratification path for a "Democracy Amendment" to the Constitution overturning Citizens United
- Language: "Congress and the States may limit or prohibit campaign contributions and expenditures by corporations, unions, and other artificial entities, and may distinguish between natural persons and other entities for purposes of election law."
- 28 states have already passed resolutions calling for such an amendment; target of 34 states needed for a convention call is achievable within a decade with sustained effort
- This is a long-horizon project but begins immediately: every piece of the short-term reform agenda builds the political case for the constitutional fix

### IV.8 Title VIII — Voting Rights and Democracy Restoration Act

This is the crown jewel of the democracy agenda — and the one most dependent on the filibuster carve-out strategy from Chapter 5.

**Sec. 8.1 — John Lewis Voting Rights Advancement Act**
- Restore and modernize the Voting Rights Act's Section 5 preclearance requirement, with a new coverage formula that survives Shelby County
- New coverage formula: any jurisdiction that has had two or more voting rights violations in the past 25 years is subject to preclearance
- Creates an administrative preclearance process at DOJ that is fast (60-day review period) and transparent
- Extends preclearance to cover: voter ID laws, polling place changes, early voting reductions, voter roll purges, and district line changes

**Sec. 8.2 — Automatic Voter Registration**
- National automatic voter registration: all U.S. citizens who interact with any federal agency (DMV-style registration through federal interfaces) are automatically registered to vote unless they opt out
- States may not impose additional requirements as a condition of voting in federal elections
- Creates a federal voter registration database that states may use but may not substitute their own more restrictive list

**Sec. 8.3 — Election Access Standards**
- Minimum federal standards for federal elections:
  - At least 15 days of early voting, including two weekends
  - No-excuse absentee voting
  - Same-day voter registration at polling places
  - Polling places may not be closed without replacement within 1/4 mile and DOJ approval
  - Mail ballot postmark standards (ballots postmarked by Election Day count if received within 7 days)
- Election Day as a federal holiday: codify in statute

**Sec. 8.4 — Voter ID: National Standard**
- Establish a federal free voter ID available to all U.S. citizens at no cost, available at any federal facility
- Voter ID accepted for federal elections must include the federal ID or any of the following: driver's license, passport, utility bill, bank statement, government check, or sworn affidavit of identity
- Prohibit states from requiring forms of ID that are disproportionately held by one demographic group without providing free alternatives

**Sec. 8.5 — Anti-Gerrymandering**
- Require all states to use independent redistricting commissions for Congressional and state legislative redistricting after the 2030 Census
- Commission criteria: (1) compactness; (2) contiguity; (3) preservation of existing political subdivisions; (4) compliance with VRA; (5) competitiveness where consistent with above
- Federal judicial enforcement: any district map drawn in violation of commission requirements subject to federal court review and remedy
- This is the structural fix for the manufactured House and state legislative majorities that have systematically distorted representation

**Sec. 8.6 — Washington DC and Puerto Rico Statehood**
- Admit Washington DC as the 51st state: DC residents pay federal taxes; serve in the military; are governed by federal laws; they deserve full representation
- Puerto Rico: authorize a binding referendum on statehood, independence, or enhanced commonwealth status; provide admission for statehood if the referendum produces a majority for it; this is the constitutionally and politically correct path — Puerto Ricans should decide

**Sec. 8.7 — Ranked Choice Voting for Federal Elections**
- Require ranked choice voting in all federal primary elections (presidential, Senate, House)
- Allow states to implement ranked choice voting in federal general elections and provide federal support for implementation
- Rationale: RCV reduces the spoiler effect, enables more competitive primaries, increases voter choice, and tends to produce more moderate winners who can win broad coalitions

### IV.9 Title IX — Government Service Delivery Modernization Act
*(The Democratic Alternative to DOGE — efficiency and accountability through democratic means)*

DOGE's appeal before its catastrophic implementation was real: government is too slow, too confusing, and too hostile to navigate. Democrats cannot simply run against DOGE without offering a competing vision of what efficient, accountable, user-friendly government actually looks like. This section is that vision.

**Sec. 9.1 — IRS Direct File (Universal)**
- Pre-fill tax returns for the ~80% of Americans with uncomplicated tax situations: the IRS already receives all the data it needs. Make IRS Direct File universal, free, and permanent by statute — eliminating the $200-$300 per-return toll that tax preparation companies charge working families.
- Estimated savings to American taxpayers: $25B+ annually across the 100M+ households currently paying tax preparers.
- Codify in statute to prevent future executive reversal.

**Sec. 9.2 — Automatic Benefits Enrollment**
- An estimated $80B+ in annual federal benefits goes unclaimed because people don't know they qualify or give up on applications.
- Require automatic enrollment: use existing government data to proactively enroll Americans in SNAP, Medicaid, CHIP, EITC, housing assistance, and LIHEAP without separate applications.
- Create a unified benefits eligibility engine: privacy-protected cross-agency data-sharing that triggers enrollment across programs from a single interaction.
- Fund 10,000 community-based "No Wrong Door" navigators at libraries, health centers, schools, and social services offices.

**Sec. 9.3 — Single Federal Digital Identity**
- Restore and expand Login.gov as the universal, privacy-respecting credential for all federal and participating state services.
- Mandate Login.gov interoperability across all federal agencies; end duplicative identity systems.
- Fund state adoption: 50% federal match for states integrating Login.gov into benefits administration.

**Sec. 9.4 — VA Modernization**
- 90-day standard for all VA disability claims decisions; 12-month appeals resolution.
- Complete the electronic health record modernization; fund 500 new community-based outpatient clinics in underserved rural and tribal areas.
- Zero-wait Veterans Crisis Line, fully staffed 24/7.

**Sec. 9.5 — Government Technology Modernization Fund**
- $50B over 10 years for legacy system replacement, cybersecurity hardening, and user experience redesign across federal benefits systems.
- Restore and permanently fund the United States Digital Service with a statutory charter and $500M annual budget.
- Every major federal benefits system meets modern accessibility and usability standards within 5 years.

**Sec. 9.6 — Civil Service Genuine Modernization**
*(Protect the civil service AND reform it — not just restore the pre-Schedule F status quo)*

The pre-Schedule F civil service had real dysfunctions that must be addressed alongside the protections:

- **Hiring speed:** Federal hiring currently takes 6-18 months. Target 30-day hiring for most positions through: streamlined background checks, automated skills-based screening, expanded direct hire authority for STEM, AI, cybersecurity, and healthcare specialties.
- **Pay reform:** Create market-rate pay bands for technical specialties where federal salaries are uncompetitive at the mid-career level; allow agencies to pay market rates without case-by-case OPM approval.
- **Performance management:** Transparent standards, coaching requirements, genuine consequences for documented poor performance — so that civil service protections shield against political firing, not against accountability.
- **Skills-based classification:** Move toward competency-based bands that allow lateral movement and rapid response to emerging workforce needs, away from the rigid GS position-classification system.
- **Rotational and exchange programs:** Fund government-university and government-industry rotational programs; bring private-sector and academic talent into government on defined terms and return government talent to the wider world with enhanced skills.

### IV.10 Title X — Drug Policy Reform

**Sec. 10.1 — Federal Marijuana Descheduling Act**
- Remove marijuana from Schedule I of the Controlled Substances Act; deschedule entirely as a federal matter and allow states to regulate as they see fit.
- 70%+ of Americans support marijuana legalization; the current federal Schedule I status is scientifically indefensible (Schedule I requires "no accepted medical use") and serves primarily to maintain the criminal penalties that fall disproportionately on Black Americans.

**Sec. 10.2 — Federal Marijuana Conviction Expungement**
- Direct DOJ to identify all federal marijuana convictions and automatically expunge those that would not be criminal under the new descheduling law.
- Fund state expungement assistance: grants to state court systems to process mass expungements of state marijuana convictions.
- Black Americans are arrested for marijuana at 3.73x the rate of white Americans despite similar usage rates; expungement is a targeted racial justice measure.

**Sec. 10.3 — Federal Harm Reduction Act**
- End the federal prohibition on using federal funds for syringe exchange programs: proven cost-effective public health intervention; the current ban has been periodically lifted and re-imposed with no public health justification for the ban.
- Fund naloxone distribution at scale: dramatically expand access to naloxone (Narcan) at pharmacies, community centers, schools, and through community health workers.
- Repeal the "crack house" statute (21 U.S.C. § 856) as applied to harm reduction sites: allow states and localities that choose to operate supervised consumption sites to do so without federal prosecution.
- Shift the federal framing from "war on drugs" to "public health approach to substance use disorder": fund treatment, recovery support, and harm reduction at least commensurate with enforcement spending.

### IV.11 Title XI — Supreme Court Reform

As argued in Chapter 5, Supreme Court reform is a second-term or late-first-term initiative. But the legislative groundwork begins immediately.

**Sec. 9.1 — Supreme Court Ethics and Transparency Act (Year 1)**
- Create a binding Code of Conduct for Supreme Court Justices, administered by a newly created Inspector General for the Federal Judiciary
- Prohibit: undisclosed gifts, travel, or income from parties with pending cases; failure to recuse in the face of an identified conflict of interest; acceptance of speaking fees from political organizations
- Require annual financial disclosure equivalent to lower court judges (currently SCOTUS Justices file disclosures with far less detail)
- The IG for the Federal Judiciary may refer violations to a Judicial Conduct Committee with authority to publicly censure; Congress may impeach for serious violations
- This provision is achievable with bipartisan support given the documented ethics scandals of the Thomas era

**Sec. 9.2 — Term Limits (Year 2-3)**
- Establish 18-year active service terms for Supreme Court Justices by statute: after 18 years, Justices become "Senior Justices" who may sit on lower courts and fill in for recusals but no longer participate in regular SCOTUS business
- One new Justice appointed every two years; President nominates, Senate confirms
- Constitutional argument: life tenure under Article III means the office exists for life, but Congress can structure the Court's composition; Senior Justice status preserves life tenure while regularizing the appointment process
- This provision will face immediate legal challenge; prepare comprehensive legal defense and accept that it may take several years to reach SCOTUS — by which time the political case will have been further developed

**Sec. 9.3 — Court Expansion (Year 3-4, conditional)**
- Expand the Supreme Court from 9 to 13 Justices: this restores one Justice per Federal Circuit, a historically grounded basis
- This is the most politically costly provision and should be pursued only if: (a) the ethics reforms and term limits have been blocked by the Court itself; (b) the Court has issued additional decisions that demonstrate the consequences of the manufactured conservative supermajority; (c) the political conditions support it
- Court expansion is not inherently wrong — FDR was right that a Court that strikes down democratically enacted law due to ideological composition, not constitutional text, is itself a form of judicial override of democracy — but the timing and political conditions must be right

OGE lost leadership independence under Trump. Restore:

- Reform FEC governance: require that any enforcement matter that achieves majority support moves forward, regardless of partisan deadlock (breaking the deadlock-by-default model)
- Create a mandatory 24-month rotation policy so no single commissioner serves as chair indefinitely

## PART VIII: MAKING DEMOCRACY REFORM DURABLE

The deepest irony of democracy reform is that it requires democracy to be working well enough to pass it. The strategy for durability:

1. **Codify everything in statute.** Executive orders protecting civil servants and IGs are revocable. Statutes are not (short of repeal with 60 votes or subsequent majority). Every EO in this chapter has a companion bill.

2. **Build bipartisan support where it exists.** The IG firing pattern alarmed Republican institutionalists — Grassley, Collins — who understand that a weaponized government can be weaponized against them next. The narrowest, least partisan provisions (stock trading ban, IG independence, FOIA) should be pursued as bipartisan wins to build the norm before pursuing the more contested items.

3. **State-level embedding.** Work with Democratic state legislatures and governors to pass state-level ethics, civil service protection, and voting rights legislation that embeds reforms below the federal level and makes them harder to reverse from Washington.

4. **Public accountability infrastructure.** A public-facing dashboard of: IG investigations and outcomes; political appointee financial disclosures; lobbying spend by issue area; campaign contribution flows; voting rights compliance by state. Sunlight is the most durable protection against corruption.

5. **The constitutional long game.** The DISCLOSE Act, the Democracy Amendment, DC statehood, and court reform are long-horizon projects. Begin them in Year 1; do not expect to finish them in Year 1. The point is to build the record, develop the political consensus, and create the legal infrastructure for reforms that will take a decade to fully achieve.

## PART IX: ACCOUNTABILITY FOR WHAT HAPPENED

A question the governance plan must address: should a Democratic administration pursue legal accountability for the corruption and abuses of the Trump years? This is genuinely difficult terrain.

**The case for accountability:**
- The rule of law requires that it apply equally; if DOGE's data access violated federal law, those violations should be prosecuted regardless of who is responsible
- Failing to pursue accountability for documented lawbreaking creates a precedent that presidents and their allies are above the law
- The public deserves to know the full scope of what happened to their data, their government, and their institutions

**The case for caution:**
- A Democratic DOJ aggressively prosecuting Republican officials will be characterized as "weaponizing the DOJ" — the exact charge Democrats leveled at Trump — regardless of the merits
- Prosecution decisions should be made by career prosecutors on the evidence, not by political leadership setting targets
- The political capital consumed by high-profile accountability prosecutions may crowd out the affirmative agenda

**The framework's position:**

Restore DOJ independence and let career prosecutors follow the evidence. The Democratic political leadership should neither direct nor discourage accountability investigations. The institutional posture should be: we are restoring the rule of law, and the rule of law will be applied without political direction. That is distinct from both "prosecute them all" and "look forward, not backward."

The commission models (DOJ Political Interference Commission, DOGE Audit) provide public accountability and historical record without requiring criminal prosecution as the only accountability mechanism. These should be pursued in parallel with the DOJ restoration.
