## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This second audit covers the three chapters written since the first audit (Economic Fairness, Climate & Energy, Foreign Policy & Defense) plus the two new structural documents (Master EO Inventory, Master Governance Calendar). It also checks whether the first audit's corrections propagated cleanly.

**Overall assessment:** The new chapters are substantively strong and politically well-calibrated. However, the process of writing detailed chapters has — as with healthcare — produced more precise cost estimates that exceed the Chapter 2 framework totals in two domains. This is a recurring pattern: Chapter 2's allocations are based on earlier estimates; detailed chapters correct them upward. The cumulative effect is that the revised spending total is now approximately **$9.0 trillion**, not the $8.25 trillion shown in Chapter 2's revised scorecard.

Additionally, a direct procedural conflict exists between Chapter 8 and the Master Governance Calendar on the placement of the carbon price, and the Civilian Climate Corps cost has tripled between Chapter 2 and Chapter 8 with no reconciliation.

This audit identifies **four categories of findings** requiring action, plus a fifth category of ongoing substantive gaps.

## SECTION B: PROCEDURAL AND STRUCTURAL CONFLICTS

### B.1 Carbon Price Bill Placement Conflict (High Priority)

**The conflict:** Chapter 8's fiscal table places the carbon price and dividend in the **FY2031 Fairness Reconciliation Bill**. The Master Governance Calendar places it in the **FY2030 Building Bill** (the calendar states "Carbon price enacted (or closest achievable via reconciliation)" as a Building Bill accomplishment).

**The resolution:** Chapter 8 is correct. The FY2031 placement is right for three reasons:
1. The IRA restoration (FY2030 Building Bill) should demonstrate clean energy investment success before adding a carbon price signal
2. The carbon price is politically harder — it benefits from the IRA's success creating a constituency for climate action before asking voters to accept higher energy prices
3. The carbon price pairs naturally with wealth tax revenue in a "Structural Year" bill; both are ambitious Year 3 actions

**What needs to change:** Update the Master Governance Calendar to move carbon price from FY2030 Building Bill accomplishments to FY2031 Fairness Bill accomplishments. Also update Chapter 5's reconciliation table to explicitly list carbon price in the Fairness Bill.

### B.2 Carbon Price Absent from Chapter 5 Reconciliation Table (Medium)

Chapter 5's revised reconciliation table lists the Fairness Bill (FY2031) as containing: "Wealth tax; capital gains reform; FTT; drug price negotiation expansion; long-term care insurance; housing investment." The carbon price is not listed — an omission that should be corrected since it is the most consequential provision in Chapter 8.

**What needs to change:** Add "Carbon price and dividend" to the Fairness Bill entry in Chapter 5's reconciliation table.

### B.3 Chapter Numbering/Ordering Error (Low Priority, Cosmetic)

Chapter 8's closing line reads: *"Next chapters: Chapter 9 — Messaging & Communications | Chapter 10 — Foreign Policy & Defense"*

In practice, Foreign Policy became Chapter 9 and Messaging & Communications has not yet been written. The chapter that will be written next (Messaging & Communications) will be Chapter 10, and the document naming convention is now off.

**What needs to change:** Correct Chapter 8's closing reference and establish clear numbering: Ch9 = Foreign Policy (already written), Ch10 = Messaging & Communications (pending).

## SECTION C: FACTUAL CLAIMS REQUIRING FLAGS

### C.1 SCOTUS Case Name: *Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump* (Medium)

Chapter 7 cites the IEEPA SCOTUS ruling as "*Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump*." The user confirmed the ruling occurred in February 2026, but the specific case name was not confirmed. Learning Resources Inc. was indeed among the first companies to file suit against the IEEPA tariffs in 2025. Whether their case is the one that produced the SCOTUS ruling, or whether another case was consolidated or superseded it, should be verified.

**What needs to change:** Add a bracketed note: "[Case name to verify — IEEPA tariff ruling confirmed by user; confirm specific case name before publication]." Do not remove the reference.

### C.2 $166B vs. $175B Tariff Revenue Inconsistency (Medium)

Chapter 7 uses two different tariff revenue figures in close proximity:
- "$166 billion from more than 330,000 businesses in IEEPA tariffs that the Supreme Court found unconstitutional" — this refers specifically to IEEPA tariff revenue
- "$175 billion in extra government revenues was a tax on Americans" — this appears to refer to total tariff revenue including non-IEEPA tariffs

Both figures may be accurate if they refer to different things, but the passage doesn't make the distinction clear, creating potential confusion. The $166B is IEEPA-specific (the unconstitutional portion subject to refund); the $175B appears to be the broader tariff revenue figure.

**What needs to change:** Clarify in Chapter 7: "The government estimated that it collected $166 billion specifically from IEEPA tariffs now subject to refund. Total tariff revenues across all mechanisms ran approximately $175 billion — all of it paid by American businesses and consumers, not by foreign countries."

### C.3 EV Credit Income Threshold Framing (Low Priority)

Chapter 8 says the EV tax credit expansion moves income eligibility "up from $150,000 single." The original IRA Section 30D had differentiated thresholds: $150,000 for single filers, $225,000 for head of household, and $300,000 for married filing jointly. The OBBBA eliminated the credit entirely rather than modifying thresholds. The Chapter 8 proposal to set a $200,000 threshold for all filers would actually represent an increase for single filers but a decrease for joint filers — this could face political resistance from two-income households.

**What needs to change:** Revise to: "Expand income eligibility: $200,000 for single filers (up from $150,000); $300,000 for married filing jointly (maintaining the original IRA threshold)." This is both more accurate and more politically defensible.

### C.4 Civilian Climate Corps Peak Enrollment vs. 2033 Benchmark Inconsistency (Low Priority)

Chapter 8 states the CCC target is "300,000 workers by Year 3; 400,000 at peak." But the 2033 win conditions table shows "300,000+" as the benchmark. If the program ramps to 400,000 by Year 4, the 2033 benchmark should be "350,000–400,000" to reflect the full ramp-up.

**What needs to change:** Update Chapter 8's win conditions table: Civilian Climate Corps enrollment: "350,000–400,000" rather than "300,000+."
