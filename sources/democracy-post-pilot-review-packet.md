## A. 2030 registration service: two paths plus the broader commitment

| Path | Service specification | Gate and reach limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Assisted floor | At an existing authorized Medicaid/marketplace contact, offer an official election application; provide lawful prefill/confirmation and accessible/offline help; accept/transmit and return a receipt; support county disposition/correction | Requires affirmative application. No claim that this equals automatic enrollment or reaches people without a contact. Existing duties continue during testing. |
| Conditional Oregon automatic | Transfer only qualifying, lawfully usable records through verified processes; provide the required notice/decline opportunity and signature completion; keep assisted exceptions | Require current federal/state permission, rules and production/test evidence. ORS 247.018 machinery/operative date alone does not establish approval or an operating service. |
| Broader DEM-1E | Extend lawful automatic service to the adopted benefit pathways as each becomes operational, with exception handling and a national resistance/service analysis | No national ready date or price proved. Staging that loses reach requires Espen’s express decision, not a technical relabeling of delivery. |

### Transaction specification for review

1. **Health service first.** Process health eligibility/enrollment independently of election choices. A decline, missing signature or election error must not block the health transaction.
2. **Identify the adult election applicant.** Do not infer citizenship or qualification from benefit eligibility or a child/spouse’s record. Determine the applicable jurisdiction, deadline, age and affirmation. Handling of a future-eligible minor needs a separate applicable-law rule.
3. **Source permission before fields.** Use a field-level inventory stating source, purpose, legal authority, confidence, retention and permitted recipient. Identity/address/citizenship/signature data are not interchangeable, and ordinary identifiers remain safeguarded. Restrict health/tax/immigration information from election payloads unless specifically lawful and necessary. Do not create an unrestricted cross-program adult file.
4. **Choose a lawful path.** Assisted application uses explicit adult confirmation and submission. The automatic path is gated on verified authority/process, not silence in an ordinary health form. Use lawful prefill where supported; separate legal purpose need not require typing every field twice.
5. **Decline and accessibility.** Give confidential decline, accessible/plain-language notices, interpretation, offline service and assisted exception handling. Staff training must explain that declining election service does not affect benefits.
6. **Receipt and disposition.** Issue a transaction reference/status that avoids revealing protected health data. Track receipt at election administration, acceptance, rejection/reason and correction. A transmitted record is not completed registration; portal activity is not reach.
7. **Signature and ballot readiness.** Identify missing signatures and support completion before relevant deadlines. Oregon’s conditional rule permits a registration record without the signature but does not assure a countable ballot. Do not report registration as ballot readiness.
8. **Correction and government error.** Notify the affected adult promptly through an authorized channel, support review and repair before the actual deadline, preserve audit evidence and prevent duplicate/deceased/incorrect-person records. Counsel must assess election, immigration and criminal exposure and government-error protection; no blanket immunity is presumed.
9. **Funding and continuity.** Price health-only, election-only and shared increments separately with a without-proposal baseline, allocation basis and continuing owner. Do not presume Medicaid matching, spare CAP capacity or the ONE contract’s continuation past June 2029. Every real cost remains unpriced here.
10. **Rollout gate.** Test representative lawful cases, accessibility and county exception workloads. A safe path can start earlier when ready; a simpler assisted path must not indefinitely delay a lawful automatic service. If only assistance is supported for 2030, return its reduced reach and proposed staging to Espen.

### Acceptance cases and failure handling

| Case | Required disposition to test | What remains to verify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eligible adult, complete data | Confirm lawful submission, receipt and county disposition | Actual interface and election deadlines |
| Benefit recipient not qualified to vote | No inferred election eligibility; protect health service; lawful notice/error handling | Exact qualification and government-error rules |
| Shared family account or child benefit | Separate adult record and confirmation where required | Custody, identity and proxy-access safeguards |
| Ex parte renewal / automatic payment | Do not count as an interactive offer or completed enrollment | Real channel volumes and unique-person denominators |
| Missing signature | Registration status distinct from ballot readiness; supported completion route | Oregon/current destination rules and completion evidence |
| Decline / language / disability / no internet | Confidential decline and equivalent accessible assistance | Real usability and offline completion tests |
| Transmission failure or county rejection | Receipt, traceable disposition and timely correction | County staffing, escalation and deadlines |
| State nonparticipation / refusal | Preserve existing duties and identify lawful service/enforcement alternatives | Federal/state authority, resources and remedy; no invented turnkey federal system |

**Measurements:** report contacts, unique qualified adults, offers, applications/automatic records, receipt, registration, signature readiness and timely correction as separate denominators. Report missingness, subgroup reach and people absent from contact channels. Do not infer turnout or election effects. Compare assisted and automatic delivered reach, error burden, correction and costs; illustrative transaction dollars are not cost-effectiveness estimates.

**Permission/operations evidence packet, unsent:** current CMS/OHA determinations and conditions; implementing rules/agreements; permitted fields/retention; interface tests and county dispositions; signature/notice completion; volumes by contact type; successor contract/work order; current workload and allocation method. Seek existing public records first. Sending requests or any email requires express permission.

## B. Parallel Title III prototype modules

**Drafting status:** the clauses below are working formulations to expose choices for specialist review. They are not bill-ready text, proposed final findings, an approved litigation position or a narrowing of adopted protection. No legal standard is deemed constitutional by this packet.

### R: restoration direction retained

**R-1, liability prototype:** A voting qualification, practice or procedure violates this provision where, in its actual social and electoral setting, it causes members of a protected racial or language-minority group to have less opportunity than other eligible voters to participate and elect representatives of their choice. The claimant must identify the challenged practice, affected voters, causal mechanism and a practicable lawful alternative. No right to proportional representation follows from this provision.

**Choices requiring review:** articulate the causal/totality test and relation to existing Section 2, not merely re-enact results wording. Separate vote-denial, dilution and districting applications. Test constitutional enforcement power, present-day record and geographic/application fit; identify where Article I supplies an additional ground for congressional elections. Do not treat all maps or jurisdictions alike.

**R-2, remedy prototype:** Upon a supported violation, a court shall identify the injury and adopt effective relief tailored to it, with findings on necessity, feasible alternatives, geographic reach and duration. A race-conscious remedial application requires its own constitutional analysis; this Act does not deem strict scrutiny satisfied.

**Choices requiring review:** narrow tailoring, race predominance, lawful alternative maps, election proximity, state cure opportunity, appeal/stay risk and whether the remedy actually operates before candidate filing.

### I: separable intent/enforcement protection

**I-1, independent claim prototype:** Independently of the effects-based provision, this Act prohibits intentional discrimination on protected racial or language-minority grounds in covered voting practices. Intent may be shown through admissible direct and circumstantial evidence, assessed with alternative explanations and the record as a whole.

**Choices requiring review:** exact defendants/practices, protected classifications, constitutional predicates and interaction with existing law. Disparity alone is not an irrebuttable intent presumption. Race and party cannot be treated as interchangeable.

**I-2, evidence/enforcement prototype:** Covered mapmaking authorities must preserve and disclose legally publishable proposed maps, criteria, underlying nonprotected data and explanations of departures. An affected person alleging a concrete injury under this Act and the Attorney General may seek relief through an expressly defined action. Courts may order expedited lawful production and tailored relief under the applicable jurisdiction and equitable standards.

**Choices requiring review:** privilege, immunity/abrogation, discovery limits and sanctions, standing, private action, defendants, venue, fees, three-judge jurisdiction and appeal. Fund public archives, plaintiff access to experts and continuing enforcement rather than assume existing appropriations cover them.

**Protection lost compared with R:** an effects-only injury may remain unremedied; proving intent imposes evidence, resource and delay burdens. Enacting I is not completion of restoration and does not authorize dropping R.

### C: congressional standards coordinated once with DEM-2

**C-1, specification prototype:** Congressional districting must follow a published hierarchy of lawful criteria, disclose proposed maps and nonprotected data, provide meaningful public review, and explain departures and rejected alternatives. Any statutory partisan-favoritism prohibition must define a manageable test and disclose the method and reasonable sensitivity checks.

**Choices requiring review:** concrete hierarchy, communities-of-interest definition and exceptions; purpose/material-effect versus neutral-ensemble outlier standards; political geography and election-data uncertainty. No mechanical racial quota, efficiency-gap threshold or single seat forecast is selected here.

**C-2, cure/backstop prototype:** After a judicial finding under the enacted congressional standards, provide a practicable state cure opportunity linked to the real election calendar and a defined judicial backstop on default. Preserve DEM-2A’s commissions and the express 2034 congressionally mandated redraw exception.

**Choices requiring review:** Article I authority, manageable standards, jurisdiction, timeline, appeal and map implementation. This is not a general state/local map code, a switch to DEM-2E’s voluntary safe harbor or a substitute for racial-discrimination remedies.

### S: separability and applications prototype

If a specified provision or application is held invalid, preserve independently lawful provisions/applications only to the extent they can function as enacted. Draft distinct liability, funding, enforcement and remedy authorities so that a court can assess actual independent operation. A severability clause does not bind a court to preserve a nonfunctional scheme.

| Injunction scenario | Potentially usable protection | Loss and unresolved dependency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Restoration effects test blocked | Independently valid I; independently valid congressional standards; unaffected Titles I/II/IV | Effects-only injuries lose the intended route; no assurance that shared remedy/funding survives |
| Race-conscious remedial application blocked | Other lawful relief and independently valid liability | A liability finding may lack an effective timely map remedy |
| Congressional standards blocked | Independently valid racial-discrimination provisions and DEM-2 applications | No assumed partisan-map remedy or general state-map authority |
| Enforcement/jurisdiction clause blocked | Only separately lawful causes, defendants and forums | A right without an available action/remedy is not equivalent protection |
| Late stay near filing/election | Lawful surviving duties and relief actually allowed by the court | No guaranteed redraw, emergency stay or new election; D1 scenario requires reassessment |

### Focused specialist questions and record

Ask specialists to mark each operative clause: authority, protected injury/person, elements/proof, defendant, forum, remedy, funding and independent operation. Request present-day supporting and contrary evidence, not invented legislative findings. Review current Callais and later controlling cases; the prior primary review was selected passages, not a comprehensive subsequent-case survey. Section 2 already has results language. Rucho recognizes a distinct congressional legislative role; it does not uphold these prototypes.

For each state’s 2031 process, work backward from mapmaking, candidate filing and election administration: data, state proposal, challenge, cure, appeal/stay, district coding and voter notice. None has a validated duration here. Price DOJ staff, experts, discovery, public data, technical review, state/local changes, notices and ongoing enforcement separately from matching funds and DEM-11/CAP.
