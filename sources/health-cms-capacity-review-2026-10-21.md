# Project 2050 — CMS capacity review
## Findings and proposal for Espen
**Review date:** October 21, 2026; updated after Espen's approval. **Status:** recommendations accepted for implementation; whole-surge timing/availability decision adopted. Agency sizing and all new administrative costs remain provisional/unpriced.
**Project baseline:** `fm58cn6g4d-stack/project-2050`, branch `claude/pensive-mendel-g1lwl5`, commit `3797741b409d3a986b2c2bba0ed81388af8f2b5b`.
**Scope:** CMS delivery requirements for HLT-1 through HLT-6, the CMS share of CAP-7S and CAP-7, implementation timing, funding boundaries and workforce sensitivity. The original findings use the pinned baseline below. Implementation now records the Oct 21 decision, a CMS annex/cost register, conditional R1 routing and a generated availability-versus-cash diagnostic; no new allocation amount is adopted.

## Recommendation

Keep the existing CMS allocation provisional. The evidence does not establish either that $50B is necessary or that it is excessive. It establishes that the present allocation is not yet a costed implementation plan.

Before resizing it, use the CMS implementation annex and work register to cost the workload and reconcile funding. Espen approved full multi-year funding for ALL surge agencies immediately on enactment, targeting R1 in FY2029. This supersedes the report's earlier framing of a separate extra CMS bridge appropriation. Price FY2029 use within the provisional surge and distinguish federal setup, continuing operations and state implementation. Preserve the adopted health benefits and dates as planning targets while making their delivery dependencies explicit.

The most important correction is to how the money is described: the project currently implies about **$90.8B in supplementary CMS capacity over 2030–39**, not just $50B. That combines the surge and the permanent increment. It is a project-derived estimate, not an official budget score.

## 1. Reviewed baseline amounts (cash schedule superseded as an availability rule)

At the reviewed baseline, the governing document set D9's accepted working figures: a $600B agency surge in FY2030–35 and a separate $490B sustained increase over FY2030–39. The capacity domain allocates CMS $50B of the surge and applies the same agency shares to both rows. These are increments above baseline, not CMS's total budget. The agency split is explicitly unsourced and provisional. [P1–P3]

| CMS component | Calculation | Amount | Basis |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| Surge, FY2030–35 | Existing allocation | $50.0B | Project estimate; not researched when allocated |
| Exact implied share | 50 / 600 | 8.333% | Derived; the displayed 8% is rounded |
| Sustained increment, FY2030–39 | 490 × 50 / 600 | $40.8B | Derived from approximate project totals |
| Combined increment, FY2030–39 | 50 + 40.833 | $90.8B | Derived, before overlap reconciliation |
| Annual sustained increment around FY2040 | 65 × 50 / 600 | $5.4B/year | Derived from approximate project GDP path |

Do not use the rounded 8% to allocate money: it would understate the CMS share. These 2030-39 amounts and the following annual path are the OLD cash proxy. Full multi-year availability moves to enactment under the Oct 21 decision. FY2029 cash is unpriced; once costed, restate the window rather than silently adding spending on top of the lifetime allocation.

The implied surge is approximately $5.83B, $10B, $10.83B, $10B, $8.33B and $5B across FY2030–35. Adding the sustained increment produces roughly **$14.9B of extra CMS capacity in FY2032**. This is a scale and absorption warning, not proof of waste. An agency cannot necessarily turn a large appropriation into working systems and trained staff on that schedule.

D4 distinguishes temporary investment from permanent operations. Recruitment backlogs, initial training and a finite system build can support the surge's return case. Recurring claims processing, call centers, hosting, audits and retained staff need continuing funding. A temporary appropriation should not conceal a permanent operating obligation.

## 3. What CMS must deliver

The following is a proposed operational decomposition of the existing package, not additional benefits. Program owners are proposed assignments; legislative and organizational responsibilities must be confirmed in drafting.

| Project item and target | Required capability | Proposed lead and partners | Main unresolved issue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| HLT-1: restoration for plan year 2030 | Eligibility and subsidy rule changes; state instructions; marketplace recalculation; consumer notices; enrollment assistance | CMS's marketplace and Medicaid teams; states; Treasury/IRS | A coverage-gap promise needs a defined federal or state delivery route; reversing rules is not enough |
| HLT-2: physician pay, $5,000 traditional-Medicare cap; vision/hearing 2031, dental from 2033 | Claims edits; beneficiary cost-sharing accumulators; coordination with other coverage; provider onboarding; benefit definitions; appeals | Medicare operations; contractors; SSA where premiums/enrollment require coordination | Define what counts toward the cap and how claims across providers accumulate; confirm provider participation |
| HLT-3: public option in 2031, automatic enrollment in 2032 | Plan administration; pricing; provider participation; claims and payment contracts; enrollment, billing and collections; assistance and appeals | CMS public-plan delivery team; marketplaces; Treasury/IRS; states | Medicare rates do not by themselves create a provider network or a functioning insurance plan |
| HLT-4: Medicaid/HCBS/safety net from 2030 | State plan and waiver work; eligibility and payment systems; quality oversight; HCBS readiness | CMS with state Medicaid agencies | Health centers, Title X and workforce programs involve other HHS functions; do not charge all delivery to CMS |
| HLT-5: hospital cap and commercial drug measures from 2031, with phase-ins | Applicable-price definitions; data submission; compliance; complaints; enforcement referrals | Proposed CMS/HHS role with Labor, Treasury and competition authorities | Self-funded employer coverage and antitrust measures require a jurisdictional crosswalk |
| HLT-6: MA savings, expanded negotiation and site-neutral payment | Payment rules; actuarial and clinical analysis; coding/data validation; contract changes; litigation support; price effectuation | Medicare payment, drug-pricing and integrity teams | Separate savings from statutory payment changes from savings that depend on auditing and recovery |

Existing Medicare claims and coordination functions already involve contractors and distinct units, as CMS describes [S8]. The proposal should reuse useful infrastructure rather than assume every function requires a new platform.

### Automatic enrollment needs an operating policy

Before procurement, settle:
- Who can be enrolled, from which data, and when; how to handle stale income, addresses and household composition.
- Whether the default plan has zero premiums for the person enrolled. If not, resolve payment collection and the consequences of nonpayment explicitly.
- Coordination with Medicaid, Medicare, employer coverage and state marketplaces, including duplicate coverage.
- Notice, correction, opt-out, appeal and coverage-continuity procedures.
- Data access authority, purpose limitations, security, accessible alternatives and an assisted path for people whose records do not match.

These are proposed design questions. Existing data-sharing arrangements do not automatically authorize every proposed exchange or enrollment use.

## 4. Evidence on implementation risks

**State eligibility operations.** GAO's September 2026 Medicaid report found weaknesses in CMS review and cross-state analysis of eligibility corrective action plans, with caseworker errors prominent in the material reviewed. Its work included seven selected states, so it is not an estimate of a nationwide staffing shortfall. The practical lesson is to fund training, workflow design and error correction alongside IT; an automated eligibility system is not a substitute for competent casework. [S4]

**Medicare Advantage savings.** GAO's July 2026 report found that CMS lacked a sufficiently detailed plan for expediting risk-adjustment data validation audits, including cost, staffing, timing and performance measures. HHS reported an acceleration initiative, but GAO still sought documentation. This supports a specific audit-delivery workstream, not an assumption that a larger agency budget automatically realizes all of HLT-6's savings. [S5]

**Consumer protection.** GAO's 2026 marketplace work identified weaknesses in consent verification, limits on agents' access and notification of changes. Its state-marketplace comparisons covered three selected marketplaces. For the proposed public option, enrollment integrity and easy consumer correction must be designed into delivery. Statutory automatic enrollment is a distinct activity from unauthorized broker enrollment; the latter is evidence of control needs, not an argument against the former. [S6]

**Acquisition.** GAO's historical Healthcare.gov review documented changing requirements, weak contract oversight and launch-readiness failures. Those findings are historical, not a claim that all those weaknesses persist today. Use them to justify modular delivery, a named integration owner, independent testing and early readiness reviews. [S7]

**State systems.** GAO's Medicaid IT review supports reuse and risk-based oversight, but its recommendation statuses show implementation progress through 2025. Do not present its 2020 findings as untouched current failures. [S9]

## 5. Preventing double counting

The health source documents and ledger input table do not explicitly decompose administrative expenses inside the title-level scores. That means overlap is **unresolved**, not zero. The go-big table also includes Medicare at 60 and older savings assumptions that D6 subsequently changed; the canonical health domain governs.

| Cost | Proposed accounting home | Boundary that must be verified |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Existing CMS activities and retained capability | Explicit current-services baseline | Do not finance the same baseline twice or assume it survives unchanged to 2029 |
| Finite federal build, initial conversion and backlog clearance | CMS portion of CAP-7S, to the extent not already in HLT scores | Register each work package and existing source of funding |
| Continuing federal operations and retained staff | CMS portion of CAP-7, or a clearly identified HLT administrative allowance | Assign each expense once; do not let surge spending mask permanent costs |
| State Medicaid systems and casework | HLT-1/HLT-4 administrative component and applicable Medicaid matching arrangements | Separate federal and state shares; subtract baseline and previously scored administration |
| Public-plan claims administration, premium functions and reserves | Explicit HLT-3 actuarial/administrative component, reconciled with CAP | Do not assume the $1.9T midpoint includes or excludes these costs |
| Health centers, workforce and other HHS administration | Relevant HLT-4 delivery agency | Avoid treating CMS as the whole health department |
| Medicare integrity and drug-pricing capacity | Relevant existing mandatory/discretionary accounts plus incremental HLT-6/CAP need | Map unspent authority, allowable purpose and expiration before claiming additional need |
| Revenue agency interfaces, SSA interfaces, general state-capacity grants | Respective domain allocations, with shared interface responsibilities | Check CAP-7's IRS/SSA allocations and CAP-13 before adding another shared-service charge |

Medicaid guidance describes enhanced federal matching for qualifying systems: 90% for design/development and 75% for operations, subject to conditions. These are federal contributions, not free resources, and eligibility does not extend automatically to every public-option or state staffing cost. [S10]

**Illustration, not a cost estimate:** if an approved state-system project costs $1B and qualifies entirely for a 90% federal match, record $0.9B federal and $0.1B state—not $1B of federal CAP funding plus another $0.9B of federal Medicaid funding.

A reusable cost register should record: work package; responsible organization; existing funding/account; included HLT score; gross new need; federal/state share; price year; obligation and outlay years; recurring versus temporary expense; and the one ledger row that owns the net increment.

## 6. Fix the sequence before resizing the envelope

The ledger inputs assume HLT-1/4/6 enactment in April 2029, HLT-2 in October 2029 and HLT-3/5 in February 2030. Those are model assumptions, not guaranteed enactment dates. The old cash proxy starts in FY2030; it no longer restricts when funding is available. The health domain targets restoration in 2030, public-option coverage in 2031 and automatic enrollment in 2032. [P2–P5]

FY2030 starts October 1, 2029. That exposed a preparation gap under the old design. **Decided by Espen, Oct 21:** all CAP-7S funding is multi-year and immediately available on enactment, targeting R1 in FY2029. Availability, obligations and cash outlays must be tracked separately. A January 2030 plan year still requires preparation before it begins.

| Proposed preparation stage | Output | Release/readiness condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2027–28 opposition preparation | Draft authorities, benefit specifications, architecture alternatives and data inventory | Planning only; no assumption that opposition staff can procure or operate federal systems |
| Early FY2029 implementation after legal authority/funding | Accountable delivery leadership; hiring/acquisition teams; state readiness review; HLT-1 conversion plan | Use immediately available multi-year CAP-7S funding; cost FY2029 use within the provisional allocation; R1 eligibility checks pending |
| Remainder of 2029 | Subsidy/eligibility changes tested; state instructions; contractor orders; notices and assisted service ready | End-to-end payment and enrollment tests, including non-digital paths |
| 2030 | Operate restoration; build and test public option and Medicare changes | No dependence on completing nationwide auto-enrollment to deliver restoration |
| Before plan year 2031 | Public plan's payment, participation, service and appeal functions ready | Independent operational readiness assessment |
| Before automatic enrollment in 2032 | Data/eligibility matching, notice, correction and duplicate-coverage controls ready | Measured error and coverage-continuity performance |
| Before dental phase-in in 2033 | Benefits, claims, providers and assistance ready | Access and payment tests; preserve the adopted benefit rather than treating delay as a hidden saving |
| FY2034–35 closeout | Convert builds to steady-state operations; document remaining backlog and asset costs | Explicit operating budget for retained capability |

**No FY2029 cash amount is scored here.** The decision is immediate multi-year availability of the whole surge, not an extra bridge budget. FY2029 cash falls outside the default 2030–39 presentation and must be disclosed separately. Reallocate the lifetime cash path when priced; do not normalize away pre-window costs. The availability decision adopts no new borrowing-envelope amount. Reconcile actual cash, authority and D4 treatment before declaring accelerated-path fiscal tests complete. A readiness review should trigger early remediation and reporting, not silently amend adopted benefit dates or establish indefinite executive discretion to withhold benefits.

## 7. Preliminary staffing sensitivity, not a staffing recommendation

The sources establish that CMS uses multiple personnel and contracting channels. They do not supply effort estimates for this particular bill. A defensible statutory headcount floor cannot yet be calculated.

To make the next costing exercise concrete, test an **illustrative** incremental federal workforce of 1,500, 2,500 or 3,500 FTE at an assumed fully loaded $250,000–$350,000 per FTE-year in a common 2030 price basis.

| Additional FTE | Annual employment cost at $250K | Annual employment cost at $350K |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1,500 | $0.375B | $0.525B |
| 2,500 | $0.625B | $0.875B |
| 3,500 | $0.875B | $1.225B |

These are proposal assumptions and arithmetic, not federal salary data, contractor bids or an agency workforce forecast. They exclude new claims-processing contracts, state staff, software delivery, hosting and beneficiary services. They must not be added to contract estimates that already include the same personnel or overhead. Funding the federal staff alone would not explain the existing roughly $5.4B annual sustained CMS increment; a continuing-operations bill of work is essential.

Build the workforce estimate by function: public-plan operations; Medicare benefits and provider access; Medicaid/state support; MA payment integrity; drug-pricing analysis; consumer assistance/appeals; engineering, acquisition, security and legal support. Identify where baseline staff can carry the work and where they cannot. Prefer permanent government ownership of requirements, contracts, data and clinical/actuarial decisions, with contracts for defined services and an exit/recompetition plan.

## 8. Funding durability and D4

A statutory staffing floor is not, by itself, a complete funding mechanism. GAO's appropriations-law guidance distinguishes a direction/authorization from actual budget authority. The project should pair any floor with explicit funding and scope rather than claim that a floor alone prevents hollowing-out. This is an analytical caution; the enforceability of a specific floor needs counsel's review. [S11]

The preferred design to evaluate is a finite multi-year appropriation for the rebuild plus a durable continuing appropriation for named operating functions, consistent with D4 and the standing design rules. This review does **not** certify Byrd eligibility, committee jurisdiction, appropriations treatment or legal drafting. CAP-7's own instrument question remains open.

Keep Medicare HI, SMI, general-fund costs, state shares and fee collections separate. Do not sweep HI reserves to cover a CMS operating need. Do not book speculative fraud recoveries or generalized productivity gains as new pay-fors; reconcile any recognized HLT-6 savings with the existing score instead of counting them twice.

Because D4 passes narrowly in the project baseline, any **net increase** in continuing capacity must be tested in FY2040 and later, even if the ten-year balance appears comfortable. Reallocating within the provisional CAP envelope still needs a demonstration that the agency losing funds can deliver its responsibilities.

## 9. Approved direction and remaining decisions

Espen accepted the recommendations and next steps on Oct 21, with the following timing correction. Implementation changes follow; numerical costing and unresolved design/legal questions do not become adopted merely through this approval:
1. **Keep the $50B surge and implied permanent share provisional**, with no cut, increase or release claimed as a saving from this review.
2. **Require a CMS implementation annex covering all six HLT titles**, rather than describing CMS's job mainly as auto-enrollment and dental/vision/hearing.
3. **Make all CAP-7S funding multi-year and immediately available on enactment, targeting R1 in FY2029.** Cost each agency's FY2029 use within its provisional allocation, disclose it separately and reconcile D4. No new dollar amount, extra envelope or blanket reconciliation certification is adopted.
4. **Separate the federal and state budgets and reconcile embedded HLT administration** before adopting a new allocation.
5. **Use dated readiness milestones and independent testing**, with corrective action that preserves the adopted policy targets.
6. **Treat permanent funding design and any staffing floor as separate drafting questions**; do not assume a headcount mandate alone secures operations.
