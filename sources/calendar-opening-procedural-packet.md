## D2: specify the mechanism before estimating the clock

The adopted direction is F: 60, 57, 54 and then a majority, with ten guaranteed amendments per side and equal time. It is a first-Senate-week action. The repository has this policy specification and a dated decision brief; it does not contain a complete, verified adoption resolution and implementation precedent. The external Claude options artifact was not retrieved for this packet. A summary of Block 3 is not a substitute for its operative text.

Under the published standing rules, changing the rules and ending debate on a rules change raise different thresholds from ordinary legislation. D2's majority-precedent route therefore needs a concrete ruling/appeal sequence, alongside the resolution and minority protections. It cannot be modeled as simply introducing a resolution and obtaining an ordinary majority vote. The linked rules edition is the 117th Congress edition; counsel must check subsequent precedents and standing orders. [P1]

| Continuity | Define scope after failed rounds, extended votes or a disputed amendment | A five-sitting simulation convention does not settle these rules |

The pre-inauguration vote count deserves its own test. Congressional terms begin January 3 and the new vice president takes office January 20. Assuming all other senators oppose, a 51-seat majority with one holdout has only 50 supporters and cannot count on the incoming vice president in week one. A 52-seat majority with one holdout has 51 supporters; a 53-seat majority with two has 51; a 54-seat majority with three has 51. These are arithmetic examples, not predictions about senators. Abstentions and the actual parliamentary question can change the count. Do not conceal this issue inside a generic “survives at 51” tag.

**Smallest decision needed:** retain the approved first-week target and commission the exact adoption text/precedent packet. Obtain member-level support for each critical question before describing D2 as secured. A later route requires an explicit recovery decision, not an unnoticed model reschedule.

A provision that funds an institution is not automatically eligible merely because rebuilding capacity is important. The immediate R1 companion route for ineligible rules must be identified without claiming that its time is zero. These are text-specific reviews, not a finding that the package is ineligible. ### Working sequence, with decision checkpoints

- **Transition through January 2:** negotiate common text, instruction map, eligibility issues, draft scores and delivery milestones. Private preparation does not exercise the future administration's authority.
- **January 3–9:** organization and D2 route; budget work and House preparation concurrently. Verify the pre-inauguration rules votes.
- **January 10–February 2:** advance the resolution and committee products, resolve scores and parliamentary issues, and complete common text. R1 uses its own privileged route rather than waiting for D2.
- **February 3–16:** preserve the weeks-3–4 signature preference. Complete necessary chamber returns; distinguish an announced signed benefit from an implemented one.
- **By February 16 if unfinished:** identify the exact critical-path obstruction, recovery votes and effect on March work. This is a proposed management checkpoint, not a new policy deadline.
- **March:** retain R1's outer completion objective and DEM's separate floor-start requirement. A March 1 recovery plan should expose any conflict and named displacement. Other ready priorities can still move.

May–June R1 is a damaged-delivery scenario: it delays legal authority, consumes preparation time for benefits, overlaps summer R2 and strains the normal pairing with DEM. It cannot be treated as an equally good opening plan. No calendar automatically causes a one-year delay in every benefit; each delivery path must be recalculated.

The floor-start objective is March 2029 at latest, normally after early R1 signature and benefits announcement. ## Historical check: measure the right interval

Official records establish this limited ARP trace: Senate budget-resolution adoption on February 5, 2021; motion to proceed to H.R.1319 on March 4 at 2:35 p.m.; Senate passage on March 6 at 12:12 p.m.; House agreement March 10; signature March 11. The first two Senate bill events are 45 hours 37 minutes apart, and resolution adoption to signature spans 34 calendar days. These are elapsed intervals, not active floor hours or a template for 2029. They refute treating a statutory debate ceiling as the whole schedule, while showing that a bill's final floor phase can be much shorter than total preparation. [P4–P8]

`index/tools/calendar/evidence-check.py` reproduces the date arithmetic. No fitted duration distribution or replacement of the model's new hour ranges is justified by this one episode. Obtain daily proceedings and time agreements before deriving an active-hour estimate.
