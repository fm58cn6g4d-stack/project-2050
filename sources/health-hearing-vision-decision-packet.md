## Decision brief

**Recommendation: do not put January-2030 hearing/vision into R1 on the present evidence. Preserve rapid R1 and the adopted 2031 fallback.** Early R1 remains a conditional route if complete benefit text, a bill-specific score/pay-for, instructions and an executable CMS plan already exist before text freeze. It must not become a reason to delay February-preferred/March-latest R1. The earlier report's conditional preference for R1 is qualified by an existing payment route for MA enrollees; early authority is valuable preparation time, not a demonstrated legal prerequisite for 2030 access.

If Espen elects to pursue January 2030 after the missing specifications are resolved, **test summer R2 with the existing Original-Medicare bridge first**, alongside an explicit MA-contract alternative. The bridge can separate access from the annual MA cycle, but leaves split payment, supplier access and cost-sharing problems. Do not select it merely because it exists. Express MA transition becomes preferable only if its payment/contract machinery can actually be completed more reliably than that split system. Neither summer route is ready for a go decision now. No exams-only or reduced-device substitute is recommended.

Carry forward 2029 restoration component by component. Prospective restriction repeal, in-year premium assistance and operating support through existing channels have stronger timing foundations than nationwide case repair, a new gap backstop or new HCBS slots. ## 1. Benefit rule sheet: what is fixed and what must be chosen

The adopted direction includes full hearing and vision, with no Medicare eligibility expansion. **The sources do not specify a complete priced benefit.** These fields cannot responsibly be filled with invented defaults. Each open branch below must be resolved in the same text for all three acceleration routes; otherwise their costs and access are not comparable. Ordinary Part B rules are a comparison branch, not a newly approved project benefit design. [S1–S3]

| Population | Existing Medicare eligibility; no age-60/55 add-on. Identify Part B enrollment requirement and treatment of MA, dual-eligible, disabled and ESRD beneficiaries under the same benefit | No new means test, hearing-severity cutoff or enrollment condition may be inferred |
| Hearing services | Specify routine assessment, evaluation, fitting, adjustment, counseling/rehabilitation and repair separately from currently covered diagnostic care | Decide frequency/medical-necessity rules and which follow-up belongs in the new benefit. Exams alone are insufficient |

### Drafting instructions for the three routes

These are proposed instructions to counsel, not enacted text or an opinion that all amendments are required.

- **All accelerated routes:** enact affirmative Part B services/devices/payment authority with an express January 1, 2030 service-date clause; conform §1862(a)(7), §1861 definitions and the selected payment/cost-sharing sections. Provide lawful implementation funding, transitional coding/pricing and claims correction authority where needed. Do not change other HLT-2 starts by an undifferentiated title-wide effective clause.
- **Route 1:** confirm actual incorporation in the rate assumptions/announcement and June bids. If not, evaluate the same §1852(a)(5)/§422.109 bridge; do not label it normal-cycle integration simply because R1 passed in February.
- **Route 2:** expressly displace §1852(a)(5)(A)'s delay for these benefits and CY2030, provide actuarially grounded payment adjustments under §1853, and a limited §1854 bid/rebate/premium reconciliation process. Specify whether any FFS exception ends at January 1 or at an adjusted contract's effective date; prohibit duplicate payments during any handover. Set notices, network/access, appeals, data and retrospective true-up rules. Reopening a bid after submission but before the contract year still needs a defined process.
- **Route 3, existing-law minimum:** a new Part B benefit is necessary, but a new general FFS bridge is **not shown necessary** if the enacted benefit qualifies under §1852(a)(5)/§422.109 and retains ordinary liability rules. Obtain the determination and publish the payer map. Administrative claims instructions may implement existing rules; they cannot rewrite plan obligations or create cost-sharing relief.
- **Route 3, amendments if needed:** if significance is not established or predictable statutory access is required regardless of determination, add a narrow HLT-2 transition clause specifying direct Part B payment for these items for MA enrollees during the unadjusted period, expressly resolving §1851(i)(1) **and (2)** and §1852(a)(5), with an end tied to the first adjusted contract year. If the intended rule departs from §422.109(c)(2)–(4), specify exactly which residual plan duties, beneficiary liability or accounting rules change and fund them. Do not waive all of §1851(i) or all MA requirements. Such a carve-out is a new policy/drafting choice, not use of unchanged law.

**Correction flow to cost and test:** on a misrouted claim, MAC and plan identify the responsible payer, transfer required data under lawful authority, preserve the original filing date if authorized, correct payment and refund any improper collection. Name a CMS escalation owner and distinguish Part B claim appeals from MA organization determinations. Filing-date tolling, provisional payment and a beneficiary hold-harmless rule need specific legal review; they are proposed remedies, not existing universal guarantees. Test a device with no supplemental benefit, a partially overlapping allowance, exhausted allowance, diagnostic-plus-fitting bundle, replacement, a plan change, QMB billing and the CY2031 handover. A provider referral between payers is not successful access if it leaves a person unable to obtain the device.

## 5. Restoration companion: separate authority, repair and receipt

The [CSV schedule](health-delivery-component-register.csv) records conditional prospective/remedy timing and responsible work packages. The existing 2030 baseline stays. **No national first-receipt or full-rollout date is certified.** A case may be repaired after the legal effective date and still need its earlier claims paid; a clinic award may precede new appointments by months.

- **Marketplace assistance:** compare an explicit tax-year-2029 entitlement with prospective assistance from a selected 2029 coverage month. State whether earlier eligible months receive tax reconciliation, insurer premium adjustment or both with offsets; APTC is an advance of PTC, not a second benefit. Treasury/IRS, federal/state marketplaces and insurers need rules, tested calculations, notices and assistance for existing and new enrollees. Retroactive credits do not themselves create retroactive insurance. CMS's March 11/April 1, 2021 ARP precedent establishes an in-year route, not a national 21-day promise. [S9]
- **Medicaid prospective repeal and case repair:** identify every H.R.1 provision being undone, effective date and affected cohort against refreshed 2029 law; the repository does not contain that full repeal crosswalk. Stop future repealed adverse actions on the selected legal date; separately triage pending applications, work-rule terminations, renewal losses and other losses. Decide automatic restoration where records establish continued eligibility versus assisted review where they do not; do not reimpose a repealed barrier through unnecessary reapplication. Specify lookback, retroactive eligibility, interim coverage authority, managed-care reconciliation, claims/refunds and filing-limit relief. Preserve unrelated eligibility rules. The existing hearing-continuation rule is conditional and cannot supply a universal bridge. [S10]
- **Children's continuity, waiting-period restoration and Medicaid dental:** separate continuity protections for current children from repair of already-closed cases; identify the statutory waiting-period provisions/cohorts being restored without inventing new eligibility. Define Medicaid dental service/payment changes separately from Medicare dental, which stays from 2033. State plans, notices, casework and provider supply determine prospective receipt; any retroactive eligibility or provider reimbursement requires explicit remedy treatment. Other maternal-health/Title X and safety-net grants need their own statutory/award maps, not CMS-only ownership.
- **Coverage gap:** compare (a) a federally financed Medicaid-equivalent backstop with a named federal administrator/contracting and claims route in noncooperating states and (b) an enhanced Marketplace route with explicitly specified zero-premium/cost-sharing/provider and enrollment protections. These are unresolved implementation branches, not interchangeable or adopted substitutes. Automatic enrollment and Medicaid-equivalent protection cannot be presumed from a PTC expansion. Decide benefits/eligibility consistency, provider payment, data/default/opt-out authority, state refusal response, appeals and financing before any national 2029 receipt claim. Do not borrow the later HLT-3 public plan or 2032 auto-enrollment machinery as if already operating.
- **Health centers and other existing grants:** identify HRSA/other HHS accounts, continuation versus incremental awards, draw schedules and services purchased. Operating support may sustain staff/appointments in 2029; construction and 14,000 residency slots cannot be counted as immediate clinicians. Separate grant obligations, cash and actual service capacity. Residency allocations have a longer training/accreditation path.
- **HCBS financing/capacity:** compare a specified time-limited FMAP enhancement for existing authorized services with targeted grants/rate/workforce support; new slots and entitlement scope remain as adopted unless Espen changes them. Define eligible expenditures, state maintenance/anti-supplantation rules if selected, state share, payment pass-through, waiver/state-plan approvals and continuing funding after the down payment. FMAP boosts and grants must not finance the same invoice twice. Eligibility-system 90/75% matching is not the HCBS benefit match or a universal caseworker subsidy. Rate/retention support can precede new workforce and slots; assess provider acceptance, wages/hours, authorized hours actually filled, waiting-list validity and rural capacity separately. [S11–S12]

Reuse the completed [CMS-IMP-02 pilot](health-cms-imp-02-pilot.md) and [evidence follow-up](health-cms-imp-02-evidence-followup.md): ONE's negotiated rates need quantities; ceilings are not available balances; the real training increment concerns SNAP, not Medicaid restoration. Current APD/allocation approvals, restoration orders and actual workload remain missing. Use these as focused questions; do not restart the pilot, extrapolate Oregon nationally or send the unsent requests.
