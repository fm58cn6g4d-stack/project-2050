## 1. Medicare benefit and payment instructions

The selected design adds full benefits to normal Medicare Part B, with no age-60/55 expansion, severe-only hearing threshold, exams-only substitute or fixed cash allowance adopted. Use affirmative coverage/payment amendments and conform exclusions; repealing §1862(a)(7) alone does not create a complete benefit. [L1–L3]

| Rule | Instruction for the next bill/implementation draft | Boundary still requiring evidence or text |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hearing services | Cover clinically indicated assessment, fitting, adjustment and counseling/rehabilitation with the covered device and necessary repair/maintenance | Crosswalk current diagnostic coverage, new services and supplier scope; do not bill a fitting twice in a device bundle and a service fee |
| Hearing devices | Appropriate prescription or FDA-compliant OTC devices, one or both ears as needed; ordinary replacement once per ear in five years with medically necessary exceptions | Establish device categories, included supplies/warranty and exception documentation. No unreviewed dollar or accessory allowance; OTC is not sufficient for every patient |
| Vision | Routine examination/refraction and needed fitting; appropriate glasses including lenses/frames or contact-lens supply instead, on ordinary two-year conventions with medically necessary exceptions | Preserve disease treatment and post-cataract coverage; define a nonduplicative eyewear event. An interval rule must not become a categorical denial of a clinically necessary exception |
| Participation/payment | Direct payment to appropriately enrolled/licensed professionals and suppliers; mandatory assignment for the new covered benefit | Professional service fee under the applicable statutory schedule where lawful; device fee or defined bundle under express payment authority. Fee amounts, geographic adjustments, updates and allowed upgrades need specific text/evidence |
| Patient share | Apply the ordinary Part B deductible and 20% coinsurance to the applicable allowed amount, with existing QMB/other lawful protections and coordination | A patient share is not a fee above the allowed amount. No blanket zero-sharing benefit or 2030 acceleration of the $5,000 cap; protect QMBs even if a state pays less than the full sharing amount |
| Fulfillment/claims | Proposed operating rule: distinguish assessment date, fitting, delivery/acceptance and repair; pay a device against documented delivery rather than an order alone; check prior paid device and warranty records | Coding, ordering and service-date rules require CMS/legal mapping. Prior private/MA purchases must not silently disqualify a person or reset an interval: transition treatment remains an explicit specification question |
| Notice/correction | Proposed operating rule: identify payer and approved patient share before collection where practicable; provide a single assisted correction path and restore improper collections | Provisional payment, filing-date preservation and a no-extra-charge rule for good-faith misrouting need explicit legal/funding treatment; they do not automatically waive ordinary lawful sharing |

**Claim arithmetic, not a price estimate:** on a $1,000 assigned covered claim with $100 deductible remaining, the selected ordinary rule allocates $100 deductible plus $180 coinsurance: Medicare $720, non-Medicare share $280. For a QMB, patient liability is $0; do not change Medicare's $720 to $1,000 or assume that a state necessarily pays $280. The [generated checks](health-design-model-checks.md#selected-specification-accounting-examples) exercise these boundaries, including an unknown price that stays unknown. Actual household effects also depend on other coverage, eligibility and service-specific rules. [L2, L5]

### MA-enrollee access: an explicit routing specification

Use service-date enrollment, the published significant-cost determination, existing covered/supplemental obligations and the first adjusted contract year to determine the path. Do not use the date a claim happens to arrive to move responsibility across the contract handover. [L4]

1. FFS enrollee, newly covered item: Part B claim under the new coverage/payment rule.
2. MA enrollee, diagnostic, existing rate-included or already-promised supplemental item: plan responsibility to the legal/contractual extent under §422.109(c)(2), even during a significant-change bridge.
3. MA enrollee, significant new benefit, no overlapping plan obligation: direct Original-Medicare payment for the qualifying residual item before adjusted contracts apply, under §422.109(c)(1)/(3).
4. Partial or exhausted supplemental allowance: obtain the actual item/quantity/liability terms and adjudication history. A nominal dollar allowance is not sufficient evidence to compute the FFS residual; route remains unresolved until the map exists. No patient should be told to exhaust or surrender a plan benefit merely from a spreadsheet subtraction.
5. CMS determines the change nonsignificant: §422.109(b) makes MA responsible from the statutory effective date. Do not open the significant-cost FFS route by assumption.
6. Adjusted contract year: MA furnishes/arranges/pays under §422.109(d); prevent duplicate FFS payment for the same covered service. Reprocess mistakenly routed claims using the lawful correction path.

**Required implementation artifact:** a versioned code/item map with columns for FFS coverage, current MA obligation, incremental bridge coverage, amount methodology, patient liability, supplier category, service date and handover. Actual HCPCS/CPT assignments and fee amounts are not invented in this specification. A gap in that map is a launch blocker for the affected item, not authority to substitute reduced benefits. The amendment options in the merged packet still govern §1851(i)(1)/(2), §1852(a)(5), §1853(c)(7) and §1854; an express carve-out is needed if the selected rule departs from existing authority. [L4]

## 2. Provider enrollment and the conditional allowance comparison

The [seven-category work map](health-enrollment-work-map.csv) separates reusable machinery from marginal work, with primary-source references and readiness artifacts. CMS-855I/PECOS already handles audiologists and physician/optometry categories; existing Medicare supplier processes already support covered eyewear. Ordering-only enrollment is not billing authority. DMEPOS enrollment, where applicable, uses its own supplier standards, accreditation/bond rules and exemptions, NPE contractors, location/NPI and EFT requirements. Do not impose that entire route on every clinical professional or presume every new aid/OTC seller fits it unchanged. [L6]

**Recommendation:** continue direct-payment implementation first. The evidence rejects “enrollment is wholly new” as a general premise. It does not establish sufficient providers or fast processing. Require the following scoped inventory, without restarting the CMS pilot:

| Evidence to assemble from available records | Decision it resolves | Owner/package |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Existing active billing categories, covered-item routes and legal scope, by professional/supplier group | What can be reused without a new enrollment category? | CMS-IMP-04, with Medicare enrollment/legal owners |
| Marginal categories and rule/form/specialty-code changes; applicable exceptions and quality controls | What genuinely needs new authority/system work? | CMS-IMP-04; partner licensing/quality duties separately |
| New applications and location changes, already-enrolled participation interest, processor work/queue and geographic access | Will the additional work endanger actual service/device receipt? No denominator or staffing ratio assumed | CMS-IMP-04; CMS-IMP-01 acquisition/workforce where incremental |
| End-to-end claim, delivery, QMB, warranty, refund and accessibility tests | Can a person obtain the selected full benefit and have the right payer pay? | CMS-IMP-03/11/13, one owner per shared task |

**Conditional 3B comparison, not selected substitution:** evaluate (i) a temporary allowance/reimbursement route, and (ii) a permanent allowance only if evidence establishes a genuine enrollment-driven rollout problem. For each, state covered items, indexation, point-of-sale versus reimbursement, supplier/quality verification, required receipt/order evidence, low-income liquidity protection, uncovered price exposure, interaction with ordinary Part B sharing/MA promises, and the transition back to direct pay if temporary. An allowance may require a new payment system and still require recognized suppliers. Current Medicare's supplier requirement for covered eyewear does not disappear because the patient submits the claim. [L6–L7]

Compare total implementation effort, critical-path timing, reach and patient cost for the **same** full benefit. Do not declare the allowance cheaper by leaving fitting/support outside it, pushing device prices onto patients or omitting its own administration. Return any actual change of instrument, scope or allowance level to Espen with this comparison. There is no automatic fallback trigger or delegated discretion to reduce the benefit in this draft.

## 3. OBBBA lookbacks for streamlined coverage restoration

**Latest user clarification controls:** look back to find people pushed off coverage and help them regain current coverage. Do not undertake a general program to reopen/pay medical claims from the reduced-eligibility period. Earlier drafting that developed multi-year resubmission/refund machinery went beyond this clarified purpose and is superseded here. Medical-debt relief remains a separate option for review, not an adopted remedy.

The [nine-cohort register](health-restoration-remedy-cohorts.csv) preserves current-law OBBBA dates as search triggers. Its scope status now distinguishes current-coverage restoration, normal prospective application rules and separate tax-policy questions. It is not a patient list, a finding of current eligibility or a historical-claims budget. The earlier first-loss dates identify how far records may need searching; they do not set a retroactive benefit start.

Proposed coverage-restoration workflow:

1. **Find affected people:** use historical denial/termination reasons, application/renewal records and notices to identify losses plausibly tied to repealed restrictions. Missing attribution remains unresolved; do not count every unenrolled person as an OBBBA loss.
2. **Reuse lawful records:** prepopulate identity and previously verified information where lawful and reliable. Do not require people to reconstruct the whole former application. Check what remains current, what needs confirmation and which accessible contact method works.
3. **Establish current eligibility:** remove the repealed barrier, consider other eligibility bases and update material facts such as income, residence and other coverage. Past enrollment is useful evidence, not automatic proof of present eligibility. Use assisted review for incomplete records rather than an avoidable full reapplication.
4. **Restore current coverage:** establish the lawful effective date under the restored program, send a clear notice, offer enrollment/provider assistance and resolve erroneous present-day records. History alone does not backdate coverage to the initial cut. Any presumptive/interim-coverage authority or special effective-date rule needs specific authority and review.
5. **Verify the result:** track successful contact, completed eligibility review, effective enrollment, access to care, time to correction and unresolved cases/appeals. Outreach and a corrected database field are not the same as restored usable coverage.

**Keep three distinct boundaries.** First, reversing §71112 may restore the ordinary retroactive-eligibility framework for applications under the restored law; that is different from reopening all cut-period claims. Second, independently existing legal duties or correction rights are not extinguished by declining to create a new general historical-claims program. Third, correction of misrouted claims for newly operating Medicare benefits and QMB billing protection in sections 1–2 still apply; they address operation of those benefits, not payment for every past uninsured period.

The five Marketplace credit/recapture cohorts remain a separate tax-restoration crosswalk. This medical-remedy clarification neither approves broad past-tax-year refunds nor silently rescinds an adopted credit. Any proposed past-tax relief, offsets and limitation-period change must be put forward separately. No such amount is priced here.

### Medical debt: a separate possible relief measure

Espen is open to some medical-debt relief. For a later small comparison, distinguish targeted assistance/negotiated relief for people with documented coverage loss from broader medical-debt purchase/forgiveness or provider-side relief. Identify debts/people reached, collection and credit consequences, provider/creditor participation, administrative cost, funding and overlap with the existing HLT-4 medical-debt source provisions. Paying a provider's original bill, settling a debt and purchasing debt at a discount have different costs and beneficiaries. Do not infer a purchase price, face-value-to-cost conversion, entitlement to reimbursement or new debt program from this openness. No debt instrument or spending amount is selected.

**Costing consequence:** estimate record matching, outreach, assisted current-eligibility review, case/system correction and current-coverage delivery. Exclude a new blanket historical medical-claims payment/refund/tolling program from this work's cost scope. Show any independently owed correction and any separately proposed debt relief in their own lines, without double counting. The completed CMS-IMP-02 pilot still supplies the state/system cost-chain method; missing workload and cash remain unpriced.

## 4. Federal coverage-gap backstop: concrete legislative architecture

**New evidence:** House Report 117-130, part 2, §30701 proposed an express new Title XIX §1948 federal gap program. Its proposed (a) covers the federal establishment/eligibility/benefit/hearing duties; (b) allows federal contracts with Medicaid managed-care organizations and third-party administrators; (e)/(f) addresses transitions and Exchange coordination; (g)/(h) covers third-party liability and integrity. It is a drafting precedent, not enacted authority. Its 2025 date, contractor counts, payment formulas, no-cost-sharing provisions and other parameters are not adopted by citing it. [L11]

Use that architecture to draft a new federal authority, with section numbering and conforming amendments checked by counsel:

| Clause | Proposed instruction consistent with selected 5A | Decision/evidence boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Federal duty/trigger | Identify residents excluded from the selected ACA expansion-equivalent coverage because the state does not provide it; establish a federal coverage duty independent of state consent | Explicit population/eligibility crosswalk to HLT-1; do not infer a new immigration rule, income expansion or authority to compel state administration |
| Benefits/financial protection | Specify an expansion-equivalent covered-service and protection crosswalk, including necessary transport and continuity; identify applicable cost sharing rather than treat a PTC as identical coverage | Final benefit/protection text and score; do not transfer Medicare's 20% rule to Medicaid |
| Administration | Authorize federal eligibility/enrollment, claims, beneficiary assistance, accessible federal hearings and integrity functions; permit Marketplace front-door reuse without making this an HLT-3 plan | New lawful data-purpose/access and acquisition authority; validate each reusable interface; no dependence on 2032 auto-enrollment |
| Contract/payment routes | Compare federal managed-care contracts against a federal third-party administrator route paying covered services; preserve a means of delivery where a contractor declines an area | Network participation, rates, risk/reserves, procurement and service continuity need evidence. No chosen MCO premium, Medicare-rate default or contractor count |
| State refusal/transitions | Provide a federal operating route without mandatory new state duties or withdrawal of existing Medicaid funds to force expansion; transition people if state expansion later supplies coverage | NFIB/anti-commandeering, federalism, preemption, continuity and state data/cooperation reviewed on actual text; no broad legal certification |
| Funding/overlap | Express federal benefit and administrative funding for the federal route; assign it once to HLT-1, with CMS shared CAP residual only after reconciliation | A state match cannot be presumed from a refusing state. The existing gap score does not establish this architecture's price; identify the financed increment/offsets before selection |

**Recommendation:** develop both contracting variants within 5A for a narrow operating comparison; the direct federal statute is the legal path under assessment. Do not first build a second national public-option platform or automatically add a temporary Marketplace bridge and two transitions. A new statute can define necessary federal powers; current general CMS authority is not treated as sufficient. NFIB's coercion remedy is a design constraint, not a holding approving this particular backstop. Reconciliation jurisdiction/Byrd, constitutional defenses, substantive benefit crosswalk, rates and contracts still need specific review. [L11–L12]

## 5. HCBS: combined match and targeted support specification

Selected 6A includes both financing channels. Assign costs to mutually exclusive invoice/work units before selecting a rate or amount:

| Pool | What it pays for | Controls/owner and continuing-finance question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Existing authorized service spending with enhanced match | Eligible HCBS claims under defined state-plan/waiver channels, with rate/retention changes where selected | State/CMS HCBS owner, CMS-IMP-08. Identify base expenditure, match increment, state maintenance and pass-through; increased federal contribution is not automatically more service spending |
| Targeted workforce/provider/accessibility grants or other support | Defined training/retention, provider entry, accessibility and other bottlenecks not already financed in the service pool | Specify recipient, eligible cost, deliverable, wage/access measures and continuing support; separate agency administration from provider resources; no same wage/training invoice charged twice |
| Eligibility/IT/assurance support | State/federal systems, casework, accessible notices, test and oversight changes | CMS-IMP-02/08/11/13 as justified; state APD and conditional systems match separate from benefit FMAP; reconcile CAP-13 and existing HLT administration |
| Ongoing service/capacity after down payment | Recurring staff, care hours, provider payments and maintenance | Name durable financing and federal/state shares; finite grants/surge cannot silently fund permanent obligations after expiration |

Proposed award/match conditions for drafting: eligible period and expenditures; state maintenance/anti-supplantation; invoice/program allocation; payment-to-worker/provider trace where required; accessibility/non-digital service; filled authorized hours, missed visits, turnover and provider acceptance; and public correction reporting. The exact grant mechanism, match percentage, duration, required pass-through share and evaluation thresholds remain to be selected and scored, not derived from historical ARP rates. Existing §1915 channels and the §71121 current-law HCBS route form the baseline. [L8, L13]

The [model check](health-design-model-checks.md#selected-specification-accounting-examples) makes the boundary concrete: **illustrative** 100 service units with 60% base match plus 10 points and 10 units of distinct grants yield federal 80, state 30 and resources 110, not 180. A federal match increment reallocates who finances the existing service pool unless spending actually grows. New slots/filled hours and workforce capacity therefore need their own quantities, not just a funding announcement. None of those illustrative rates or units is a 2029 estimate.

## 6. Marginal costing and launch gate

Prepare one cost worksheet, not an unsupported grand total. For each hearing/vision item, remedy cohort, gap route and HCBS pool, record: baseline versus selected change; population/service or work quantity; unit price basis/year; service period; federal/state/private incidence; existing payment/administration offset; one ledger owner; authority/obligation/cash year; and recurring versus finite status. Link existing CMS-IMP packages and contract/pilot evidence. The [cohort](health-restoration-remedy-cohorts.csv) and [enrollment](health-enrollment-work-map.csv) registers supply estimating units, not quantities or available budgets.

**Hearing/vision gross claim amount** requires people × covered use × allowed payment by item, with deductibles/coinsurance, QMB/other coverage, MA residuals, replacement and existing diagnostic/cataract payments resolved. **Net federal funding** then needs premiums/GF/state buy-in and administrative contributions shown without duplicate receipts. Do not treat the gross claim as the GF bill or import the combined $18 premium as a marginal hearing/vision amount.

**Coverage restoration** requires identified people, current eligibility/enrollment months and casework, not historical uninsured months multiplied by an average Medicaid payment. Any tax-restoration amounts or separately considered medical-debt relief need their own scope and evidence. **Gap** needs enrollment, covered services/payment/contract cost and administration, not uncommitted HLT-3 capacity. **HCBS** needs separate service resource growth and financing shifts, plus grant support not already paid elsewhere. Carry pre-window FY2029 cash and later shifts separately. Borrowing authority is not inferred from a one-time benefit/payment label; preserve D4, the HI reserve, annual cash/envelope and no-dynamic-revenue rules.

The existing fiscal/calendar/capacity analysis continues to apply: R2's central conditional October completion gives only 85 calendar days; capacity costs and FY2029 cash remain unknown; the maturity cushion is narrow and conditional on the old surge proxy. None of these outputs prices this specification. **No launch go decision** until the selected-benefit payer/supplier map, lawful funding and supported release/claim/access plan are complete. If January 2030 cannot meet those conditions, preserve the adopted 2031 fallback and report the named constraint; do not weaken the benefit or delay R1 automatically.
