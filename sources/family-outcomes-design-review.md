# Family security and child poverty: outcomes and design review

October 24, 2026 · Review draft; no policy change. Source: `main`, `31d2e584d790c6dc6e3c243e1643b1ab3a8d050e`. No open PRs were found at the start. Espen authorized this bounded assignment after the health design review: use completed research and tools to assess outcomes, compare consequential choices and produce one public family example. This is a synthesis of existing research, not a new future-poverty forecast or an official score.

## Judgment

The adopted child-policy package makes a meaningful contribution to family security, but the modeled results do not establish a pathway close to eradicating child poverty. The next design question is whether to strengthen the package's material reach and adequacy, alongside making it easier to receive. The evidence warrants a focused comparison of the already-filed larger child credit and wider rental assistance; it does not select either alternative or establish how much the full agenda would reduce poverty.

The charter treats child poverty as a defining outcome while also valuing dependable care, practical freedom and participation. Those purposes need more than a count of benefits authorized or an annual poverty rate. The public account should explain a credible improvement in family life and the remaining limits. The [companion family example](family-security-public-example.md) tests that account without claiming to represent the average family.

## What the completed model establishes

The source-year baseline is the 2026 CPS ASEC measuring 2025 resources. The 2030/2034 labels select phasing and stated price/population conventions; they are not forecasts of those years. The headline includes CTC, care/pre-K expense reductions, food assistance, capped new vouchers and an employee leave-contribution cost. It omits leave benefits, earnings responses and many other agenda effects. All changes below are joint scenario comparisons using the same baseline construction, not independent effects to add.

| Existing case | 2030 child poverty | 2034 child poverty | 2034 poor children | Interpretation |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Moderate delivery | 10.61% | 10.29% | 6.87m | Analyst receipt/access assumptions; same-scenario baseline about 13.4% |
| Enhanced delivery | 10.36% | 10.03% | 6.70m | About 170,000 fewer poor children than moderate in 2034; improvement is not a measured intervention effect |
| Full modeled coverage/receipt/access | 10.17% | 9.87% | 6.59m | About 279,000 fewer than moderate in 2034; still capped vouchers and fixed eligibility/service conventions |
| Index credit amounts from 2025 rather than 2030, moderate delivery | 10.10% | 9.69% | 6.47m | About 399,000 fewer than moderate in 2034; a benefit-size/dollar-anchor sensitivity, not FAM-1D or an adopted indexing decision |

Moderate 2034 deep poverty falls from about 3.45% to 2.28%, leaving about 1.52m children deeply poor. Among remaining poor children, about 73% live in units with positive earnings and 65% in renter units; those groups overlap. No-positive-earnings units have much higher poverty risk. These patterns support examining both income adequacy and housing costs, without assigning the residual to a single cause or assuming that obtaining a job resolves it.

Coverage can also fail unevenly: concentrating the same assumed coverage gap in the lowest-resource quarter raises the moderate 2034 result to 10.67%. That is a stress test, not evidence of anyone's SSN/ITIN status. It makes the distribution of exclusion and nonreceipt consequential even when the national average looks similar. Including unresolved claimant links through the existing proxy changes that result only to 10.24%; fixing those 327 sample records is not equivalent to removing statutory exclusions.

**What the full-receipt comparison does not establish:** the 6.59m residual is not a measured count of children who need a larger cash benefit. It also reflects scarce housing assistance, fixed eligibility proxies, unmodeled channels and source limitations. No percentage of residual poverty is assigned causally to administration, exclusion or inadequate benefits.

These are complementary mechanisms, but their effects and costs cannot be summed without a joint run and overlap review. No new benefit amount, eligibility rule, housing entitlement, residual supplement or numerical poverty target is selected here. A broader credit or housing scenario is not yet in the completed results; those rows deliberately contain no invented poverty reduction or cost-per-child ranking.

## Connection to the wider agenda and governing choices

The health review improves the account of coverage, current-eligibility restoration and care capacity. It has not supplied a household-level medical-expense estimate suitable for this model. The existing carbon-plus-30%-medical-expense sensitivity reaches 9.56% in the moderate 2034 case, but the 30% assumption is uncalibrated and cannot be credited to the selected health design. Nor should its poverty change be added to the indexing or delivery rows.

Likewise, a small modeled care-expense gain does not imply that care has little value. Families reporting no paid care can gain access, time and freedom without a large measured deduction reduction. The paid-leave calculation includes contributions without event-specific benefits; it cannot rank the insurance program's net merits. Housing construction, better wages, education and longer-run child development are not converted into immediate cash in the model.

This review supports retaining the durable monthly credit, full refundability and supply-with-demand approach. It identifies questions for future governing review: whether the selected material package is adequate for the charter's ambitions; how exclusion fits those aims; and which lasting financing can support improvements. It does not reopen all D1–D9 or infer that D3's quintile averages establish gains for every family. D4, HI reserves, channel boundaries and the ban on counting dynamic revenue remain constraints.

## A small outcomes record

| Purpose | Evidence now available | What would show progress beyond an appropriation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reduce child poverty and deepest deprivation | Source-year 13.39% child SPM; conditional results above | Comparable observed poverty/deep-poverty and remaining disparities; no adopted near-zero numerical target |
| Make family resources dependable | Monthly credit selected; annual receipt scenarios | On-time payment, missed months, correction and hardship; annual SPM alone cannot establish reliability |
| Provide usable care | Phased FAM-2A and supply/workforce commitments; access assumptions | Affordable slots and hours that fit families, disability/infant access, worker retention and continuity |
| Improve housing security | Capped awards and lease-up scenarios; housing supply agenda | Successful leases, rent burdens, displacement/eviction and accessible units; award counts alone are insufficient |

This is the initial outcomes record within this review, not a new dashboard or a claim that the measures are fully specified. Public influence and access to correction should be considered across all four purposes; no turnout or democratic-trust effect is inferred from benefits receipt.

## What to decide, and where to stop

**Recommended next decision:** authorize a bounded, jointly costed comparison of FAM-1A with filed FAM-1D and one specified wider rental-assistance alternative, preserving the adopted package meanwhile. Start by reconciling their rules and score bases. Reuse the current poverty model where its inputs support the comparison, and the existing fiscal/incidence tools for funding and distribution; disclose remaining gaps. Do not commission a future-poverty forecasting platform.

An alternative is to retain the package for now, improve delivery and focus the next research on omitted whole-agenda effects. That can strengthen the evidence, but it would leave the demonstrated adequacy/reach questions open. No full-agenda eradication claim is justified by either path today. Deciding on a new target or actually adopting an expansion is a separate subsequent choice.

Stop this assignment at the review and family example. Detailed enrollment procedures, agency playbooks, additional dashboards and an integrated 2050 simulation are deferred. The fiscal savings/maturity-margin review and authorized democracy work remain queued, not launched here.
