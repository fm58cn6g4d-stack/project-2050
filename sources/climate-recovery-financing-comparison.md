## Accounting and limits

- Uses PR #26’s exported annual general-fund net path through 2050, including CX and carbon-channel shortfalls. Adds finite grid/disaster capital deficit costs once. The $195B capacity-surge reduction is already in the reference and is never deducted again.
- D7 remains 80% dividend / 20% transition. Unused transition receipts remain reserved for that channel; they are not swept into the general fund. This uses the same fully committed channel convention as the recovery reference, rather than claiming its surplus as a new debt saving.
- General-fund net costs are represented as outlay-equivalent adjustments solely to compute deficits and debt. These scenario revenue/outlay totals are not a gross carbon-account crosswalk. The underlying recovery reference and household diagnostic still use the historical carbon schedule. Neither is a complete post-PR #26 baseline.
- Annual capital timing reconstructs the documented equal-real grid profile and disaster ramp, normalized to exact exported window totals. After 2050, net general-fund cost grows with the pricing model’s inflation assumption; this is an explicit extrapolation, not modeled emissions or receipts. Loan principal financing, repayments and financial assets need a separate public-debt financing crosswalk beyond deficit-cost scoring.
- Existing reforms and the four-point upper-income comparator retain the same illustrative education/SS rates and $300 credit as the earlier comparison. The latter is not a wealthy-only tax. Climate cost cases are not comprehensive macro downside cases or probability forecasts.
- No additional CLI-13 removal purchases or federal reinsurance backstop are counted. Both remain separate decisions. No avoided-disaster losses or dynamic growth receipts are banked.
