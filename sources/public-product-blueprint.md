## 1. The public promise

Project 2050 should help a reader imagine a life with more security, freedom and public influence, then inspect what would make it possible. The promise is: **see how the program connects to ordinary life, and follow any consequential claim into its policy, evidence, cost and unresolved conditions.** That gives the ambition an analytical foundation while allowing people to challenge the program on terms they can understand.

The initial public audience is politically engaged, policy-curious people, including skeptical readers who want a concrete account of what this agenda would change. They should not need to know a policy ID or understand a fiscal model to begin. Journalists, researchers, advocates and government practitioners need a second entrance organized around policies and questions. These audiences overlap. A reader investigating care for a relative may want more detail than a professional arriving to understand the overall vision.

The two levels should therefore share records rather than become separate projects. Public scenes introduce a question. A short explanation identifies the mechanism and its limits. An evidence panel names the policy, authority, research and calculation. The professional record provides the specification, provenance and reproducible material. Readers can enter at any level, and professional findings should change the scene when its implied promise cannot be supported. Detailed operating manuals are deferred; the immediate professional product is a usable explanation of design, dependencies and what remains unknown. [1–3]

The agenda's connective argument is broader than household assistance. Security gives people room to make plans; housing, transport and education make opportunities usable; dependable care depends on workers as well as coverage; climate preparation involves land, power, insurance and who must move; democratic renewal concerns rights and influence as well as faster service. Labor, competition and AI policy ask who can shape economic change. Courts, executive constraints, integrity and capacity ask how power can act and remain answerable. Immigration, civil rights, public safety, social insurance and national security belong in the account of whose security and freedom count. These are relationships to explain and investigate, not additional quantified effects to add together.

The prototype gives climate, work, public influence and transport independent places alongside home and care. It links all 21 canonical domain/transportation homes. The next edition should deepen a few of these connections rather than expand a decorative map into a checklist of every policy.

## 2. Three genuinely different entrances

The [screen treatments](../index/public-2050/directions.html) show actual sample copy, layout and a three-step storyboard for each direction. They are static design treatments; the first direction also has a working experience.

| Direction | Editorial and visual structure | Interaction and sample journey | Strength and limitation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A day in a possible 2050** — recommended | Illustrated community; warm paper, restrained green/terracotta, large editorial type. Each place exposes the choices behind a recognizable experience. | River → recovery and continuing losses → CLI-7/8, unscored costs and displacement → housing or public influence → professional record. | Connects the agenda without requiring a lecture. A single appealing neighborhood can obscure regional differences and harm; explicitly identify fiction and introduce contrasting places later. |
| **A life with room to change** | Turning-point narrative, portrait/household scenes, a chapter spine organized around starting out, caring, work changing and aging. | Caring for someone → dependable versus interrupted support → time, services and rights → HLT-4/FAM-3/LAB-3 and delivery evidence. | Makes time, relationships and usable care concrete. Risks presenting one “normal” life or reducing the program to family policy. Requires nonparent, disabled, migrant and other lives on their own terms. |
| **The public casebook** | An editorial evidence workspace: question, claim, support, uncertainty, source. Tables and annotations rather than a world illustration. | “What would it take to fund the program?” → current gap → compatible case comparison → assumptions and reproducible export. | Gives skeptical readers and practitioners direct access. Can bury the purpose under caveats and spreadsheets; works best as the professional entrance. |

Use the community as the initial front door, the casebook structure as its evidence layer and life-course stories as later authored features. Do not build three parallel applications. The community's principal advantage is that the same place can connect several mechanisms: a library can be public infrastructure, a place to participate and a test of maintenance and inclusion. Its principal weakness is that visual completion can look like policy completion. Every scene therefore has a visible fiction label and a “what still has to work” passage. No pictured building is described as a selected local project.

The working first edition contains six places, six short scenes, policy explanations, evidence disclosures, related-place links, a governing-path overview, a financing disclosure and the wider policy directory. The illustration is authored SVG, not a geographic map. It presents one plausible form of community, not a representative United States. A later rural or coastal treatment should change the underlying questions, and Tribal material must respect sovereignty and government-to-government responsibilities rather than become a cosmetic skin.

## 3. What an initial release can responsibly say

Define the initial release narrowly: an introductory public experience, selected professional explanations and inspectable research results. It is not a comprehensive funded manifesto, a household entitlement service, a national forecast or a claim that the program is ready for administration. The charter permits this work while analysis continues. Its broader governing-decision review remains relevant before a major release; this pass does not restart an agenda-wide audit. [1–3]

| Proposed public claim/use | Before initial release | May remain openly unresolved | Useful later; not a prerequisite for this claim |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| “This is the future we are trying to make possible” | Fiction labels beside scenes; separate aspiration from selected instruments; show losses, exclusions and prerequisites. | Whether the pictured arrangements can be achieved in each place. | More communities, interviews, commissioned illustration and local hazard maps. |
| “AFA plus one million total new voucher awards is selected” | Use the October 24 adoption, 2030–33 phasing and award/lease distinction consistently; remove historical FAM-1A/400,000 defaults from any promoted public view. | Exact statutory AFA mapping, supply and actual rental reach. Broader assistance remains optional. | Exact early-year cases and a qualified eligibility explainer; no need for a personal calculator now. |
| “Selected channels reduce child poverty in these conditional experiments” | Identify source income year, valuation reference, SPM measure, baseline, package and delivery assumptions adjacent to the table. Link uncertainty and limitations; label historical experiments. | Annual statutory analogue, legal/receipt proxies, nonmodeled channels and model uncertainty. Near-eradication is the ambition, not an achieved finding. | Full-agenda overlays only as calibrated evidence becomes available; a new long-run forecast is unnecessary. |
| “We can fund the whole program” | **Do not publish this claim.** Publish the working FY2040 gap and financing status instead. Recheck its sign, units and source at release. | Holistic financing redesign; none of the three comparisons selected. Current DEC gap is $53.69B nominal GF primary, rounded to about $54B. CAP-7S cash and other prices remain incomplete. | Common-baseline/joint scores and supported annual financing through maturity are required before a fully-financed claim, not before this qualified introduction. |
| “Middle-income households come out ahead” | **Do not turn the aggregate quintile screen into a household promise or claim full D3 verification.** Explain the objective and proxy limits. | AFA-specific losers, legal exclusions, within-quintile distribution and the incidence of redesigned financing. | Faithful net taxes/transfers, health out-of-pocket, wage and leave-event mapping before personalization. |
| “People can depend on care” | Present as aim; identify coverage, providers, workforce, payments, state systems and remedies separately. Preserve adopted dates and health research status. | Incremental costs, FY2029 cash and actual access; reuse CMS pilot evidence. | Service-level metrics, marginal scores, specialist review of particular operational claims; manuals deferred. |
| “Climate action makes this place safe” | **Do not publish this claim.** Describe mitigation and preparation choices with residual risk. No implied local engineering design. | CLI-7 score, concentrated losses, local costs, maintenance and relocation questions. | Regional hazard overlays, engineering and insurance analysis before place-specific assurance. |
| “Democratic reform gives people meaningful influence” | Explain the intended mechanisms, advisory versus binding participation, eligibility and protected-data boundaries, remedies and reciprocity. Do not promise trust or electoral victory. | Title III legal risk, permissions and actual reach. Keep DEM-1E's automatic ambition; an assisted demonstration is not its replacement. | Text-specific legal/operational review before readiness claims; reuse the completed DEM pilot. |
| “These dates show what households receive” | **Do not equate enactment, authority, operational availability, receipt and reach.** Preserve DEM deadlines, MOV's second-Congress exception and health dates. | Capacity, votes and delivery; calendar inputs are illustrative. | Calibrated durations and supported delivery milestones before predictive date views. |
| “Inspect the evidence” | Accessible, anonymous professional summaries and stable source passages; remove access-dependent Library links from the public path. Verify every promoted claim at the release commit. | Clearly named inaccessible source detail, provided the visible claim does not depend on unseen proof. | Full-text search, broad dossier expansion and downloadable specialized workbooks. |

The necessary release work is selective: finalize this claim set and its source passage review, fix any inconsistency actually surfaced to readers, publish accessible professional summaries, validate the release build and obtain editorial approval. A financing solution is a blocker for claiming the agenda is financed, not for publishing that financing is unfinished. An exact AFA mapping is a blocker for statutory or household arithmetic claims; it does not prevent describing the adopted design or transparently presenting the annual analogue. Likewise, unpriced CMS work prevents a readiness claim, not an explanation of the care agenda.

A short internal comprehension review should precede publication. Ask readers to identify what is imagined, what is adopted and what is measured; find the financing gap; explain why a voucher award is not a lease; and locate a source that could challenge a claim. Evaluate misunderstanding and navigation failure, not merely whether people like the illustration. No outside recruitment or contact is part of this pass.

## 4. Architecture: one evidence base, several ways to read it

### Existing assets to reuse

The repository already has a common Python/Node rebuild, input tables, stable policy IDs, fiscal/incidence/fund/carbon/transport models and generated dashboard exports. Its calendar explorer combines a searchable ledger with 146 deterministic scheduling cases; its HTML embeds roughly 721 KB of data. Its poverty page is a smaller, useful conditional comparison, explicitly labeled historical after the family adoption. Neither constitutes an agenda-wide public entry point. The externally hosted ledger dashboard is named in the tools readme, but this pass did not verify its live UI or synchronization. Reuse the exports and methods; do not infer that a hosted artifact updates with the repository. [4–7]

Reuse CMS-IMP-02 and DEM-1 as evidence structures, with their dated provenance and current corrections. Their public explanations show the useful distinction between a benefit, an administrative contact and a result. Do not restart either pilot. The initial prototype links their readable repository copies and keeps current governing timing visible beside historical material. [8–9]

### Content and data contracts

The repository remains the source of truth. A small publishing adapter produces a versioned, allowlisted static edition. It does not parse arbitrary Markdown sentences into numbers or write model inputs. The first implementation uses authored JSON for scenes, stable path references and explicit selectors into two existing aggregate exports. All public source links are pinned to the source commit, and the builder rejects source drift until an editor reviews the edition.

For the fuller product, extend that adapter with these records only as needed:

| Record | Minimum fields and relationship |
| --- | --- |
| Policy reference | Existing policy/option ID, canonical path/section, selected/optional/held status, authority commit and decision date. A reference into canon, not a second editable policy definition. |
| Claim | Stable ID; exact public wording; aspiration/fiction/observed/conditional/estimated/proposed type; policy IDs; source passage/table; population, measure, units and year; limitations; review owner/status. |
| Scene or essay | Title, authored body, illustration/alt text, claim IDs, related topics, geography/fiction status and editorial sequence. Claims do not inherit certainty from the image. |
| Source | Title, original URL, version/date, locator, inspection extent, access/license conditions, repository text path and content hash. Research synthesis and original source remain distinguishable. |
| Package version | Immutable selected policy/option IDs and authority revision, exclusions, accounting-owner references; separately named alternatives. Research package differs from adopted package where mapping is approximate. |
| Scenario/run | Model-native case ID, package version, external conditions, delivery assumptions, source vintage, units, valuation/horizon, model/input hashes, exclusions and compatibility notes. |
| Output view | Exact export selector, transformation/rounding, model relationship, allowed cases, display caption and uncertainty type. Unknown remains null, never zero. |
| Release | Content commit, policy/source commit(s), model/input/output hashes, schema version, checks, approval and artifact hash. Keep previous editions and corrections retrievable. |

The prototype's [manifest](../index/public-2050/manifest.json) implements the critical subset: pinned source commit, source/code hashes, output hashes and numeric selectors. The complete release Git commit also identifies the editorial content. It does not claim to implement a full claims CMS, ontology or model-run registry. A future CMS should edit this schema through reviewable changes; it should not become an independent policy database.

### Scenario reconciliation before shared controls

| Existing system | Actual meaning | Publishing treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fiscal `DEC` | Current ledger claims plus accepted option IDs C18/C35/C19b/C20/C38/C39/C40/C41/C43; FY2026–56 paths, 2030–39 score window. Adoption changed underlying claims. | Map to the selected package version with profile and unpriced-cost caveats. Keep `DEC-DYN`, `DEC+ENV` and declined-trim cases research-only; no dynamic revenue toward D4. |
| Incidence `Q` | Same accepted revenue-option list, with household allocation keys and 2030–39 profiles; annual-average quintile effects. | Related package, different coverage and unit. AFA-specific mapping is provisional. Never interpret a quintile mean as a person's benefit. |
| Poverty `adopted_cases` | FAM-1D mapped as `AFA_2025_real`, one million awards and other selected channels; moderate/enhanced/full research delivery. 2025 source income, 2034 valuation reference. | Display discrete conditional cases with their baseline and exclusions. The historic eight-case HTML is not the selected package. Later-year labels do not create forecasts or new populations. |
| Calendar | Conditions, capacity, strategy, merger/frontloading and shock assumptions over a bill inventory; assumes votes/agreement. | Separate scheduling scenario namespace. A calendar change does not rephase fiscal costs, accelerate benefits or alter adoption. |
| Carbon, funds, transport and vote tools | Sectoral paths, dedicated channels, authorization/receipt schedules or conditional political simulations with their own baselines. | Preserve units, channel ownership and stipulations. No shared slider or probability until an explicit, reviewed adapter establishes the relationship. |

Begin with a mapping registry, not a new engine. Declare fiscal DEC ↔ incidence Q **partially compatible** for package identity, with distribution caveats. Declare poverty comparisons **partial scope** and calendar cases **independent scheduling assumptions**. A join requires policy scope, baseline, year basis, population and accounting reconciliation. Reject a proposed global selection when those are missing, rather than silently map “enhanced” delivery to “favorable” politics. Add contract tests for option sets, exclusivity, duplicate costs, source hashes, year/unit mismatch and null preservation when such joins are implemented. [4–7,10]

### Interaction boundaries

- **Navigation:** map points, topic links and chapter choices change the explanation being read. They promise no numerical effect. This is the prototype's interaction.
- **Fictional illustration:** possible households and places explore values and conditions. They have no automatic model output attached.
- **Supported comparison:** a table or selector retrieves named, precomputed cases from one model with shared scope and visible assumptions. The prototype's poverty table does this without a selector; its three rows are not time steps.
- **Calculation:** a control changes a defined input and reruns a documented formula/model within a meaningful range. No such control is included. Future amounts require eligibility, units, overlap, range, sensitivity and boundary tests; a “funding” or “ambition” slider has no legitimate default interpretation.

### Technology, performance and accessibility

Keep the first pass static. The repository already depends on Python and Node, and this experience needs no server computation. A standard-library Python publisher produces semantic HTML with a small JavaScript enhancement and inline SVG. It works offline and with JavaScript disabled, and sends no analytics or personal information. The build has no new runtime dependency. This is a choice based on the task and maintenance cost, not a commitment to hand-maintain a comprehensive site as one HTML file.

For the next larger edition, evaluate a static site generator when reusable article/policy templates, typed content validation and search exceed the small publisher's useful scope. Astro's static output and isolated interactive components are a plausible candidate; Eleventy is another if Markdown and simple templates remain dominant. Compare both against the existing Python publisher with the same scene/policy sample. A client-routed SPA or server framework currently adds deployment, hydration and dependency costs without a corresponding user need. Do not choose a framework before that comparison.

Keep numerical model execution offline in the existing tools. Ship only the aggregates needed by the selected view; do not embed all calendar cases on the landing page or ship CPS records. Cache immutable assets by hash. Initial budget: under 200 KB uncompressed HTML/CSS/JS/SVG for the opening experience, no third-party requests, with large research exports loaded only on demand. Track the actual artifact size and test on a throttled mobile profile before release; passing a byte budget is not a measured performance score.

Use WCAG 2.2 AA as the release target. Provide semantic headings and landmarks, text equivalents to map points, keyboard-operable disclosure, visible focus, descriptive links, table headers/captions and status text independent of color. Support deep links, browser back, reduced motion, reflow at 320 CSS pixels and readable zoom. Keep the map optional; all six chapters remain available without JavaScript. Human screen-reader and zoom review is still required before claiming accessibility conformance. The prototype has no animation, autoplay, drag-only interface or account gate.

### Hosting and release maintenance

Recommend **static hosting on Cloudflare Pages for a future approved public edition**, using a GitHub Actions-built artifact and a custom domain. The reason is simple output, CDN delivery, HTTPS, deploy history/rollback and configurable headers without application servers. GitHub Pages is a viable lower-configuration alternative if its access and header constraints are acceptable. Sites remains an option for a later hosted iteration, but would require a deliberate decision about its source/deployment workflow alongside this repository. No host has been provisioned here.

Build only the public directory; do not publish the repository root. Before public release, render approved professional summaries and permitted source excerpts into that artifact so readers do not depend on private GitHub or Library access. Leave raw microdata, private writing samples and internal credentials out. Add a restrictive content-security policy using hashes or externalized scripts/styles, a release log and a correction/contact route approved by Espen. No account system or behavioral analytics is needed for the first release.

Proposed release flow: source/claim change → PR → generate models when affected → validate adapter and rendering → private artifact review → editorial approval → deploy that immutable artifact → verify URL, headers and source edition → retain rollback. Public deployment is a separate task. Changes to adoption, fiscal inputs or dates retain their existing approval requirements; editing a scene never grants those permissions.

## 5. Prototype scope and next decisions

The prototype is designed to answer whether the community entrance makes the program coherent and whether the evidence path earns its place in the experience. It is intentionally small: six authored scenes, an SVG neighborhood, navigation and evidence disclosures, two reused numeric views, a policy directory and three design treatments. It does not model the fictional people or local environment. Dates in the governing path describe distinct stages, not a slider through a forecast.

The next decisions are few:

1. **Confirm the editorial direction.** Recommend the community entrance with an independent evidence/casebook entrance. If the life-course direction feels more compelling, select it before commissioning substantial art or expanding the content model.
2. **Choose the first release boundary.** Recommend a candid introductory edition with financing and delivery questions visible, rather than making fully financed or whole-agenda outcome claims. This does not select a financing instrument or postpone its redesign.
3. **Select the next contrasting experience after review.** Recommend a rural/climate-preparation community, developed with distinct evidence, to test whether the concept survives beyond a walkable neighborhood. Do not expand every domain simultaneously.

Framework procurement, final hosting setup, accounts, a new forecasting engine and implementation playbooks are not decisions needed now. The stopping point is this prototype and a focused review of its direction.
