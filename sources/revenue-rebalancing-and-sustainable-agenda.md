# Revenue rebalancing and a sustainable governing agenda

Working decision plan, October 26, 2026. Implements Espen's revenue-first direction. The financing settlement and strategic spending changes remain under development; Espen decides consequential downsizing at the end. This document is the maintained reading entry for that work.

## What we are trying to decide

Project 2050 should offer a fairer fiscal settlement: recover revenue from large corporations and wealthy households, restore reliable public services, protect people against hardship, and invest in a more affordable economy. Its spending commitments must fit annual and long-run resources, including Social Security and Medicare obligations. An attractive ten-year total cannot conceal an unfunded permanent promise.

My working recommendation is to build a durable progressive core, protect early recovery and essential services, and make the scale and timing of further expansions explicit choices. I would avoid making broad new income contributions the automatic residual whenever the existing agenda costs too much. Payroll financing still has an institutional role in education and social insurance, but the combined household burden must be shown and protected.

The immediate research priority is the **reliability and additionality of receipts already counted**. The project already includes major corporate, capital-income and high-income reforms. Some are scaled from estimates predating the 2025 law; some depend on rough overlapping tax bases. We cannot promise their receipts twice or treat speculative upside as the foundation of an essential benefit.

## Construct three revenue cases before choosing spending

| Case | Substance | Role in the decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Durable core | Corporate rate/base reform; high-income rate and tax-expenditure reform; enforceable capital-income and estate provisions; supported enforcement yield | Establish resources on which recurring essentials can reasonably depend |
| Expanded progressive | Explicit incremental alternatives beyond that core, including higher corporate rates, stronger international rules, large-estate and very-high-income alternatives | Show what greater ambition buys and what avoidance, incidence and legal risks it adds |
| Adverse receipts | Weaker collections, implementation delay, joint-base erosion and loss or replacement of legally exposed instruments | Test which commitments remain deliverable and what reserve or adjustment is needed |

These are designs to price, not selected rates or scored revenue ranges. Each must use the same current-law baseline and dates. Show source-window figures separately from translated FY2030–39 estimates. Pair each expansion with an alternative that achieves similar distribution through a different instrument; do not merely add every available tax.

The first reconciliation should cover these clusters:

| Cluster | What is already counted | Consequential issue to resolve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Corporate rate and base | G1, G5, G7, portions of C38 | Score rate changes together with minimum tax, foreign rules and deduction changes; higher rates alter the yield of base broadening |
| High-income ordinary income | G3, G9, C39, C43 | Reconcile reversal of tax cuts with existing top rates, deduction limits and the already-adopted bracket above $5M; do not count C43 again |
| Capital income and wealth | G6, G11, estate increment C35, trust reforms | Gains taxed annually cannot also be fully taxed as previously untaxed gains at death; retain a durable fallback and show its timing |
| Social insurance high-income bases | H1a/H1b, SS-1/SS-2/SS-5 | Track GF versus fund receipts, overlapping investment/pass-through bases and interactions with ordinary-income reform |
| Enforcement | G8 net of the $250B receipt-risk allowance | Preserve hiring/collection lags and distinguish official legislative scoring from expected budget receipts |
| Broad contributions and protection | Education/leave and illustrative SS closer; additional income schedules | Price total household exposure and relief; do not treat earmarking as distributional protection |

The revenue ledger's `in_base=no` is not proof an instrument is available: several are already adopted through option rows. Follow the instrument into the actual integrated model. Publish one reconciliation from current law to existing counted reforms to additional alternatives.

## Determine the usable spending envelope

Start with total-government revenue and spending, including current-law commitments and debt service. Add the joint reform receipts once. Finance the chosen solvency paths, household protection, essential administration and a stated uncertainty reserve. Then identify the remaining room for expansions, distinguishing recurring operating resources from finite investment borrowing. Internal GF-to-fund transfers do not create national resources.

Present both the central annual path and adverse outcomes. Keep D3/D4 intact. Show whether debt stabilizes throughout the required years, each fund avoids depletion, and households gain at the relevant dates. Also show immediate cash costs and practical access before monetizing health/service gains. A tax change borne by corporations can still affect wages and prices; test that incidence rather than labeling it automatically harmless to workers.

## Compare three spending packages

The packages should share a costed early-recovery foundation: usable health coverage, monthly income support, meals, rental assistance, functioning applications and appeals, and participation supports. Their scale must still be tested. Protection of people unable to work or form a conventional family remains central.

1. **Recovery and reliability.** Prioritize essential coverage, benefit floors and delivery, while staging major new expansions. Identify exactly what would arrive later, who waits, and whether delay creates greater downstream costs.
2. **Balanced expansion.** Add the strongest affordability and opportunity investments within the durable revenue envelope. Compare housing supply/assistance, education/care workforce, health improvements and climate infrastructure on costs, distribution and delivery capacity—not only headline allocations.
3. **Broader transformation.** Retain more of the current agenda or expand selected high-value programs using the expanded progressive revenue case. Make the added tax instruments, uncertainty and implementation burden explicit.

The current agenda is the common comparator, not the default winner. Each package needs a table of retained, resized, delayed and replaced commitments, with ten-year and mature-year costs. Early retirement of a low-value subsidy, a smaller entitlement and a delay in construction are different choices and should not appear as interchangeable savings.

Initial candidates for comparison are the proposed capacity surge reduction; climate credits versus grid/adaptation/procurement; education component ramps and financing; health benefit expansion versus delivery and cost control; and housing expansion pace versus supply and actual lease-up. No cuts are selected here. Program spending removed from an agency's capacity category must remain visible somewhere if the underlying service is retained. Likewise, one-time savings cannot finance a permanent expansion.

## What the next completed decision packet should contain

A reconciled revenue inventory with primary-source vintages and overlap treatment; three annual financing cases; household cells covering workers, nonworkers, retirees, disability, self-employment and family types; three costed spending packages; and a recommendation explaining which combination best serves the goals. The education current-law/care boundary and early IDEA ramp belong in that accounting, not in a separate fund exercise.

The unresolved judgment for Espen will be the desired balance of breadth, benefit generosity, timing and financing—not whether an opaque solver found another tax rate. I will distinguish demonstrated results from assumptions, identify commitments that fail under adverse conditions, and recommend a package rather than leave an undifferentiated menu.
