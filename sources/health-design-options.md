## Why these choices, and their exact boundaries

### 1. Define the full benefit without borrowing a narrower precedent

**Proposed specification A:** retain existing Medicare eligibility, including disabled and other eligible beneficiaries; require the applicable Part B enrollment for a Part B benefit, subject to the completed legal crosswalk. Hearing covers assessment, fitting/adjustment, counseling or rehabilitation when indicated, clinically appropriate aids for one or both ears as needed, and necessary maintenance/repair. Cover appropriate prescription and FDA-compliant OTC paths; an OTC-only route is insufficient for people with more severe loss, while a severe-only threshold excludes people OTC devices are intended to help. Existing diagnoses and clinically indicated treatment remain covered on their existing basis. Do not require a routine assessment at a fixed annual frequency merely to receive a benefit.

Vision covers routine refraction/examination, fitting where needed, and one pair of medically appropriate glasses including frames/lenses or an appropriate contact-lens supply instead. Cost an ordinary examination/eyewear interval of two years and hearing-aid replacement once per ear in five years, with earlier service or replacement for documented changed need, failure that cannot reasonably be repaired, or loss/damage under a defined exception. No exception quantity or cost is assumed. Device warranties and repair should prevent duplicate payment; preserve existing cataract-related benefits and avoid paying twice for the same item. Optional cosmetic/premium upgrades are outside the proposed medically appropriate core; any permitted upgrade process needs informed choice and cannot hide balance billing for a covered item. These are proposed specifications, not definitions already adopted by D6.

The **two-/five-year numbers are drafting conventions from historical proposals, not clinical evidence of an optimal interval**. A clinician/beneficiary review may change them before adoption. Alternative B is the same service/device scope with clinical-need timing rather than ordinary intervals; it is not a reduced allowance or exams-only stage. No clinical threshold, dollar schedule, accessory quantity or optional-upgrade rule becomes final from this document. [E1–E3]

The House's September-2021 vision proposal used a two-year exam/device interval and dollar limits; the later House-engrossed hearing proposal used five-year aid replacement and a moderately-severe/severe/profound limitation. They are **different versions of proposed legislation**, not an enacted demonstration that a 2030 benefit can launch. Borrow their statutory hooks for covered services, supplier categories, payment and assignment; do not import all their limitations. The later FDA OTC framework further changes the design context. [E1–E3]

### 2. Make the patient share visible before selecting affordability improvements

Ordinary Part B deductible/20% coinsurance is the least additional departure from the baseline payment structure to estimate first. This is not evidence that it is the most equitable final benefit, and current sources do not establish that the existing combined HLT-2 score already prices this exact rule. Keep QMB billing protections and any other applicable assistance; do not assume every low-income beneficiary qualifies, or that every MA enrollee has supplemental coverage for the FFS bridge.

**Mechanical example, not a device price:** a $1,000 allowed covered claim after the deductible would divide $800 Medicare/$200 patient before other lawful coverage, under ordinary 80/20 rules. With a new zero-cost-sharing rule, Medicare pays $1,000 and the patient $0. That is $200 more Medicare payment for the same claim, before premium/state effects or changed use; it is not a $200 increase in national resource use for the identical item. For claims before the deductible, actual liability differs. With zero sharing, use and provider participation may also change. Neither a device price nor a population count is supplied by this example. [E4]

On either branch, propose that a person who followed the published payment route owes no **additional** bill because the MAC and plan disagree about payer responsibility. This requires a funded correction/provisional-payment and refund mechanism, not a label saying “hold harmless.” It does not silently waive ordinary lawful patient sharing. Clinical-trial MOOP/deductible rules in §422.109(e) cannot be borrowed for this new-benefit bridge. [E5]

### 3. Reuse Medicare payment machinery, with precise supplier access

Recommend direct payment to appropriately enrolled/licensed professionals and suppliers, assigned claims and a published allowed-price schedule. Price assessments and clinical services through applicable professional schedules where lawful; establish device codes/fees or a defined device/service bundle with a repair/warranty rule. A final payment schedule, inflation update and differentiation for clinically complex devices need evidence and approval. The historical hearing proposal has useful assignment and provider-definition language; its particular practitioner discount and competitive-bidding provisions are not adopted. [E1–E2]

Supplier onboarding and payment approval are separate from clinical licensure or FDA device status. For an OTC path, do not make a discretionary expensive specialist visit the default toll booth; determine what documentation and enrolled retail/online supply route Medicare needs, with referral for red flags and a supported clinical path. FDA authorization to sell OTC is not authorization to bill Medicare. No assertion is made that the present supplier category/enrollment rules already suffice. A fixed cash allowance can be investigated if Espen prefers it, but it risks uncovered residual prices and up-front household spending. It would need explicit scope, indexation, direct-pay alternatives and cost-sharing decisions. [E3]

### 4. Repair the harm, with a defined cohort and evidence rule

Recommend estimating repair from the first loss attributable to each selected repealed restriction. Different restrictions began at different times; there is no single universal January-2027 lookback. For example, the current original-law record has October-2026 funding restrictions for some immigrant cohorts and January-2027 work/renewal/retroactivity changes. Refresh all operative provisions and later rules at drafting. HLT-4 waiting-period restoration is distinct from reversing the 2025-law immigration-funding restriction. [Packet S13–S16]

Use records to restore cases without unnecessary reapplication when they establish eligibility for the relevant month. Use assisted review where the record does not establish it; preserve legitimate changes in income/residency/other applicable eligibility. Distinguish stopping future work-rule/renewal losses from correcting old denials and from paying past medical claims. Define provider resubmission, patient reimbursement/refunds, MCO reconciliation, erroneous debt collection and filing-limit relief separately. A new prospective three-month retroactivity rule for applications does not repair every termination from years earlier.

Compare full attributable-loss repair with a new twelve-month lookback, retaining any remedies already owed under current law. For a hypothetical March-2029 enactment, a rolling twelve-month window would generally omit earlier 2026–27 losses; that is an excluded cohort to show, not a free administrative simplification. No exact enactment date, cohort size or retroactive budget is selected. Marketplace relief must separately identify tax years, coverage months and APTC/PTC offsets; a tax refund does not create insurance retroactively.

Use the completed [CMS-IMP-02 pilot](health-cms-imp-02-pilot.md) and [contract follow-up](health-cms-imp-02-evidence-followup.md) to scope actual changes, state effort, reusable work, approvals and cash. The ONE June-2029 contract boundary, missing current APD/order chain and different SNAP/Medicaid requirements remain relevant. Neither a contractual ceiling nor removed verification work is a funded remedy budget or spendable saving.

### 5. Choose gap-closure architecture by the coverage it must deliver

**Recommended development priority A:** define a federal Medicaid-equivalent backstop in nonexpansion/noncooperating states, with explicit federal administrator, benefit package, provider/payment route, eligibility/default/opt-out data authority, accessible appeals and continuity. It must work without making an otherwise-eligible person's coverage conditional on a state's new expansion agreement. Existing Medicare contractors may be reusable, but no contract, statutory authority or spare capacity is assumed. A federal backstop is not the HLT-3 public plan, whose adopted 2031 opening/2032 auto-enrollment dates remain.

**Alternative priority B:** specify a Marketplace route with explicit below-poverty eligibility, premiums, cost-sharing assistance, covered services, provider participation and continuous enrollment. Require a benefit-by-benefit crosswalk including nonemergency transport, continuity and essential Medicaid services; “99% actuarial value” or zero premiums alone does not mean zero charges or the same services. The 2021 House proposals supply both federal-program and Marketplace precedent, but neither is current authority for Project 2050. Historical temporary dates, eligibility and allowances cannot be copied as policy. [E1, E6]

A Marketplace bridge before a federal backstop is a possible **additional** design, not automatically selected by A. It can add two transitions, duplication and uncertainty about who pays when the backstop starts. Develop it only if evidence shows the earlier access is worth those burdens. On present evidence, neither route supports a national 2029 receipt claim; preparations may start sooner than enrollment and care. Selecting a priority here does not approve broader eligibility or a weaker benefit than canonical HLT-1.

### 6. Connect HCBS financing to actual care and continuing capacity

**Recommended priority A:** specify enhanced federal matching for eligible HCBS expenditures, combined with a separately scoped state/provider implementation and workforce component where needed. First examine rate adequacy, retention and filling already-authorized hours through existing providers; then identify what new slots/workers can actually be supported. Set state share, eligible expenditures, maintenance/anti-supplantation rules, wage/retention pass-through and payment verification explicitly. Support existing services and workforce without promising that funding immediately clears waiting lists.

ARP §9817 is a useful historical example of enhanced matching tied to additional HCBS activity and spending plans; **its ten-point rate, dates, amount and procedures are not proposed defaults for 2029**. Distinguish the federal increment, retained state spending and new service resources, and identify continuing funding when the down payment expires. Existing §1915(c)/(i)/(j)/(k) channels and the 2025-law HCBS baseline need their own overlap map. [E7–E8; packet S13]

**Alternative priority B:** targeted grants for specific workforce, provider, accessibility and systems bottlenecks, with services financed separately through existing Medicaid or another approved channel. It may reach a defined capacity gap more directly, but an award is not a recurring reimbursement stream. Neither channel automatically solves state refusal or workforce supply. CMS-IMP-02/08 and relevant partner costs apply once; 90/75% systems matching does not price HCBS benefit payments or all caseworker work. No FMAP percentage, grant total, start date or new entitlement is chosen.

## What the built tools changed in the recommendations

The [generated model checks](health-design-model-checks.md) and [JSON/source hashes](health-design-model-checks.json) are reproducible through `index/tools/health/decision_analysis.py` after the common runner. The adapter calls the **existing** fiscal `debt_roll`, calendar `run` and capacity `assess` functions; it does not replace them, alter their inputs or invent a new health microsimulation.

- The working-capacity central calendar case completes R2 on **October 8, 2029**, leaving **85 calendar days** to January 1, 2030; favorable leaves 189 days and difficult is unfinished. These are uncalibrated conditional procedure outputs, not enactment or CMS readiness forecasts. They establish why “summer R2” cannot be assumed as guaranteed engineering time. Preserve the 2031 fallback; carry 2030 feasibility as conditional on actual enactment and the critical path. No new floor burden was guessed.
- Under the existing interim surge cash proxy, the smallest modeled maturity headroom for an added unfinanced cost growing with GDP from 2040 is about **$3.297B in FY2040**. It is not a pay-for, score or available allocation. This supports pricing permanent operations and any cost-sharing improvement before adoption; it does not justify automatically reducing benefits. The fiscal engine's combined HLT-2 calibration cannot isolate hearing/vision by moving its title-wide start or dividing its total.
- An **illustrative unit**, not a cost estimate: unfinanced additional $1B FY2030 GF outlays adds about $1.504B to FY2040 debt under the existing interest convention; shifting the same $1B setup from FY2030 to FY2029 adds about $0.058B debt by FY2040 through interest while adding no lifetime primary spending. The real bridge must establish net federal cost first; no borrowing or acceleration financing is approved.
- The capacity module still returns FY2029 cash unpriced and acceleration validation incomplete. All seven relevant CMS package increments/cash lines remain unknown. The completed pilot supplies actual cost-chain questions, not an envelope to spend twice.
- The incidence model already separates combined HLT-2 benefits, premiums and MA extras; it does not supply an enrollee-level H/V copayment distribution. The new beneficiary example explains a tradeoff the aggregate D3 average cannot answer. No poverty rerun or invented household distribution was used.

Thus the recommendation remains ambitious on full coverage and remedy, cautious about committing dates and unpriced permanent resources, and concrete about which specifications need a decision. Passing the common rebuild is validation of execution/accounting, not the primary basis for clinical or legal recommendations.
