## PREFACE: PURPOSE AND PHILOSOPHY OF THIS DOCUMENT

This document is the first iteration of a living, working framework for:

1. **A 2026 midterm campaign platform** capable of winning a House majority and strengthening the Senate
2. **A 2028 presidential campaign platform** capable of winning the Electoral College and a popular mandate
3. **A governance blueprint** for a hypothetical Democratic federal trifecta beginning January 20, 2029

Unlike previous Democratic efforts, this framework insists that campaign and governance be designed together from the start. The lesson of Project 2025 is that power comes to those who plan before they have it. The lesson of the Biden administration is that governing without a pre-built, coordinated execution plan — however good the intentions — produces diffuse wins that are politically inarticulable and easily reversed.

This framework proceeds from a clear set of convictions:

- **Populism is the terrain.** Economic grievance is real and legitimate. Democrats must not cede it to authoritarian pseudo-populism.
- **Specificity builds trust.** Voters don't trust "we'll work on it." They trust "here's the bill, here's what it does, here's when."
- **Reversal isn't enough.** Restoring what existed before 2025 is necessary but insufficient. The conditions that made Trump possible — inequality, rigged systems, non-responsive government — predate him. They must be addressed.
- **Speed and sequencing matter.** A Democratic trifecta will be time-limited. Actions must be sequenced by durability, speed of impact, and coalition maintenance.
- **Democracy protection is prerequisite.** No other agenda survives if elections can be stolen, voting rights suppressed, or judicial independence erased.

## PART ONE: POLITICAL ANALYSIS — THE LANDSCAPE AS OF MARCH 2026

### 1.1 What Happened and Why It Matters

The 2024 election loss was a multi-factor failure, but its core drivers are now clearer:

- **Economic perception gap**: Inflation recovery didn't feel real to millions of households. Voters blamed incumbents.
- **Cost-of-living specificity**: Housing, groceries, childcare, healthcare — voters wanted to hear Democrats name the villain (corporations, monopolies, rigged tax system) and give a specific fix. They got process language instead.
- **Cultural exhaustion**: Voters who might have been persuadable tuned out when they felt lectured rather than heard.
- **Turnout and enthusiasm collapse**: Young and non-college voters saw insufficient reason to turn out in decisive numbers.
- **Messaging incoherence**: Biden's genuine legislative wins (IRA, CHIPS, Bipartisan Infrastructure) were never translated into a coherent narrative of material change.

The 2025-2026 period has changed the terrain significantly. Trump's second term has:
- Signed the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (July 4, 2025), the largest cut to healthcare and the social safety net in American history: $1.02 trillion from Medicaid, causing 11.8 million Americans to lose coverage from Medicaid provisions alone; combined with the expiration of ACA enhanced subsidies, approximately 15–17 million people total lost coverage; ~$175B from SNAP; student loan caps and restrictions
- Deployed DOGE to gut federal agencies — USAID effectively dismantled, thousands of career civil servants fired, sensitive government data systems accessed and copied
- Signed 230+ executive orders, many of which have faced legal challenge; SCOTUS struck down IEEPA-based tariffs in February 2026
- Conducted mass immigration deportations, chilling effect in communities across the country
- Paused enforcement of the Foreign Corrupt Practices Act
- Moved to politicize the DOJ, the Fed, regulatory agencies, and the federal civil service
- Overseen US-Israel military strikes on Iran (mentioned in emerging reporting)
- Escalated trade war with China, Canada, Mexico, and other allies

**Approval is low, discontent is real, but Democrats must not count on anger alone.** Anger activates the base; it does not build durable majorities. The 2026 and 2028 strategy must pair anti-Trump contrast with a compelling positive vision.

### 1.2 The Voter Coalition Architecture

A winning Democratic coalition in 2026 and 2028 must hold AND expand:

**The Base (must maximize turnout)**
- Young voters (18-29): Respond to student debt, housing, climate, anti-oligarchy, genuine idealism, and demonstrated action (not just promises)
- Black voters: Especially in Southern states; respond to economic equity, voting rights, healthcare, anti-racism in concrete policy
- Latino voters: Heterogeneous; economic issues dominate; immigration matters but economy and respect dominate more; must not be treated as monolith
- College-educated suburban voters: Won in 2018-2022; need reassurance on competence, rule of law, stability, and economic fairness

**The Persuadable Expansion (must make inroads)**
- Non-college white voters (especially women): Greatest 2024 loss; respond to healthcare, drug prices, rural hospital closures, cost of living, anti-corruption — NOT cultural identity framing
- Rural voters: Not a lost cause where rural hospitals are closing because of OBBBA, broadband is absent, and farm incomes are squeezed by trade wars
- Small business owners: Harmed by tariff chaos, regulatory whiplash; respond to stable rules, fair competition, anti-monopoly
- Older voters on Medicare/Medicaid: OBBBA effects will be felt viscerally; this is a mobilization opportunity

**Coalition Coherence Test**: Every major policy should be able to answer: *Who does this materially help? Can a non-college voter in rural Ohio understand and feel it? Does it build or fracture coalition sub-groups?*

### 1.3 The Tension to Navigate

A genuine internal party debate exists between:
- **Economic populist/structural change camp** (Warren, Sanders, Mamdani, AOC): Tax the rich, break up monopolies, democratic socialism framing, big structural change
- **Moderate/pro-growth camp** (Searchlight Institute, some centrists): Shed "woke" baggage, appeal to business community, avoid cultural alienation

**This framework's position**: These are not opposites. The correct synthesis is **economic populism with competence and inclusivity** — name the villains (oligarchs, monopolies, corrupt politicians), deliver material benefits, demonstrate governmental efficiency and accountability, and refuse to let cultural wedge issues define the party. Elizabeth Warren is right that you cannot build a durable big tent by flattening policy to please billionaire donors. The Hill is right that cultural alienation matters. The answer is: fight hard on economic fairness and worker power; be thoughtful and grounded (not moralistic or preachy) on social issues; be firm but humane on immigration.

## PART TWO: THE CAMPAIGN MESSAGE AND PLATFORM FRAME

### 2.1 Core Narrative

**Thesis**: The system is rigged — against working people, by and for the very wealthy — and we will fix it with specific laws, not just speeches.

**Enemy**: Not Trump personally (he may not be relevant by 2028) but the system of oligarchic influence, corporate capture of government, and rigged rules that concentrate wealth and power at the top. This is a coherent and verifiable villain that mobilizes a broad coalition.

**Hero**: Working Americans, small business owners, families — "the people who actually built this country."

**The Promise (must be specific and measurable)**:
- Your healthcare is protected and expanded
- Your rent will come down
- Your wages will go up and your boss can't steal them
- Your vote counts and can't be suppressed
- Your tax dollars go to *you*, not to billionaires
- Your government will actually work

**Brand words**: *Fair. Accountable. Ours.*

### 2.2 The "Seven for the First Hundred Days" Campaign Promise

Drawing lessons from FDR's 100 Days and Contract with America, we propose a specific public commitment of concrete first-100-days legislative and executive actions. Specificity is the antidote to distrust.

1. **Restore your healthcare** — Executive order on Day 1 reopening ACA enrollment; legislation to reverse OBBBA Medicaid cuts
2. **Tax the rich, not you** — Legislation to reverse TCJA tax cuts for top 1%, fund middle-class tax relief
3. **Crack down on price gouging** — Day 1 executive order on grocery/drug/housing price gouging; FTC revitalization
4. **Clean up the corruption** — Inspector General restoration, DOJ independence protection, DOGE audit
5. **Rebuild what they broke** — Emergency executive order restoring gutted agencies; federal workforce protection
6. **Protect your vote** — Day 1 filibuster carve-out vote in the Senate, putting every senator on record; if it passes, enact the John Lewis Voting Rights Act immediately; if any Democrat blocks it, America will know who they are and they will answer for it in the next primary. Automatic voter registration executive push in parallel.
7. **Build housing** — Emergency housing executive authority; federal-local housing partnership program

### 2.3 What We Won't Do — The Credibility Firewall

Every Democratic candidate should be able to recite these commitments without hesitation. They are the preemptive answer to every Republican attack line:

- **"We will not raise taxes on any household earning under $400,000."** Not one dollar. Full stop.
- **"We will not cut Social Security or Medicare benefits."** We will expand them and protect their solvency.
- **"We will not open the borders."** We will enforce immigration law — fairly, constitutionally, with due process.
- **"We are not coming for your gas stove, your pickup truck, or your way of life."** We are coming for the corporations that rig energy markets and gouge you at the pump.
- **"We will not punish success."** We will stop success from rigging the rules against everyone else.
- **"We will not govern by executive order alone."** We will pass laws that last.

These are not political cover — they are genuine constraints on the agenda, and they should be stated as such. Governing within constraints builds trust. Pretending constraints don't exist destroys it.

### 2.4 What Success Looks Like — Governing Win Conditions

Project 2029 defines success in concrete, measurable terms. By January 2033, if the governing plan has been well-executed:

- **Healthcare**: Uninsured rate below 5% (down from ~15% post-OBBBA); drug prices reduced by 25%+ for Medicare beneficiaries; zero rural hospital closures attributable to federal policy
- **Economic fairness**: Real median household income up 8–12% over the term; child poverty rate at or below 10% (sustained ARP-era gains); wage growth exceeding inflation for the bottom 60% of earners
- **Housing**: 2 million additional affordable units built or funded; rent-to-income ratio stabilized in top 50 metros
- **Climate**: Clean electricity share of grid at 70%+ (up from ~40%); U.S. emissions on trajectory for 50% reduction by 2035; 300,000 Civilian Climate Corps jobs created
- **Democracy**: Voting rights preclearance restored; automatic voter registration active in 45+ states; dark money disclosure required for all major federal election spending; zero IGs fired for political reasons
- **Fiscal**: Deficit-to-GDP ratio improved relative to OBBBA baseline; wealth tax enacted and withstanding legal challenge; IRS enforcement collecting $300B+ in additional revenue annually
- **Government trust**: Presidential approval for government competence and honesty at highest sustained level since early 2000s; IG offices fully staffed; zero federal workforce political purges

These are the metrics a second-term campaign is run on. They should be public from Day 1.

### 2.5 The Peace Connection — Domestic Agenda Meets Global Stability

The user's original mandate includes "promote peace." This is not a separate foreign policy add-on — it is woven through every chapter:

- **USAID restoration** is peace-building: development investment prevents the conflicts and displacement that cost far more to respond to militarily
- **Climate investment** is a national security agenda: climate displacement is already a documented driver of regional conflict, migration pressure, and state fragility
- **Immigration reform** is partly a peace dividend: reducing the violence, corruption, and economic desperation that drives migration from Central America requires the kind of sustained foreign aid and development investment the Trump administration gutted
- **Anti-corruption reform** builds peaceful democracy: governments that are accountable and competent are less likely to fail, creating instability that draws U.S. military engagement

The foreign policy chapter (forthcoming) will develop the full peace and international agenda — rebuilding NATO, supporting Ukraine's sovereignty, managing China competition without military escalation, rejoining multilateral institutions, and restoring U.S. credibility as a nation that keeps its commitments. But every chapter in this document is also a peace document.

The 2026 campaign must center on a handful of vivid, provable contrasts. Democrats should campaign on:

### 3.1 Healthcare

- **Contrast**: Republicans took healthcare from up to 17 million Americans — 11.8 million from Medicaid cuts alone, and 5 million more from the expiration of ACA enhanced subsidies — to give tax cuts to the top 1%.
- **Promise**: We will restore Medicaid and the ACA. We will add a public option. We will cap insulin at $35.
- **Proof points**: Name specific OBBBA provisions and specific constituents harmed; name the rural hospitals closing.
- **Policy specifics for campaigning**: Restore ACA enhanced subsidies, repeal OBBBA Medicaid work requirements, restore Planned Parenthood Medicaid funding, pass Medicare negotiation authority for all drugs.

### 3.2 Economic Fairness and Cost of Living

- **Contrast**: Republicans lowered taxes for billionaires and raised costs for everyone else (tariffs = regressive tax on consumers).
- **Promise**: We will make corporations pay their fair share. We will pass a $20 minimum wage. We will cap credit card interest. We will break up monopolies driving up grocery prices.
- **Key policies**: Reverse TCJA for households over $400K; corporate minimum tax; Anti-Price Gouging Act; worker right to organize; $20 federal minimum wage.

### 3.3 Corruption and Democracy

- **Contrast**: DOGE gave Elon Musk access to your Social Security data. Trump paused corruption enforcement for his foreign business partners. They want to make sure you can't vote them out.
- **Promise**: We will ban billionaire-funded shadow government. We will restore independent oversight. We will protect your vote with federal guarantee.
- **Key policies**: DOGE Accountability Act; Inspector General Independence Act; Dark Money Disclosure Act; Automatic Voter Registration Act.

### 3.4 Jobs, Workers, and the Economy

- **Contrast**: Tariff chaos hurt farmers, small businesses, and consumers while enriching a few well-connected industries. They gutted worker protections.
- **Promise**: We will build a stable trade policy that protects American workers (not just corporations). We will strengthen unions. We will invest in American manufacturing with strings attached.
- **Key policies**: Sensible industrial policy that preserves IRA clean energy jobs; PRO Act; overtime protection restoration; worker ownership incentive act.

### 3.5 Immigration (The Hard One)

The platform must be direct, humane, and specific — not defensive.

- **Contrast**: Trump's mass deportations terrorized communities, separated families, and left crops rotting in fields. They ended legal immigration pathways and made America less safe by diverting resources from actual crime.
- **Promise**: We will secure the border with smart investment in technology, personnel, and legal processing. We will create an orderly, legal immigration system with a pathway to citizenship for DREAMers and long-term residents. We will not weaponize immigration enforcement against law-abiding families.
- **What to avoid**: Defensive posturing. Framing as "compassion vs. enforcement" binary. The message is: their approach is expensive, cruel, and ineffective. Ours is secure, orderly, and humane.

## PART FOUR: THE 2028 PRESIDENTIAL PLATFORM

The 2028 platform expands the midterm platform with bolder structural changes that require a presidential mandate to achieve. The candidate should be prepared to defend and explain each plank in populist, material terms.

### 4.1 Healthcare: Universal Coverage

- **Goal**: Move toward universal healthcare coverage within the term
- **Path**: Medicare public option available to all (not just those without coverage); auto-enrollment for uninsured; pharmaceutical price controls; long-term care expansion
- **Longer horizon**: Move toward single-payer or Medicare for All as the demonstrated success of the public option creates political momentum
- **Institutional fix**: Restore and expand FDA independence; restore CDC and NIH funding gutted in 2025; rebuild public health infrastructure

### 4.2 Economic Democracy and Anti-Oligarchy

- **Wealth Tax**: 2% annual tax on wealth over $50M, 3% over $1B (Warren plan)
- **Corporate Accountability**: CEO pay ratio disclosure requirements; shareholder democracy reform; corporate board worker representation requirements (co-determination)
- **Anti-Monopoly**: Aggressive antitrust enforcement; break up platform monopolies; revitalize FTC/DOJ Antitrust Division
- **Worker Power**: PRO Act; sectoral bargaining; worker ownership incentives; profit-sharing requirements for firms receiving federal contracts
- **Financial Reform**: Reinstate Glass-Steagall-like separation; crack down on private equity strip-mining; cap credit card interest rates; expand postal banking

### 4.3 Housing

- **Federal Housing Investment**: $500B over 10 years in affordable housing construction; federal land made available for housing; pre-empt exclusionary zoning at federal level (tied to federal infrastructure funding)
- **Renter Protections**: National anti-price gouging standard for rent; right to renew; transparency in corporate landlord pricing algorithms
- **Homeownership**: First-generation homebuyer credits; Community Land Trust expansion; reform and expand LIHTC

### 4.4 Climate and Energy

- **Restore and Expand IRA**: Restore and expand Inflation Reduction Act clean energy provisions gutted by Trump executive orders
- **Carbon Price**: Implement carbon pricing with border adjustment (protecting US manufacturers against carbon dumping)
- **Justice40**: Restore and strengthen commitment that 40% of climate investments go to disadvantaged communities
- **Civilian Climate Corps**: Employ hundreds of thousands in climate resilience, conservation, and clean energy installation
- **Rejoin Paris Agreement**: Immediately, Day 1; host major climate leadership summit within first 100 days
- **Grid Modernization**: Federal investment in transmission infrastructure; distributed energy; grid security
- **End Fossil Fuel Subsidies**: Redirect $20B+ in annual fossil fuel subsidies to clean energy transition

### 4.5 Education

- **Student Debt**: Broad debt cancellation up to $50K through legislative authority; income-driven repayment reform
- **Free Community College**: Universal free community college and vocational training
- **K-12 Equity Funding**: Federal formula reform to equalize K-12 funding across wealthy and poor districts
- **Restore Department of Education**: Reconstitute and strengthen; restore Title IX protections; restore civil rights enforcement
- **Universal Pre-K**: Federal investment in universal pre-K for 3- and 4-year-olds

### 4.6 Democracy and Anti-Corruption Reform

This is the structural foundation without which all other reforms are fragile.

- **Voting Rights**: John Lewis Voting Rights Advancement Act; Automatic Voter Registration Act; Election Day national holiday; independent redistricting commissions federally mandated
- **Campaign Finance**: DISCLOSE Act; Small Dollar Democracy matching program; overturning Citizens United (constitutional amendment pursuit and statutory limits in the meantime)
- **Anti-Corruption**: Presidential financial disclosure requirements by law; blind trust requirements; revolving door ban expansion; STOCK Act enforcement; lobbying reform
- **Supreme Court Reform**: 18-year term limits for justices (by statute — appointees serve 18 active years, then remain in advisory capacity); binding ethics code with enforcement mechanism; expansion of Court to restore balance (politically difficult but necessary to address manufactured imbalance)
- **Congressional Reform**: Multi-member districts with ranked-choice voting; Senate filibuster reform (carve-outs for voting rights and democracy protection legislation at minimum)
- **Institutional Independence**: Restore independence of DOJ, FEC, FTC, CFPB, NLRB from White House political interference; codify Humphrey's Executor protections

### 4.7 Immigration Reform

- **Comprehensive Reform**: Path to citizenship for 11M undocumented residents, prioritizing long-term residents, DREAMers, farmworkers, essential workers
- **Legal Immigration Reform**: Expand legal immigration pathways; streamline visa processing; eliminate green card backlogs; restore refugee admissions to historic levels
- **Border Management**: Smart investment in border technology and personnel; immigration court funding to eliminate backlog; regional asylum-processing partnerships
- **End the Weaponization**: Restore constitutional due process for immigration proceedings; end family separation; close private detention centers; restore Temporary Protected Status for eligible populations
- **Fix the Root Causes**: Restore foreign aid (through a reformed USAID successor); invest in Central American/Caribbean development compacts to address migration drivers

### 4.8 Foreign Policy and Defense

- **Rebuild Alliances**: Restore and deepen NATO commitments; repair bilateral relationships damaged by Trump's second term; rejoin WHO and other multilateral institutions
- **Ukraine**: Restore security assistance; support diplomatic resolution that respects Ukrainian sovereignty
- **Iran/Middle East**: Diplomatic framework to de-escalate; support for two-state solution with accountability mechanisms; restore JCPOA or successor nuclear deal
- **China**: Competitive but managed relationship; reduce strategic dependence on Chinese supply chains through allied manufacturing; avoid military escalation over Taiwan through deterrence and diplomacy
- **USAID Rebuild**: Reconstitute international development capacity; restore HIV/AIDS, food security, and humanitarian programs (estimated 700K+ deaths from DOGE cuts to USAID programs per researchers)
- **Defense Reform**: Support strong defense while reforming acquisition, auditing Pentagon spending (which has never passed an audit), and redirecting savings to human security needs
- **Arms Control**: Restore New START successor; lead global AI weapons governance initiative

## PART FIVE: THE GOVERNANCE PLAN — IMPLEMENTATION BLUEPRINT

*This section provides the detailed governing roadmap for a hypothetical Democratic trifecta beginning January 20, 2029. It is organized by timeline and priority.*

### 5.1 Pre-Inauguration (November 2028 — January 2029)

**Transition Planning Infrastructure**
- Stand up a fully staffed transition operation immediately after election; hire 500+ policy and implementation experts before Day 1
- Conduct immediate inventory of all Trump executive orders, appointments, regulatory actions, and agency damage
- Recruit experienced career civil service leaders for immediate reappointment
- Prepare 50-100 Day 1 and Day 1-30 executive actions in draft form, pre-reviewed by DOJ OLC
- Brief Senate on priority confirmation slate; prepare for expedited hearing schedule
- Establish a "Damage Assessment Task Force" across all agencies to document and cost what needs repair

**Legislative Pre-Coordination**
- Work with House and Senate leadership on legislative calendar; identify Day 1-100 reconciliation package
- Negotiate pre-agreement on filibuster reform for democracy protection legislation
- Prepare standalone bills for immediate introduction in each priority area
- Commission Congressional Budget Office preliminary scoring for major legislation

### 5.2 Day One Executive Actions

**Healthcare and Social Safety Net**
- EO reopening ACA special enrollment period immediately; directing HHS to restore Medicaid guidance
- EO directing CMS to pause implementation of OBBBA Medicaid work requirements pending legislation
- EO restoring Planned Parenthood and reproductive health providers to Medicaid

**Government Integrity**
- EO restoring all Inspectors General fired by Trump
- EO restoring DOJ independence norms; ending political interference in prosecutorial decisions
- EO revoking Trump's pardons and commutations (where legally contestable)
- EO restoring civil service merit protections; ending Schedule F
- EO directing DOGE audit — full accounting of what was accessed, changed, deleted, and where funds went
- EO restoring all fired federal employees in unlawful terminations (pending legislation confirming)

**Climate and Environment**
- EO rejoining Paris Climate Agreement
- EO restoring EPA's authority and regulatory posture
- EO pausing implementation of Trump energy dominance orders pending review
- EO restoring methane regulations and clean car standards
- EO restoring Biden-era NEPA guidance

**International**
- EO restoring refugee admissions to 125,000/year
- EO restoring USAID/humanitarian aid programs immediately
- EO directing State Department to restore multilateral engagement
- EO restoring foreign corrupt practices enforcement

**Immigration**
- EO ending "Remain in Mexico" policy successor
- EO ending family separation as policy
- EO restoring DACA and directing DHS to protect current DACA recipients
- EO directing ICE to prioritize enforcement against violent offenders only

### 5.3 Days 1-30: Emergency Legislative Push

**The Restoration and Relief Reconciliation Package** (using reconciliation for budget matters)
- Repeal OBBBA healthcare cuts; restore Medicaid to pre-OBBBA eligibility; restore ACA subsidies
- Reverse TCJA tax cuts for income over $400K and corporations; use revenue for below
- Child Tax Credit expansion (restore to ARP levels, make permanent)
- SNAP restoration
- Emergency rural hospital stabilization fund

This package is the non-negotiable first act. It restores what was taken from people and pays for itself with tax fairness. It is the clearest possible contrast statement.

### 5.4 Days 30-100: Democracy and Accountability Package

**Legislative**
- John Lewis Voting Rights Advancement Act: Restore and strengthen preclearance; nationwide automatic voter registration; same-day registration; early voting minimums
- DISCLOSE Act: Full disclosure of dark money in elections
- Presidential Accountability Act: Financial disclosure requirements by law; blind trust requirement; anti-nepotism statute strengthening
- Inspector General Independence Act: Codify protections against political removal
- Schedule F Reversal Act: Codify civil service protections; ban Schedule F-style reclassification
- STOCK Act Enforcement Act: Close loopholes; automatic enforcement; ban congressional stock trading

**Executive**
- Begin Senate confirmation of 200+ priority appointments; use recess appointments where available
- Launch DOJ Office of Democratic Integrity (audit of Trump DOJ actions)
- Commission full forensic audit of DOGE activities; refer criminal matters to revitalized DOJ
- Reconstitute FEC with independent members; restore CFPB and FTC independence
- Appoint new FTC and DOJ Antitrust Division leadership with mandate for aggressive enforcement

### 5.5 Days 100-365: The Building Agenda

**Healthcare**
- Medicare Public Option Act: Create public option available to all Americans; funded through Medicare trust fund expansion
- Prescription Drug Fairness Act: Medicare negotiation for all drugs; drug pricing transparency; price-gouging penalties
- Mental Health Parity Enforcement Act: Mandatory insurance coverage for mental health at physical health levels
- Long-Term Care Act: Create federal long-term care insurance program
- Rural Health Stabilization Fund: $50B for rural hospital rebuilding beyond OBBBA damage

**Economic Fairness**
- American Worker Protection Act (overtime restoration + wage theft enforcement — budget-adjacent provisions via reconciliation; full PRO Act introduced and floor vote held in Year 1, with passage dependent on filibuster carve-out, realistically Year 3)
- *Note: The PRO Act in full requires either 60 Senate votes or a filibuster carve-out. It will be introduced, voted on, and put every senator on record in Year 1. Passage is a Year 3 target once democratic infrastructure is secured.*
- Federal Minimum Wage Act: $20 minimum wage phased in over 3 years; indexed to inflation thereafter
- Anti-Price Gouging Act: FTC authority to penalize unjustified price increases in essential goods
- Corporate Accountability Act: CEO pay ratio disclosure; shareholder proxy access; worker board representation in companies over 1,000 employees
- Antitrust Restoration Act: Reverse Trump-era merger approvals; direct DOJ/FTC to bring cases against platform monopolies

**Housing**
- American Housing Investment Act: $300B over 10 years for affordable housing construction; federal land for housing; pre-empt exclusionary zoning through federal funding conditions
- Renters' Rights Act: National anti-price-gouging standards; right to lease renewal; ban on algorithmic rent price coordination
- Community Land Trust Expansion Act
- First-Generation Homebuyer Tax Credit

**Climate**
- Climate Restoration Act: Restore IRA provisions; expand clean energy tax credits; create domestic clean manufacturing supply chain program
- Carbon Price and Dividend Act: $50/ton starting price, rising $15/year; dividends distributed to all Americans as monthly checks (revenue-neutral, progressive)
- Clean Electricity Standard: 100% clean electricity by 2035 with reliability backstop
- Civilian Climate Corps Authorization: 300,000 jobs in climate resilience and conservation
- Grid Modernization Act: $200B for transmission and distribution modernization

**Education**
- American Opportunity Act: Free community college and vocational training; restore public service loan forgiveness; income-driven repayment at 5% of discretionary income
- Student Debt Relief Act: Broad cancellation up to $50K; address OBBBA student loan caps
- Universal Pre-K Act: Federal-state partnership for universal pre-K
- School Funding Equity Act: Reform federal funding formula to close rich/poor district gap

**Infrastructure and Transportation**
- National High-Speed Rail Act: Federal authority and investment for intercity rail network; connect 50 largest metro areas within 15 years
- Broadband Universal Access Act: Complete Biden-era broadband buildout; ensure rural and low-income access
- Clean Transit Investment: Urban and rural transit; electric bus fleets; first-last mile solutions
- Emergency Preparedness Infrastructure Act: Climate-resilient infrastructure standards; FEMA reform and expansion

### 5.6 Year Two and Beyond: Structural Reform

**Tax System Overhaul** (requires sustained political capital)
- Wealth Tax: 2% on net worth above $50M; 3% above $1B; invested in healthcare and housing
- Capital Gains Reform: Tax capital gains as ordinary income for income over $1M
- Corporate Tax Reform: Raise corporate rate to 28%; close offshore tax haven loopholes; minimum global corporate tax enforcement
- Estate Tax Restoration: Restore to 2009 levels (45% on estates over $3.5M)
- Financial Transactions Tax: 0.1% tax on stock, bond, and derivative trades; revenue for public investment

**Democracy Structural Reforms** (require either constitutional amendment or court-durable legislation)
- Supreme Court Reform Act: 18-year term limits; binding ethics code with enforcement; court expansion from 9 to 13 (to restore balance)
- Electoral Count Reform Act 2.0: Further strengthen 2022 reform
- National Popular Vote: Support interstate compact or constitutional amendment
- Washington DC and Puerto Rico Statehood
- Senate Reform: Filibuster carve-outs for voting rights, budget bills, judicial nominations
- Anti-Gerrymandering: Federal mandate for independent redistricting commissions

**Financial System Reform**
- Modern Glass-Steagall Act: Separate commercial and investment banking
- Private Equity Reform Act: Carried interest elimination; anti-stripping rules; worker notification requirements
- Postal Banking: Restore postal banking for basic financial services
- Public Option for Banking: State-chartered public banks; expansion of credit unions

**Rebuilding International Institutions**
- Restore and reconstitute USAID (possibly renamed, with accountability reforms)
- Lead a Democracy Alliance Summit in Year 1
- Push for reformed global financial architecture to address developing world debt
- Climate finance: Meet and expand US obligations to international climate fund

### 5.7 Protecting the Reforms: Making Them Durable

One of the deepest lessons of the Obama and Biden years is that executive action is fragile. Laws are durable. This framework prioritizes:

1. **Legislation over executive orders wherever possible.** Every EO should have a companion bill.
2. **Agency institutional design.** Restore and strengthen statutory independence of agencies; codify norms as law.
3. **State-level coordination.** Use federal funding, guidance, and partnership to embed reforms at the state level.
4. **Public accountability and transparency.** Every major program should have real-time public dashboards, inspectors general, and annual Congressional reporting requirements.
5. **Coalition maintenance.** Year-1 and Year-2 actions should prioritize delivering visible material benefits that voters can feel — that is the foundation for Year-3 and Year-4 structural changes.
