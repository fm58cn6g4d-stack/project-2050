At home

Room to plan beyond Friday.

A reliable income helps a family plan. Affordable housing gives that plan a foundation. Child care and paid leave make room for work, relationships and the unexpected. Each part does more when the others are there.

Policy designs supporting the story

  • Meals at school and food support in summer
  • Monthly support for raising children
  • Affordable child care and pre-K
  • Paid time to care
  • Building more homes
  • Help paying the rent
  • Repairing public housing
Research questions and delivery conditions

An award must become a usable lease. Payments must reach eligible families, and housing and care supply must grow alongside assistance. The annual AFA model still needs exact statutory mapping; it cannot determine this family's eligibility or net gain.

The care visit

Care that leaves room for a life.

Affordable coverage can protect a household’s finances. Care at home can give an older or disabled person more control over daily life. Better jobs for care workers help make that support dependable. These are parts of the same promise.

Policy designs supporting the story

  • Paid time to care
  • Restoring affordable coverage
  • More complete Medicare coverage
  • A public health-insurance option
  • Care at home and a stronger care workforce
Research questions and delivery conditions

Coverage is only part of access. Providers, pay, state systems, trained staff and routes to correct mistakes all matter. The completed CMS/Oregon pilot identifies real delivery dependencies; incremental costs and FY2029 cash remain unpriced. Earlier restoration work has not changed adopted launch dates.

Along the river

Prepare for the water that still comes.

A cleaner energy system reduces the pollution driving climate change. A stronger grid and better homes make that system useful in daily life. Preparing for hazards protects people while the climate continues to change.

Policy designs supporting the story

  • Investment in clean energy
  • A climate dividend for households
  • A cleaner electricity system
  • Practical support for the energy transition
  • A stronger, more accountable grid
  • Disaster preparation and recovery
  • Building with climate risk in mind
Research questions and delivery conditions

Disaster and flood-insurance costs remain unscored. Local hazards, maintenance, displacement and who bears the cost require place-specific evidence. A climate-bill merger is under study, not adopted. No national emissions calculation can establish that this neighborhood will be safe.

The workshop

A say in how work changes.

A new technology can make work easier without giving workers more security. Bargaining power, fair standards, learning opportunities and support through a job change help people shape what progress means for them.

Policy designs supporting the story

  • Support when technology changes your job
  • Human review of automated public decisions
  • A stronger voice at work
  • Fair standards for care and service work
  • Apprenticeships into useful work
Research questions and delivery conditions

Training needs employers and real opportunities. AI-4's measurement and trigger require specification, and its effects depend on how work changes. Entry-level hiring credits and a transitional employment guarantee are options, not selected promises. This scene does not predict employment or wages in 2050.

The public library

A government you can question.

People need more than a chance to speak. They need fair elections, rights that can be enforced, institutions that respond and public places where collective life can happen. A government capable of delivering should also be open to challenge.

Policy designs supporting the story

  • Public institutions with the capacity to serve
  • Fair access to elections
  • Libraries, parks and places to gather
  • A public voice in public decisions
  • Accountable congressional mapmaking
  • Limits on officials’ financial conflicts
  • Knowing who funds political spending
  • More influence for small donors
Research questions and delivery conditions

The DEM pilot identifies legal and operational questions, including protected data, voting eligibility, remedies and the Title III litigation risk. Benefit receipt is not proof of voting eligibility and must not depend on registration. A public meeting alone does not give residents meaningful influence.

The bus stop

More of life within reach.

An opportunity only helps if you can reach it. Homes near useful connections, reliable transport, well-supported schools and welcoming public places can make more of life available to people.

Policy designs supporting the story

Inspecting the analytical foundation

What the models can—and cannot—tell us.

Model results answer particular questions under particular assumptions. A household effect, a federal budget cost and a legislative timetable are different things; they should not be added together or treated as one forecast.

What the child-poverty experiments show

Same-scenario baseline: 13.40% child poverty using the Supplemental Poverty Measure, which considers resources after taxes and transfers and certain necessary expenses. Deep poverty means resources below half the poverty threshold.

These are conditional results for credit, care/pre-K expense relief, meals and housing channels using 2025 source income and a 2034 valuation reference. They are not forecasts of 2050, full-agenda effects or adopted delivery rates. They do not include the full health, paid-leave, tax or earnings effects of the agenda.

The annual American Family Act analogue, eligibility proxies and assumed receipt and lease-up limit the comparison. Conditional sampling intervals in the results exclude model uncertainty. Ending child poverty remains a goal, not a finding established by these experiments.

How the funding strategy is being assessed

The program’s fiscal rule calls for permanent programs to be funded at maturity and afterward. Financing strategy remains under holistic redesign; none of the three recent financing illustrations has been selected.

The current working case shows a FY2040 general-fund primary gap of $53.69 billion in nominal dollars. That is about 0.10% of modeled GDP. It is a fiscal-model finding, not a measure of the whole program’s value or an official score. It does not establish that the agenda is fully financed.

The existing agency-surge cash profile remains a proxy, and some costs remain unpriced. These questions belong in the continuing funding and distributional analysis.
