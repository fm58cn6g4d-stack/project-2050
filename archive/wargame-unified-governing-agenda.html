The Governing Agenda

Ossoff–Moore, 2029: one program for the material and institutional repair of the country.

This is the merged agenda — the transformative legislative core built in the Floor Time war game, combined with the broader Ossoff–Moore program of executive action, foreign-policy reconstruction, and domain build-out. Where the two overlapped, the more progressive version was kept; where the broader program reached into ground the core never covered, it was folded in. The result is a single coherent governing project rather than two lists.

Diamonds (◆) mark the transformative core: the most-felt, most-redistributive measures, several of which exist nowhere else in the broader program. Tags mark how each measure moves.

◆ Transformative core — preserved from the Floor Time agenda  ·  RECON reconciliation   FLOOR regular order (needs filibuster reform)   EXEC executive action   ×STATES constitutional amendment

How the agenda moves

Day 3
Democracy carve-out
Simple-majority cloture for voting-rights & election law only. Unlocks the democracy plank immediately.
Month 3
Talking filibuster
Restore the burden of the floor to the obstructing minority. Raises the cost of blocking.
Month 14
Full elimination
After a documented pattern of obstruction, majority cloture chamber-wide — unlocking everything else.

Two reconciliation vehicles carry the fiscal core around the filibuster from the start: an emergency-relief bill (~$2.1T) for housing, health, and the expanded Child Tax Credit, and a structural-transformation bill (~$2.4T) for climate, care, and the public option — each pairing spending with its own pay-for from the revenue plank. The ~$4.6T / 10-year revenue package (Plank XII) funds the whole program and holds the deficit picture together.

IDemocracy & Anti-Corruption

The administration's founding thesis: you cannot deliver for a country whose institutions are rotting, so repair the machinery of self-government first and fastest.
Freedom to Vote ActFloor · carve-out
Automatic and same-day registration, standardized early voting and vote-by-mail, paper-ballot backups, and Election Day access — now paired with small-dollar public matching (6:1 up to $200) to rebuild a small-donor base against big money.
John Lewis Voting Rights Advancement ActFloor · carve-out
Restore Section 2 scope, a new preclearance regime, and a federal private right of action after the Court's retrenchment. Signed at the Edmund Pettus Bridge; upheld 5–4.
D.C. Statehood — Washington, Douglass CommonwealthFloor · carve-out
End the disenfranchisement of 700,000 residents; two senators, one representative, a shrunken federal district, and repeal of the 23rd Amendment. The most permanent act on the board — and unique to this agenda.
Puerto Rico Status ActFloor · carve-out
A binding, federally sponsored referendum among statehood, independence, and free association — with Congress committed to honor the result. Self-determination for 3.2 million disenfranchised citizens; the democracy plank's unfinished half.
DISCLOSE Act & Campaign-Finance TransparencyFloor · carve-out
Dark-money donor disclosure, beneficial-ownership rules for LLC political spending, and digital-ad transparency (Honest Ads).
Government Ethics & Anti-Corruption ActDay-1 EO+ Floor
A Day-1 divestiture executive order (mandatory blind trusts, closed foreign-payment/LLC loopholes) codified into law: the congressional stock-trading ban, real-time foreign-contact disclosure, and criminal emoluments penalties. Paired with a multi-agency enforcement reorientation (DOJ, SEC, CFTC, FTC, CFPB) and DOJ investigations into documented prior-administration corruption.
Supreme Court Ethics & Accountability ActFloor
A binding code of conduct, recusal standards, and gift/travel disclosure for the one federal court that lacks them — the most contested of the anti-corruption cluster.
FEC RestructuringAppointments
Two genuinely independent (non-party) commissioners nominated to break the historic 3–3 deadlock, enabling enforcement majorities for the first time since Citizens United.
Citizens United Constitutional Amendment×STATES · long-term
An explicitly decade-long campaign, run out of an Office of Democracy Renewal, to overturn Citizens United by amendment — securing state ratifications, including first Republican-controlled chambers.

IICare, Health & Family

The most-felt half of the program — the line items in a household budget the government can actually move, and the coverage no market will provide.
Universal Pre-K & Child CareRecon · Package B
Cap child-care costs at 7% of income, universal voluntary pre-K for every 3- and 4-year-old, supply-side grants, and worker-wage parity. Absent from the broader program — restored here as a pillar.
Permanent Expanded Child Tax CreditRecon · Package A / relief
$3,600/$3,000 per child, fully refundable, paid monthly — the 2021 credit that halved child poverty, made permanent. Also absent from the broader program; the single fastest-felt benefit in the agenda.
Paid Family & Medical LeaveRecon
Twelve weeks of paid family and medical leave as national social insurance (the FAMILY Act model) — ending America's status as the only rich country without it. Fully fiscal and reconciliation-eligible.
Social Security Expansion ActFloor · §310(g)-barred
An across-the-board benefit increase, an accurate elderly cost-of-living index, and the payroll cap lifted above $250K. The Budget Act explicitly bars Social Security from reconciliation — a second Byrd-style wall, so it needs full reform.
Public Option & Medicare at 55Recon · structural
A public plan on every exchange at Medicare-plus rates and Medicare eligibility lowered to 55 — more transformative than a benefits expansion alone, and the spine this plank is built around.
Medicare Benefit ExpansionRecon
Add dental, vision, and hearing; a long-term-care framework; and enforced mental-health parity, layered onto the public option and lowered eligibility age.
Medicaid Expansion Completion & Drug PricingRecon
100% federal match to finish expansion in holdout states (9 of 12 expand), expanded Medicare drug-price negotiation, and the $35 insulin / out-of-pocket caps extended to all payers.
Public-Health RebuildRecon
NIH and CDC funding restored and expanded after prior cuts; community health centers grown from 32M to 36M patients served.

IIIWork, Wages & Worker Power

Rebalance bargaining power toward the two-thirds of Americans who work for wages — through law, through the floor, and through the agencies.
PRO Act + Sectoral BargainingFloor
The full PRO Act — ABC test, card-check recognition, striker protections, override of right-to-work — combined with sectoral bargaining frameworks (restaurant, home care, gig) and portable benefits, the more transformative structural reform the broader program contributed.
Living Wage & Small-Business Transition ActFloor · Byrd-barredCredit: Recon
$17, then permanently indexed to two-thirds of the national median — with a longer phase-in for small employers, a regional circuit-breaker that slows (never lowers) the climb in low-wage rural counties, a refundable Small-Business Wage Transition Credit, and a recession off-ramp. The wage mandate needs full reform; the small-business credit can ride reconciliation. Absent from the broader program; a rising tide designed to lift every boat.
NLRB & OSHA Executive ActionExec
Restore captive-audience and joint-employer precedents, 21-day election timelines, an OSHA emergency heat-exposure standard, and a wage-theft enforcement surge ($2.3B+ recovered). Union density modeled rising from 10.1% to 15.8%.

IVHousing & Cities

Attack the shortage from every side at once — emergency relief now, structural supply for the decade, and public capital where markets won't build.
Emergency Housing ResponseRecon · relief
An emergency housing declaration and $85B in rental and construction assistance in the first reconciliation bill.
Housing Abundance & Supply ActFloorRecon core
LIHTC expansion and a new middle-income credit, a Neighborhood Homes rehab credit, HUD funding conditioned on local upzoning, and a social-housing pilot. Builds directly on the ROAD to Housing Act (enacted July 2026 without a presidential signature) — supplying the capital and HUD capacity that deregulatory law lacked, so reforms already on the books finally get executed.
Construction & Public Housing InvestmentRecon · structural
A $250B mandatory revolving construction-loan fund, the Public Housing Capital Fund raised $3.5B→$9.2B against an $80B repair backlog, 400,000 new Section 8 vouchers, and 450,000 construction apprentices.

VClimate, Energy & Decarbonization

The one plank where the engineering and the politics finally point the same way: a target, the wires to hit it, and a price whose revenue comes back as a check.
Clean Energy Standard & Carbon Dividend ActFloorFee & dividend: Recon
An 80%-clean-electricity standard by 2035 (100% by 2040), plus an economy-wide carbon fee returned to households as a quarterly Clean Energy Dividend — the price-and-rebate mechanism the broader program lacked, and the move that buys the climate policy its own durable constituency.
Transmission & Permitting OverhaulFloor
Federal transmission-permitting authority, FERC interconnection-queue reform with binding timelines, NEPA reform cutting transmission permitting from ~9 years to ~4, and direct-build authority on federal lands — the steel in the ground the standard depends on.
Clean-Energy Finance & JusticeReconExec
A reactivated DOE Loan Programs Office ($400B), $900B in clean-energy tax credits with domestic-content phase-ins, and an expanded EPA environmental-justice office with Superfund reprioritization.

VICompetition, Consumers & Technology

A more competitive, less extractive economy — and rules for the technologies reshaping it faster than law can follow.
Antitrust Enforcement & Competition ActFloorEnforcement: Recon
Merger-standard overhaul, killer-acquisition and platform self-preferencing rules, a near-total non-compete ban, and DOJ/FTC enforcement capacity — with real cross-ideological reach.
Junk Fees & Consumer Protection ActFloor
All-in upfront pricing, bans on surprise overdraft/late/convenience fees, click-to-cancel — felt at the moment of purchase. Absent from the broader program; restored here.
AI & Platform GovernanceExec · OSTP
A National AI Safety Framework (explainability, bias auditing, human oversight for government use and procurement), FTC antitrust against platform monopolies, and FCC rules on deepfakes and algorithmic curation.

VIIImmigration & Belonging

Move millions of long-settled people out of legal limbo, and rebuild the agencies that lost the capacity to process them humanely.
Comprehensive Immigration ReformFloor
An earned pathway to citizenship for Dreamers, TPS holders, farmworkers, and residents with 10+ years and clean records; cleared green-card backlogs; expanded agricultural and care-worker visas.
DHS RestructuringExec+ Floor
Separate customs enforcement (retained) from interior immigration enforcement, replacing the latter with a Humanitarian Processing Service; rebuild USCIS capacity (12,000 hires) and set a 180-day asylum-processing timeline.

VII·bRights & Equality

The rights pillar the agenda was missing: bodily autonomy and equal citizenship, codified in statute rather than left to the courts.
Reproductive Freedom ActFloor
Codify Roe's protections nationally (WHPA), protect contraception and IVF, and shield interstate travel and providers — the post-Dobbs restoration, facing certain litigation but backed by the commerce power.
Equality ActFloor
Sexual orientation and gender identity written into federal civil-rights law — employment, housing, credit, public accommodations — codifying and extending Bostock.

VIIIJustice & Public Safety

The most evidence-backed measures available — and the recognition that economic security is itself a form of safety.
Universal Background ChecksFloor
Background checks on all sales and transfers, closing the private-sale and Charleston loopholes. Absent from the broader program; the deepest safety story otherwise runs through the childcare slot, the covered illness, and the livable wage.
Justice & Public Safety Reform ActFloor
Federal cannabis legalization with expungement, sentencing reform (retroactive crack parity, mandatory-minimum relief), and national policing standards (qualified-immunity reform, a decertification registry). The mass-incarceration pillar the agenda was missing — with real libertarian-right crossover on cannabis.

IXEducation & Opportunity

Fund the obligations already on the books, and lower the cost of the credential the economy now demands.
K-12 InvestmentRecon
Title I restored and expanded ($11.2B→$26B) and a funding pathway toward the full statutory 40% IDEA obligation.
Higher-Education AffordabilityReconExec
Pell Grant maximum raised ($7,395→$9,500), SAVE-plan restoration, and PSLF timeline improvements.

XVeterans

Deliver the care already promised, faster and closer to where veterans live.
Veterans Health & Capacity ActRecon+ Exec
12,000 new mental-health clinicians, a claims-processing overhaul toward a 125-day target, and expanded rural community care — with veteran suicide down 12% from peak by Year 3.

XIForeign Policy & National Security

Democracy as foreign policy: end the inherited war, rebuild the tools of civilian power, and compete with clear eyes.
War Powers & the Iran TransitionFloor
The War Powers and Iran Accountability Act — a first-ever congressional-notification framework before any strike — plus a VP-chaired Iran Transition Task Force, a Gulf-monitored ceasefire (~Month 8), and a new nuclear-oversight regime replacing the collapsed JCPOA (~Month 10).
Civilian-Power ReconstructionReconExec
USAID rebuilt from the studs ($12B emergency funding; staffing 800→8,000), a "democracy as foreign policy" framework, NATO Article 5 recommitment, and career diplomats over donor appointees.
Managed Competition with ChinaExec
Cooperation on climate, pandemics, and nuclear stability; firm pushback on Taiwan, Xinjiang, and trade.

XIIFiscal Architecture

The revenue that pays for all of it, raised almost entirely at the top — the pay-for is the point, not an afterthought.
Billionaire Minimum Income TaxRecon · Package A
A 25% minimum on income including unrealized gains for households worth more than $100M. The most legally exposed provision (Moore/realization) — which is why the broader corporate/income base sits beneath it.
Corporate & High-Income Tax FairnessRecon · Package B
Corporate rate 21%→28% with a 15% book minimum, top marginal rate to 39.6%, carried interest closed, capital gains reform, GILTI and buyback-excise increases. The durable, court-safe revenue floor.
Enforcement & the EnvelopeRecon
IRS enforcement restored ($8B/year, ~6:1 return) as part of a ~$4.6T / 10-year revenue package; domestic discretionary +28% real, defense +3.2% real with procurement savings redirected to readiness.

The transformative core (◆) — universal child care, the permanent Child Tax Credit, paid family leave, the Social Security expansion, the public option and Medicare-at-55, the carbon dividend, D.C. and Puerto Rico self-determination, the living wage, reproductive freedom and the Equality Act, justice reform, universal background checks, Supreme Court ethics, and the junk-fee ban — carries the redistributive and most-felt weight of the program, and several of these appear nowhere else in the broader Ossoff–Moore list. The surrounding planks supply the reach: foreign-policy reconstruction, the anti-corruption enforcement machinery, education, veterans, and technology governance. Together they are one agenda, sequenced around a single filibuster-reform pathway and funded by a single revenue base.
