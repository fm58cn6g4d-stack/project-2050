A future we can build together

A fair shot
at a good life.

Enough security to make plans. Care you can depend on. The freedom to choose a different path—and a real say in the decisions that shape your life.

Project 2050 connects that ambition to a governing program for a fairer, more connected and hopeful America. Making it real will take people coming together, electing a coalition for change and holding power accountable long after election day.

What guides us

More room to live. More power to shape our future.

A good life includes security, opportunity, relationships and a sense of belonging. Public choices can make those possibilities available to more people. Four principles guide the program.

01

Freedom you can use

A right matters more when you have the time, security and support to exercise it. A fair shot at a good life means being able to care for someone, change jobs, learn, make a home and take part in your community.

02

A society that cares for its people

Children should grow up without poverty. Illness, disability and aging should bring dependable support. The people who provide care deserve the pay, time and respect that make good care possible.

03

Power that answers to people

Government needs the ability to act and a public with the power to question it. Fair elections, equal rights, independent scrutiny and meaningful participation belong together—even when the people in power are on our side.

04

Responsibility across generations

Prepare for a changing climate and economy, maintain what we build and leave institutions able to learn. Progress should endure because it serves people, with room to correct mistakes and choose a better course.

01 / At home

Room to plan beyond Friday.

Imagine 2050 · an illustrative scene

A parent looks at the week ahead. There is rent to pay, a school trip to arrange and a shift that might change. In this possible future, a missed day does not have to become a cascade of unpaid bills.

A reliable income helps a family plan. Affordable housing gives that plan a foundation. Child care and paid leave make room for work, relationships and the unexpected. Each part does more when the others are there.

How the choices work together

  1. Income families can count on

    A monthly, fully refundable child credit would reach families with little or no income-tax liability, with extra support for young children and newborns. Regular payments help support the everyday costs of raising a child.

  2. A stable place to call home

    Expand rental assistance by one million new voucher awards, alongside investment in housing construction and repair. Helping with rent and increasing the supply of homes need to move together.

  3. Time to work, care and grow

    Affordable child care and voluntary pre-K, school and summer food support, and paid family and medical leave would help families meet needs that a cash payment alone cannot solve.

What progress would mean

Progress means fewer children growing up in poverty, fewer families pushed from their homes and more parents able to plan their lives. It matters most whether help reaches people who have been hardest to reach.

02 / The care visit

Care that leaves room for a life.

Imagine 2050 · an illustrative scene

An older neighbor receives support at home. Her daughter is still part of her care, but their time together is no longer spent only repairing gaps between services. The care worker has time to listen.

Affordable coverage can protect a household’s finances. Care at home can give an older or disabled person more control over daily life. Better jobs for care workers help make that support dependable. These are parts of the same promise.

How the choices work together

  1. Health coverage that stays with you

    Restore Medicaid and marketplace protections and build a public health-insurance option, so access to affordable coverage depends less on a particular job or where someone lives.

  2. Medicare that meets more everyday needs

    Add dental, vision and hearing benefits and a limit on out-of-pocket costs in traditional Medicare. Being able to see, hear and eat comfortably belongs in an account of health and independence.

  3. Support at home, and support for the people providing it

    Invest in home- and community-based care and the workforce behind it. Pair care services with paid leave so relatives can be present without having to carry every responsibility alone.

What progress would mean

Judge progress by whether people can actually get care, choose how they live and avoid financial hardship—and by whether care workers can build a sustainable life from their work.

03 / Along the river

Prepare for the water that still comes.

Imagine 2050 · an illustrative scene

After a storm, neighbors check on one another. The river has space to rise, and a public building offers shelter. Some households have moved to safer ground. There are still losses, and difficult decisions about what to rebuild.

A cleaner energy system reduces the pollution driving climate change. A stronger grid and better homes make that system useful in daily life. Preparing for hazards protects people while the climate continues to change.

How the choices work together

  1. Clean power that can reach people

    Combine clean-energy investment and standards with a grid able to carry power where it is needed. The plan connects what we build to the rules and institutions needed to make it work.

  2. Help households share in the transition

    Return most carbon-fee receipts to adults through a climate dividend, while supporting households and communities through the transition. Pair cleaner technology with practical help to use it.

  3. Prepare before disaster—and support recovery afterward

    Strengthen disaster recovery, flood-insurance support and the use of forward-looking risk in building decisions. The aim is to reduce harm while helping people face difficult choices about staying, adapting or moving.

What progress would mean

Look at pollution, safety, reliability and affordability together. Progress includes protecting people with the fewest resources and being honest about the losses that remain.

04 / The workshop

A say in how work changes.

Imagine 2050 · an illustrative scene

A worker and a colleague are learning a new tool. Across town, someone whose job disappeared is looking for a way into a different occupation. The technology has changed; whether either person gains security or influence is still a public choice.

A new technology can make work easier without giving workers more security. Bargaining power, fair standards, learning opportunities and support through a job change help people shape what progress means for them.

How the choices work together

  1. A stronger voice on the job

    Strengthen organizing and bargaining rights and improve labor standards. Workers should have more power to influence pay, conditions and how their workplaces change.

  2. Real routes into new work

    Expand apprenticeships and connect support for workers affected by AI to wage insurance and training. Learning needs to connect to useful skills and actual opportunities.

  3. A human answer when a system gets it wrong

    Require explanations, human review and appeals when federal agencies use algorithms in consequential decisions. More capable technology should come with more accountable institutions.

What progress would mean

Ask whether work offers security, a fair share of the gains and a meaningful voice. Count people whose jobs disappear or whose first opportunity never arrives, as well as people who benefit.

05 / The public library

A government you can question.

Imagine 2050 · an illustrative scene

Residents meet about an investment proposal. They can inspect the evidence, challenge the presenter and find out what the authority did with their recommendations. Some disagree with the result. Their ability to organize and contest it remains part of the promise.

People need more than a chance to speak. They need fair elections, rights that can be enforced, institutions that respond and public places where collective life can happen. A government capable of delivering should also be open to challenge.

How the choices work together

  1. Fair elections and equal political voice

    Protect voting access, strengthen election protections and make congressional mapmaking more accountable. Make it easier for eligible people to participate, without tying access to benefits to voting or political support.

  2. Public service in the public interest

    Strengthen disclosure of political spending, curb conflicts of interest and give small donors more influence through voluntary public matching. Public office should serve the public.

  3. Places to gather and ways to be heard

    Support libraries, parks and community spaces, and create advisory citizens’ assemblies with a defined public response. Build the staffing and expertise that turn a commitment into useful service.

What progress would mean

Judge democracy by who can participate, whose rights are protected and whether people can get an answer or remedy. Apply those standards to leaders we support as well as those we oppose.

06 / The bus stop

More of life within reach.

Imagine 2050 · an illustrative scene

Someone waits for a bus to a class, a shift or a friend. The value of the connection is what it makes possible. In another place the distances are longer, and the right transport answer may look very different.

An opportunity only helps if you can reach it. Homes near useful connections, reliable transport, well-supported schools and welcoming public places can make more of life available to people.

How the choices work together

  1. Connections to jobs, care and one another

    Renew roads and bridges while investing in public transit and passenger rail. Different communities need different ways to reach the places that make everyday life possible.

  2. A fair chance to learn

    Strengthen public-school and special-education funding, build the educator workforce and make higher education more affordable. Education should widen the choices people can make throughout their lives.

  3. Homes and shared places that support a community

    Connect housing investment to libraries, parks and community spaces. Public investment should create places worth belonging to and support the work of keeping them useful.

What progress would mean

Look at what people can reach and take part in, including people with disabilities and those in rural communities. Tribal governments and communities need approaches that respect their sovereignty and distinct responsibilities.

The power to make it happen

A program becomes possible when people move together.

These changes will take a movement that can connect everyday needs to a shared public purpose, win elections and sustain the work of governing. No single election completes that work.

People will bring different experiences and disagree about important choices. A durable coalition needs room for those disagreements, a commitment to equal rights and a common willingness to make things better.

  1. Build a shared purpose

    Start with the changes people want to see in their lives. Listen across communities, connect struggles that are too often treated separately and organize around a program people can understand and help shape.

  2. Elect a coalition for change

    Turn that shared purpose into the democratic power to govern. Elect leaders committed to the program, press them to explain how they will deliver it and build a coalition broad enough to act together.

  3. Make the promises real

    Winning office opens the door. Laws, public investment and capable institutions have to carry change through it. Stay involved as decisions become services, rights and opportunities in people’s lives.

  4. Keep showing up—and keep asking

    Hold leaders and ourselves accountable over the long term. Look at who benefits, who is still excluded and what causes harm. Defend progress, listen to criticism and change course when the evidence demands it.

Bring a question from your own life.

What would give you, your family or your community more freedom to plan? Explore the related policies, discuss them with others and ask what a coalition for change should be prepared to deliver. Keep that question alive after the votes are counted.
