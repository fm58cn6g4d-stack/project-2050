# Scenario calendar: review, integration and delivery implications

October 23, 2026. Espen authorized double-checking, improvements and project-wide integration of the scenario calendar. Source branch: `claude/pensive-mendel-g1lwl5`, baseline commit `214a7acc1144d86b547c1bc39d8f99c75533dc32`. All 176 baseline repository files in the isolated working copy were verified against their remote Git blobs before editing.

The reviewed system is now part of the normal rebuild, with an editable assumptions file, live inventory, tests, generated reports, explorer and dashboard data. Its numeric ranges remain illustrative and uncalibrated. Integration adopts the method; it does not adopt a new enactment forecast, climate merger, launch date, scope reduction, fiscal profile or vote parameter.

The useful components retained are the full inventory, explicit units, procedural sitting spans, separate chamber capacity, shared stress conditions, scope-preserving merger comparison, protected timing checks and unweighted comparison of original policy coverage. The changes correct interpretation and integration while keeping those strengths.

## What the figures mean

The initial 485/958/1,830 Senate-hour envelope remains an illustrative active-work input across favorable/reference/difficult conditions. It is not an empirically estimated confidence interval. The same hour inputs can produce different completion dates when availability, procedural spans, preparation or House agreement changes. Eight-hour equivalents normalize work quantities; they are not sitting days.

The old 172 versus 163 and 15 plus 2 reconciliation charges remain traceable in legacy tables. They are not a law of congressional capacity. The 15-day claim originated in the framework's assertion that reconciliation cost does not vary with scope; that assertion has been corrected. R1/R2/R3 now have separate debate, voting, return-work, House and readiness assumptions. The statutory Senate debate ceilings—50 hours for an ordinary budget resolution and 20 for reconciliation—are constraints on parts of the process, not bill-duration estimates. [1–2]

The engine assumes votes, lawful eligibility and eventual agreement. Its House concurrence stage simplifies possible amendment exchanges. Standard F bills conservatively retain five initial sitting dates as a whole-date representation of the legacy approximately 4.5-session-day span; the adopted minority guarantees are not waived. Whether the active-hour allowances can accommodate them needs floor-manager review. The first-week rules case also requires actual senator support before the incoming vice president takes office. No saved 51-seat stress tag substitutes for that vote count.

The default strategies protect rules, R1, DEM and SS while allowing concurrent preparation and independent House work. An R1 delay does not postpone DEM indefinitely: a modeled pre-signature recovery is flagged as requiring an explicit decision. Later budget-resolution dependencies are conservative planning assumptions, not universal legal rules. R1-slot loss still requires a distinct fallback architecture; the model does not automatically transplant its scope or claim a free calendar saving.

## Could more policies be felt by November 2030?

**Yes, as a plausible opportunity, not yet a demonstrated forecast.** Earlier enactment can leave time for the same implementation work to occur before the election. The new screen makes that question inspectable for 49 ledger policies rather than inferring it from a package's passage date. It does not count a simulated law as an observed benefit.

In the favorable, working-capacity, deadline-led case with opening concentration, the legacy-lag screen places monthly CTC payments in mid-2029; restoration in the 2030 plan year; school-meal effects, clean-energy credits, initial paid-leave and education effects during 2030; and the dividend in 2030. Those dates use unsupported legacy lags and hypothetical passage, so they identify priority delivery investigations rather than promises. Some direct protections may matter sooner than those coarse lags suggest, but their clauses, effective dates, remedies and actual access need analysis. No numerical count of proven additional felt benefits is justified.

There are important limits to the optimistic interpretation:

- HLT-3 remains a 2031 public-plan launch and 2032 automatic-enrollment program. Earlier passage buys preparation time; it creates no new-plan registration contact in 2030.
- Hearing/vision remain 2031 and dental 2033. SS benefit timing remains the adopted first-COLA-after-enactment schedule; assurance about future solvency is a different kind of effect.
- FAM-2's initial 2030–31 phase differs from its full 2032 cap. A single 20-month legacy lag can obscure early, partial access; its phase-specific delivery path needs replacement rather than mechanical acceleration.
- Tax-year effective dates, procurement, staff, provider supply, state resistance, court orders, non-filer reach and county disposition can prevent an early signature from becoming timely help.
- Effects on recipients, recognition of the law and electoral behavior are three different claims. The calendar establishes none of their magnitudes.

The generated `index/calendar-delivery-opportunities.md` is therefore an opportunity register. It adds the unchanged legacy lag to modeled completion and applies named adopted date constraints. JSON retains conditional dates for reproducibility. Neither the opportunity arithmetic nor a successful rebuild validates the lag. Formal fiscal/household profiles have not been rephased.

## Validation and limitations

New tests cover scope preservation under merger; retained CLI-7; MOV exclusion; monthly/seasonal windows; DEM target kind; D2 and signature dependencies; zero capacity; procedural span occupancy; daily resource conservation; repayment of shifted other business; statutory debate bounds; unweighted dominance; and protection against accelerating HLT-3 through a zero-lag scenario. The standard project rebuild runs these with existing transportation, carbon, funds, surge and approved-calendar checks.

Validation confirmed byte-identical original financial/vote/benefit inputs and unchanged generated fiscal, incidence, dedicated-fund and carbon outputs. The full rebuild passed. The consistency report retains its existing one stale headline, one unknown ID, one ledger/register mismatch, four reconciliation rows and two table findings; these are outside this calendar integration and were not hidden. Calendar timing/label changes are intended; no unexplained financial change was accepted.

## Recommended next sequence

1. **Calibrate the opening sequence.** Produce one D2/R1/DEM procedural packet: actual rules text and votes; available FY2029 instructions; common House/Senate text; score/Byrd review; amendment and voting blocks; enrollment and March recovery. Review the F sitting span and the opening other-business allocation. This is the highest-value way to improve the calendar now.
2. **Test the optimistic delivery opportunity.** Build short, phase-specific paths for CTC first payments, health restoration, school meals, initial pre-K/lowest-income care, paid leave and the dividend. Add the earliest lawful date, prerequisites, responsible actor, recipient reach and a verifiable milestone. Replace the coarse lags only where supported; keep adopted health and family phases.
3. **Develop the complete climate bill and comparator.** Retain chosen CLI-3/4/6 scope and the separate-bill route. Obtain title-level coalition, referral, amendment and House-agreement estimates before selecting the merger or claiming savings.
4. **Return to the shared project queue.** In parallel, complete the CMS cost/authority chain and the priority health-savings/revenue checks already identified. These matter to delivery and fiscal claims even if floor time is ample. Use the post-election D1 rebaseline when results exist; do not reopen adopted policy merely to balance uncalibrated day totals.

This sequence seeks earlier consequential delivery while preserving the ability to change strategy as evidence improves. It does not require an exhaustive agenda audit before the next bounded packet can advance.
