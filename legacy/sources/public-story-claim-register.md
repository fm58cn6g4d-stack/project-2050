## Claims deliberately left out

- **That the whole program is fully financed.** The blueprint forbids it, and the current central case fails the project’s own fairness test for the fourth income fifth. The last essay says so plainly.
- **That middle-income households come out ahead.** The aggregate quintile screen is not a household promise, and the current central case does not pass it. Only the carbon channel’s narrower result appears, labeled as one channel.
- **The FY2040 general-fund gap and debt-to-GDP paths.** These depend on unselected illustrative tax schedules and, for the recovery budget, a climate package not yet integrated. The blueprint keeps the gap in the analysis companion rather than the public argument.
- **That climate action makes a place safe.** Listed as do-not-publish in the blueprint. The climate essay states residual risk explicitly.
- **Dates as household receipt dates.** Dates in the timeline are planned starting points under the project’s favorable planning scenario, not enactment, operational availability or reach.
- **Program cost totals (for example, health or family ten-year costs).** Many are rough planning ranges pending a common-baseline score; the essays use envelopes only where a decision fixed them and avoid summing across programs.
- **Political forecasts, including the November 2026 results.** The project rebaselines after actual results exist, and no model-derived political certainty is claimed.
- **Scenario-model figures from the authored stress-test narrative.** That narrative is a hypothesis generator, not evidence. The work essay cites only its qualitative design warning.
- **A cost estimate for the housing guarantee.** The guarantee is a direction under development and has not been priced; the planning arithmetic for added awards rests on assumptions that differ from the funded HOU-4D envelope, so the brief states the fiscal rule instead of a number.
