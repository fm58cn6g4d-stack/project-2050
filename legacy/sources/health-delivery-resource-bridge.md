## Restoration findings

HLT-1/4 already target R1. The research supports component-specific 2029 paths, not moving those titles into R1 or declaring the whole titles ready:
- Existing Marketplace subsidy recalculation can deliver in-year premium help. CMS's 2021 ARP precedent is evidence against a universal plan-year wait, not a transferable 21-day delivery promise.
- Prospective Medicaid restriction repeal and stopping unnecessary renewal actions can begin before all terminated cases are repaired. Reinstatement, retroactive eligibility, provider claims and reimbursement require separate explicit transition/remedy choices.
- Existing health-center grants can fund operating capacity sooner than new buildings or residency pipelines deliver services.
- Familiar HCBS funding/rate/workforce channels may deliver some early support; broad new slots require actual providers and workers.
- Coverage-gap closure lacks a settled operating mechanism. Its schedule cannot be inferred from the fastest APTC change.

## Hearing/vision: timing issue and missing comparison

The report conditionally prefers Route C, narrow early-R1 hearing/vision authority, if fully specified and financed without jeopardizing rapid R1. Its principal new argument is the MA cycle: under ordinary current rules, first-Monday April/June deadlines fall on April 2 and June 4, 2029, during CY2030 preparation. These dates are calendar arithmetic under existing rules, not a published future CMS schedule. Actual announcement timing and later law must be checked; late-March R1 does not guarantee enough actuarial/operating time.

Section 1852(a)(5), 42 USC 1395w-22(a)(5), addresses qualifying significant unpriced benefit changes between announcements. Its contract-delay provision matters, but its payment-exclusivity exception matters too. Current 42 CFR 422.109(c) provides direct Original-Medicare payment for certain new benefits before adjusted MA contracts take effect. It preserves plan responsibilities for specified related care and existing supplemental obligations, and beneficiary cost sharing remains relevant.

**Qualification:** the report does not examine that existing payment bridge. Do not convert its conditional C preference into a finding that January-2030 access necessarily requires early R1 or an entirely new mid-cycle MA statute. Equally, the existence of the bridge does not establish that it supplies the chosen full benefit responsibly. Exact scope, CMS significance determination, supplier access, claims, existing supplemental benefits, cost sharing and financing require review.

Distinguish benefit access for MA enrollees from inclusion in MA plan contracts. Compare:
1. Early R1 authority and normal-cycle MA implementation.
2. Summer R2 with express MA payment/contract transition provisions.
3. Summer R2 with an Original-Medicare payment bridge under existing authority and any exact amendments needed.
4. Adopted 2031 fallback.

Routes 2 and 3 are subroutes of the existing R2 development option, not separately adopted policies.
