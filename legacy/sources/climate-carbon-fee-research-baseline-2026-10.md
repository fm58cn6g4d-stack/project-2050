# Carbon fee and dividend: research baseline (2 Oct 2026)

## 1. Legislative landscape

- **Energy Innovation and Carbon Dividend Act (EICDA)**: $15/t CO2e, +$10/yr real (+$15 if targets missed), 100% of net revenue as equal dividends (adult 1 share, child 0.5), border adjustment, conditional pause on EPA stationary-source authority. Introduced 2018 (115th), H.R. 763 (116th, 86 cosponsors), H.R. 2307 (117th), H.R. 5744 (118th). Per C2ES's Feb 2026 tracker it has NOT been reintroduced in the 119th Congress.
- **119th Congress carbon pricing bills (C2ES, Feb 2026)**:
  - America's Clean Future Fund Act, S. 2712 (Durbin): $75/t in 2027, +$10/yr real; 75% to household rebates (rising to 80%), rest to a climate finance corporation and transition aid; border adjustment. Closest live fee-and-dividend vehicle.
  - MARKET CHOICE Act, H.R. 3338 (Fitzpatrick/Carbajal): $40/t in 2027, +5%/yr real; revenue to infrastructure trust fund; border adjustment; EPA moratorium through 2038.
  - Climate Pollution Standard and Community Investment Act, H.R. 6918 (Tonko): cap-and-trade, $15 floor.
  - TEPS Act, H.R. 2177 (Casten): power/thermal sector, $50/t in 2028.
  - Clean Competition Act (Whitehouse/DelBene, reintroduced Dec 2025): $60/t on emissions above industry-average intensity, +6%/yr real, domestic plus imports.
  - Foreign Pollution Fee Act, S. 1325 (Cassidy/Graham, Apr 2025): import-only fee, no domestic price.
  - Sector bills: Polluters Pay Climate Fund, FAT CAT, Clean Cloud, maritime.
- **Earlier designs worth keeping as comparators**: Baker-Shultz/Climate Leadership Council ($40, +5%, full dividend, regulatory swap); Climate Action Rebate Act (70% dividend, income-capped); Healthy Climate and Family Security Act (cap-and-dividend); American Opportunity Carbon Fee Act and Raise Wages Cut Carbon (payroll/tax-credit recycling); Waxman-Markey (2009 cap-and-trade, passed House only).
- **International**: Canada zeroed its consumer fuel charge and ended the Canada Carbon Rebate in 2025. EU CBAM entered its definitive phase in 2026.

## 3. Baselines today

- Rhodium: 2025 GHG emissions 18% below 2005 (up 2.4% on 2024). Taking Stock 2026 (policy as of June 2026): 27-41% below 2005 in 2040, 3.8-4.8 Gt.
- EIA AEO2026: energy CO2 falls 11-38% between 2025 and 2050 across cases; some cases flatline around 2040.

## 4. Gaps

1. Baselines are stale: almost all fee-and-dividend modeling predates OBBBA, repeal of EPA vehicle and power rules, and data-center load growth.
2. Horizon: most runs stop at 2030 or 2035; EMF 32 explicitly declines to quantify beyond a decade. Nothing credible models a $200-300/t price in 2050.
3. Model disagreement on price response: at ~$65-80/t, NEMS gives ~17-20% below baseline, E3 gives ~35-40%.
4. Dividend path is not modeled dynamically: revenue and dividend peak then decline as the base erodes. No study traces the per-household dividend to 2050.
5. Revenue accounting: gross vs net of the 25% income/payroll offset, dividend taxability, administrative cost, and export rebates are treated inconsistently.
6. Distributional work is static, year-one, and pre-2021 prices; little on horizontal variation within income groups (rural, heating fuel, commute).
7. Border adjustment: revenue and leakage rarely integrated with the domestic fee; interaction with EU CBAM and existing tariffs unmodeled.
8. Policy interaction: fee layered on surviving credits, state programs (CA, WA, RGGI), and a regulatory pause when the regulations are already repealed.
9. Non-CO2 gases, process emissions, and carbon removal credits are thinly covered.
10. Ratchets/TAMs, and political durability (Canada) are not in the quantitative models.
11. Benefits side: updated social cost of GHGs and health co-benefits are missing from most runs.

## 5. Own reduced-form projection (illustrative, not a model run)

Assumptions: 2027 start; 2025$; energy CO2 only; 2005 = 5.99 Gt and 2025 = 4.85 Gt (approximate EIA values, to be verified); baseline falls 25% from 2025 to 2050 (midpoint of AEO2026's 11-38%), linear; two response curves fit to published runs: low a(P)=0.0462*P^0.316 (AEO2022 fee cases), high a(P)=0.00968*P^0.84 capped at 80% (RFF E3 CLC run); shares = 0.89 x population (CBO: 349m in 2026, 364m in 2056); no border-adjustment revenue; "net" applies 25% offset.

| Design | Year | Price | Energy CO2 below 2005 | Gross revenue | Dividend per adult share (gross / net) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline (no fee) | 2035 / 2050 | - | 27% / 39% (range 23-31% / 28-50%) | - | - |
| EICDA-style $15 +$10/yr | 2035 | $95 | 41-59% | $231-334bn | $730-1,050 / $550-790 |
| | 2050 | $245 | 55-88% | $178-657bn | $550-2,040 / $415-1,530 |
| CLC-style $44 +5% | 2035 | $65 | 40-51% | $192-235bn | $610-740 / $455-555 |
| | 2050 | $135 | 52-76% | $198-385bn | $615-1,190 / $460-895 |
| CBO-style $25 +5% | 2035 | $37 | 38-42% | $129-138bn | $405-435 / $305-325 |
| | 2050 | $77 | 50-62% | $176-228bn | $545-710 / $410-530 |
| Durbin-style $75 +$10/yr (100% returned for comparability) | 2035 | $155 | 44-76% | $224-523bn | $705-1,650 / $530-1,240 |

In each range, the larger emissions cut pairs with the smaller revenue and dividend. Prices above ~$130 are outside the range either curve was fit on.
