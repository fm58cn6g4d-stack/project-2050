# Priority delivery paths before the 2030 midterms

October 23, 2026 · Preliminary evidence and implementation specification. Baseline `f159cdf01a8f3352478eaa74cf08c501376af06e` on `claude/pensive-mendel-g1lwl5`. This develops the approved opportunity screen; it does not adopt benefit changes or new launch dates.

The strongest next claim is that early legislation can create more time for delivery, not that a specified number of new benefits will be felt by election day. Initial access, broad reach and mature-program outcomes need different milestones. The evidence below supports separating components whose delivery mechanisms differ. It does not support assigning them all a common lag or summing their effects.

## Evidence that changes the questions

The 2021 advance CTC began disbursement July 15. Relative to ARP's March 11 signature, that is 126 calendar days. IRS guidance distinguishes automatic payments using prior records from non-filers who needed another route. This is a useful feasibility precedent for existing-record payment delivery; it does not certify a five-month 2029 launch or the reach of families absent from IRS records. [D1–D2]

CMS reported that enhanced Marketplace assistance became available through HealthCare.gov April 1, 2021, following March 11 enactment—21 calendar days. Other ARP eligibility-related assistance was scheduled for July. One law therefore had multiple implementation paths. These are system-availability milestones, not proof that every enrollee received an immediately reduced bill. The precedent does not override Project 2050's adopted 2030 restoration plan year. [D2–D3]

Washington's official employer bulletin records a 2017 paid-leave law, premium withholding from January 2019 and benefits beginning in 2020. It illustrates distinct collection, employer-reporting and benefit stages, with years of institution-building. It is neither a minimum federal lead time nor evidence that a new national system can launch in 14 months. [D4]

USDA records a permanent Summer EBT authorization in December 2022 and operation beginning in summer 2024. Existing benefit infrastructure helped, but program availability still depended on participating jurisdictions. The 2023 implementing rule illustrates plans, agency approval and system-cost requirements. This supports a state-by-state service ledger, not treating national enactment as nationwide household receipt. [D5–D6]

### FAM-3: administrative architecture is a real design dependency

The canonical design permits SSA or a new office with state partnerships. Compare these before requesting a date:

| Route within the existing design space | Reusable components to investigate | New work and risks | Minimum review product |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SSA-led administration | Wage/identity and payment capabilities, subject to lawful access and fit | Short-duration certification, employer interaction, claims/appeals, workload and mission conflicts; do not borrow OASDI funds or assume systems are transferable | Function-by-function capacity and authority map; staffing/IT workload; score and funding boundary |
| Dedicated office with state partnerships | Existing state program operations and lawful interoperability where applicable | New federal institution, uncovered-state backstop, varying eligibility/wage systems, interstate cases and opt-in scope | Federal/state service map and backstop; transition, employer reporting and operational cost comparison |

Neither route is selected here. A state opt-in cannot silently shrink the adopted national benefit. Contributions, benefits, reserve management and the repayable Treasury bridge must be synchronized; earlier payroll collection is a household cost, not an early benefit. Keep the existing 0.6%-to-0.85% design and no-open-general-fund-backstop direction.

### An acceptance test should follow a person, not a portal

For each path, trace one ordinary case and at least one exception: a filer/non-filer, a household with a changed child/custody record, an enrollee facing a state/system mismatch, a school with kitchen constraints, a family lacking a nearby care provider, a worker with multiple/interstate employers, or an adult dependent missing from a tax-only dividend roster. Record the responsible actor, lawful data fields, authority/funding, decision and receipt dates, refusal/error reason and a remedy that arrives in time. These are proposed test cases, not claims that current data access permits the transaction.

The earlier CMS and DEM pilots supply structures for permissions, incremental cost boundaries and county disposition. This packet does not reopen their record requests, assume Medicaid matching for election work or claim that contract ceilings are available funding. No emails were sent.

## Record corrections versus decisions

**Record clarification:** the opportunity screen's precise not-before dates operationalize canonical year-level directions. January 1 is an earliest-within-year convention where the policy specifies only a year; it is not a newly adopted exact launch day. Keep statutory entitlement, system availability, first receipt and full rollout separate.

**Research direction:** replace one lag per broad package with component/phase milestones. Prioritize CTC, restoration and meals; develop the care workforce/supply and paid-leave architecture in parallel. Examine the dividend/fee timing bridge before presenting an incidence result as reflecting recipients' actual cash flow.

**Substantive decisions requiring Espen:** selecting the paid-leave administrator; any narrower state coverage, changed effective date, benefit phase, financing or data-sharing authority. This packet recommends comparative development and specialist review, not adopting one of those changes.

The smallest useful next output is six short transaction sheets with source-linked legal dates, named institutional owners, resource dependencies and explicit unknowns. A claimed first-receipt date requires the entire relevant chain, not just an earlier model passage date. The child-poverty goal should be assessed against families still missing from those chains, not average benefits among recipients alone.
