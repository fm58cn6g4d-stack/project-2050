October 23, 2026 · Bounded follow-up for Espen Swanson

## Finding and decision

Develop two drafting tracks together: the adopted restoration approach, and independently operative protections that counsel can assess if restoration is narrowed or enjoined. Keep the bill together under DEM-1A. Do not present the narrower provisions as equivalent protection, or adopt them as a replacement without Espen's decision.

The consequential legal finding is stronger than a drafting ambiguity. The current Section 2 text already uses results language. *Louisiana v. Callais*, decided April 29, 2026, construes that text through Congress's enforcement-power limits and links liability to circumstances supporting a strong inference of intentional discrimination. The majority also invalidates the challenged race-predominant remedial map. Repeating the results language or declaring all compliance maps compelling cannot ensure survival. This is an inference from the statute and majority opinion, not a prediction of a future case. [L1–L2]

## 2. Three developed alternatives

| Local alternative | Mechanism and scope | Authority to develop | Protection and tradeoff | Principal legal risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Restoration** — adopted direction, more fully specified | Amend Section 2 to establish an express effects-based equal-opportunity claim, define causation and relevant circumstances, and authorize narrowly tailored race-conscious relief after a supported violation finding. Cover congressional, state and local voting practices within the constitutional authority established. | Fourteenth Amendment §5 and Fifteenth Amendment §2; separately identify Article I grounds for congressional applications. Build a present-day record connecting prophylaxis to constitutional violations. | Closest to DEM-1's ambition; reaches injuries that cannot be proved through direct intent evidence. Preserve the distinction between opportunity and proportional representation. | Congress cannot conclusively declare the constitutional standard changed. The fit of liability and remedy to current violations, and race predominance in a remedial map, remain exposed. |
| **Intent and effective enforcement** — narrower fallback proposal | Expressly prohibit intentional racial discrimination; preserve circumstantial proof, records, expedited discovery, expert access and enforceable remedial orders. Specify an express private action for the new provisions and DOJ enforcement. | Amendment enforcement powers, with jurisdiction and remedies separately drafted. | A more focused route to discover and remedy deliberate discrimination. It leaves effects-only injuries without equivalent protection and may be expensive for plaintiffs to prove. | An irrebuttable intent presumption from disparities would recreate the constitutional problem. Burden shifts, privilege rules and any immunity abrogation require counsel. |
| **Congressional map standards** — complementary proposal | Enact concrete federal rules for congressional maps: disclosed criteria and data, population equality, contiguity, documented communities, transparent alternatives and a defined prohibition on partisan favoritism, enforced through a statutory action and timely judicial backstop. | Article I, §4. *Rucho* recognizes Congress's role while rejecting the constitutional partisan-gerrymandering claims before it. [L3] | Can constrain partisan rationales that otherwise obscure racial injury, without making a racial quota the rule. Does not supply a general federal mandate for state legislative maps or replace racial-discrimination protection. | Standards must actually be manageable, neutral and supported. Congress's authority does not exempt a race-predominant map from equal protection or guarantee every commission/backstop design. |

**Recommendation for development, not adoption:** draft Restoration and the Intent track as separable modules; develop congressional standards once, in coordination with DEM-2. Request counsel's comparison of the protections that remain under each injunction scenario. Do not quietly switch DEM-2A's mandatory commissions to DEM-2E's voluntary safe harbor.

### Restoration: operative choices to put before counsel

Define the injury as unequal opportunity attributable to the challenged practice in its actual setting. Identify the relevant comparison and causal showing; require more than the observation that a preferred candidate lost. Preserve the statute's rejection of a right to proportional representation. A map simulation is evidence of feasible alternatives, not a legal entitlement to a particular racial composition. [L1; proposal]

Build the record around current practices: rejected maps, contemporaneous communications, departures from announced criteria, demographic and election evidence, actual explanations, litigation findings, costs of obtaining relief and recurrence after relief. Identify why narrower enforcement fails to detect or prevent particular constitutional injuries. Record contrary evidence and variation among jurisdictions. A national historical narrative alone does not establish that every provision fits every present application.

Authorize a remedy through findings about the violation, affected voters, causal mechanism and feasible alternatives. Ask courts to explain the remedy's necessity, geographic reach and duration, and why less race-conscious alternatives fail. Do not use statutory wording to deem strict scrutiny automatically satisfied. Counsel must assess the independent constitutional footing of each remedial application; a legislative record cannot guarantee the result.

### Intent track: make proof and relief usable

Require preservation of map versions, underlying data, criteria, material official communications and explanations at adoption, with a funded public archive that excludes protected personal data. Set enforceable production duties and sanctions for unlawful destruction; legislative privilege and other protected material require text-specific review.

Allow evidence from comparators, procedural departures and properly designed map ensembles. Require parties to disclose modeling choices and plausible competing explanations. Race and party cannot be treated as interchangeable. Decide with counsel whether a rebuttable production obligation is justified after a defined prima facie showing; do not assume that reallocating the ultimate burden is lawful.

Provide resources for affected voters to obtain counsel and experts. Define who may sue and for what injury, fee rules, defendants, venue, appeal route and available relief in the bill itself. Article III injury cannot be created by assertion. A statutory private action for these new provisions avoids relying silently on unresolved enforcement assumptions about existing Section 2.

### Congressional standards: a usable specification

Publish a ranked hierarchy and exceptions rather than a loose list of desirable criteria. Require a mapmaker to explain departures, disclose proposed maps and data in usable formats, hear public objections and respond before adoption. Communities of interest require a definition and evidence; they cannot become an undisclosed racial or partisan proxy.

For a proposed partisan-favoritism rule, counsel and technical experts should compare a purpose-plus-material-effect test with an outlier test using a disclosed neutral ensemble. Neither an efficiency-gap number nor one election forecast should mechanically decide liability. Test political geography, uncontested races, turnout variation, population data and alternative reasonable specifications. Choose a rule only after the tradeoff between administrability and missed harms is explicit.

A three-judge backstop needs express jurisdiction, triggers and deadlines. Require notice and an opportunity for the state to submit a lawful cure, followed by an independently reviewed map if it fails. Prevent a voluntary commission safe harbor, if eventually adopted under DEM-2E, from immunizing racial discrimination or other constitutional violations. Retain the adopted 2034 exception and distinguish court-ordered cures from opportunistic mid-decade redraws.

## 4. Legislative and implementation route

DEM-1 remains a regular-order bill, with DEM-1F's floor sequence after R1 and enactment targeted for spring 2030, inside D1's mid-2030 outer deadline. D2's adopted rules posture remains necessary to the project scenario. This follow-up does not turn the civil-rights provisions into reconciliation provisions or add uncosted floor days. [P1–P2]

Before introduction, the proposed work is a joint legislative record, operative text and clause-by-clause counsel review. Relevant House/Senate election and judiciary committees must resolve referrals and jurisdiction; their counsel and parliamentarians determine actual procedure. Adoption, enactment and appropriations must each be tracked. A proposed owner is not an official assignment.

After enactment, proposed owners are DOJ for enforcement and litigation; state mapmaking authorities and courts for lawful maps; election officials for boundaries, voter notices and administration; and the designated federal funding administrator for grants and reporting. Technical support must have an accountable public owner rather than vesting substantive map choice in a vendor or evaluator.

Work backward from each state's real 2031 mapmaking and candidate-filing calendar. Required milestones are data availability, map proposal, public review, state decision, challenge, cure, appellate contingency, district coding and voter notice. These are dependencies, not supported duration estimates. Enactment in spring 2030 creates preparation time but does not demonstrate sufficient litigation time. Preserve the earlier calendar's unresolved CLI-7, SS-1, MOV and floor-capacity conflicts; these alternatives do not resolve them by changing dates.

## 5. Cost, evaluation and correction incentives

Prepare separate cost lines for federal enforcement staff, public data/archive services, litigation and experts, independent technical review, state/local map implementation, notices and continuing monitoring. Distinguish funded baseline duties from the increment. Trace any DEM-11 grant to its recipient, eligible purpose, appropriation, award, obligation and cash; do not also charge the same work to CAP-7S. No supported estimate establishes that existing personnel or contracts have spare capacity. Continuing enforcement needs a continuing funding home under D4/D9, not an indefinitely extended surge.

**Candidate evaluation design:** publish the time from a credible complaint to a decision and an effective remedy; the proportion of substantiated injuries actually corrected before the relevant election; recurrence; plaintiff burden and access to experts; and independent assessments of whether the remedy addressed the proven mechanism. Publish missingness and uncertainty. Review both successful and unsuccessful complaints, not just cases DOJ selects to litigate.

Do not pay evaluators or enforcement staff for cases won, minority-seat counts, partisan gains or redistricting volume. Those rewards encourage weak claims, concealed defeats or inappropriate map targets. Fund independent publication, reproducible methods and review of adverse findings. Separate the evaluator's measurement role from the court's decision and the elected branches' policy choices.

**Proposed correction process:** a public owner must answer an adverse evaluation within 30 days, identify actions and responsible officials, and publish a dated follow-up. This is an illustrative service rule, not an enacted deadline. A finding of exclusion triggers a search for affected cases and a remedy proposal, rather than merely revising the dashboard. Complaints and litigation remain available while evaluation proceeds. Release legally publishable aggregate evidence while protecting personal data, privileged material and sealed records. Decisions to change substantive legal standards remain with the authorized institutions.

## 7. Public explanation

Project 2050 proposes rules that let eligible people participate fairly and obtain a remedy when election rules discriminate. Title III addresses district lines that diminish equal opportunity. Its current design would restore a results-based protection and permit carefully justified remedies.

Congress can write new law, but a new law still has to satisfy the Constitution. The proposal therefore needs both a strong account of current discrimination and protections that can operate if a court blocks part of the bill. The alternatives differ in who would be protected and how hard a claim would be to prove. A narrower approach would leave some people without the remedy the present proposal intends. That is a decision to make openly.

The test of success is whether people can secure fair treatment and a timely correction. Spending money, creating a commission or winning a lawsuit is only part of that work.

## 8. Handoff, sources and limits

**Findings:** Restoration requires more than repeating statutory language; intent enforcement and congressional standards are usable but incomplete alternatives. **Usable components:** three design specifications, remedy/default table, implementation dependencies and evaluation/correction rules. **Unresolved:** constitutional fit, proof standards, immunity/standing, judicial process, state calendars and actual costs. **Decisions for Espen:** whether to commission both drafting tracks; whether to advance DEM-2E for a separate decision; and what loss of protection, if any, would be acceptable in a fallback. None is adopted here.
