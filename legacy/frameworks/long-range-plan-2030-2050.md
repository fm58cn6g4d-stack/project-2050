# Project 2050: long-range plan, 2030-2050

Reasoning and a work plan, not decided policy (frameworks/). Drafted Oct 21, 2026 at Espen's request, as the "synthesis plan document" on the open queue. Decisions stay Espen's; each workstream below ends in options for them.

## Why a longer view now

The first-trifecta package (2029-30) is close to fully specified: every bill is priced, scheduled and voted in simulation, and the D3 and D4 tests run on every rebuild. What the project does not yet have is a picture of 2031-2050: what the second phase must do, what keeps the first phase from being repealed, and whether the country the program builds is on course by 2050. The current state is the starting point for the plan below:

- **The fiscal position.** The ten-year gap after accepted options is about +$0.26T, and the general fund passes D4-B's 2040 test by about 0.01% of GDP (REV-SC1 adopted Oct 21), a thin margin. A structural fiscal gap of about 1.5% of GDP remains as a reported sustainability track (index/fiscal-long-term-model.md).
- **Emissions.** The decided climate package reaches about 41% below 2005 in 2035 and 62% in 2050, against net zero (index/climate-carbon-model.md).
- **Social Security.** It is about 64% solvent on the 75-year test (84% with SS-5), with depletion around 2062 (index/dedicated-funds.md).
- **The calendar.** Floor time is 9 days over budget under graduated cloture.

## Three horizons

| Horizon | Window | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| H1. First trifecta | 2029-2032 | Enact the decided package; close the 2040 shortfall; build delivery capacity before benefits start |
| H2. Consolidation | 2033-2040 | Survive a possible House loss (2030) and the 2033-34 redistricting; second-phase bills (DEM-3/4/9, Moving America's later acts, the climate step-ups); reach D4-B's maturity test in FY2040 |
| H3. Transformation | 2041-2050 | Net zero; a falling debt path; programs that adjust themselves; institutions that hold under hostile control |

## 2050 benchmarks (proposal; each needs a baseline and a source before adoption)

| Area | Benchmark for 2050 | Interim check | Tool that tracks it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fiscal | Debt held by the public falling as a share of GDP from the 2040s; net interest under ~3% of GDP; the fiscal gap closed | FY2040 run-rate test (D4-B) | fiscal model (extend to 2075) |
| Social insurance | Social Security, HI and the Highway Trust Fund solvent for 75 years with sustainable solvency | 75-year balance at each Trustees report | funds model |
| Climate | Net zero greenhouse gases; 100% clean electricity | 2035: the U.S. NDC range (61-66% below 2005, to be confirmed); 2040 sector milestones | carbon model (add agriculture, land, removal) |
| Wellbeing | Child poverty under 5%; uninsured near zero; life expectancy at the peer-country average; rent burden for low-income renters under 30% of income | Annual Census and NCHS data | incidence model v2 plus an outcomes table |
| Economy | Productivity growth of at least 2% a year; a rising labor share; R&D at 3.5% of GDP; regional convergence | Five-year reviews | new macro and benchmark sheet |
| Democracy | Fair maps everywhere; universal voter registration; turnout above 70%; small-donor financing | Each election cycle | democracy scorecard (new) |
| Rights | Statutory protections in place of court-dependent ones; measurable gaps closed (policing, housing, lending, voting) | Biennial | rights scorecard (new) |
| State capacity | Federal hiring in under 60 days; benefits delivered automatically; permits decided on statutory clocks | Annual | capacity metrics (CAP-) |
| Durability | Core programs survive a simulated hostile trifecta in 2037 and 2041 | Each major bill | Talarico stress test, made a model |

## Workstreams

Each lists the questions, the deliverable, the tool work and the decisions it will bring to Espen.

### 1. Fiscal: the 2040 shortfall, the sustainability layer, debt strategy
- **Done (Oct 21):** REV-SC1 adopted (C43) to close the 2040 shortfall; the margin is thin (about 0.01% of GDP), so the uncertainty run (task 7) should report the odds it holds.
- **The sustainability layer** (the ~1.5% of GDP fiscal gap): a menu of health cost growth, revenue that grows with the economy, SS-5 and automatic adjusters, scored to 2075.
- **Debt strategy:**
  - the interest-rate risk (each point adds ~36 points of debt by 2056 in the model);
  - Treasury's maturity structure;
  - replacing the debt limit with a statutory fiscal path;
  - automatic stabilizers that pay out in a recession and phase back afterward;
  - a rule for when a deficit-financed investment counts as paying for itself.
- **Tools:** extend the fiscal model to 2075; add interest feedback and recession scenarios; Monte Carlo the D4 tests (task 7).

### 2. Revenue architecture for 2040-2050
- The current base leans on the top 1% and corporations, plus a carbon base that shrinks by design. By 2050 the project needs revenue that grows with the economy as emissions fall and AI shifts income from wages to capital.
- **Session agenda:**
  - the payroll raisers (EDU-9B, SS-7, REV-ED1);
  - a progressive consumption tax or VAT with a rebate, as an option only;
  - land value capture;
  - the AI levy (AI6, compute or token);
  - the fiscal gap that opens as the carbon base erodes;
  - what follows the CX offset convention once the carbon fees shrink.
- **Deliverable:** a 2040-2050 revenue map by channel, with the D3 test run at five and ten years and at maturity.

### 3. Program design and sustainability
- **Health cost growth.** It is the largest long-run driver. The work: a cost-growth target with enforcement, all-payer rates, the public option's role, long-term care (not yet in the agenda).
- **Automatic adjusters.** Index every permanent program to a sustainable rule, with triggers in place of cliffs (the paid-leave step-up is the model).
- **Evaluation built in.** Every program carries outcome targets and a sunset of its pilots, not of its benefits.
- **Aging and demography.** Immigration levels (IMM-) as a fiscal and labor-force lever; the old-age dependency ratio to 2050.

### 4. Institutions, government design and technology
- **A modern administrative state:** digital delivery (one federal identity and payments rail for the dividend, the child credit, leave and benefits); civil service capacity (CAP-); procurement; in-house software.
- **AI in government (AI-):** where it speeds service and adjudication; rules against automated denial; public compute.
- **Congress and the courts:** the legislative calendar problem (9 days over budget now), a second-phase rules plan, CRT- reforms and their durability.
- **Constitutional track (AMD-):** which amendments to start now for ratification by 2050.
- **Federalism:** where states lead (climate, labor), and floor-not-ceiling preemption.

### 5. Democracy
- Durability of DEM-1 through the 2031 and 2034 redistricting; the 2033 bills (DEM-3, DEM-4, DEM-9); election administration funding; disinformation and AI-generated media; campaign finance after the courts.
- **Tool:** extend the vote simulator to 2033-2040, with the Nov 3 re-baseline (open queue 3) and House scenarios.

### 6. Civil and human rights
- Rights written into statute where they now rest on precedent (RTS-); enforcement capacity; measurement of the gaps in policing, housing, lending, health and voting; immigrant rights alongside IMM-1.
- **Deliverable:** a rights scorecard and a list of which rights a hostile Court or Congress could undo, with the statutory hedge for each.

### 7. Climate to 2050
- **The model:** extend the carbon model to agriculture and land use, carbon removal and adaptation costs (CLI-7, CLI-8).
- **Questions:**
  - what follows 2037 on the fee path;
  - when road fuels converge (REV-C1D);
  - whether CLI-3's 80% target is feasible given grid access (the model says not by 2035 without CLI-10's federal build-out);
  - how far the dividend shrinks as the base erodes;
  - the international side (carbon clubs, the EU border charge).
- **Deliverable:** a 2050 net-zero pathway with sector milestones and the policy step needed at each.

### 8. American wellbeing
- Move the household test from income alone to wellbeing: health, housing, time, security.
- **Incidence model v2** (task 8): the D3 test at five and ten years and at maturity; the top 10%, 1% and 0.1%; typical families by type, as with the dividend benchmarks.
- **An outcomes table:** child poverty, uninsured, rent burden, life expectancy, deaths of despair, with the program row that moves each.

### 9. Whole-of-economy strategy
- Industrial policy and competition (COM-): which sectors, what conditions, how to end support.
- Labor (LAB-): wages, bargaining, the AI transition (AI-4, LAB-8).
- Housing supply (HOU-): units per year needed to 2050.
- Trade and supply chains; regional development; productivity.
- **Tool:** a light macro sheet (output, employment, prices, interest) so the fiscal and carbon models stop assuming a fixed economy.

### 10. National security
- Defense is held flat in real terms (NSC-1A). The study of reducing it over time (domain-national-security.md 1a), alliances, cyber and AI risk. Fiscal room from the study feeds workstream 1.

## Tools to build, in order

1. **Task 7:** one packages file shared by every model, and Monte Carlo on the gap and the D3 and D4 tests.
2. **Task 8:** incidence v2, the reconciliation vehicle checker (Byrd tests), fiscal fixes (including SS-1's flat post-2039 path from the funds model).
3. **A 2050 integrated scenario engine.** Fiscal, funds, carbon, demography and a light macro in one run. It reports every benchmark above by year to 2050 (2075 for the fiscal and trust-fund tests).
4. **A durability model.** The Talarico stress test as a simulation: which programs survive a hostile trifecta in 2037 or 2041, given their instruments (mandatory, trust fund, statute, standing disbursement authority).
5. **A benchmarks dashboard,** extending the ledger dashboard with a 2050 tab.
