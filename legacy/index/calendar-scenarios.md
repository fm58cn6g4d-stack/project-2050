# Calendar scenarios: demand, capacity and strategy

**Integrated method; illustrative, uncalibrated numbers.** These are conditional procedural completions assuming votes, eligibility and bicameral agreement. They are not enactment probabilities or household-impact forecasts. The old 172/163 comparison remains a legacy crosswalk, not the governing capacity constraint.

Generated by models/tools/calendar/build.py from the live bill inventory and calendar-scenarios.json inputs. See legacy/sources/calendar-model-review-and-integration.md for changes, assumptions, source limits and priorities.

## Timing definitions

- DEM March is a floor-start check; spring enactment and the June outer check remain separate. An after-R1 recovery needs an explicit decision, not an automatic postponement.
- Month targets use their final date; spring and summer remain windows. No arbitrary day 28 is assigned. Spring SS uses a March–May modeling convention, not a newly adopted exact deadline.
- Both chambers have independent capacity. Opening concentration adds hours and shifts other business, with every shifted hour charged back later.
- Explicit hypothetical blackouts replace closing August and December wholesale. These are experiments, not future congressional schedules.
- Vote and fiscal inputs are unchanged. Calendar completion is not multiplied into war-game vote probabilities.
