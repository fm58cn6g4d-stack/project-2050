# Possible effects before the 2030 midterms

**Opportunity screen, not a forecast.** Dates below add each policy’s existing, unvalidated felt-month lag to conditional package completion, respecting the named adopted not-before dates. This does not certify delivery, change launch dates or rephase fiscal/household models. A candidate means the arithmetic permits an initial effect; it does not establish reach, magnitude, legality, operational readiness or attribution.

The optimistic implication is plausible as a research direction: early benefits, legal protections and initial service phases may have more time to operate. The public plan (2031), its auto-enrollment (2032), Medicare hearing/vision (2031), dental (2033) and the first SS benefit COLA (2031) are not pulled into 2030 by this exercise.

## What to investigate first

1. Monthly CTC and health restoration: earliest lawful first receipt, non-filer/coverage reach, IRS/CMS/state readiness and real staffing/cash.
2. R2 initial services: school meals, pre-K/lowest-income care, dividend, housing and wage-transition dependencies; distinguish tax-year rules, initial access and full rollout.
3. Early protections: reproductive/civil rights, executive guardrails, ethics and registration can matter before a new national benefit system matures; identify enforceable dates, courts, remedies and who can actually use them.
4. SS and new health benefits: preserve adopted start dates; distinguish assurance of future solvency from a current household payment.
