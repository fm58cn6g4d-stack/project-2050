# If a Republican wins in 2028: the plan's branch

Role: what the 2029 plan becomes if a Republican wins the presidency in 2028 while Democrats keep the Senate: which parts of the plan survive, and what Project 2050 could do in that world. It answers question 7 of `compounding-2029-2050.md` (scenario D in `2028-outlook.md`). Status: draft (stage B, step 3, round 2), November 8, 2026. **Options, not decisions.** Under `DEC-2026-10-29-01` the plan assumes a Democratic president from January 2029 (D1's stipulation); this file is a contingency for that assumption failing, not a change to it. Facts were read November 8, 2026.

## In brief

- **The branch is not remote.** In the seat-by-seat model in PR #82 (another session's work, not yet merged), a Republican wins the presidency in 34% of runs under its evidence-weighted environment, and Democrats keep Senate control (51 or more, against a Republican Vice President) in **85%** of those runs, with about 53 seats on average (`senate-model-outputs.md`, section 3, on that branch). The likely shape is a Republican President, a Republican House (the map leans about 3 points Republican, `2028-outlook.md`) and a Democratic Senate.
- **A Democratic Senate blocks the Republican megabill.** Reconciliation needs a budget resolution adopted by both chambers; without the Senate, a 2029 Republican trifecta-minus-one cannot pass a 51-vote tax or spending bill (analysis from the Budget Act; `2027-28-playbook.md`, section 2a). That protects whatever the 2027-28 baseline holds, and it makes the **2028 lame duck** decisive for OBBBA's tips, overtime and senior deductions, which expire after tax year 2028.
- **The Senate's strongest power is saying no to people, and *Slaughter* weakened it.** It can refuse any nominee and hold a Supreme Court seat open, as in 2016 (Garland waited 293 days) [SRC-1001]. But a President can now fire commissioners at will (*Trump v. Slaughter*, June 29, 2026, overruling *Humphrey's Executor*) [SRC-1003] and run agencies through acting officials; 27 Senate-confirmed officials (11.4%) left within 18 months of Trump's second term, against an average of 4.3 for Bush, Obama and Biden [SRC-1004].
- **The 2030 midterm would favor Democrats, and it draws the 2031 maps.** Under a Republican President, the midterm penalty falls on Republicans in the very elections that pick the governors and legislatures who redistrict in 2031 (`compounding-redistricting.md`). The branch is worse for the 2029 agenda and better for the decade's House maps.
- **The nominee is not settled.** The PollCheck average (a UK aggregator) has Vance 37.4 and Rubio 24.8, with Rubio up about 7 points in a month; one AtlasIntel poll fielded October 28 to November 1 had Rubio ahead, 45.6 to 32.9 [SRC-1002]. The branch is written for "a Republican President," with Vance's stated positions as the main guide.

## 1. What survives of the 2029 plan

| Part of the plan | Survives? | How, and its limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reconciliation (D5's R1-R3) | **No** | Needs the House and the President |
| Regular-order bills (DEM-1, LAB-1, IMM-1, CLI-3) | No | Need the President's signature |
| **Blocking Republican reconciliation** | **Yes** | No budget resolution passes without the Senate (analysis) |
| **Senate confirmation** | Yes, weakened | Refuse nominees; hold a Supreme Court vacancy (the 2015-16 slowdown is now the template: 6 judicial confirmations from January to mid-September 2015, against 23 in 1987, 17 in 1999 and 29 in 2007, by a Brookings count) [SRC-1005]; blue slips still bind district judges and U.S. attorneys while a Democrat chairs Judiciary [SRC-1006]; pro forma sessions block recess appointments (*Noel Canning*, 2014; holding from memory, verify). Limits: actings under the Vacancies Act (210 days, extended while nominations are pending; agency-specific statutes can sidestep it) [SRC-1007], and at-will removal after *Slaughter*. Fed governors keep protection for now (*Trump v. Cook*) [SRC-1003] |
| **Appropriations** | Yes, as a veto | A shutdown has not won policy for the side opposing the President: 35 days in 2018-19 failed for Trump, and the 43-day 2025 shutdown failed for Democrats, with no ACA extension [SRC-1008]. Guardrails need the President's signature; GAO calls pocket rescissions illegal, and the administration says it is not bound [SRC-1009] |
| **Oversight** | Yes, slowly | Courts back congressional subpoenas, but compliance has come after the President leaves: Miers (contempt 2008, testimony 2009) and McGahn (subpoena 2019, testimony June 2021) [SRC-1010] [SRC-1011] |
| **Debt limit** | Yes | 60 votes, or a Republican reconciliation bill the Senate can refuse |
| **Democratic states** | Yes, unevenly | Fiscal programs hold: state ACA backfills (at least 10 states; only New Mexico fully) [SRC-1012], paid leave (13 to 15 states by different counts), minimum wages. Regulatory programs are exposed to preemption: a federal court struck New York's climate superfund law (about August 31, 2026) [SRC-1013]; the Justice Department is suing California over vehicle rules [SRC-1014]. Funding conditions have mostly lost in court (sanctuary conditions enjoined for 34 jurisdictions in 2025; the 2026 appeal's outcome not found) [SRC-1015] |
| The Congressional Review Act | No | Disapproval needs both chambers and the President |
| The 2030 census | At risk | Taken in April 2030 under the Republican administration, which also transmits apportionment by the end of 2030 (`compounding-2029-2050.md`, section 4) |

## 3. What Project 2050 could do in that world

Options, not exclusive.

1. **Re-aim the plan at January 2033.** R1 becomes a FY2033 resolution; the dossiers' designs carry over, with baselines restated for 2033. The House-trifecta stipulation in D1 (DEM-1 and DEM-2 in force before the 2031 maps) cannot be met federally, so it is replaced by state action (option 3).
2. **A Senate program for 2029-32.** Confirmation strategy (which posts to fill, which to refuse, a Supreme Court plan); appropriations guardrails; the debt limit; deals on the section 2 overlaps; Vacancies Act and Impoundment Control Act reforms written to bind any President.
3. **States as the delivery arm.** Democratic trifectas pass state versions of the flagships that rest on state taxing power (paid leave, child care, ACA backfills, minimum wages, a state rental guarantee pilot under memo 2B's design), and avoid designs exposed to preemption. Democratic renewal moves to state commissions and ballot measures before 2031.
4. **The 2030 midterm as the target.** Governors and legislatures elected in November 2030 draw the 2031 maps; the branch's best structural gain is there (`compounding-redistricting.md`).

## 4. What to do in 2027-28 that serves both branches

These are worth doing whoever wins in 2028 (analysis):
- **State certification and elector laws** in Pennsylvania and Wisconsin before 2028 (`2027-28-playbook.md`, section 3).
- **Rules that bind any President:** Vacancies Act limits, Impoundment Control Act enforcement (GAO suit authority, "shall obligate" deadlines), apportionment transparency. A Democratic Congress can write them now; they need Trump's signature, so the odds are low, but they would bind a 2029 Republican too.
- **A debt-limit raise that lasts into 2030**, so the first year of either branch has no default fight.
- **A plan for the 2028 lame duck.** In the Republican branch, the OBBBA expirations (tips, overtime, senior deductions) lapse unless a lame-duck deal extends them; a Democratic Senate then holds the line through 2032. The household test on extending them (`compounding-2029-2050.md`, question 4) decides the lame-duck position in both branches.
- **Census oversight and funding** in FY2028-29 bills.
