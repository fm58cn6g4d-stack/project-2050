# Housing in three 2029 worlds

Role: which parts of the housing design can move in each of three 2029 governing worlds, by which vehicle and when; what the 2027-28 window can pre-build or lock in; and which open housing decisions have a best answer that changes with the world. Status: draft (stage B, step 3, rounds 4 and 5: three current-law points checked against the statute and appropriations record), November 8, 2026. **Analysis only.** It ties the scenario work back to stage C: the housing design is in `dossiers/home/3-design.md` and its memos; nothing here decides a memo or edits `charter/`. Espen's choices so far, treated as working directions until recorded: **memo 1 B** (match the tool to the market; in tight markets part of HOU-4A's 2032-33 awards are attached to homes), **memo 2 B** (the money as the first two stages of a guarantee) with **a stage 3 for extremely low-income renters with disabilities from 2038, funded outside the $135 billion**, and **HOU-7B** (keep the ROAD Act's investor limit). Memos 3-8 are undecided.

## In brief

- **The worlds and their weights.** From the House model in PR #88 (another session's work, not yet merged; evidence-weighted environment): a **Democratic trifecta** in about **58%** of runs; a **Democratic President with a Republican House** about **7%** (66% Democratic President times 11% without the House); a **Republican President with a Democratic Senate** about **29%** (34% times 85%); a full Republican government about 5%. The design should work in the first world and degrade gracefully in the other two.
- **The heart of the housing plan needs reconciliation.** HOU-4A's million awards, memo 2's guarantee stages and HOU-3C's capital are mandatory money on R2, the FY2030 reconciliation bill the legacy plan targets for summer 2029 (`3-design.md`, "Starting points"). Without a trifecta none of it moves in 2029-32.
- **Three recommended designs work in every world** because they use current law or annual money: memo 4 B (pay for results through the ROAD Act's Innovation Fund, which only needs appropriations), memo 5 B (Housing First by revoking the 2025 order, an executive act), and HOU-7B (no action). These are the safest bets.
- **Current law already holds much of memo 1 B.** Housing agencies may attach an extra 10% of vouchers to buildings in areas HUD finds vouchers "difficult to use" (42 U.S.C. 1437f(o)(13)) [SRC-1055], so a Democratic HUD can act on 1 B without new law.
- **Three open decisions change with the world** (section 3): memo 6 (a standing emergency-rent fund needs mandatory law; outside a trifecta only annual or one-time money exists), memo 3 part 1 (narrowing the tax credits is a reconciliation tax change; in divided government the likely bipartisan deal goes the other way, toward broader credits), and memo 3 part 2 (a federal builder needs an authorizing law no divided government would pass).
- **Memo 6 can be one rulebook with three funding taps** (section 5): a standing fund in world 1, an annual line in worlds 2 and 3, and a one-time surge in any world, all on the same delivery rules.
- **2027-28 can pre-build the appropriated layer, not the guarantee.** Voucher renewals and new vouchers (including disability-specific Mainstream vouchers, the natural forerunner of stage 3), HOME, homeless grants and the Innovation Fund all move through FY2027-28 appropriations, which fit every memo option (`2027-28-playbook.md`; `compounding-prebuild.md`, home rows).

## 3. Decisions whose best answer changes with the world

These are inputs to memos 3-8, which Espen has not decided. Each says what the world changes, not what Espen should choose.

1. **Memo 6, prevention (standing fund or one-time).** The recommended C (a standing fund plus a smaller start) needs mandatory law, so it exists only in world 1. In worlds 2 and 3 the only federal tools are one-time or annual appropriations, which favor a design that can be switched on in a crisis (memo 6's option A or B shape) and state funds. *Question for memo 6:* should the standing fund be designed so its delivery rules can also run on one-time money?
2. **Memo 3 part 1, tax credits (narrow or keep).** Narrowing the three credits with permanent affordability (recommended B) is a tax change that rides reconciliation in world 1. In worlds 2 and 3, recent bipartisan law has expanded the credit: a 12.5% increase in state allocations for 2018-21 [SRC-1056], then the 2025 law's permanent 12% increase from 2026 and a bond-financing test cut from 50% to 25% for buildings with bonds issued after 2025 (Pub. L. 119-21, section 70422) [SRC-1057]. The housing dossier's 2029 baseline already includes the 2025 change (`3-design.md`, "The baseline in 2029") [EV-HOU-113]. *Question for memo 3:* if a 2027-28 or post-2029 bipartisan bill expands the credit, does the plan treat it as a floor to narrow later, or oppose it?
3. **Memo 3 part 2, a federal builder.** Only world 1 can authorize it. *Question:* is a small builder worth a regular-order fight under D2 in 2029-30, or should its role go to HOU-3C's acquisition channel (part 3), which reconciliation can carry?
4. **Memo 1 B's attached help.** Robust in world 1; in world 2 much of it exists in current law: the extra 10% of project-based vouchers and the 40% per-building cap already apply in areas HUD determines vouchers are "difficult to use" [SRC-1055]. *Question:* should section 4 (specification) define memo 1 B's tight-market trigger so HUD can adopt it as that determination, giving 1 B a path that needs no new law?
5. **Stage 3 for disabled renters.** Funded outside the $135 billion from 2038 in world 1; in worlds 2 and 3 the only route is Mainstream vouchers in annual bills. *Question:* should stage 3 be specified as an expansion of Mainstream vouchers, so that annual increases from 2027 on build toward it in any world?
6. **Memo 8, who first.** Inside HOU-4A's law in world 1; in world 2 only guidance to local agencies. The choice of order does not change, but its reach does.

Unchanged by the world: memo 4 B and memo 5 B (both reach their goal through current law or executive action in two of three worlds), and HOU-7B.

## 5. A design sketch for memo 6: one rulebook, three funding taps

Analysis only, for memo 6 (`dossiers/home/memos/6-prevention.md`), which Espen has not decided. The idea: write the emergency-rent program's delivery rules once, so the same rules run whether the money is a standing fund (world 1), an annual appropriation (worlds 2 and 3) or a one-time emergency appropriation (any world, in a recession or disaster).

**The rulebook (the same in every world).** Drawn from memo 6's own evidence:
- *Who:* renters at imminent risk of losing their home (arrears, an eviction filing or notice, a utility shutoff), under an income line; a short risk screen to aim help at those most likely to lose housing, since many recipients would otherwise have stayed housed [EV-HOU-108] [EV-HOU-111].
- *What:* a capped one-time payment (back rent, a month forward, a deposit, legal help), paid to the landlord or the tenant.
- *How:* renters can state their own income and hardship, with sample verification afterward, because spending doubled in the month the last program allowed self-attestation [EV-HOU-150]; landlords can apply on a tenant's behalf; payment errors are measured from year one, the gap the Treasury inspector general found in the last program [EV-HOU-154] [EV-HOU-182].
- *Who runs it:* the existing local network (the 2021 emergency rental assistance grantees and Emergency Solutions Grants recipients, who already fund prevention), so no program is built from scratch in a crisis.
- *Evidence:* every grantee reports outcomes on one data standard, so the program is evaluated as it runs.

**The taps.**

| Tap | World | How it is funded | What it adds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Standing fund | 1 (trifecta) | Mandatory money on R2, with an automatic increase when unemployment rises | Help is always there, and grows in a downturn |
| Annual line | 2 and 3 | An appropriation into the same account (for example, a prevention set-aside within Emergency Solutions Grants) | A smaller, steady program on the same rules |
| One-time surge | Any | An emergency appropriation in a recession or disaster, into the same account | Money reaches people in weeks because the rules and grantees exist |
| States | Any | State money run through the same rulebook, with a federal match where funded | A route in world 3 |

**What 2027-28 could do (analysis).** An authorizing bill that writes the rulebook and an account with "such sums as necessary" and no money could be bipartisan, as the ROAD Act was; or a FY2027-28 appropriation could fund a pilot on the rules. Either would give memo 6's option C a running start in any world, and make a recession-year one-time surge a matter of money, not design.

**What it means for memo 6's options.** It does not choose between A, B and C. It changes C's risk in worlds 2 and 3 (the rules survive without the mandatory fund) and A's and B's start-up problem (a one-time package would run on rules already in place).
