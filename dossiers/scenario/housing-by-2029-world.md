# Housing in three 2029 worlds

Role: which parts of the housing design can move in each of three 2029 governing worlds, by which vehicle and when; what the 2027-28 window can pre-build or lock in; and which open housing decisions have a best answer that changes with the world. Status: draft (stage B, step 3, round 4), November 8, 2026. **Analysis only.** It ties the scenario work back to stage C: the housing design is in `dossiers/home/3-design.md` and its memos; nothing here decides a memo or edits `charter/`. Espen's choices so far, treated as working directions until recorded: **memo 1 B** (match the tool to the market; in tight markets part of HOU-4A's 2032-33 awards are attached to homes), **memo 2 B** (the money as the first two stages of a guarantee) with **a stage 3 for extremely low-income renters with disabilities from 2038, funded outside the $135 billion**, and **HOU-7B** (keep the ROAD Act's investor limit). Memos 3-8 are undecided.

## In brief

- **The worlds and their weights.** From the House model in PR #88 (another session's work, not yet merged; evidence-weighted environment): a **Democratic trifecta** in about **58%** of runs; a **Democratic President with a Republican House** about **7%** (66% Democratic President times 11% without the House); a **Republican President with a Democratic Senate** about **29%** (34% times 85%); a full Republican government about 5%. The design should work in the first world and degrade gracefully in the other two.
- **The heart of the housing plan needs reconciliation.** HOU-4A's million awards, memo 2's guarantee stages and HOU-3C's capital are mandatory money on R2, the FY2030 reconciliation bill the legacy plan targets for summer 2029 (`3-design.md`, "Starting points"). Without a trifecta none of it moves in 2029-32.
- **Three recommended designs work in every world** because they use current law or annual money: memo 4 B (pay for results through the ROAD Act's Innovation Fund, which only needs appropriations), memo 5 B (Housing First by revoking the 2025 order, an executive act), and HOU-7B (no action). These are the safest bets.
- **Three open decisions change with the world** (section 3): memo 6 (a standing emergency-rent fund needs mandatory law; outside a trifecta only annual or one-time money exists), memo 3 part 1 (narrowing the tax credits is a reconciliation tax change; in divided government the likely bipartisan deal goes the other way, toward broader credits), and memo 3 part 2 (a federal builder needs an authorizing law no divided government would pass).
- **2027-28 can pre-build the appropriated layer, not the guarantee.** Voucher renewals and new vouchers (including disability-specific Mainstream vouchers, the natural forerunner of stage 3), HOME, homeless grants and the Innovation Fund all move through FY2027-28 appropriations, which fit every memo option (`2027-28-playbook.md`; `compounding-prebuild.md`, home rows).

## 3. Decisions whose best answer changes with the world

These are inputs to memos 3-8, which Espen has not decided. Each says what the world changes, not what Espen should choose.

1. **Memo 6, prevention (standing fund or one-time).** The recommended C (a standing fund plus a smaller start) needs mandatory law, so it exists only in world 1. In worlds 2 and 3 the only federal tools are one-time or annual appropriations, which favor a design that can be switched on in a crisis (memo 6's option A or B shape) and state funds. *Question for memo 6:* should the standing fund be designed so its delivery rules can also run on one-time money?
2. **Memo 3 part 1, tax credits (narrow or keep).** Narrowing the three credits with permanent affordability (recommended B) is a tax change that rides reconciliation in world 1. In worlds 2 and 3, the bipartisan tax-credit coalition has historically pushed to *expand* the credit (memory, verify), and the 2025 law may already have enlarged it (memory, verify against the statute). *Question for memo 3:* if a 2027-28 or post-2029 bipartisan bill expands the credit, does the plan treat it as a floor to narrow later, or oppose it?
3. **Memo 3 part 2, a federal builder.** Only world 1 can authorize it. *Question:* is a small builder worth a regular-order fight under D2 in 2029-30, or should its role go to HOU-3C's acquisition channel (part 3), which reconciliation can carry?
4. **Memo 1 B's attached help.** Robust in world 1; in world 2 a partial executive version exists (project-basing existing vouchers in tight markets). *Question:* should section 4 (specification) write 1 B so that its tight-market rule can also guide HUD's use of existing project-based authority, giving it a non-reconciliation path?
5. **Stage 3 for disabled renters.** Funded outside the $135 billion from 2038 in world 1; in worlds 2 and 3 the only route is Mainstream vouchers in annual bills. *Question:* should stage 3 be specified as an expansion of Mainstream vouchers, so that annual increases from 2027 on build toward it in any world?
6. **Memo 8, who first.** Inside HOU-4A's law in world 1; in world 2 only guidance to local agencies. The choice of order does not change, but its reach does.

Unchanged by the world: memo 4 B and memo 5 B (both reach their goal through current law or executive action in two of three worlds), and HOU-7B.
