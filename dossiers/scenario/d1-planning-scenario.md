# D1, the planning scenario (proposed update)

Role: the proposed replacement for legacy decision D1, "Which scenario do we plan for?" (`legacy/governing/project-2050-governing-decisions.md`, D1). Status: **proposed, for Espen's decision** (tier 3). Until Espen approves it and it is logged in `charter/decisions.md`, the legacy D1 stays in force. The legacy file is read-only (`charter/STANDARDS.md`), so the updated decision lives here and the decision log points to it. The evidence is `1-results.md` and `5-senate-2028.md`. D2 to D9 are unchanged.

## What changes and why

The legacy D1 planned for a Senate of 52-54 seats, tested every item at 51, and treated 56 or more as upside. It was written before the 2026 elections and assumed Democrats would hold the Senate through 2036 if they reached 52 or more in 2026.

Two things are now known:
- **2026 produced about 55-56 seats** (54 called; Mississippi likely and Alaska leaning Democratic), above the old band (`1-results.md`).
- **The 2029 Senate is set by the 2028 elections.** Democrats defend 16 seats and Republicans 19. Conditional on a Democratic president, the scenario estimate is about 53-57 seats as the central range, 52 or more very likely, and 58 or more an upside (`5-senate-2028.md`). A midterm environment has usually reverted by several points in the next presidential year (four of six cases since 1994), so the 2026 result alone would likely overstate 2029.

## The options

| Option | Planning band | Stress test | Upside | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. Keep the legacy D1 | 52-54 | 51 | 56+ | Safe, but below the central estimate: items that need 55 holdout-proof votes are left out of the base package |
| **B. Move the band to the estimate (recommended)** | **53-56** | **51** | **58+** | Matches the central range while keeping the 51-seat test as the floor; the stress test is unchanged, so no item that survives today is dropped |
| C. Plan for the 2026 result | 55-57 | 52 | 60 | Assumes no reversion from a 13-point midterm; fails in an even year, when the estimate centers near 52-54 |

**Recommendation: B.** It follows the evidence without betting on a repeat of 2026. Keeping the stress test at 51 means the change adds options for a larger Senate without removing any protection for a smaller one. The probabilities behind it are this project's exploratory estimate, not a forecast, and should be rechecked when the 2026 results are certified and the first published 2028 ratings appear.

## The decision text, if B is chosen

**D1, decided: plan for a Senate of 53-56 seats; test every item against 51; treat 58 or more as upside.** Each domain file designs for the 53-56 band and reports the stress test as before: ✔ survives at 51 (reconciliation, state, executive, House-only); ◐ needs a D2 majority-precedent path; ✖ needs 60, 67 or Republican votes in any scenario.

**Scenario stipulations.** For modeling, the project assumes:
- **White House:** a Democrat holds it from January 2029 to at least January 2037 (unchanged).
- **Senate:** Democrats hold 53-56 seats from January 2029. Holding the Senate through 2036 is no longer stipulated outright: the 2030 class (elected in 2024) and above all the 2032 class, in which Democrats defend six or more seats won in Republican-leaning states in a D+13 year (Texas, North Carolina, South Carolina, Kansas, Iowa, Maine, and Alaska and Mississippi if won), are real exposures. Plans that run past January 2033 state what they need if the majority narrows.
- **House:** Democrats enter 2027 with about 256-263 seats. A 2029 majority is assumed with the presidency; it is possibly lost or narrowed in 2030; a trifecta again from January 2033 on the condition that the electoral and civil-rights package (DEM-1, with DEM-2 and the *Callais* response) is in force before the 2031 redistricting (unchanged in substance). *Louisiana v. Callais* (April 29, 2026) and the 2025-26 mid-decade redraws make DEM-1's timing more pressing, not less.
- **Permitting:** a version of the bipartisan permitting bill (S. 5653, the American Affordability and Jobs Act) is enacted in the 2026 lame duck or the 2027-28 Congress (unchanged; its status is to be recorded after the lame duck).
- **First-trifecta scope:** unchanged from the legacy D1.

These are modeling assumptions, not forecasts.

## Where the new D1 bears on D2 to D9 (notes only; no change proposed)

- **D2, Senate rules:** a 53-56 band absorbs more holdouts on a rules change than 52-54: at 55, five Democrats could defect and graduated cloture (F) would still pass with the Vice President. The 2027 caucus's stated positions differ from the legacy count (`4-2029-plan.md`), so D2's whip count should be re-run whichever band is chosen.
- **D5, reconciliation:** unaffected by the band; the FY2029 slot is now safe from Republican use, with one procedural question open (`3-framework-2027-28.md`, question 6).
- **D8, Social Security:** a regular-order bill under D2's rules change either way; a larger band makes the spring 2030 bill easier to pass but does not remove the need for the rules change.
- **D3, D4, D6, D7, D9:** no direct effect. A larger Senate widens what can pass, not what the fiscal rule allows.
