# What 2028 looks like: scenarios and drivers

Role: the presidential race, the House map, the governor races and the national environment in 2028, written as scenarios with the drivers that would move between them. The 2028 Senate is modeled in `5-senate-2028.md` and is used here, not repeated. Status: draft (stage B, step 3), as of November 8, 2026. **Scenarios, not predictions.** No 2028 ratings, primary polls fielded after November 3, or 2028 general-election polls exist yet. Every source was read on November 8, 2026 unless another date is given. Figures marked "(analysis)" are an author's interpretation; publishers' leans and stakes are named where they matter.

Under `DEC-2026-10-29-01` this belongs to the 2027-28 exception: it asks what kind of 2029 the plan should expect, and it decides nothing.

## In brief

- **The Republican succession has already split over the war.** On November 4 Vice President Vance posted that "the reason we lost is the war," and Donald Trump Jr. endorsed it; President Trump answered that he "would have done nothing different" and "maybe he won't run" [SRC-0800]. Vance led the pre-election primary polls (NYT/Siena: Vance 33, Rubio 23) [SRC-0803], while Vance himself leads the indirect Iran talks [SRC-0828]. Whether Vance can own an end to the war while staying Trump's heir is the main Republican driver.
- **The Democratic field is open and the calendar is new.** No major candidate has declared. The DNC's early window opens in South Carolina on January 22, 2028, then Nevada, New Hampshire, New Mexico, Michigan and Virginia; Iowa is out again and New Hampshire's state law sets up a conflict [SRC-0808]. Republicans keep Iowa first [SRC-0807].
- **The House map leans about 3 points Republican.** Under the 2026 lines the median seat is Trump +4.5 (WI-1), against a national Trump +1.5 in 2024 [SRC-0817]; one academic estimate puts the Republican map edge at about 10 seats and the Democratic break-even near D+3 [SRC-0818]. A midterm majority has usually shrunk in the next presidential year [SRC-0568]. A 2028 Democratic House is likely at D+5 or better and at risk below about D+3.
- **History allows anything from D+5 to more than D+13.** In four of the six midterms since 1994 the environment reverted 4 to 8 points two years later; in 2006-08 the wave grew (`5-senate-2028.md`, section 2). Analysts split: Nate Silver reads 2006 as the warning for Republicans; Brookings and a Northeastern political scientist call the record inconclusive [SRC-0812] [SRC-0810].
- **The 2030 census changes the target after 2028.** On four published projections Texas gains 4 seats and California loses 3 or 4 [SRC-0825] [SRC-0826]. Applied to the 2024 map, the Harris states plus Wisconsin, Michigan and Pennsylvania fall from 270 electoral votes to 258, and the tipping point moves from Pennsylvania to Georgia [SRC-0827]. 2028 is the last presidential race on the 2020 apportionment; from 2032, a Democrat needs the Sun Belt.

## 2. The House map in 2028

**What carries over.** Most 2026 lines stay. California's Proposition 50 map runs through 2030; Texas keeps its 2025 map; Missouri voters rejected the new map about 62-38, so its 2022 lines stay; Virginia's 10-1 Democratic map was struck down on May 8, 2026, and no new amendment can reach the ballot before 2028 [SRC-0815] [SRC-0816] [SRC-0829] [SRC-0958]. Utah's court-drawn map stays.

**What could still move before 2028.**

| State | Possible change | When it is decided | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| New York | Constitutional amendment allowing a redraw: second passage by the 2027 legislature, then a **November 2027 referendum**, effective 2028 | 2027 | [SRC-0815] |
| Maryland | Question 3 passed on November 3 (about 1.1 million to 718,000, unofficial), aimed at MD-1, the state's one Republican seat | Map in 2027 | [SRC-0957] |
| Wisconsin | Two Democratic suits seeking a new map for 2028, heard by the state supreme court (5-2 liberal) in September 2026 on whether their dismissal was proper | Ruling in 2027, then a remand and trial; the April 2027 court race can widen the majority but not flip it (`2027-races.md`) | [SRC-0959] |
| Florida, Tennessee, Louisiana, Alabama | Post-*Callais* maps that removed Black-majority districts; litigation continues | 2027-28 rulings | [SRC-0816] |
| Mississippi | Governor Reeves expects redistricting before the 2027 state elections | 2027 | [SRC-0815] |
| Georgia | Governor Kemp declined to call a session before Lance Bottoms takes office | Closed for now | [SRC-0815] |
| Colorado | State supreme court held mid-decade redistricting unconstitutional (June 29, 2026); Amendment 86, which would have limited redraws further, failed (about 46% against a 55% threshold, early returns) | Closed | [SRC-0815] [SRC-0961] |
| Others | Democrats could redraw in up to 12 states before 2028 and Republicans in 16, but commissions in Colorado, New Jersey, New York and Washington need voters' approval to bypass | 2027 sessions | [SRC-0817] |

**The structural lean.** Under the 2026 lines Trump would have carried 234 districts to 201, against 230-205 under the 2024 lines, and the median seat moved from AZ-1 (Trump +3.1) to WI-1 (Trump +4.5) [SRC-0817]. Sam Wang (Princeton Gerrymandering Project, a reform advocate) puts the Republican map edge at "around 10 seats" in 2028 and Democrats' break-even at about D+3 [SRC-0818]. Both sources lean progressive or reform; the figure needs corroboration from a rater (Cook, Inside Elections) when 2028 ratings appear.

**What history says.** The party that wins the House in a midterm has usually lost seats in the next presidential year (Republicans in 2012, 2016 and 2024; Democrats in 2020); the last party to gain in both was Democrats in 2006 and 2008 [SRC-0568].

## 5. Four scenarios

The environment is stated as the 2028 Democratic presidential popular-vote margin. The Senate figures come from `5-senate-2028.md` (40-seat base, the more likely one); the House reads use the D+3 break-even above. **None is a forecast**, and no probability is attached.

| Scenario | National margin | How it happens (drivers) | Signposts to watch in 2027 | Senate, January 2029 | House | What it means for the 2029 plan |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A. The wave holds (2006-08)** | D+7 or more | The war drags on; prices stay high; Trump stays the issue; Republicans fight over the war; Democrats deliver some visible wins | Trump approval below 38; inflation above 3%; a contested Republican primary; Democrats win Kentucky or Mississippi's governorship (`2027-races.md`) | About 57 (the D+6 row; 58 or more is about one in three) | Holds with a large margin | D1's upside: room for more regular-order bills, but 60 is still remote (6% at D+6) |
| **B. Normal reversion** | D+3 to D+6 | The war winds down; inflation eases toward 2.5%; the 2028 race becomes a choice between two new nominees | A ceasefire in 2027 that reopens Hormuz (the single most important signpost); approval near 40; Democrats split on the filibuster or Medicare for All | About 55-56 | Holds, smaller | D1's proposed working band (53-56, PR #80) |
| **C. Toss-up** | D+0 to D+3 | A credible Vance running as the end-of-war, cost-of-living candidate; Democrats blamed for a shutdown or "nothing passed" | Vance credited with a ceasefire and a signed child tax credit; FY2028 standoff blamed on Congress | About 53-54 | At risk: below the D+3 break-even it likely falls | A presidency won narrowly with a thin or lost House: reconciliation only if the House holds; the 51-seat stress test applies |
| **D. **Reading across them.** The Senate is less sensitive than the House: even scenario C leaves a likely Democratic Senate because of the 2028 map. The House is the hinge between B and C, because of the roughly 3-point map lean. That makes three 2027 levers worth more than they look: the New York and Maryland redraws, the Wisconsin map case, and the post-*Callais* litigation in the South (`2027-races.md`, section 6). It also makes the cost record (`2027-28-playbook.md`) the main thing Democrats control.
