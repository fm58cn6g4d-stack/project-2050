# Stress tests for the 2027-28 scenario

Role: hard questions the drafts of `2027-races.md`, `2027-28-playbook.md`, `2028-outlook.md`, `compounding-2029-2050.md` and the round-2 files raised, with the research done to answer each and the revisions made because of it. Status: draft (stage B, step 3), as of November 8, 2026. Three rounds of three questions. Answers are **analysis** built on the cited sources, which were read November 8, 2026. The combined "Next questions" list for Espen and for later sessions is at the end.

## Round 1

### 1. What if Trump signs more than expected?

**Why it is hard.** The playbook's odds assume most of the agenda is vetoed or blocked. If Trump wants wins before 2028, or Vance does, Democrats could get laws, and lose credit and design control.

**What the research found.**
- Trump's pattern on the ACA credits was trade, not principle: in November 2025 he said he would "rather not" extend them but that "some kind of extension may be necessary to get something else done" [SRC-0988].
- Health experts expect the deal space to be drug prices (most-favored-nation pricing, PBMs, patents), prior authorization and price transparency, with ACA relief possible only if paired with a Trump priority such as health savings accounts (KFF's Larry Levitt, nonpartisan) [SRC-0972] [SRC-0970]. Axios's sources call piecemeal affordability fixes "the most realistic option" [SRC-0974].
- Relief can be backdated: a 2027 deal could refund 2027 premiums to January, and Democrats want open enrollment reopened past January 15 [SRC-0970] [SRC-0971].
- Levitt: Democrats likely have **one year, not two**, since "Congress tends not to do a lot during the years of presidential campaigns" [SRC-0972].

**What it changes.**
- *Playbook:* adds "front-load 2027" as the timing rule and a row on the price of a deal (section 2c and new section 2e). ACA odds stay at 25-40%, with the likely form a time-limited extension carrying a Trump priority.
- *Compounding:* adds codified most-favored-nation drug pricing to the lock-in table. If Trump signs his own pricing design into law, the 2029 drug-price starting point is his design, which Care's dossier has not weighed (section 2).
- *2028 outlook:* signed cost laws in 2027 move toward scenario B or C; credit is shared, and Vance would claim it.

### 2. What if the Supreme Court rules for the RNC in *Mi Familia Vota*?

**Why it is hard.** The playbook treats election protection as mostly a state job. A ruling that lets states demand documentary proof of citizenship on their own forms, and purge rolls inside the 90-day quiet period, would change the 2028 rules in Republican states, which Democratic trifectas cannot reach.

**What the research found.**
- The questions: whether the NVRA (or a 2018 consent decree) bars Arizona's proof-of-citizenship rule for its state form, turning on whether a state agency form must be "equivalent" to the federal form; and whether systematic noncitizen removals are allowed in the quiet period [SRC-0989] [SRC-0984]. The federal form remains protected by the Court's 2013 ruling either way [SRC-0983].
- About 21.3 million citizens of voting age, 9.1%, lack ready access to proof of citizenship (Brennan Center, center-left) [SRC-0983]. On the right, election-integrity groups argue that affirming the Ninth Circuit could make noncitizens or false registrations impossible to remove [SRC-0985].
- A decision by about June 2027 leaves about 16 months before the 2028 election, enough for legislatures meeting in 2028 to copy Arizona.

**What it changes.**
- *Playbook (section 4):* the row now names the two questions and the federal-form fallback: wherever a state demands proof of citizenship, registration through the federal form becomes the protection, and that needs organizing and money in 2027-28, not legislation.
- *Compounding:* DEM-1's voting floor would need to restate the NVRA's protections if the Court narrows them; a note for democratic renewal.

### 3. What if Democrats split, on the filibuster, on leadership, or on what reconciliation pays for?

**Why it is hard.** The playbook's 65-80% odds that a reconciliation bill passes Congress assume near-unanimity among 55-56 senators who range from El-Sayed to Hamilton.

**What the research found.**
- Unity is real on the first bills: Senator Cortez Masto, who recruited many of the moderates, expects agreement on restoring the ACA credits, tariffs, the war and ICE [SRC-0976]; NPR reports "near-total agreement" on the first-100-hours list [SRC-0977].
- The splits are on pay-fors and on the next big idea. Semafor notes California voters rejected the billionaire tax even in a wave, so paying for restoration with taxes on the wealthy "is challenging" [SRC-0973]. House Democrats' 84-page health memo plans first-year votes on Medicare for All, a public option and a Medicare buy-in [SRC-0978]; Sanders wants Medicare at 55 through reconciliation [SRC-0979]; Schatz says bolder policy "is for 2028" [SRC-0977]. Red-state senators will diverge on energy (Cortez Masto on Talarico and oil and gas); Turek says he would have voted with Republicans on farm and data-center bills [SRC-0976].
- On the filibuster, four named opponents, which leave exactly 51 votes at 55 seats and 52 at 56: abolition needs every other Democrat (playbook, section 6).

**What it changes.**
- *Playbook:* reconciliation passing Congress lowered to **55-75%**, with the risk named: the pay-for package, not the restoration. The sequencing rule (consensus first, the bigger health debate in hearings) is now stated as the input docs' advice and as the evidence's.
- *2028 outlook:* a public Medicare for All fight is listed as a scenario C driver (already there); no change.

## Round 2

### 4. What if Vance ends the war in 2027?

**Why it is hard.** The 2028 scenarios treat the war as a driver toward Democrats. A ceasefire that reopens the Strait of Hormuz would lower energy prices under Trump and give Vance the record he says Republicans lacked.

**What the research found.**
- Talks run through Qatar and Pakistan; Iran's condensed plan would reopen the Strait of Hormuz within two days of a deal in exchange for lifting the U.S. blockade of Iranian ports, unfreezing assets and waiving oil sanctions, with nuclear issues later. Trump rejected the seven-step plan in September but says a deal "could happen"; Vance: "we've certainly made some progress" [SRC-0980] [SRC-0982]. Iran says there is "no new progress" and Washington's demands are "excessive" [SRC-0981]. Oil fell nearly 4% on November 2 on hopes of a deal [SRC-0982].
- Gasoline rose 34% over the year to September (CPI) [SRC-0903]; EIA's October outlook already has retail gasoline falling from $3.91 in 2026 to $3.56 in 2027 [SRC-0905].

**What it changes.**
- *2028 outlook (section 4):* the war row now says how a deal would move prices (the Hormuz route), not only casualties; scenario B's "ceasefire in 2027" signpost is the single most important one to watch.
- *Playbook:* the war-powers lever's value as receipts falls if a deal comes; the cost record matters more. Democrats' options if a deal is near: support it and hold oversight of its terms, rather than contest it.

### 5. What if the Southern maps hold and Republicans redraw more for 2028?

**Why it is hard.** `2027-races.md` concluded that the 2027 redistricting decisions favor Democrats on net. That rests on Missouri, Texas and Georgia closing, which may not hold everywhere.

**What the research found.**
- NCSL's tracker (bipartisan) confirms Georgia's legislature declined to redraw, South Carolina's Senate rejected a map, Indiana's Senate voted one down in December 2025, and Alabama's 2023 map is back in force after the Supreme Court reversed a post-*Callais* ruling against it on June 2, 2026 [SRC-0986]. It also records that **on May 18, 2026 the Supreme Court reversed a 2024 order requiring Mississippi to redraw its legislative districts**, and the Fifth Circuit reversed the order on its supreme court districts [SRC-0986]. That removes the legal basis of the districts that broke the Mississippi Senate's two-thirds majority in 2025.
- AP (November 4): "What started as a Republican redistricting effort could become a Democratic redistricting movement ahead of the presidential election"; Maryland's Governor Moore is "ready to engage" on a map [SRC-0987] [SRC-0986].
- The post-*Callais* Southern maps (Florida, Tennessee, Louisiana, Alabama) look durable: the first federal application of the new test upheld Alabama's map [SRC-0986].

**What it changes.**
- *2027 races:* Mississippi's row now says the court orders behind the 2025 districts were reversed, so a 2027 redraw is legally easier. The net reading stands (Democrats gain more options than Republicans in 2027), but the Southern maps are likelier to stay than the first draft implied.
- *2028 outlook:* the Republican map lean of about 10 seats stays as the planning figure; the Southern litigation is no longer a likely offset.

### 6. What if the debt limit binds in February 2027, before the FY2027 bills are done?

**Why it is hard.** The playbook put the debt limit beside FY2027 in the first quarter. If the limit is reached in February and the stopgap runs out at about the same time, the first test of the new Congress could be a joint shutdown-and-default standoff.

**What the research found.** Hitting the limit is not the X-date. Wall Street desks expect the limit to be reached in mid-to-late February 2027 but a funding crunch only in late 2027 [SRC-0886]; BPC notes an earlier contact can mean a later X-date, because a longer debt-issuance suspension unlocks more extraordinary measures [SRC-0884]; NTU's X-date is February 2028 [SRC-0528].

**What it changes.**
- *Playbook (section 2a):* the debt limit's leverage moment is late 2027, beside FY2028 (October 1), not February. The likely shape is a single fall-2027 package or standoff on FY2028 and the debt limit together, landing as the 2028 primaries begin. A raise sized to last into 2030 is the option that serves the 2029 plan.

## Round 3 (after the lead's round-2 questions)

Round 2's new files (`2028-republican-branch.md`, `compounding-prebuild.md`, `compounding-redistricting.md`, `compounding-defense.md`) raised three more.

### 7. What if the Republican nominee is Rubio, not Vance?

**Why it is hard.** `2028-republican-branch.md` reads the deal space from Vance's stated positions. The PollCheck average shows Rubio up about 7 points in a month, and one AtlasIntel poll (October 28 to November 1) had him ahead, 45.6 to 32.9 [SRC-1002].

**What it changes (analysis).** The branch's structure (a Democratic Senate blocking reconciliation, confirmations, the 2030 midterm) does not depend on the nominee. The overlap table does: Rubio co-wrote the 2017 Rubio-Lee child tax credit amendment (memory; verify), so the child credit stays the likeliest deal, while Vance's war restraint and antitrust record are his own. A Rubio presidency is likelier to keep the Iran war's military posture, which keeps the war as a 2030 midterm driver. Revision: the branch now says it is written for "a Republican President," with Vance's positions as a guide only.

### 8. What if DEM-2 passes in 2029 and the Court strikes the commission mandate before 2031?

**Why it is hard.** `compounding-redistricting.md` values DEM-2 at about 5 to 10 seats a cycle, but its riskiest piece (mandated commissions) faces a Court with three justices who dissented when it upheld state commissions in 2015 (memory; verify).

**What it changes (analysis).** If the statute is not severable, the whole package can fall with the commissions, leaving 2030's winners to draw. A severable design keeps the mid-decade ban and federal criteria, the two lower-risk pieces, which still remove the 2028-30 redraw wars and some of the partisan swing. Revision: the redistricting file now puts the split as Espen's question 2, and the pre-build pipeline lists DEM-2 at direction level only.

### 9. What if Republicans refuse parity and FY2027-28 run on full-year stopgaps?

**Why it is hard.** `compounding-defense.md` treats parity as a trade Democrats can choose. FY2025 ran on a full-year stopgap (the Ledger's own history [SRC-0545]), and a Republican Senate minority can force one again.

**What it changes (analysis).** Full-year stopgaps land near path 1: defense roughly flat in nominal terms (below flat real), nondefense frozen. NSC-1A's base would be the lowest of the paths, but the nondefense rebuild the Ledger wants would be deferred, so CAP-7S's 2029 surge grows by roughly the forgone increases. The defense lock-in and the nondefense rebuild move together: the cheapest defense path is also the most expensive 2029 rebuild. Revision: noted in the defense file's reading of the parity trade.
