# The 2027-28 governing playbook, as options

Role: the levers a Democratic Congress, Democratic governors and the courts give Democrats from January 2027 to January 2029; for each, what it could achieve, rough odds, and how it sets the conditions for 2028 (the cost-of-living record, the "who blocked it" receipts, and a protected 2028 election) and for the 2029 plan. Status: draft (stage B, step 3), as of November 8, 2026; revised after two rounds of stress tests (`2027-28-stress-tests.md`). **Options, not decisions.** Under `DEC-2026-10-29-01` this is the 2027-28 exception: it serves the 2029 plan and replaces none of it. It builds on `3-framework-2027-28.md` (levers, vehicles, success options A-F, open questions 1-12) and uses the two input docs, "Deliver" [SRC-0544] and "The Lame-Duck Ledger" [SRC-0545], as inputs, not constraints.

**How to read the odds.** Every probability here is **this project's judgment (analysis)**, stated as a range with the reason, so a reader can disagree with the reasoning rather than the number. Where the Ledger gives odds, both are shown. Facts are cited; every source was read on November 8, 2026 unless another date is given.

## In brief

- **The veto is real but has been used sparingly and for leverage, not policy.** Trump has vetoed two bills in his second term, both minor bipartisan bills on December 29, 2025, and the House sustained both [SRC-0840]. In March 2026 he said he would sign no bills until the SAVE America Act passed [SRC-0841]; the ROAD to Housing Act became law unsigned [SRC-0842]. Before the election: "I veto everything, and then the Democrats come and they want to make a deal" [SRC-0843]; after it: "we can do a lot of things together" [SRC-0845]. The input docs' "popularity-seeker with a veto pen" fits the record better than "Mr. Veto," but his no's have been about leverage over unrelated demands, which is a different risk: a hostage, not a policy veto.
- **Democrats have two reconciliation bills in 2027 without touching FY2029.** CRS confirms a new Congress can adopt a budget resolution after its fiscal year has begun [SRC-0875]. A FY2027 resolution early in 2027 (instructions lapse September 30, 2027) and a FY2028 resolution later (usable into September 2028) give two 51-vote vehicles. That answers framework question 6: the FY2029 slot that D5 reserves for R1 never needs to be used in 2028.
- **The binding constraint on bills Trump might sign is the Senate filibuster, and the votes to end it are fragile, not absent.** Talarico, El-Sayed, Stratton, Flanagan and Jackson want abolition; Turek and Andrews prefer a talking filibuster; Fetterman, Hamilton, King and probably Cooper oppose ending it [SRC-0868] [SRC-0869]. With Vance breaking ties, a rules change needs 51 Democrats: those four no's leave exactly 51 at 55 seats and 52 at 56, so one more opponent (at 55) or two (at 56) ends it, and most returning senators' positions were not found (section 6). Abolition is possible on paper; a talking filibuster or narrow carve-outs is the likelier outcome, which fits D2's sequence.
- **Election protection is mostly a 2027 job, and mostly outside Congress.** The Supreme Court decides *RNC v. Mi Familia Vota* (Arizona proof of citizenship and voter-roll purges) by about June 2027 [SRC-0867]; a Kavanaugh concurrence suggests the Postal Service ballot-mail rule could return for 2028 with enough lead time [SRC-0895]; the Justice Department has lost 27 of its voter-roll suits and won none [SRC-0897]. Federal election-security grants fell to $15 million in FY2025 and $45 million in FY2026; the House FY2027 bill has $15 million [SRC-0899] [SRC-0900]. On January 6, 2029 Vice President Vance presides over the count, likely as a candidate in it (section 4).
- **There is probably one legislative year, not two.** Health analysts expect little to pass in the 2028 campaign year; ACA relief passed early in 2027 can be backdated to January [SRC-0972] [SRC-0970]. Each deal will likely carry a Trump priority as its price (section 2e).
- **Governors' biggest 2027 test is Medicaid work requirements on January 1, 2027.** CMS's rule lets a state seek a good-faith exemption through December 31, 2028, but CMS expects to approve about 2 of 10 requests [SRC-0888] [SRC-0889]. Nebraska, which started early, denied about 560 people, 84% for verification paperwork [SRC-0889]. Delivery here is measurable by 2028: coverage kept or lost.

**The FY2029 slot (framework question 6, answered with a caution).** CRS: two resolutions in one calendar year are established (FY2017 and FY2018 in 2017; FY2021 and FY2022 in 2021), and a resolution can be adopted after its fiscal year begins, "even into the next calendar year in which a new Congress has begun"; reconciliation instructions cannot be acted on after their fiscal year ends [SRC-0875]. In 2021 the parliamentarian advised that a section 304 revision can carry new instructions, "perhaps serially," a route never used and contested by a conservative procedural scholar [SRC-0876] [SRC-0872]. **Reading (analysis):** a 2027-28 Congress that adopts FY2027 and FY2028 resolutions has two vehicles, and leaving FY2029 untouched keeps R1 clean for January-February 2029. If Democrats did use FY2029 for veto bait in 2028, a 2029 Congress would have to rely on the untested section 304 route. The option that protects D5 is to not adopt a FY2029 resolution in 2028.

**Housing: funding the ROAD to Housing Act in FY2027-28** (housing board row 6a; the full map is `dossiers/home/road-funding.md`). Options, for Espen; amounts are this project's judgment.
- **Where it stands.** The ROAD to Housing Act (P.L. 119-101) appropriates nothing [SRC-0438]. The FY2027 stopgap runs to December 11, 2026 at FY2026 rates and bars new starts, so no ROAD program can begin until a full-year bill names it [SRC-2200]. The House committee's THUD bill, written before ROAD was law, has no ROAD line, zeroes the $50 million PRO Housing grants and cuts HOME from $1.25 billion to $500 million [SRC-2201] [SRC-1901]. The Senate has no THUD or Agriculture bill [SRC-1901] [SRC-2116]; the House-passed Agriculture bill raises rural rental assistance by $80 million [SRC-2202].
- **The asks, in the FY2027 package (early 2027) and FY2028's.** About $0.5 billion in FY2027 and $0.6 billion in FY2028: the Innovation Fund at its authorized $200 million a year (first priority); the whole-home repair pilot ($100-150 million); PRICE for manufactured-home communities ($100 million); planning grants in PRO Housing's place ($50 million); RESIDE conversions ($50-100 million); pattern books and single-stair grants ($10-20 million). The larger fight is defensive: HOME, CDBG and public housing capital grants at FY2026 levels.
- **Why they are winnable.** Each is a level Congress authorized, or a pilot it set up, in a law that passed 85-5 and 358-32 [SRC-0437]. They are small, voluntary for localities and run through existing grantees, and the rural and manufactured-home items reach Republican states. Odds (analysis): the ROAD lines in a signed FY2027 or FY2028 bill, **50-65%** if the full-year bills pass at all (section 2a's 65-80% and 55-70%), since appropriators of both parties fund authorized housing programs at modest levels; HOME held near $1.25 billion, **55-70%**, as the Senate writes its bills on a bipartisan topline and the House cut has no Senate counterpart.
- **Write it to survive impoundment.** Money available until spent, dates for HUD's Innovation Fund method and notices, and quarterly obligation reports, so anything HUD sits on is still there in 2029 (section 4).
- **One authority to extend now, at no cost:** CDBG-DR's disaster recovery program ends July 11, 2029 [SRC-0438]. A general provision in FY2028 appropriations or a disaster supplemental can extend it; reconciliation cannot, since it has no budget effect.
- **2029 effect.** The plan already funds the three largest ROAD channels from FY2030 (the Innovation Fund, the repair fund that replaces the pilot, and PRICE). Written as total program levels, a 2027-28 win lowers the plan's cost and is not counted twice (`dossiers/home/6-cost.md`, 2.10).

### 2e. The timing and the price of a deal

- **One legislative year.** KFF's Larry Levitt (nonpartisan): Democrats are "probably looking at what Democrats can do in the next one year, not two," since little passes in a presidential year [SRC-0972]. ACA relief passed early in 2027 can be **backdated** to January and paired with reopened enrollment, since open enrollment closes January 15 [SRC-0970] [SRC-0971]. That argues for putting the signable health, drug-price and extender items first, and the veto-bait reconciliation bill next.
- **The price.** Trump's own words on the ACA credits in 2025: an extension "may be necessary to get something else done" [SRC-0988]. Expect each deal to carry a Trump priority: health savings accounts with ACA credits (Levitt's example) [SRC-0972], codified most-favored-nation pricing on his design [SRC-0974], perhaps SAVE-style election provisions (the 2026 stock-ban bill carried photo ID [SRC-0852]). The option for Espen is which prices are acceptable; an election-rules price conflicts with DEM-1.
- **Where deals are likeliest** (health experts across outlets): drug prices, PBMs, patents, prior authorization and price transparency [SRC-0970] [SRC-0971] [SRC-0974]. Several of those also generate savings that a 2029 coverage expansion could have used (Axios's sources) [SRC-0974]: a lock-in cost (`compounding-2029-2050.md`, section 2).

## 5. Working with Trump and through Vance

**What the record says (data).** Two vetoes, both retaliatory-looking and both sustained [SRC-0840]; a signing boycott tied to SAVE [SRC-0841]; a major bipartisan law left unsigned [SRC-0842]; a promise to "veto everything" until Democrats deal [SRC-0843]; then "a lot of things together" and an offer to stop attacking Democrats "for six months" for a deal [SRC-0845].

**Two readings (analysis).** (1) *Leverage-seeker:* the veto is a bargaining chip; he signs bills he can own, especially ones that keep his own promises (card cap, stock ban, drug prices). The input docs take this view. (2) *Hostage-taker:* he ties signatures to unrelated demands (SAVE in 2026; perhaps immigration or the war in 2027), so even bills he likes stall. Pillsbury (a law and lobbying firm) expects "narrower agreements where the parties' interests overlap," on must-pass bills [SRC-0848]; Senator Schatz argues against proposing bills "guaranteed to veto," while Representative Khanna wants to "make him veto Medicare for All" [SRC-0843]. **The test comes early:** whether the first standalone signable bill (the stock ban or a health extender) is signed clean or held for SAVE.

**Through Vance (analysis).** Vance has now said in public that the war and costs lost the election (`2028-outlook.md`, section 1). That makes him a plausible partner on a ceasefire, a child tax credit and cost bills, and a rival for credit on all of them. Deliver's rule fits: "take only deals whose policy outlasts the credit he'd get."

The four named opponents (Fetterman, Hamilton, King, probably Cooper) leave abolition alive only if **every other** Democrat votes yes, including Peltola and Colom, who have no public position, and the returning senators whose views this review did not find. So abolition in 2027 is possible but needs near-unanimity, and one or two more opponents end it (analysis; positions can change). A talking filibuster or appropriations and debt-limit carve-outs has more room: it could reach 51 even with several abolition opponents, if Fetterman, Hamilton or King accept a version short of abolition (none has said so; gap).

**Leadership.** Six or more senators-elect say they will not back Schumer; Van Hollen is the name most often raised but has not declared; the secret-ballot vote is expected in early December [SRC-0873] [SRC-0874]. A long fight would cost the first months, the input docs' "factional breakdown" risk.

**The Merkley-Daines package** keeps 60 for final passage and speeds everything else (no cloture on motions to proceed, 12 hours post-cloture, a two-thirds suspension calendar, fast tracks for bipartisan and appropriations bills); it needs 67 votes [SRC-0529] [SRC-0530]. A conservative procedural scholar warns it shifts power to committee leaders [SRC-0872]. No post-election movement was found.

**Options (D2, not decisions).** (a) Hold everything for the first week of 2029, as D2 decided; (b) force cloture votes in 2027 on bills Trump would sign, to build the record that the rule is the obstacle, and hold Rules Committee hearings on D2's paths; (c) adopt C or carve-outs in 2027 only on a clean trigger (Republicans filibustering a bill Trump endorsed), if 51 votes exist; (d) back Merkley-Daines as a floor. Option (b) has no downside for D2; (c) spends D2's opening offer early (the input doc's own caution).
