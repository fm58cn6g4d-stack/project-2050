# How the 2026 result compounds: the 2029 plan and the long run

Role: how the 2026 result and the plausible 2027-28 paths change the 2029 plan and the project's long-run goals: which flagships could be pre-built as House-passed bills; what the 2029 starting point looks like if 2027-28 laws pass; the order of stages in `charter/PLAN.md`; and the long run to 2050 (courts, the 2030 census and redistricting, durable majorities). Status: draft (stage B, step 3), as of November 8, 2026. **Options and questions for Espen; nothing here is decided.** Under `DEC-2026-10-29-01` the plan begins in January 2029 and 2027-28 is a labeled exception. No 2029 design is final (`DEC-2026-10-30-02`, `DEC-2026-10-30-03`); housing memos 1B, 2B (with a stage for disabled renters) and HOU-7B are treated as working directions, not recorded decisions. PR #80's D1 update awaits Espen.

## In brief

- **The 2026 governors will not draw the 2031 maps.** Governors elected to four-year terms in November 2026 serve until January 2031; the maps after the 2030 census are drawn in 2031 under governors and legislatures elected in **November 2030**, a midterm that historically punishes the President's party. D1 already expects the House "possibly lost or narrowed in 2030"; the same year decides the decade's maps (analysis). That makes DEM-2's commissions and mid-decade ban, in force before 2031, worth more than the 2026 state gains.
- **The 2030 census moves the target to the Sun Belt.** On four projections Texas gains 4 seats, Florida 2-4, and California loses 3-4; on the 2024 map the Harris states plus the Blue Wall would fall from 270 electoral votes to 258 [SRC-0825] [SRC-0826] [SRC-0827]. The census is taken in April 2030 under the 2029 administration, but much of its design and funding is set in FY2027-29 appropriations, which the 2027-28 Congress writes.
- **Two of the most popular 2027-28 deals would move the 2029 baseline most.** A time-limited ACA extension puts a cliff into the 2029 baseline that R1 must meet; a Social Security deal on the Warren-Moreno "donut" would settle D8's revenue design before its dossier decides it. Defense parity is the quiet one: holding the Ledger's FY2028 target ($905 billion) flat in real terms under NSC-1A adds about **$0.3 trillion** of defense over 2030-39 against a flat-real path from FY2026 (this project's arithmetic, section 2).
- **Only restorations are ready to pre-build as full text.** HLT-1's ACA and Medicaid restoration and CLI-1's clean-energy credits restore prior law, so a House bill fixes little that a dossier would decide. Almost everything else is L1 (`DEC-2026-10-30-03`); a House bill would fix a design first.
- **2028 falls on the critical path of three stages that come later in the plan:** fair financing (OBBBA's tips, overtime and senior deductions expire after tax year 2028), national security (the FY2028 defense level, decided by October 2027) and democratic renewal (2027 state election laws). Section 3 gives the options.

## 4. The long run: courts, the census, redistricting and durable majorities

### Courts

- **The Senate holds every confirmation in 2027-28.** Any Supreme Court vacancy in those two years would face a Democratic Senate; whether to confirm, negotiate or hold it open to 2029 is a choice the caucus would face in real time, with the 2016 Garland precedent cutting the other way (analysis). The two oldest justices will be about 80 and 78 in 2028 (public biographies; not re-read for this draft).
- **Lower courts.** A Democratic Senate can slow or stop Trump's judicial nominations for two years, leaving vacancies for a 2029 president (analysis). The Senate's 2027 rules choices (section 6 of the playbook) bear on that: the Merkley-Daines package would end cloture on nominations [SRC-0529].
- **The Court's 2027 and 2028 terms.** *Mi Familia Vota* (by about June 2027), the Postal Service mail rule's likely return, the Fed removal question, and any case granted after mid-January 2027, decided about June 2028, just before the election [SRC-0866] [SRC-0867].
- **What it means for 2050 (analysis).** The democratic-renewal foundation's court items (ethics, structure, jurisdiction) are long-run; the 2027-28 lever is only the Senate's power to say no.

The projections disagree mostly on immigration; in 2020 New York lost fewer seats and Texas gained fewer than projected [SRC-0825].

- **Electoral College from 2032 (analysis, DDHQ).** On the 2024 map, Trump's states would be worth 322 rather than 312; the Harris states plus Wisconsin, Michigan and Pennsylvania 258 rather than 270; the tipping point moves from Pennsylvania to Georgia [SRC-0827]. 2028 is the last presidential race on the 2020 apportionment.
- **Who runs the 2030 count.** The count is in April 2030 under the 2029 administration, and the President transmits apportionment by the end of 2030. In 2020 the outgoing administration tried to exclude undocumented immigrants from the apportionment base; the 2025-26 administration has signaled similar aims (memory; not re-read, verify before relying). The 2027-28 levers are appropriations for the Census Bureau's ramp-up and oversight of its design.

### Redistricting in 2031

- **Who draws (analysis).** Most governors elected in 2026 serve to January 2031; the 2031 maps are drawn under the governors and legislatures elected in November 2030. Of the six new Democratic trifectas, Minnesota, Pennsylvania and Wisconsin have had court-drawn maps that a trifecta could replace by statute [SRC-0953]; Michigan and Arizona use constitutional commissions and Nevada's legislature draws (memory; verify). Holding them through 2030 matters more for the decade's House than winning them in 2026 did.
- **The 2027-28 bridge.** Wisconsin's 2027 court race and Pennsylvania's 2027 retentions shape who reviews 2031 maps (`2027-races.md`, section 3); North Carolina's court is on the 2028 ballot.
- **DEM-2 and the project's position.** D1's stipulation makes the House trifecta of 2033 conditional on DEM-1, with DEM-2's commissions and mid-decade ban, being in force before the 2031 redistricting. The 2026 result does not change that; it raises the stakes, because the 2030 midterm decides the maps otherwise.

## 5. Questions for Espen

Numbered for reference. None is urgent before the lame duck ends; 3 and 4 have 2027 deadlines.

1. **Pre-building (section 1):** pass restorations (HLT-1, CLI-1) as full text and the rest as direction-level bills; or hold all text until each dossier decides; or full text wherever the legacy files have a design, accepting lock-in.
2. **ACA credits (section 2):** if a time-limited extension is on offer in 2027, prefer a sunset aligned with R1 (end of 2029), the longest available, or decline anything short of permanent?
3. **Defense (section 2; framework question 5):** the FY2028 level sets NSC-1A's base; the gap between paths is about $0.1-0.4 trillion over 2030-39. Decide before FY2028 appropriations (about summer 2027)?
4. **The 2028 tax expirations:** run the household test on extending OBBBA's tips, overtime and senior deductions in 2028, before the fight, rather than waiting for stage G?
5. **Social Security (section 2; framework question 7):** is a 2027-28 donut deal worth settling D8's design early, given the dossier's design is not done?
6. **Mid-decade redistricting:** DEM-2 would ban it from 2029, while Democrats pursue redraws in New York and Maryland in 2027. Should the project's position distinguish defensive redraws (answering Republican ones) from a permanent ban, or stay silent on 2027?
7. **Scenario D:** should the project write a branch for a Republican presidency in 2029 (states as the delivery arm, a Senate that confirms or refuses), or keep a single 2029 plan?
8. **Stage order (section 3):** add short notes in 2027-28 for defense, the 2028 tax fight and state election laws, or keep the order and accept that those decisions may happen without the project's input?
9. **The census:** should democratic renewal take up the 2030 census (funding, residence rule, apportionment base) in 2027, while the 2027-28 Congress can still act through appropriations?
