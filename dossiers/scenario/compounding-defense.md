# The defense lock-in: three FY2028 paths and NSC-1A's base

Role: the arithmetic for framework question 5 (`3-framework-2027-28.md`): what 2027-28 defense appropriations do to NSC-1A, which holds defense flat in real terms from the FY2029 base (`legacy/domains/domain-national-security.md`, NSC-1A; D9). Status: draft (stage B, step 3, round 2), November 8, 2026. **Options, not decisions.** The arithmetic is this project's (`tools/compounding-defense.py`); the dollar paths are the Lame-Duck Ledger's proposals [SRC-0545], not sourced estimates, except the FY2026 enacted level.

## In brief

- **The choice made in 2027-28 is carried for a decade.** Against a defense bill held flat in real terms from FY2026, the Ledger's parity path ($870 billion in FY2027, $905 billion in FY2028) puts about **$26 billion a year more** into the FY2029 base and **about $0.30 trillion more over 2030-39** under NSC-1A. A middle path (the FY2027 deal, then flat real) adds about $0.13 trillion; the Ledger's FY2027 stretch deal held flat adds about $0.42 trillion.
- **The project's own fiscal tests will not see it.** NSC-1A "costs nothing against CBO's baseline, which already grows defense with inflation" (Decision Brief 2). Whatever FY2028 level is enacted becomes the baseline, so the ledger shows zero in every path, and the D4 guardrails, measured against the same baseline, move with it. The lock-in shows up only in total federal spending, in debt, and in where the defense-reduction study (NSC-1 point 1a) has to start.
- **Parity is a trade, not a cost.** Under the 2013-19 parity rule each defense dollar buys an equal nondefense dollar. The real choice is how much defense to accept for how much nondefense in FY2027-28, knowing the defense side is held for ten years and the nondefense side pre-loads CAP-7S and CAP-7 (D9).

## 1. The paths

FY2026 enacted Defense appropriations bill: $838.7 billion base discretionary budget authority (the Ledger's sourced figure). Deflators: 2.4% for FY2027 and 2.3% for FY2028 (the Ledger's), then 2.1% a year (this project's assumption). Billions of dollars, nominal.

| Path | FY2027 | FY2028 | Real growth, FY2026-28 | FY2029 base (NSC-1A) | 2030-39 total | Above flat real, 2030-39 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Flat real from FY2026 | 858.8 | 878.6 | 0% | 897.0 | 10,074 | — |
| 2. Middle: the Ledger's FY2027 deal, then flat real | 870.0 | 890.0 | +1.3% | 908.7 | 10,206 | **+131** |
| 3. Ledger parity: the FY2027 deal, then its FY2028 target | 870.0 | 905.0 | +3.0% | 924.0 | 10,377 | **+303** |
| Upper case: the Ledger's FY2027 stretch deal, then flat real | 895.0 | 915.6 | +4.2% | 934.8 | 10,499 | +424 |

**Sensitivity.** With a 1.8% or 2.5% deflator from FY2029 the gaps move by under 3% (path 3: $297-311 billion). The result depends on the FY2028 level, not on the later inflation assumption.

**What it is measured on.** The Defense bill is a proxy for the national defense function (050), which also holds DOE nuclear programs and other agencies; the gaps scale roughly in proportion. NSC-1A's base excludes one-time reconciliation money, so the Republicans' proposed $350 billion of mandatory defense money [SRC-0524] and the $87.6 billion war supplemental request [SRC-0525] would not enter it if enacted as one-time money. Defense the 2027-28 Congress moves into the base bill instead would.

## 2. Why the plan's own numbers will not show it

Decision Brief 2 recommended NSC-1A because it "costs nothing against CBO's baseline, which already grows defense with inflation" (`legacy/governing/decision-brief-02-trims-scope-and-fiscal-rule.md`). CBO projects discretionary spending from the latest enacted level, so:
- The ledger line for NSC-1 is $0 in all four paths.
- D4's guardrails (debt below CBO's current-law path; net interest below the baseline's share of GDP) are measured against a baseline that already includes the higher level.
- The difference appears only in absolute outlays and debt, and in the starting point for NSC-1 point 1a, Espen's request to study ways to bring defense down over time.

So the 2027-28 decision is invisible to the plan's tests and fully visible to its outcomes (analysis).

## 3. What the parity trade buys

Under dollar parity, nondefense gains what defense gains. In the Ledger's own FY2028 target, nondefense gains more than defense ($89 billion against $66 billion over FY2026, about 6% real against 3%) [SRC-0545].

| Path (defense) | Defense gain over FY2026 by FY2028 | Nondefense gain under strict dollar parity | Nondefense real growth on a $783 billion FY2026 base (analysis) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Flat real | +$40 billion | +$40 billion | about +0.3% |
| 2. Middle | +$51 billion | +$51 billion | about +1.7% |
| 3. Ledger parity | +$66 billion | +$66 billion (the Ledger asks +$89 billion) | about +3.5% (+6.3% at +$89 billion) |

Nondefense FY2026 base: the Ledger's $1,622 billion total less $838.7 billion of defense. **Reading (analysis):** holding defense flat in real terms under strict parity also holds nondefense roughly flat, which forgoes the FY2027-28 rebuild the Ledger wants. Restoring nondefense without raising defense needs a break from parity, which Republicans have resisted since 2013. The Ledger's target already breaks parity in Democrats' favor in FY2028. If Republicans refuse any deal and FY2027-28 run on full-year stopgaps, as FY2025 did, defense lands near or below path 1 and nondefense freezes: NSC-1A's base is lowest, but the forgone nondefense increases add to CAP-7S's 2029 surge (stress test 9).

**Scale of the lock-in.** Path 3's $0.30 trillion over 2030-39 is about half of CAP-7S's agency rebuild (about $0.6 trillion, D9) and about twice HOU-4D's $135 billion rental envelope (`dossiers/home/memos/2-how-broad.md`). These are comparisons, not trade-offs the plan has made.

## 4. Options for Espen (framework question 5)

| Option | FY2028 defense | 2030-39 effect | What it means for nondefense in 2027-28 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. Hold defense flat in real terms | About $879 billion | Baseline | Under parity, nondefense near flat; a real rebuild needs a parity break |
| B. Middle path | About $890 billion | +$0.13 trillion | Some nondefense growth under parity |
| C. Ledger parity path | About $905 billion | +$0.30 trillion | The Ledger's rehabilitation budget becomes reachable |
| D. Accept parity in FY2027 only, hold defense flat in FY2028 | About $890 billion (path 2) | +$0.13 trillion | Front-loads the nondefense rebuild into FY2027 |

D is arithmetically the same as B for NSC-1A. Each option assumes the 2027-28 deals look like the Ledger's proposals; the administration has sought far more (about $1.5 trillion in its defense proposal, against appropriators' roughly $1.1 trillion, as reported by a law firm) [SRC-0848], so a parity deal may itself be the Democratic floor.
