# 2027-28: a framework of questions

Role: a way to think about the two years of divided government before the 2029 plan begins: who holds which levers, which vehicles exist, what success could mean, how the two years feed the 2029 plan, what could change the picture, and the questions only Espen can answer. Status: draft (stage B, step 1), November 8, 2026. **Questions and options, not decisions.** Under `DEC-2026-10-29-01` this is one of the plan's two labeled exceptions; the main work stays a plan ready to carry out from January 2029. No housing memo or 2029 design choice is assumed decided.

Facts are from `1-results.md` unless cited here. Section 6 checks the two input docs, "Deliver" [SRC-0544] and "The Lame-Duck Ledger" [SRC-0545], and the lead's reading of them.

## 1. Who holds which levers

**The arithmetic.** From January 3, 2027: a Democratic House of about 256-263 and a Democratic Senate of 55 or 56 (54 called; 56 more likely, since Alaska leans Peltola), with President Trump and Vice President Vance. Overriding a veto takes 290 House and 67 Senate votes, so contested overrides are out. Ending a filibuster takes 60, so 4 or 5 Republicans per bill. In 2027-28 the Vice President breaks Senate ties for Republicans, so a rules change by majority precedent would need 51 Democratic votes.

| Lever | Held by | What it does in 2027-28 | Can Trump block it? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Floor agenda, committees, hearings | Democratic House and Senate | Decide what gets a vote; House-passed bills become a record and pre-drafted text | No |
| Oversight and subpoenas | Committee chairs (expected: Garcia, Oversight; Raskin, Judiciary; [SRC-0523]; DeLauro, Appropriations [SRC-0524]; all projections until steering committees act) | Investigations, documents, testimony | Partly: the executive can refuse and litigate, and the Justice Department will not prosecute contempt of Congress against its own officials |
| Senate confirmation | Democratic Senate | Every nominee, judges included, needs Democratic votes; departures are already reported (Dhillon at Civil Rights; Rubio reportedly weighing leaving State) [SRC-0522] | No, though acting officials and the post-*Slaughter* removal power let him run agencies without confirmed heads [SRC-0547] |
| Budget resolution | Congress (a concurrent resolution, not signed by the President) | Sets toplines and allows a 51-vote reconciliation bill | The resolution, no; the reconciliation bill, yes, by veto |
| Appropriations | Congress writes; President signs | Funding levels, riders, guardrails; the stopgap runs out December 11, then FY2028 on October 1, 2027 | Yes by veto, at the price of a shutdown he would share |
| War powers | Congress | The House passed war powers measures in June and July 2026; the June one was a concurrent resolution never sent to the President, and the July joint resolution failed in the Senate 47-49 [SRC-0534] | Yes by veto; a concurrent resolution has no legal force |
| Impeachment | House impeaches; Senate convicts by 67 | Massie (R) plans articles against Defense Secretary Hegseth [SRC-0524] | No, but conviction is out of reach |
| The veto | President | "I have the ultimate power — it's called 'veto'" [SRC-0520] | — |
| Executive orders, rules, enforcement | President | Most of the 2025-26 agenda ran this way | — |
| Spending already enacted | Locked in | ICE and Border Patrol are funded through FY2029 by the Secure America Act (signed June 10, 2026, about $70 billion) [SRC-0537] | Congress cannot defund them in the annual bills; it can restrict uses and claw back unspent balances |
| Impoundment and pocket rescissions | President, contested | GAO found the $810 million pocket rescission illegal on September 29, 2026 [SRC-0536]; courts have narrowed private suits | Congress's remedies are guardrail language, GAO and litigation, not certainty |
| Tariffs | President, narrowed | IEEPA tariffs were struck down February 20, 2026; the administration moved to Sections 122, 232 and 301 [SRC-0535] | Congress can pass limits; he can veto them |
| States | 30 Democratic governors (if Kansas and Alaska hold); about 22 Democratic trifectas | Medicaid work requirements start January 1, 2027, and SNAP cost shifts begin in FY2027 and FY2028, so states choose how to carry them out; state versions of blocked federal policy | No, within federal law; federal cost shifts constrain state budgets |
| The courts | | *Slaughter* (June 29, 2026) ended for-cause protection at the FTC and put the NLRB, FERC and similar bodies in doubt [SRC-0547]; *Callais* (April 29) narrowed Section 2 of the Voting Rights Act [SRC-0546] | — |

**What Trump cannot block:** the agenda, hearings, investigations, the record, a budget resolution, the Senate's refusal to confirm, a House impeachment, and states acting within their powers. **What he can block:** any law, any reconciliation bill, and any appropriation, at a price. **What 41 Senate Republicans can block, even where Trump might sign:** anything outside reconciliation and appropriations deals.

## 3. What could count as success

These are options. Each implies different work and a different measure. They are not exclusive, but they compete for floor time and political capital.

| Option | What it means | How to measure it | Tension with the others |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. Laws that lower costs now | Signed, durable laws on health, energy, housing and grocery costs | Number signed; households and dollars reached by 2028 | Signable bills are smaller and may lock in weaker designs |
| B. A clear record | Popular bills passed and vetoed or filibustered, so the contrast is plain in 2028 | Votes recorded; vetoes and Senate Republican blocks on named bills | Can read as "nothing passed"; uses floor time |
| C. A plan ready for 2029 | Bill text, scores and delivery plans for the first 2029 vehicles, tested in hearings and House votes | Share of R1 and DEM-1 provisions with text and a CBO score by November 2028 | House bills written before dossier designs are decided can pre-commit designs |
| D. A protected 2028 election | Election administration funding, oversight of federal interference, state preparedness | Funding enacted; litigation readiness; no disrupted certification | Needs Republican votes or riders |
| E. A government left to rebuild | Documented agency damage and appropriations that slow it | Staffing and capacity data by agency, used to size CAP-7S | Oversight capacity is shared with A and B |
| F. States as proof | Democratic trifectas pass state versions of blocked federal policy | Programs running in 2028 | State budgets absorb federal cost shifts at the same time |

The input docs propose a concrete bar for the end of 2027: at least 10 bills signed, two of them visibly lowering health costs; FY2027 and FY2028 enacted with real increases and anti-impoundment guardrails; ACA credits restored in some form; the Iran war ended or bounded by law [SRC-0544]. That bar weights A heavily. Whether the project adopts any bar, and which, is question 1.

## 4. How 2027-28 feeds the 2029 plan

- **Pre-built text.** Bills the House passes in 2027-28 can become 2029 text, which helps the R1 and DEM-1 timetable in D5 and D1 (`legacy/governing/project-2050-governing-decisions.md`). The catch: most flagship designs are not decided (`DEC-2026-10-30-02`, `DEC-2026-10-30-03`); a House bill drafted now fixes a design before its dossier has weighed the options.
- **The 2029 baseline moves.** The plan states current law as it will stand in 2029 (`DEC-2026-10-29-01`). Whatever is enacted in 2027-28 enters that baseline: appropriations levels, any ACA credit restoration, any delay of Medicaid work requirements, the 2028 tax expirations. A provision enacted early leaves the 2029 ledger and enters the baseline.
- **The FY2029 reconciliation slot is safer than the legacy plan feared, and still exposed.** D5 warned that Republicans might use the FY2029 budget resolution first. With Democrats running Congress in 2028, they cannot. But Democrats themselves could use a FY2029 resolution in 2028 for a veto-bait reconciliation bill. Whether a 2029 Congress could then revise it and still reconcile is a procedural question this review has not answered; it needs a CRS or parliamentarian source before anyone relies on either reading (question 6).
- **Rules carry over.** The Senate is a continuing body, so a precedent or rule adopted in 2027 is in force on January 3, 2029 (D2).
- **Lock-in.** An early deal can settle a weaker design: a $15 minimum wage against LAB-4A's $17 and indexation, a partial ACA restoration against HLT-1, a Social Security deal against D8, a defense level above NSC-1A's base. Some are floors the plan can build on; some foreclose options. Which is which is question 2.
- **Evidence.** Oversight produces facts the dossiers need: what was dismantled at each agency (CAP-7S's bottom-up rebuild), how Medicaid work requirements play out in practice from January 2027, how the ROAD Act's programs start.

| Odds, whip counts, FY2027-28 dollar targets, the Social Security estimates of 20-25% | **The author's judgment**, labeled as such in the docs; not evidence |

**The lead's reading, tested.**

- *Lead with costs:* **supported, with a qualification.** Costs are the broadest concern (89% crucial or major) and the reading with the widest support across the spectrum (`2-analysis.md`, reading 2). But corruption was crucial for more voters (55% against 49%), and health care, Democrats' largest lead, was the top issue for only 8%. "Costs and corruption, tied together" fits the data better than costs alone.
- *House-passed bills pre-build 2029:* **supported, with a design caution.** They give text, scores and a record; they also fix designs before dossiers decide them (section 4). One answer is to pass bills at the level of direction where a dossier has not decided, and in full where one has.
- *Governors as the delivery arm:* **supported by the map, constrained by money.** Six new Democratic trifectas and 30 governors, but SNAP administrative cost shifts begin in FY2027 and benefit cost shares in FY2028, and Medicaid work requirements start January 1, 2027 (OBBBA, as summarized by the landscape research; recheck against statute).
- *The institutional-buyer bill duplicates current law:* **confirmed.** ROAD Act section 1001 bars large investors (350 or more single-family homes) from buying more, from January 7, 2027 until its sunset about 2042, with built-in reporting (`dossiers/home/road-act.md`). Per the lead's brief, Espen chose to keep that limit as enacted (HOU-7B); that choice is not yet recorded in the charter or the agenda, so this review treats it as the working direction, not a recorded decision. A 2027 bill on the same ground would add nothing unless it changes the limit.
- *Dollar-for-dollar defense parity raises the FY2029 base:* **confirmed by the docs' own numbers.** The Ledger's likely FY2027 deal puts the defense bill at $870 billion against $838.7 billion in FY2026, about 1.3% real growth, and its FY2028 target at $905 billion, a further 4% nominal [SRC-0545]. NSC-1A holds defense flat in real terms *from the FY2029 base*, so every dollar added in 2027-28 is carried into the 2029-39 plan. The docs' figures are proposals, not sourced estimates.
- *"Trump is a popularity-seeker with a veto pen":* **one analyst's judgment.** Evidence runs both ways: he says he is willing to work with Democrats [SRC-0521], reportedly "unless they want to be destructive" (CBS, search summary only), and that vetoes lead to deals [SRC-0520]; he also let the ROAD Act become law unsigned rather than claim it, sought to overturn a count in Alaska, and calls himself "Mr. Veto." Treat it as a hypothesis to test on the first bills, not a premise.
- *An early deal can lock in weaker designs:* **agreed**, and it is the central design question for the two years (question 2).

## 7. Open questions for Espen

Numbered for reference; the first five matter most.

1. **What are 2027-28 for?** Weigh the success options in section 3: laws that lower costs now (A), a clear record (B), a plan ready for 2029 (C), a protected 2028 election (D), a government left to rebuild (E), states as proof (F). The project's time frame already says C and preparation come first; the question is how much else it takes on.
2. **When is an early deal worth its lock-in?** Options: (a) take any durable gain; (b) take a deal only where it is a floor the 2029 design can build on (a minimum wage that 2029 can raise, a credit 2029 can widen); (c) take a deal only where the dossier has decided the design. Examples now in play: a $15 minimum wage, a partial ACA restoration, a Social Security deal, defense levels.
3. **Should the project rebaseline D1 now or after 2028?** The 2026 result (55-56 seats) exceeds D1's 52-54 working band, but the 2029 Senate is set by the 2028 elections, not 2026. Options in `4-2029-plan.md`.
4. **Should any Senate rules change happen in 2027, or wait for the first week of 2029 (D2)?** With Vance in the chair, a 2027 change needs 51 Democrats; it would carry into 2029. Options: hold everything for 2029; adopt narrow carve-outs (appropriations, debt limit) on a clean trigger; back the bipartisan Merkley-Daines package (67 votes).
5. **What should 2027-28 appropriations do to the FY2029 defense base?** Options: hold defense near flat in real terms through FY2028; accept dollar parity with nondefense; or accept parity in FY2027 only. Each sets the base NSC-1A then holds flat.
6. **Should a 2027-28 Democratic reconciliation bill be used at all, and with which fiscal-year resolution?** Needs a procedural check first (section 4): could using a FY2029 resolution in 2028 cost the 2029 trifecta its R1 slot?
7. **Social Security in 2027-28?** D8 plans a solvency-first bill in spring 2030; SS-10 (a bipartisan path on the Warren-Moreno framework) is an option, not chosen. Pursue SS-10 now, hold it as a 2030 fallback, or support a vote without seeking enactment?
8. **Housing in 2027-28.** Should anything move before the housing memos are decided? Appropriations for vouchers and HOME are compatible with every memo option; a new investor bill duplicates ROAD section 1001.
9. **How much project work goes into 2027-28?** Options: this folder only; a short "2027-28 window" note in each dossier's delivery section; or a separate 2027-28 agenda file. The plan's credit limits argue for the lightest.
10. **Where does scenario work live?** It is in `dossiers/scenario/` because "Where things live" has no row for it. A row in `charter/STANDARDS.md` would be a tier 3 change.
11. **Which readings of the result should the public case rest on?** `2-analysis.md` shows readings 1 and 2 with the broadest support; the moderate-versus-left and turnout readings wait for validated data in 2027.
12. **When to recheck.** After certification (mid-December) and after the validated studies (spring and summer 2027), or on a fixed schedule?
