# The 2027 off-cycle races

Role: every election on the 2027 calendar that bears on 2028 or on the 2029 plan: three governor races, eight legislative chambers, two state supreme courts, a U.S. Senate special election, the largest mayoral races, ballot measures, and the redistricting decisions that run through 2027. For each: the stakes, a baseline lean with sources, and what a Democratic win or loss would signal. Status: draft (stage B, step 3), as of November 8, 2026. Every source was read on November 8, 2026. **No rater has published 2027 ratings yet**; every "lean" here is this project's reading of the baselines and is marked as analysis. Under `DEC-2026-10-29-01` this is part of the 2027-28 exception.

## In brief

- **The calendar is thin but not empty.** Kentucky, Louisiana and Mississippi elect governors; Virginia, New Jersey, Louisiana and Mississippi elect both legislative chambers; Wisconsin (April 6) and Pennsylvania (November 2) hold supreme court elections; Minnesota holds a **U.S. Senate special election** in November 2027 [SRC-0920] [SRC-0927] [SRC-0950].
- **Three corrections to the brief.** Wisconsin's Bradley seat was decided in April 2026 (Taylor won 60-40, making the court 5-2 liberal); the April 2027 race is for retiring conservative Annette Ziegler's seat, and no conservative had entered by July [SRC-0939] [SRC-0941]. Pennsylvania has two retentions (Todd, D; Mundy, R) **and** an open seat from Donohue's age-out [SRC-0942] [SRC-0943]. Louisiana's 2027 state races keep the old all-candidate primary (October 9, runoff November 20); the new closed primary covers only federal and some state offices [SRC-0922] [SRC-0931].
- **The Senate special counts for 2029.** Governor-elect Klobuchar will appoint her own successor; the special-election winner serves to January 2031, so that seat sits in the Senate a 2029 Democratic president works with [SRC-0950] [SRC-0951]. It is safe on the baseline (Flanagan won Minnesota's other seat by about 10 points), but `5-senate-2028.md` does not yet count it.
- **The 2027 races that move the 2028 House are about maps, not candidates.** New York's redistricting amendment needs second passage and a November 2027 referendum [SRC-0956]; Maryland's Question 3 passed [SRC-0957]; Mississippi's governor wants congressional, legislative and judicial lines redrawn before the 2027 elections, aimed at Bennie Thompson's MS-2 [SRC-0944] [SRC-0960]; Virginia's restart depends on who holds the 21-19 state Senate after November 2027 [SRC-0928] [SRC-0937].
- **Governors are the national test.** Kentucky is an open Democratic seat in a Trump +30 state; Mississippi and Louisiana are Republican seats where Democrats ran within 2 points for Senate in 2026 (`1-results.md`). A Democratic win in either Deep South state in a year with no federal wave would be the strongest early signal for scenario A in `2028-outlook.md`; holding Kentucky would be the ordinary one.

**What each result would signal (analysis).**
- *Kentucky held:* the Beshear model (a moderate governor in a red state) survives him; little national meaning. *Kentucky lost to Comer*, who chaired House Oversight: a normal partisan reversion, and a Republican story that "the wave is over."
- *Mississippi or Louisiana won by a Democrat:* the 2026 Deep South shift (Black turnout near 96% of 2024 levels by one estimate, `2-analysis.md`) held without a federal race. That would be the strongest sign for scenario A (`2028-outlook.md`). In Louisiana it would put Kennedy's 2028 Senate seat, safe Republican in `5-senate-2028.md`, back in question; in Mississippi it would put a Democratic veto in front of any redraw from 2028 through the 2031 maps.
- *Both lost narrowly:* consistent with scenario B. *Both lost badly:* the 2026 shift was federal and anti-Trump, not a realignment.

**Virginia's map (the 2027 race with the clearest 2028-30 consequence).** Voters approved a redistricting amendment 52-48 on April 21, 2026; the state supreme court struck it down 4-3 on May 8 because first passage came in a special session after early voting in the 2025 House elections had begun, leaving no valid intervening election; the U.S. Supreme Court denied relief [SRC-0936] [SRC-0937]. The 2021 court map (6 D, 5 R) stays. A clean restart needs first passage in the 2027 session *before* the November 2027 House election, second passage in 2028, then a referendum (analysis from the timeline; Governor Spanberger calls 2028 talk "outrageously premature") [SRC-0938]. So a new map for 2028 is unlikely; for 2030 it depends on holding both chambers in November 2027.

## 4. The Minnesota Senate special election

Klobuchar is sworn in as governor January 4, 2027 and resigns her Senate seat; she, not Governor Walz, makes the appointment. State law requires a special election the November after the vacancy, so **November 2027**; the winner serves until January 2031. It is Minnesota's first odd-year Senate race since 1923. Names in the press for appointment: Rep. Angie Craig, Rep. Kelly Morrison, Secretary of State Steve Simon [SRC-0950] [SRC-0951]. If a House member is appointed, that House seat also goes to a special election (analysis).

**Lean (analysis): likely D.** Flanagan won Minnesota's other seat by about 10 points in 2026 (`1-results.md`). A loss would cost a seat in the 2029 Senate; a narrow win against a weak national climate would be an early warning for the House.

**Other Senate vacancies.** Blackburn (Tennessee) becomes governor and appoints her successor; the special's timing is in `5-senate-2028.md` (read there as November 2028) [SRC-0952]. No House vacancies were found for 2027 (check after January 3).

**Ballot measures.** As of October 27, only two statewide measures were certified for 2027: a Louisiana judicial amendment (April 17) and Oklahoma's SQ 848 constitutional-convention question [SRC-0948]. **Reading it (analysis).** On the published record, the 2027 decisions mostly favor Democrats on net: Missouri's map is gone, Texas and Georgia are closed to new Republican redraws, and New York and Maryland are open; AP calls it a possible "Democratic redistricting movement ahead of the presidential election" [SRC-0987]. Mississippi and Indiana are the main Republican options. The post-*Callais* Southern maps look durable: the first federal application of the new test, in Alabama, was reversed in the state's favor by the Supreme Court on June 2, 2026 [SRC-0986] (stress test 5). The scale is a handful of seats, against the roughly 10-seat Republican edge in the 2026 lines (`2028-outlook.md`, section 2). The project's own position is the open question: DEM-2 plans a ban on mid-decade redistricting for 2029, while the 2027 fights are mid-decade redraws by both parties (`compounding-2029-2050.md`, question 6).
