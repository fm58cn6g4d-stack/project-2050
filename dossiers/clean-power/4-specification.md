# Clean, affordable power and a prepared country: specification

Role: section 4 of the clean power dossier. It writes the decided designs as provisions: what each does, its parameters, who is covered, the vehicle, the agency and the evidence it rests on. Provision IDs and their fields are in `provisions.json`, built from this file's tables (`tools/build_provisions.py`). Status: reviewed (L2); L3 after the milestone review's fixes and the booking (`review-milestone.md`; this file carries the fixes). It rests on Espen's decisions `DEC-2026-11-13-06` (memos 1-7) and `DEC-2026-11-13-07` (points 1-14), and on four standing rules: `DEC-2026-11-09-13` (guide, don't police), `DEC-2026-11-13-08` (use existing machinery first, and simplify), `DEC-2026-11-10-05` (report, don't gate) and `DEC-2026-11-10-07` (what needs an answer now). Costs are in `6-cost.md`; delivery is in `5-delivery.md`.

## How to read this section

- **Status of each provision.**
  - *Decided:* Espen decided it in a memo option or a yes-or-no point in section 3, cited.
  - *Working choice:* a parameter this section sets so the draft is complete. It rests on judgment and can change without reopening a decision. Each one says so.
  - *As booked:* a legacy design the decision keeps as the budget model books it (memo 2 B, memo 6 A, point 14).
- **What this section commits to:** who pays the carbon price and at what price; who gets the dividend and how; which credits run and when they step down; the clean electricity standard's targets; the Clean Air Act's finding and the vehicle numbers; who builds the grid when no one else will; who gets the bill credit; the disaster and insurance package; and the transition guarantee's terms. Dates after enactment, schedules inside a decided target and every cost are working assumptions, with ranges in section 6.
- **Dates.** The plan begins in January 2029 (`DEC-2026-10-29-01`). Working timeline: the FY2030 reconciliation act (R2) by about October 2029 to January 2030; the FY2031 reconciliation act (R3) by about summer 2030; the clean power and climate act in regular order (FLOOR) in 2029-30. Things to attempt in 2027-28 and preparation before 2029 are labeled (`5-delivery.md`, part 3).
- **Evidence.** The last column lists the evidence IDs a provision rests on. "Project arithmetic" is `tools/clean_power_path.py`; "the carbon model read" is `tools/coverage_read.py`.
- **Agenda homes.** Provisions sit under the agenda items that already hold the legacy design (`agenda/agenda.csv`), plus three new rows this step adds for decided items with no legacy home: REV-C1G (memo 1 B's coverage), CLI-5H (memo 5 B's bill credit) and CLI-14 (points 1 and 8).

**Why this split.**
- *Reconciliation carries money* (the Byrd rule): the price, the dividend, credits, contracts, lending, grants, insurance and the guarantee's payments all change revenues or outlays. The legacy booking puts the credits, dividend and Package A's rows on R2 and the add-ons with the border adjustment on R3 (`models/data/budget/provisions.csv`), and this section follows it.
- *Rules without a budget effect go to regular order:* the standard, the Clean Air Act finding, the clean-heat standard, the grid statute, permitting and codes. Legacy files put them on the floor for the same reason (`legacy/domains/domain-climate.md`, CLI-3, CLI-6).
- *Money travels with its terms* (milestone review B-2). Who is covered and how a payment is made determine outlays, so they ride the reconciliation act that carries the money: the guarantee's coverage (CLI-11.01) and paid-first rule (CLI-11.08) with its payments in R3; the premium help (CLI-7.01) with the disaster capital in R2; the reporting duty the price's base needs with the price in R2. The reinsurance facility's codes condition is written into R2 as a condition on the facility's own coverage. If the Byrd rule strikes it, the facility waits for the clean power and climate act rather than starting without it (working choice), so point 13 holds whole.
- *The price starts with its payment.* The price and the dividend ride the same act, so the first dividend can go out before the price reaches bills (CLI-2A.03).

## Where particular groups fall

- **Households heating with gas or oil.** No direct charge (REV-C1G.02). The clean-heat standard reaches them only when they replace equipment, from 2032 (CLI-4H.01), with credits and Weatherization for low-income homes.
- **Adults with no tax filing and no federal benefit.** One sign-up at a front door (CLI-2A.02). Everyone else is paid automatically.
- **Children.** No dividend share; the child benefit covers them (FAM-1).
- **Renters whose heat is in the rent.** The bill credit is paid to them directly (CLI-5H.01).
- **Rural co-op customers.** The standard reaches them through their wholesale supplier (CLI-3A.01); grid money reaches co-ops through USDA's Rural Utilities Service as well as DOE (`5-delivery.md`, part 6).
- **The territories, Hawaii and Alaska.** The Acid Rain Program's units are only in the 48 contiguous states and the District of Columbia [EV-CLP-270], so outside them REV-C1G.01 charges fossil generating units that report under part 98 at or above its 25,000-ton threshold, as it charges large industrial facilities: Hawaii's, Alaska's and Puerto Rico's large oil- and gas-fired plants pay; small isolated diesel plants do not (working choice, following memo 1 B's "power plants"). The dividend follows the child benefit's territorial rule (FAM-1.07, `dossiers/secure-floor/4-specification.md`); the bill credit follows LIHEAP's territorial shares (working choice).
- **Households on coal- or oil-heavy grids.** Where power comes mostly from coal (West Virginia, Wyoming, Missouri) or oil (Hawaii, Puerto Rico), electricity rises more against the same dividend, so low-income households there may come out behind on the carbon channel even where the national average is ahead (`7-validation.md`, part 4). The bill credit (CLI-5H.01) and bill protection (CLI-5L.01) reach them first.
- **Tribal nations.** The bill credit matches FDPIR households and pays through tribes that run their own LIHEAP grants (CLI-5H.01); the dividend's front doors are the child benefit's (FAM-1.10); the mitigation fund has a floor for every tribe (CLI-7K.01). Tribal governments that host covered plants and mines lose lease, royalty and tribal tax revenue, not county property tax, so the declining replacement covers that revenue (CLI-11.06, `DEC-2026-11-13-11`) [EV-CLP-534]. When the Navajo Generating Station and Kayenta mine closed in 2019, the Navajo Nation's budget was projected to lose about 23% and the Hopi Tribe's about 85% (IEEFA, 2019).
- **Oil and gas field workers.** Not covered by the guarantee: their jobs fall without facility closures. They are served by the work flagship's programs (LAB-8) and the secure floor; what extending CLI-11 to them would cost is in `6-cost.md`, part 2.
