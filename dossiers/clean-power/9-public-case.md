# Clean, affordable power and a prepared country: the public case

Role: section 9 of the clean power dossier: a plain-language brief, messages, hard questions and a can-say list, built only from sections 1-8 and never ahead of them. Status: draft (step E-C2, PR 2), at L2, on `DEC-2026-11-13-06` and `DEC-2026-11-13-07`. Every number traces to its section and evidence ID in the source table at the end. Sources are named in plain text before each tag. Project estimates say "about" or "on our estimates"; each promise says when it starts.

## The brief

**Power that costs less, a country ready for the storms, and nobody left behind.** Electricity has gotten more expensive: the average home paid 13.15 cents a kilowatt-hour in 2020 and 17.23 cents in 2025 (Energy Information Administration, 2026) [EV-CLP-001]. More than one adult in five could not pay an energy bill in full in the past year (peer-reviewed study of Census survey data, 2022-24) [EV-CLP-031], while the federal heating and cooling help reaches about 17% of the households eligible for it (HHS, as reported by the National Low Income Housing Coalition, 2024) [EV-CLP-038]. Disasters costing $1 billion or more hit 23 times in 2025, at about $115 billion (Climate Central, 2026) [EV-CLP-130].

Project 2050's clean power plan does five things, starting in 2030.

**Polluters pay, and every adult gets a Climate Dividend,** from 2030. Power plants and large factories pay for the carbon they put in the air, starting at $25 a ton in 2030 and reaching $150 by 2037 [EV-CLP-700]. There is no charge on your gas bill or at the pump. Most of the money comes back to every adult as the Climate Dividend, paid four times a year, tax-free, starting before the price shows up in bills: on our estimates about $440 a person in 2035 (section 6). A border charge makes imported steel, cement and chemicals pay the same price, so American factories are not undercut.

**Clean power built faster,** from 2030. Tax credits for clean power, heat pumps and electric cars come back in 2030 and step down on a fixed schedule as new rules take over: a clean electricity standard of 80% by 2035 and 100% by 2040, and vehicle standards written into law. The grid gets public money and, where no one else will build a needed line, a public builder.

**Help with bills that comes to you,** from winter 2030-31. A household already getting SNAP, SSI or energy help gets a credit on its energy bill each year, with no new application. Cooling counts as much as heating.

**Ready for disasters,** from 2030. Money for every state and tribe to prepare, help with insurance costs for lower-income homeowners, and a federal backstop for insurers in a catastrophic year, for states that adopt modern building codes.

**A guarantee for workers and towns,** for closures from 2030. A worker who loses a job when a coal mine, a power plant or a refinery closes keeps up to 90% of their old hourly pay for two years in a new job, or 90% of their old pay while training. Workers 55 and older can draw their pension in full. Towns get their lost property tax replaced, falling over ten years.

**What it costs, on our estimates.** About $1.85 trillion over 2030-39 on the federal budget, close to what the budget already carries for these purposes, and about $147 billion a year from 2040, before the plan's financing (section 6). The worker guarantee is about $12 billion of that over ten years.

## The case in one paragraph

Americans are paying more for power, more for insurance and more after every storm, while the people who keep the lights on in coal and power towns have no promise about what comes next. This plan makes the biggest polluters pay and sends most of that money back to every adult as a Climate Dividend before any price reaches a bill. It builds clean power and the grid faster, with a public builder where no one else will. It brings help with energy bills to the families who need it without a form. It prepares every state for disasters and backs insurance in the worst years. And it guarantees the workers and towns that powered the country that they will not be left behind.

## Messages

1. **No charge on your gas bill or at the pump.** The price is on power plants and large factories; the dividend comes back to you first.
2. **A check with your name on it.** The Climate Dividend is paid four times a year to every adult, automatically for anyone who files taxes or gets Social Security, SSI, VA or railroad benefits.
3. **Clean power is the cheaper power.** New solar and wind cost less than new gas plants before any tax credit (Lazard, 2026) [EV-CLP-456].
4. **The investment already went to Republican districts.** 77% of tracked clean investment from mid-2022 through 2024 went to districts represented by Republicans (Rhodium Group and MIT's Clean Investment Monitor) [EV-CLP-208]. This plan restarts it.
5. **Help that finds you.** If you already get SNAP or SSI, your energy credit comes without an application.
6. **We keep faith with energy workers.** Coal workers who lost their jobs lost about a year and a half of pay over the following years (Census and university economists) [EV-CLP-516]. This plan guarantees 90% for two years.

## Hard questions, plain answers

- **"Isn't this a carbon tax?"** It is a price on the biggest polluters: power plants and large industrial facilities. Households pay nothing directly, and most of the money comes back to every adult before prices change.
- **"Canada tried a carbon tax with a rebate and dropped it."** Canada dropped the charge on consumers and kept the price on large emitters (Government of Canada, 2025) [EV-CLP-310]. This plan is the part Canada kept.
- **"Will my electric bill go up?"** Somewhat, at first, as the price and the clean power standard take hold; the cap on what utilities pay under the standard limits it, and that money goes back to lower- and moderate-income households. On our estimates, the lowest-income two-fifths of households come out ahead on the price and the dividend alone, before the bill credit (section 7).
- **"What does it cost?"** About $1.85 trillion over ten years on our estimates, close to what the budget already carries for these purposes; the plan's financing is set in one place for the whole plan (section 6).
- **"Does it meet the climate goals?"** Not by 2035 on its own, and we say so. On our estimates it cuts energy emissions to roughly 40-48% below 2005 by 2035 (section 7), against a national goal of 61-66% (Congressional Research Service) [EV-CLP-116]. It puts the country on the path to net zero by 2050 and requires the government to publish the count every year.
- **"Who pays if a hurricane overwhelms insurers?"** Insurers and premiums first. The federal backstop starts only above a once-in-a-century loss, is capped at $150 billion, and is paid back afterward (section 4).

## Stories (composite illustrations, not real people)

- **Linda and Ray, retired in Ohio, heating with gas.** No new line on their gas bill. Two Climate Dividends, about $880 a year in 2035 on our estimates, arrive with their Social Security (section 7).
- **Denise, a home health aide on SNAP in Detroit.** Her winter energy credit arrives on her bill without a form, starting the winter of 2030-31.
- **Marcus, 35, a coal miner in West Virginia.** When the mine closes, he trains as a lineworker at 90% of his old pay. If he takes a job at $26 an hour instead of his old $40, he is topped up to $36 for every hour he works, for the rest of the two years.
- **Rosebud County, Montana.** When the plant closes, the county's lost property tax is replaced in full the first year, then 10 points less each year over ten years, giving it time to rebuild its tax base.

## Can say / can't say

| Can say | Can't say |
| --- | --- |
| "No charge on your gas bill or at the pump" | "Your energy bills won't go up" (electricity rises somewhat at first) |
| "About $440 a person in 2035, on our estimates" | A firm dividend amount: it moves with what polluters pay |
| "Paid before the price reaches bills" (from the first quarter of 2030) | "Paid starting in 2029": nothing starts before 2030 |
| "Most low-income households come out ahead on the price and dividend, on our estimates" | "Every family comes out ahead": the top two-fifths pay more than they get on the carbon channel |
| "Puts us on the path to net zero by 2050" | "Meets the 2035 goal" |
| "About $1.85 trillion over ten years, on our estimates" | That it is paid for: the financing is set plan-wide |
| "A guarantee for workers when a mine, plant or refinery closes" | That it covers every oil and gas worker: field jobs without a closure are not covered |
| "A federal backstop for insurers above a once-in-a-century loss, capped" | "The government will cover your home" |
