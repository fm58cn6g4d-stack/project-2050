# 2. What works

Role: section 2 of the clean power dossier: the research and the record at home and abroad, and the failures to avoid. Status: draft (step E-C1). Every figure cites `evidence.json`. Analysis is presented as its publisher's view until an independent source supports it; where sources disagree, both are shown.

**What the evidence says, in brief.**
1. **Carbon prices cut emissions modestly and do not hurt the economy as a whole.** Measured cuts run from about 4-6% (European carbon taxes) to 5-15% (British Columbia) and about 10% (the EU's trading system), with no detectable loss of jobs or growth. A price alone does not deliver deep cuts; it works with standards, investment and a grid that can connect new power.
2. **Dividends make a carbon price progressive on paper, but they have not made one popular.** An equal dividend leaves most low- and middle-income households ahead in every U.S. study. Yet Canada's rebate, which did this, was repealed in 2025 because most people did not believe they came out ahead. Visibility, naming and timing matter as much as the arithmetic.
3. **The binding limits on clean power are the grid and the queue, not the price of clean power.** Solar, wind and batteries are now among the cheapest new sources, but projects wait years to connect and transmission is growing too slowly to carry them.
4. **Public finance and public builders have a better record than their reputation.** The Department of Energy's loan portfolio has lost about 3% of what it lent; TVA, Bonneville and WAPA already build and borrow at scale.
5. **Mitigation pays several times its cost, and federal backstops for catastrophe risk exist and can be run at little expected cost.** The open questions are design and moral hazard, not whether it can be done.
6. **Transitions go badly when they are left to chance and better when workers and places get guaranteed income, pensions and replacement tax base on a schedule.** Germany and Spain wrote this into law; the U.S. has mostly not.

## A. Carbon pricing and dividends

**British Columbia (2008-2025).** The tax began at C$10 a tonne and rose to C$30 by 2012, covering about three-quarters of emissions, with every resident sent a one-time C$100 "climate action dividend" in the first year [EV-CLP-300] [EV-CLP-301]. Murray and Rivers' review of the research found it cut fuel use and emissions by 5-15% with negligible effects on the economy as a whole [EV-CLP-302] [EV-CLP-304]. Pretis contests the size of the effect: he finds a clear cut only in transport (5-19% depending on method) [EV-CLP-303]. Employment rose slightly (Yamazaki) [EV-CLP-305]; Metcalf finds no harm to GDP and calls his positive estimates imprecise [EV-CLP-306]. British Columbia set the consumer tax to zero on April 1, 2025, when Canada dropped its federal requirement [EV-CLP-307] [EV-CLP-308].

**Canada's fuel charge and rebate (2019-2025).** The charge was set to rise from C$65 a tonne in 2023-24 to C$170 by 2030 [EV-CLP-309], with most proceeds returned as a quarterly rebate; a family of four received between about C$300 and C$456 a quarter by April 2025, depending on province [EV-CLP-311]. The Parliamentary Budget Officer (PBO) found most households received more than they paid when only the charge and rebate are counted [EV-CLP-313], but a net cost of 0.5-0.7% of income once wider economic effects were added [EV-CLP-314]; it later corrected that its earlier economic estimates had wrongly included industrial pricing [EV-CLP-315]. The charge was set to zero and the rebate ended in spring 2025 [EV-CLP-310] [EV-CLP-312]. **Why it failed:** people did not see the rebate. Mildenberger and colleagues found Canadians and Swiss alike underestimated their rebates, and that perceptions followed party lines rather than payments [EV-CLP-319]. In 2023 the government said 80% of households got back more than they paid while 54% of recipients believed they lost money; in 2024, 44% said they paid more and 19% said they gained [EV-CLP-321] [EV-CLP-320]. The consumer charge also did less of the work than industrial pricing (8-14% against 23-48% of Canada's projected cuts) [EV-CLP-318] [EV-CLP-317].

**The European Union.** The EU trading system, which caps power and industry, has cut those sectors' emissions 50% below 2005 by 2024 [EV-CLP-322], at prices around €65-90 a tonne in 2023-26 [EV-CLP-324] [EV-CLP-325] [EV-CLP-326]. Independent research credits the system itself with about a 10% cut at regulated plants and finds no significant job loss [EV-CLP-334], on a modest sample [EV-CLP-335]; across 30 years of European carbon taxes, Metcalf and Stock find no harm to jobs or growth and cumulative cuts of 4-6% for a $40 tax covering 30% of emissions [EV-CLP-336] [EV-CLP-337]. The EU raised €43.2 billion in 2025 and requires members to spend it on climate and energy [EV-CLP-327] [EV-CLP-328]. Its new system for buildings and road fuels was delayed a year to 2028 [EV-CLP-329] and comes with a Social Climate Fund of at least €86.7 billion, more than 90% of it for investment rather than direct payments [EV-CLP-331] [EV-CLP-332]. Its border charge on carbon-intensive imports took effect in January 2026, with certificate sales from February 2027 [EV-CLP-333], which matters for U.S. exporters whatever the U.S. does.

**Switzerland: a dividend that survives.** Switzerland's levy has been CHF 120 a tonne since 2022; about half the money goes back to the public as an equal refund credited against each resident's health insurance premium [EV-CLP-338] [EV-CLP-339], about CHF 57 a person in 2027 [EV-CLP-340]. It has lasted, but the same survey work found most Swiss do not know it exists [EV-CLP-319]: invisible delivery protects the policy from backlash but buys no support.

**Failures to learn from.**
- *France, 2018.* A scheduled rise in the carbon tax from €44.60 to €55 a tonne on fuel set off the "yellow vest" protests and was cancelled, with fuel taxes frozen to 2022 [EV-CLP-341] [EV-CLP-342]. The money was not returned to households, and rural drivers bore the cost with no alternative.
- *Australia, 2012-14.* The carbon price cut power-sector emissions about 9-10% in its two years [EV-CLP-344] [EV-CLP-345] and was repealed in 2014 after an election fought on it [EV-CLP-343].
- *Washington State.* Voters rejected a revenue-neutral carbon tax in 2016 (59% no) and a carbon fee in 2018 (57% no) [EV-CLP-346], then in 2024 kept the cap-and-invest program the legislature had passed, 62% to 38% [EV-CLP-347]. A program whose benefits people can see in their communities survived a direct vote that two abstract taxes lost.

**U.S. state programs.** California extended its cap-and-trade system, renamed cap-and-invest, to 2046 [EV-CLP-348]. The Northeastern states' power-sector program (RGGI) reports a 43% cut against 2006-08, much of it from cheap gas rather than the price [EV-CLP-349] [EV-CLP-350]; Pennsylvania left it in November 2025 [EV-CLP-351].

**What a U.S. fee and dividend would do to households.** The Treasury's 2017 analysis (Obama administration, published early in the first Trump administration) modeled a $49 tax rising to $70, returned per person: the poorest tenth's after-tax income rises almost 9% and the top tenth's falls about 1% [EV-CLP-352] [EV-CLP-353]. On its assumptions the tax alone is mildly progressive [EV-CLP-354]; CBO and the Tax Policy Center find the tax alone regressive, which is why the use of the money decides the result (CBO) [EV-CLP-360]. Resources for the Future and the Climate Leadership Council both cite about 70% of households coming out ahead with an equal dividend [EV-CLP-356] [EV-CLP-358]; Goulder and colleagues find that effects through wages and capital income offset the regressive effect through prices [EV-CLP-359]. The Energy Innovation and Carbon Dividend Act (2019) set a $15 fee rising $10 a year with monthly dividends, half shares for children [EV-CLP-355]. CBO estimated a $20 price would cut emissions about 8% over ten years [EV-CLP-361]. The legacy carbon model's own household table, for the legacy design, has the bottom two-fifths ahead on the fee and dividend alone and the top three-fifths behind [EV-CLP-707] (a project estimate).

**Lessons for design.**
- Price alone is a modest lever. In the legacy model, the fee alone cuts 2035 energy CO2 about 10 points below current law, and standards and credits add a few more [EV-CLP-704]; independent estimates of measured effects are of the same modest order [EV-CLP-337] [EV-CLP-334].
- The dividend must be seen: paid before the price rises, named, and delivered as a payment rather than a credit hidden in a premium or a tax return [EV-CLP-319] [EV-CLP-321].
- Revenue people can see invested where they live (Washington) has outlasted revenue returned invisibly (Canada).
- Road fuels are the most politically exposed part of any price (France, Canada) and, with vehicle standards in place, add the least: the legacy model finds pricing gasoline and diesel would add only 41 Mt of cuts in 2035 while giving up $1.21 trillion of receipts over 2030-39 [EV-CLP-709].

## B. Clean-energy credits: the IRA's record and the 2025 law

**The record before repeal.**
- *Cost estimates rose with take-up.* At enactment JCT scored the IRA's energy credits at $271 billion over ten years [EV-CLP-200]; by 2024 CBO put them at about $800 billion over 2025-34, a different window and measure that CBO says is not directly comparable [EV-CLP-201]. Goldman Sachs ($1.2 trillion by 2032) and the Penn Wharton Budget Model ($1,045 billion) went higher still [EV-CLP-202] [EV-CLP-203] [EV-CLP-204]. Uncapped credits deliver more building and cost more than first scored; both facts matter for the design.
- *Investment came, and mostly to Republican districts.* Clean investment ran $277 billion over the four quarters to mid-2026 (Rhodium and MIT's Clean Investment Monitor) [EV-CLP-206]; of investment tracked from July 2022 to 2024, 77% went to districts held by Republicans [EV-CLP-208].
- *Clean power is being built.* 53 GW of new capacity came online in 2025, the most since 2002, mostly solar and batteries, and developers plan a record 86 GW in 2026 as projects race the credit deadlines [EV-CLP-209] [EV-CLP-210].

**The repeal.** The 2025 law ended or cut most of the credits, saving about $500 billion over 2025-34 by JCT's estimates as read by the American Action Forum [EV-CLP-214]; the largest pieces are the clean electricity, vehicle and household credits [EV-CLP-215] [EV-CLP-216] [EV-CLP-217]. Treasury then tightened the test for when construction begins [EV-CLP-234] [EV-CLP-235] [EV-CLP-236]. The table in section 1, part F lists every date.

**What followed.** EV sales fell from over 10% of new vehicles in the third quarter of 2025 to about 6.4% a year later [EV-CLP-211]. A clean energy business group counted $34.8 billion of projects cancelled in 2025 against $12.3 billion announced [EV-CLP-212] [EV-CLP-213], and clean manufacturing investment fell 24% year on year by mid-2026 [EV-CLP-207].

**Lessons.**
- Credits work, and they also drive up the cost; their spending tracks take-up, not the score [EV-CLP-201].
- Credits written into the tax code were repealed three years after enactment by a simple majority in reconciliation. A credit is durable only while a majority wants it.
- What lasted were things already built and the jobs in places that vote for both parties [EV-CLP-208].
- The legacy design's "restore and extend" option must now be priced as a new program on current take-up. The legacy carbon model's credit set costs about twice the ledger's booking [EV-CLP-710] [EV-CLP-701].

## C. The grid and permitting

**How much grid is needed.** Princeton's REPEAT project found high-voltage transmission must grow about 2.3% a year, against about 1% in the last decade, or most of the IRA's potential cuts are lost [EV-CLP-400]. DOE's 2023 needs study found interregional capacity must grow 25% to 412% by 2035 depending on the future, and regional lines about 64% [EV-CLP-401] [EV-CLP-402]. NERC found energy shortfalls under extreme weather in every year it tested and recommends 35 GW of new transfer capacity between regions [EV-CLP-403] [EV-CLP-404].

**What slows it.**
- *Interconnection more than environmental review.* Only 13% of requested capacity gets built and the median project built in 2025 took 61 months to connect [EV-CLP-408] [EV-CLP-733]. A Clean Air Task Force and Niskanen Center study of 37 transmission projects found environmental review (NEPA) was not the main cause of delay [EV-CLP-407].
- *Review takes years when it applies.* A full environmental impact statement took 4.5 years on average in 2010-18 (CEQ, first Trump administration) [EV-CLP-411]; for solar and wind it was 27 and 45 months (Resources for the Future) [EV-CLP-412].
- *Local opposition.* Columbia's Sabin Center counted at least 888 state and local restrictions on renewable projects and 567 contested projects through 2025 [EV-CLP-413]; at least 30% of utility-scale wind and solar projects were cancelled during siting in 2018-23, largely over local opposition [EV-CLP-414]. Developers rank local ordinances, interconnection and opposition as the top three causes of cancellation [EV-CLP-415].

**Federal rules and their reversal.** FERC's Order 1920 (2024) requires long-term regional planning and cost allocation [EV-CLP-405]; it is under challenge in the Fourth Circuit, argued September 2026 [EV-CLP-406]. In 2025 DOE cancelled a $4.9 billion loan guarantee for the Grain Belt Express line [EV-CLP-435], three developers withdrew from its $1.5 billion transmission capacity program [EV-CLP-436] [EV-CLP-437], and DOE ended 321 awards worth about $7.56 billion in October 2025 [EV-CLP-438].

## D. Public power and public finance

- **The public builders exist.** TVA serves about 10 million people, has $22.1 billion of bonds outstanding against a $30 billion statutory cap, and reports residential rates below those paid by over 80% of the top 100 utilities' customers (TVA's own comparison) [EV-CLP-418] [EV-CLP-417] [EV-CLP-416] [EV-CLP-419]. Bonneville's Treasury borrowing authority was raised by $10 billion in 2021 [EV-CLP-420]; WAPA can lend up to $3.25 billion for transmission [EV-CLP-421]. Public power and co-ops together serve about 29% of customers [EV-CLP-422]. TVA's cap, set in 1979, is close to binding: its debt and financing obligations reached $24.35 billion in FY2026 [EV-CLP-464].
- **States are trying it.** New York gave its Power Authority authority to build and own renewables in 2023 [EV-CLP-425]; its 2025 plan lists about 5.5 GW of opportunities, a pipeline rather than a commitment [EV-CLP-426].
- **Rural co-ops.** The IRA's New ERA program obligated about $9 billion of $9.7 billion to rural cooperatives by January 2025 [EV-CLP-423]; USDA then required winners to strip climate goals from their plans [EV-CLP-424].
- **Federal lending has a good record.** DOE's loan office reported losses of 3.1% of disbursements and $5.3 billion earned in interest and fees against $1.0 billion lost [EV-CLP-427]. Its best-known failure, Solyndra ($535 million) [EV-CLP-430], sits beside Tesla's $465 million loan, repaid early [EV-CLP-429]. The 2025 law renamed the office and gave it $1 billion to guarantee energy infrastructure, aimed mostly at fossil and nuclear [EV-CLP-433] [EV-CLP-463] [EV-CLP-434].

## E. Costs of clean and firm power

- Unsubsidized, new utility-scale solar costs $40-98 a megawatt-hour and onshore wind $37-99, against $51-129 for new combined-cycle gas and $175-255 for new nuclear (Lazard, 2026) [EV-CLP-456] [EV-CLP-457].
- Battery pack prices fell to a record $108 a kilowatt-hour in 2025, and $70 for stationary storage [EV-CLP-458]; national-lab projections see further cuts of 22-66% by 2035 [EV-CLP-459].
- Firm clean power is the hard part. Vogtle's two reactors cost more than $35 billion, over $20 billion more than forecast [EV-CLP-432] [EV-CLP-431]; Lazard assumes the next units cost about 30% less if built as a series [EV-CLP-455]. The ADVANCE Act (2024) cut licensing fees for advanced reactors [EV-CLP-462]. Enhanced geothermal drilling costs are falling fast, by 12-26% below the national baseline in recent wells [EV-CLP-460]; one company reports far larger cuts (company figures) [EV-CLP-461].

## F. Clean industry

- **Credits and grants.** The IRA's 48C program allocated $10 billion in two rounds to factories [EV-CLP-442]; the 45X manufacturing credit is projected to cost $20.1 billion in FY2025-29 [EV-CLP-444]. The 2025 law ends 45X for wind components after 2027 and adds foreign-entity limits [EV-CLP-445].
- **The reversal.** Clean manufacturing investment fell 17% to $42 billion in 2025, with $23 billion cancelled (Rhodium's Clean Investment Monitor) [EV-CLP-446]; DOE cancelled 24 demonstration awards worth over $3.7 billion in May 2025, more than half of the industrial demonstration program [EV-CLP-440] [EV-CLP-441], and over $2 billion for two hydrogen hubs in October [EV-CLP-439]. The federal Buy Clean order was rescinded [EV-CLP-447] after GSA had committed $2 billion to low-carbon materials [EV-CLP-448].
- **Contracts for difference.** Under the UK's contracts, generators are paid when market prices fall below an agreed price and pay back when they rise: in 2022 they paid back a net £346 million [EV-CLP-449] [EV-CLP-450], though payments reached a record £2.64 billion in 2025 as prices fell and offshore wind strike prices rose [EV-CLP-451]. The protection runs both ways. Germany's carbon contracts for difference pay heavy industry the gap between clean and dirty production: 15 firms, up to €2.8 billion over 15 years, about 17 million tonnes saved [EV-CLP-452] [EV-CLP-453] [EV-CLP-454].

## G. Adaptation and insurance

- **Mitigation pays.** The National Institute of Building Sciences estimates federal mitigation grants save $6 for every $1 and current building codes $11 for every $1 [EV-CLP-610] [EV-CLP-611]; an earlier, narrower 4-to-1 figure has been stretched to cover programs it did not study [EV-CLP-612]. These are model estimates from one institute; the register has no independent re-estimate.
- **Delivery has been slow even when funded.** BRIC took a median 7-9 months to finalize awards, with 700 applications worth $2.2 billion still waiting when it was cancelled [EV-CLP-605] [EV-CLP-604]. Buyouts of flood-prone homes (43,633 in 1989-2017) went mostly to richer, larger counties able to run them, while the homes bought were in more vulnerable areas [EV-CLP-624] [EV-CLP-625]. A formula floor and local capacity matter as much as the money.
- **Federal and national backstops already exist.**
  - *Terrorism Risk Insurance (TRIA):* the federal government pays 80% of insured losses above each insurer's deductible and recoups what it pays afterward through policy surcharges; no attack has been certified and nothing has been paid [EV-CLP-616] [EV-CLP-617].
  - *France (CatNat):* a single surcharge on every property policy, 20% from 2025, backs unlimited state reinsurance through a public reinsurer [EV-CLP-618] [EV-CLP-619].
  - *United Kingdom (Flood Re):* an industry levy caps flood premiums for high-risk homes and is designed to end in 2039 as prices move to risk [EV-CLP-620].
  - *California Earthquake Authority:* a publicly managed insurer with about $19 billion of claim-paying ability [EV-CLP-621].
  - *A U.S. bill:* the INSURE Act (2025) would have Treasury reinsure catastrophic property losses above a threshold, with premiums at least half of expected losses [EV-CLP-622] [EV-CLP-623].
- **New tools.** New York City's pilot buys a parametric policy that pays cash quickly after a flood to low- and moderate-income households; there are no outcome data yet [EV-CLP-626].
- **The administration's alternative.** The FEMA Review Council proposes state-run mitigation at a 50-75% federal share and a parametric formula for disaster aid tied to state performance [EV-CLP-614] [EV-CLP-615].

## H. Workers and places in transition

- **Germany** legislated its coal exit with up to €40 billion for coal regions by 2038, partly in direct aid and partly federal projects and agencies moved there [EV-CLP-526] [EV-CLP-527].
- **Spain's** 2018 agreement for its last coal mines set up a €250 million fund for mining areas and early retirement for miners 48 and older [EV-CLP-528].
- **Canada's** 2019 task force recommended wage top-ups of up to 90% of prior earnings for two years, up to C$20,000 for retraining and pension bridging [EV-CLP-529]; most was not enacted.
- **Securitization:** New Mexico's 2019 law let its utility refinance the San Juan coal plant with $360 million of low-cost bonds, about $40 million of it for workers and the community [EV-CLP-530].
- **The U.S. record is thin.** ARC's POWER grants, $518 million since 2015, project 54,000 jobs but have never been evaluated [EV-CLP-523] [EV-CLP-524] [EV-CLP-525]. The IRA's energy community bonus raised credits by 10% for projects in fossil-dependent places [EV-CLP-522], and investment did go there [EV-CLP-531]; it is a location incentive, not a guarantee to the workers displaced.
- **What the research says about workers.** Earnings losses after mine and plant closures are large and long (part E of section 1); spillovers to local business are real but smaller than the direct loss [EV-CLP-519]. Income replacement, pensions and health coverage protect the people; replacement tax base protects the place.

## I. Failures to avoid

1. **A visible cost with an invisible return** (Canada, France).
2. **Subsidies with cliffs and no durable floor.** The 2025 law showed credits can be repealed within three years of enactment (part B); standards in statute, contracts and public ownership are harder to reverse.
3. **Building generation the grid cannot connect.** The queue, not the price, set the pace in 2020-25 [EV-CLP-408] [EV-CLP-400].
4. **Competitive grants that small and poor places cannot win** (buyouts, BRIC) [EV-CLP-625] [EV-CLP-605].
5. **Insurance programs that hold prices below risk forever**, which delays adaptation and grows the deficit (the NFIP's debt, section 1 part D); Flood Re's sunset is the alternative [EV-CLP-620].
6. **Transition help promised and not funded** (Canada's task force, the dismantled U.S. working group) [EV-CLP-529] [EV-CLP-520].
