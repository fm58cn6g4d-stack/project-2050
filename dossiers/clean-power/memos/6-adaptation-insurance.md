# Memo 6: Adaptation and insurance

Role: decision memo for Espen, fork 10 of the clean power dossier (`../3-design.md`). Status: **Decided** (`DEC-2026-11-13-06`): A, as recommended; point 13 yes (`DEC-2026-11-13-07`). Specified in `../4-specification.md`, part F.

**Decision line.** Recommended: **A, the legacy disaster package as booked: a standing mitigation fund with a formula floor for every state and tribe, a larger post-disaster mitigation share, faster survivor aid, extreme-heat and wildfire programs, premium help for low- and moderate-income households (flood, and wind, wildfire and hail), voluntary buyouts, NFIP's debt resolved with a long reauthorization, and a federal reinsurance facility above a 1-in-100-year loss with a $150 billion cap, risk-priced premiums and recoupment.** Cost as booked: about **$0.20 trillion over FY2030-39 and $9.8 billion in FY2040**, plus a $75-150 billion contingent liability disclosed and not scored. Evidence: NOAA and Climate Central disaster counts; Treasury's Federal Insurance Office and NBER research on premiums; CRS on NFIP and BRIC; NIBS on mitigation's returns (one institute's estimates); TRIA, France's CatNat and the UK's Flood Re as working backstops.

## The question

Disasters of $1 billion or more cost about $115-190 billion a year in 2024-25 [EV-CLP-120] [EV-CLP-125] [EV-CLP-130]; premiums are rising fastest where risk is highest and private insurers are leaving [EV-CLP-143] [EV-CLP-147]; flood is largely uninsured [EV-CLP-157]; and the federal mitigation program was cancelled in 2025 [EV-CLP-601]. How far should the federal government go in preparing places and backing insurance?

The legacy design (CLI-7, 7K, 7N and the reinsurance facility CLI-7C, decided by Espen in the legacy files on October 26-27, 2026) is booked; working choices under `DEC-2026-10-30-02` [EV-CLP-702] [EV-CLP-719] [EV-CLP-718].

## What the evidence says, in five lines

1. **Mitigation pays several times its cost** (NIBS, 2019, one institute's model): $6 per $1 for federal grants and $11 per $1 for current codes [EV-CLP-610] [EV-CLP-611]; earlier ratios have been stretched beyond what was studied [EV-CLP-612].
2. **Competitive grants miss poor and small places** (peer-reviewed; GAO): buyouts went to larger, richer counties [EV-CLP-625]; BRIC awards took a median 7-9 months [EV-CLP-605].
3. **Premiums now track risk, and that is pushing people out** (Treasury FIO; Keys and Mulder; Dallas Fed): the riskiest ZIP codes pay 82% more [EV-CLP-143]; premiums push mortgages into delinquency [EV-CLP-150].
4. **Backstops above a high threshold can run at low expected cost** (CRS on TRIA; France's CCR): TRIA has paid nothing and recoups what it would pay [EV-CLP-617]; CatNat's single surcharge backs unlimited state reinsurance [EV-CLP-618] [EV-CLP-619].
5. **Below-risk pricing that never ends builds debt** (CRS on NFIP): $22.525 billion owed after a $16 billion cancellation [EV-CLP-154]; Flood Re's design ends in 2039 [EV-CLP-620].

## Options

**A. The legacy package as booked (recommended).**
- *What it does:* see the decision line. The mitigation fund pays every state and tribe a formula floor, so small places do not lose to grant-writing capacity; premium help is a subsidy to the household, not a cap on the rate, so prices still signal risk; the reinsurance facility sits above a 1-in-100-year loss and charges risk-based premiums with TRIA-style recoupment [EV-CLP-718].
- *Cost:* CLI-7 $78.1 billion, CLI-7K $112.5 billion, CLI-7C $3.4 billion and CLI-7N $2.2 billion over FY2030-39, $196 billion in all; $9.8 billion in FY2040 (budget model, case `mixed`); in 2025 dollars, the core tier about $100 billion and the fuller tier about $54 billion [EV-CLP-719]. The facility's $75-150 billion contingent exposure is disclosed in every fiscal test, not scored [EV-CLP-718] [EV-CLP-703].
- *Burden:* on states, adopting current hazard-resistant codes within three years to keep the insurance help (point 13); only about a third of hazard-prone jurisdictions have today [EV-CLP-613]. On households, a premium-help application, which the plan routes through the same records as memo 5.

**B. Preparation only, no insurance role.** The mitigation fund, survivor aid and NFIP reauthorization, without premium help or the reinsurance facility.
- *Cost:* roughly half of A's ten-year cost (project estimate on [EV-CLP-719]).
- *What it gives up:* the households in high-risk places now paying the most, and a backstop for state insurers of last resort as their policy counts grow [EV-CLP-147] [EV-CLP-148].

**C. A, plus a national all-perils catastrophe program on France's model.** Every homeowners policy carries a uniform surcharge funding a public reinsurer with unlimited state backing.
- *Cost:* self-funded in expectation; a much larger contingent liability, and a uniform surcharge that has people in low-risk places pay for high-risk ones.

**D. Restore BRIC and reauthorize NFIP, nothing more.** Smallest cost; leaves the insurance problem to the states and the market.

**Why A.** It pairs the cheapest dollars (mitigation and codes) with help for the households priced out, while keeping risk visible in prices and giving states' insurers of last resort a backstop with a cap. The FEMA Review Council's alternative shifts cost to states [EV-CLP-614]; A keeps the federal role.

## Also reported: the disaster costs outside the baseline

About $15-25 billion a year of disaster appropriations recurs outside CBO's extrapolated baseline (legacy disaster file, citing GAO) [EV-CLP-720]. That is current-law exposure, not this memo's cost; section 6 will report it beside the plan's fiscal test.

## Consequences

- *A homeowner in coastal Louisiana* gets premium help sized to income while the price still shows the risk; the town gets mitigation money by formula.
- *A state insurer of last resort in California or Florida* can buy federal reinsurance above a 1-in-100-year loss.
- *Taxpayers* carry a disclosed contingent liability of $75-150 billion in a very bad year, recouped afterward.
