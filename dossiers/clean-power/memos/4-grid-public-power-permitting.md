# Memo 4: The grid, public power and permitting

Role: decision memo for Espen, forks 7 and 8 of the clean power dossier (`../3-design.md`). Status: draft, for decision (E-C1). Each part is decided on its own.

**Decision line.** Recommended: **part 1 B, the legacy grid statute and financing (interregional transfer minimums, faster interconnection, a first tranche of loans, grants and capacity contracts, a second on milestones), plus public power as builder of last resort: TVA, Bonneville and WAPA borrowing limits raised and a federal transmission authority that builds national-interest lines where no one else will; part 2, the permitting position for both cases: if the bipartisan package becomes law, amend its permit-certainty section so standards and retirements are never a damages event; if it does not, pass a narrower permitting title for transmission and clean power.** Cost: the booked first tranche is about **$0.04 trillion over FY2030-39 and zero in FY2040**; the second tranche and the transmission authority add about $0.04-0.08 trillion over FY2030-39 at subsidy cost (project estimate), borrowing-eligible capital under the fiscal rule. Evidence: LBNL's queue data; REPEAT, DOE and NERC on transmission need; DOE's loan record; TVA's and Bonneville's statutes and accounts; the permitting bills' texts as CRS and law firms describe them.

## The question

The binding limit on clean power is the grid: projects wait 55 months to connect and only 13% of those that ask are built [EV-CLP-409] [EV-CLP-408]; transmission is growing about 536 miles a year against 1,781 a decade earlier [EV-CLP-051]; NERC wants 35 GW more transfer capacity between regions [EV-CLP-403]. Who builds, who pays, and what permitting law should say.

The legacy design is a grid and utility statute (CLI-6A), a transmission credit for planned lines (inside CLI-5), and a lean first tranche of public grid financing (CLI-10G, booked) with a second tranche on milestones [EV-CLP-717]. Public power as builder of last resort (CLI-10) is an unchosen legacy option. All are working choices.

## What the evidence says, in five lines

1. **Transmission must roughly double its growth rate** (Princeton REPEAT, 2022): 2.3% a year against about 1% [EV-CLP-400]; DOE's 2023 study finds interregional capacity must grow 25-412% by 2035 [EV-CLP-401].
2. **Interconnection and local siting, more than environmental review, slow clean projects** (LBNL; Clean Air Task Force and Niskanen; Columbia's Sabin Center) [EV-CLP-408] [EV-CLP-407] [EV-CLP-413] [EV-CLP-414].
3. **Federal lending has lost about 3% of what it lent** (DOE Loan Programs Office, 2024) [EV-CLP-427].
4. **The public builders exist and are near their limits** (statute and accounts): TVA's $30 billion bond cap dates from 1979 [EV-CLP-416], and its debt and financing obligations reached $24.35 billion in FY2026 [EV-CLP-464]; Bonneville's borrowing was raised $10 billion in 2021 [EV-CLP-420]; WAPA can lend $3.25 billion [EV-CLP-421].
5. **Market-only transmission stalls when it crosses states** (DOE, 2025): the Grain Belt Express lost its $4.9 billion guarantee and developers left the capacity program [EV-CLP-435] [EV-CLP-437].

## Part 1. Who builds and how it is paid for

**A. The legacy grid statute and lean financing (CLI-6A, CLI-10G).**
- *What it does:* statutory minimum transfer capability between regions, enforced by FERC; a performance-based ratemaking standard; utility accountability rules; and $25 billion of grants, $100 billion of direct loans and $20 billion of capacity contracts in a first tranche, with a second ($40 billion, $200 billion and $40 billion) if delivery milestones are met [EV-CLP-717].
- *Cost:* the first tranche about $32 billion in deficit terms ($28-40 billion), booked at $39.4 billion over FY2030-39 [EV-CLP-717] (budget model).
- *Burden:* on utilities and grid operators, the transfer minimums and planning deadlines; on DOE, a lending office with milestone reviews.

**B. A, plus public power as builder of last resort (recommended).**
- *What it adds:* TVA's bond cap raised (point 11) and Bonneville's and WAPA's borrowing extended; a federal transmission authority, on TVA's model, that builds lines on a national-interest list where no regional builder files within a set time, recovering its costs through FERC-approved rates. The second tranche is authorized up front, released on milestones.
- *For people:* in places where no one builds today, a public builder does; costs are recovered from the users of the line, as Bonneville's are.
- *Cost:* the second tranche about $0.06 trillion at subsidy cost, on the first tranche's ratio (project arithmetic on [EV-CLP-717]), released only on milestones, so range $0 to about $0.08 trillion with the authority's start-up; borrowing-eligible capital under the fiscal rule (D4-B).
- *Burden:* a new federal entity, with the delivery risk the legacy October 26 direction warned of: it must not assume federal construction can proceed without regional workforce and delivery capacity.
- *Political risk:* "federal power lines" meet the same local opposition as any line; the authority uses existing federal siting backstops, not new eminent domain.

**C. Rules and permitting only.** No public money; FERC's planning rule (Order 1920, under challenge [EV-CLP-406]) and permitting do the work. Cheapest, and it relies on the market that stalled in 2025.

**Why B.** The evidence says the grid will not double its pace on rules alone, and that public lenders and builders have a better record than their reputation. The milestone gate limits the cost if delivery lags.

## Part 2. Permitting

The plan begins after the lame duck has decided the bipartisan permitting package [EV-CLP-258] [EV-CLP-261]. The memo asks Espen for a position in both cases.

**If the package becomes law** (case 1):
- **A (recommended).** Keep its transmission siting and faster review; amend its permit-certainty section so that emissions standards, the clean power standard and planned retirements are never an "interference" that triggers damages, and cap any damages paid from the Judgment Fund per project and year (the legacy CLI-3 fixes). The introduced text pays 25-50% of project costs [EV-CLP-255]; the Judgment Fund already pays about $3.5 billion a year [EV-CLP-256].
- **B.** Repeal the permit-certainty section outright. Cleaner, and it reopens the bipartisan bargain.
- **C.** Leave it. Every later standard then carries a contingent damages cost.

**If it does not** (case 2):
- **A (recommended).** A narrower permitting title in the first Congress: FERC backstop siting for national-interest lines, deadlines and shorter court review for transmission and clean power, and no permit-certainty damages.
- **B.** No federal permitting change; rely on *Seven County* (2025), which already narrowed review [EV-CLP-250].

**Why.** The permit-certainty section is the one provision that could turn the rest of this dossier into a damages liability; the rest of the package helps build the grid.

## Points decided with this memo

Large new loads, such as data centers, pay their own grid costs (point 10); TVA's cap (point 11).

## Consequences

- *A wind farm in Kansas* waiting in the queue gets a deadline and a line to connect to.
- *A ratepayer in Virginia* does not pay for a data center's new substation.
- *If case 1 passes unamended,* a coal plant's owner could claim damages when the clean power standard raises its costs.
