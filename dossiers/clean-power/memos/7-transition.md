# Memo 7: Workers and places in transition

Role: decision memo for Espen, fork 11 of the clean power dossier (`../3-design.md`). Status: **Decided** (`DEC-2026-11-13-06`): B, with four parameters Espen set: wage insurance capped at twice the median wage; unemployment benefits count toward the 90%; workers employed through contractors at a closing facility are covered; the top-up is paid on the hourly wage gap for the hours actually worked, up to the old schedule, with no suitable-job test, monitored by published counts with a review trigger. Specified in `../4-specification.md`, part G; repriced bottom-up in `../6-cost.md`, part 1 (about $0.012 trillion over FY2030-39, below this memo's $0.08 trillion). The tax-base replacement extends to tribal governments' lost lease, royalty and tribal tax revenue (`DEC-2026-11-13-11`).

**Decision line.** Recommended: **B, a transition guarantee in statute for workers and places losing fossil-fuel jobs: wage insurance of up to 90% of prior earnings for two years, a pension bridge for workers 55 and older, retraining money, local tax-base replacement that declines over ten years, and low-cost refinancing for retiring coal plants with a share for workers and the community, paid first from the carbon price's transition share.** Cost: about **$0.08 trillion over FY2030-39 (range $0.04-0.15 trillion) and about $6-10 billion in FY2040**, not booked (a working assumption on a legacy session estimate; the FY2040 figure assumes the guarantee keeps pace with closures). Evidence: Census and peer-reviewed studies of coal workers' losses; Resources for the Future on local fiscal dependence; Germany's, Spain's and Canada's programs; New Mexico's securitization law.

## The question

The workers and places that depend on coal, and later on gas and oil, face the transition with little to catch them: coal workers lose earnings worth about 1.6 years of pay [EV-CLP-516]; counties lose most of their tax base when a plant closes [EV-CLP-514]; the federal working group was abolished [EV-CLP-520]. What does the plan guarantee them?

The legacy design sends the carbon price's 20% transition share to "coal and energy communities and to low-income energy help" without a design, and lists worker guarantees and coal securitization only as an option (CLI-11, about $0.08 trillion, session estimate) [EV-CLP-713] [EV-CLP-700]. In the booking the whole transition share finances the add-ons (CLI-5), so nothing is reserved for workers.

## What the evidence says, in four lines

1. **Losses are large and long** (Census; peer-reviewed UK study) [EV-CLP-516] [EV-CLP-517]; Canadian evidence shows faster recovery [EV-CLP-518].
2. **Places lose their tax base** (Resources for the Future): fossil fuels are about 90% of energy-related local revenue in producing counties [EV-CLP-513].
3. **Other countries wrote guarantees into law** (government sources): Germany up to €40 billion for coal regions to 2038 [EV-CLP-527]; Spain early retirement from 48 and a €250 million fund [EV-CLP-528]; Canada's task force recommended wage top-ups to 90% for two years [EV-CLP-529].
4. **Securitization frees money for workers** (New Mexico): $360 million of low-cost bonds with about $40 million for workers and the community [EV-CLP-530].

## Options

**A. The legacy direction: the transition share to communities, design left open.**
- *Cost:* none new; the share already pays for the add-ons in the booking.
- *What it gives up:* a commitment anyone can rely on.

**B. A statutory transition guarantee (recommended).**
- *What it does:*
  - *Workers* at covered coal mines, coal and gas plants, and later refineries and other fossil facilities that close: wage insurance of up to 90% of prior earnings for two years in a new job or training, up to $20,000 for retraining, and a pension bridge for workers 55 and older to their full pension. Health coverage is the care plan's (`dossiers/care/`), so no separate guarantee is needed after 2035.
  - *Places:* counties and school districts losing a plant's or mine's property tax get replacement payments that start at its full amount and decline over ten years.
  - *Plants:* Treasury buys low-cost, ratepayer-backed bonds that refinance a retiring coal plant's remaining value, on New Mexico's model, with a share for workers and the community.
- *For people:* a 57-year-old plant operator in Colstrip bridges to their pension; a 35-year-old keeps 90% of their pay while training as a lineworker.
- *Cost:* about $0.08 trillion over FY2030-39, range $0.04-0.15 trillion (a working assumption on the legacy session estimate [EV-CLP-713]); the refinancing is lending at subsidy cost. Paid first from the transition share (memo 1), which then finances less of the add-ons, so the general fund carries the difference.
- *Burden:* eligibility from the employer's own payroll records and the closure notice the plant already files; no means test.

**C. Place-based grants only** (POWER scaled up). Grants have not been evaluated [EV-CLP-525] and do not reach the person who lost the job.

**D. General programs only** (the secure floor and the work flagship's training, LAB-8). No special status for fossil workers.

**Why B.** It is the one design the evidence supports: income replacement and pensions protect the people, replacement tax base protects the place, and both are cheap next to the rest of the dossier. It also answers the political case: the places that host these plants are where the clean investment went [EV-CLP-531].

## Consequences

- *A coal county in Wyoming* knows the schedule of its replacement payments before the plant closes.
- *The fiscal effect* is new money, because the booking already spends the transition share elsewhere.
