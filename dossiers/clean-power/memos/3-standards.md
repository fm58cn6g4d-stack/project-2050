# Memo 3: The clean power standard and Clean Air Act authority

Role: decision memo for Espen, forks 5 and 6 of the clean power dossier (`../3-design.md`). Status: draft, for decision (E-C1). Each part is decided on its own.

**Decision line.** Recommended: **part 1 B, a clean electricity standard in statute, 80% by 2035 and 100% by 2040, through tradable credits, with a capped alternative compliance payment whose revenue goes to bill protection; part 2 A, Congress writes the greenhouse endangerment finding and express authority into the Clean Air Act and sets vehicle standards' numbers in statute.** Cost: about **$0.02 trillion over FY2030-39 and $2.5 billion in FY2040** for administration (booked as CLI-3), with payments under the cap about $0.05 trillion that fund bill protection (legacy model) [EV-CLP-724] [EV-CLP-723]. Evidence: the legacy carbon model's run of the standard (a project estimate); *West Virginia v. EPA*; EPA's 2026 rescissions; LBNL's queue data.

## The question

Without a standard, the plan's clean power path rests on a price and credits that a later Congress can repeal, as 2025 showed. With one, how high and how fast? And how does the plan restore EPA's authority, which the administration rescinded in 2026 [EV-CLP-240] [EV-CLP-244]?

The legacy design first set 80% clean electricity by 2035 (CLI-3A), then raised it in Package A to 90% by 2035 and 100% by 2040 (CLI-3E), with statutory vehicle standards (CLI-4E) and a clean-heat standard from 2032 (CLI-4H) [EV-CLP-715]. All are booked at zero direct cost; all are working choices (`DEC-2026-10-30-02`).

## What the evidence says, in four lines

1. **Grid access, not price, limits the clean share** (legacy carbon model): even with the fee, credits and standards together, the clean share reaches 75% in 2035 and meets 80% only in 2037 [EV-CLP-708]. The queue (55 months, 13% built) is why [EV-CLP-409] [EV-CLP-408].
2. **A standard mostly sets a floor when the price and credits are in place, and keeps working if they are repealed** (legacy model): with the fee repealed in 2033 the credits, standard and rules still give 73% clean in 2035 [EV-CLP-723].
3. **Agency rules can be undone by the next administration and narrowed by the Court**: *West Virginia v. EPA* (2022) found no clear authority for generation shifting [EV-CLP-245]; EPA rescinded the endangerment finding and vehicle standards in 2026 and repealed most power plant standards [EV-CLP-240] [EV-CLP-244]. Litigation will not be decided before 2027 [EV-CLP-243].
4. **The permitting package may make standards costly**: its permit-certainty section, as introduced, pays damages when an agency interferes with a fully permitted project [EV-CLP-255]; the amended text narrows it [EV-CLP-266].

## Part 1. The clean electricity standard

**A. 90% by 2035 and 100% by 2040 (Package A, CLI-3E).**
- *What it buys:* the legacy portfolio model's deepest path: 48% below 2005 economy-wide in 2035 with the rest of Package A [EV-CLP-715].
- *What it risks:* on the legacy model's own run of the easier 80% target, the clean share reached only 75% in 2035 [EV-CLP-708]. Suppliers short of the target pay the capped payment, which becomes a de facto price on power bills.

**B. 80% by 2035 and 100% by 2040, with a capped payment (recommended).**
- *What it does:* retail suppliers hold credits for a rising share of clean power (renewables, nuclear, hydro, geothermal, storage-shifted clean power, and fossil with capture at its captured share); the target steps up yearly; a supplier short of credits pays a capped amount per missing megawatt-hour. Payments fund bill protection for low- and moderate-income households (memo 5). Written in statute, with its schedule.
- *For people:* a utility customer sees no new line; the cap limits how far the standard can raise bills.
- *Cost:* administration about $0.02 trillion over FY2030-39 (legacy estimate, booked as CLI-3) [EV-CLP-724]; payments under the cap about $0.05 trillion over 2030-39 on the legacy model, sent to bill protection [EV-CLP-723].
- *Burden:* on utilities, credit tracking, which most states with standards already run; on EIA and FERC, a registry.

**C. No standard; rely on the price, credits and restored EPA authority.**
- Cheaper to pass, and loses the floor that survives a later repeal of the price [EV-CLP-723].

**Why B.** The 2035 step at 90% asks for more clean power than the grid can connect by then on the project's own model; 80% is already ambitious after three years without credits, and 100% by 2040 keeps the destination. The capped payment is the bill protection the design lens asks for.

## Part 2. Clean Air Act authority

**A. The finding and the numbers in statute (recommended).** Congress finds that greenhouse gases endanger public health and welfare, gives EPA express authority for vehicles (sec. 202) and power plants (sec. 111) including generation shifting, and writes fleet-average vehicle standards through 2040 into the statute (CLI-4A with CLI-4E). If the permitting package becomes law, the same bill states that a standard is not an interference with a permit.
- *Cost:* none direct. *Burden:* on automakers, the standards; on EPA, rules on statutory deadlines with a default standard if a deadline is missed.

**B. The finding and express delegation only (CLI-4A).** EPA sets the numbers; a later administration can weaken them but cannot deny the authority.

**C. Wait for the courts.** The D.C. Circuit cases may restore the 2009 finding, but a win returns the question to an agency a later administration can redirect [EV-CLP-242] [EV-CLP-246].

**Why A.** The 2025-26 rescissions are the case for numbers in statute: a statute that states the standard runs even if a later EPA does nothing.

## Consequences

- *A family buying a car in 2032:* every automaker must meet the fleet average, so more models are electric; the credit (memo 2) lowers the price.
- *A coal plant in Wyoming:* the standard does not close it by date; it raises the cost of running unabated as the target rises, and memo 7 covers its workers.
- *If the permitting package passes as introduced* without a fix, any standard that leads to a plant's early retirement could invite a damages claim paid from the Judgment Fund [EV-CLP-255] [EV-CLP-256].
