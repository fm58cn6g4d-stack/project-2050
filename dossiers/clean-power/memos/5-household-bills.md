# Memo 5: Household energy bills

Role: decision memo for Espen, fork 9 of the clean power dossier (`../3-design.md`). Status: **Decided** (`DEC-2026-11-13-06`): B, as recommended; point 12 yes (`DEC-2026-11-13-07`). Specified in `../4-specification.md`, part E.

**Decision line.** Recommended: **B, energy help as an automatic benefit: LIHEAP's money raised and paid as a bill credit to every household already enrolled in SNAP, SSI or the low-income home energy program, without a separate application, with heat-pump retrofits and the transition bill protection as booked.** Cost: **about $0.08 trillion over FY2030-39** for the larger energy help, inside the add-ons' booked $0.12 trillion line for energy, Weatherization and water help, plus the booked heat-pump retrofits ($84 billion) and bill protection ($7 billion); **about $9-10 billion in FY2040** above the booking, because the booked add-ons end around 2040 and this help is permanent (project estimate). Evidence: HHS's LIHEAP reach as reported by NLIHC and CRS; DOE's LEAD tool; Census survey data on hardship; the legacy add-ons' split (a project estimate).

## The question

LIHEAP reaches about 17% of eligible households [EV-CLP-038], funding is set year to year and the administration proposes to end it [EV-CLP-035] [EV-CLP-036], while low-income households spend about 6% of income on energy, three times others [EV-CLP-034], and one adult in five could not pay an energy bill in full [EV-CLP-031]. Bills will move with the plan too: the price (memo 1) and the standard (memo 3) raise power costs before the clean grid lowers them.

The legacy design raises LIHEAP, Weatherization and water assistance inside the add-ons (about $0.12 trillion) [EV-CLP-721], funds low-income heat-pump retrofits (CLI-5W, $84 billion, booked) and bill protection while bills rise (CLI-5L, $7.2 billion, booked) [EV-CLP-702]. Working choices.

## What the evidence says, in three lines

1. **Reach follows money** (CRS): the share served rose from about 15% to 21-22% only in the years funding rose [EV-CLP-039].
2. **The hardship is large and concentrated** (Census survey, peer-reviewed analysis; NEADA): 22% of adults unable to pay, 37% of households under $50,000 [EV-CLP-031] [EV-CLP-032]; $23 billion in arrears [EV-CLP-028].
3. **Programs that use records the government holds reach more people** (the plan's design lens, `DEC-2026-11-09-13`; the secure floor's decided automatic payment, `DEC-2026-11-12-06`). No randomized study of automatic LIHEAP enrollment is in the register (an evidence gap).

## Options

**A. The legacy add-ons: a larger LIHEAP block grant, Weatherization heat pumps, bill protection.**
- *Cost:* as booked [EV-CLP-721] [EV-CLP-702].
- *Burden:* each household still applies to a state office each year, which is why reach is low.

**B. A, delivered automatically (recommended).**
- *What it does:* households enrolled in SNAP, SSI or the program the year before get an annual bill credit through their utility, sized to bring their energy burden toward 6% of income, without a separate application; others apply as now. States run it with federal records. Heat-pump retrofits and bill protection as booked.
- *For people:* a grandmother on SSI in Mississippi sees a credit on her summer bill without filling in a form.
- *Cost:* reaching about half of eligible households, roughly three times today's reach, takes about $12 billion a year against today's $4 billion: about $8 billion a year more, $0.08 trillion over FY2030-39 (project estimate on LIHEAP's $4 billion a year at 17% reach [EV-CLP-035] [EV-CLP-038]), inside the legacy line [EV-CLP-721]. Kept permanent, it adds about $9-10 billion in FY2040, which the booking does not carry (the add-ons are time-limited). Range: $0.05-0.12 trillion over FY2030-39, depending on reach and prices.
- *Burden:* lower for households; for utilities, accepting a federal credit file; for states, matching records.

**C. Rely on the dividend.** No new program. The dividend is not targeted on energy burden and comes once a quarter; under memo 1 B it is smaller.

**Why B.** The money is about the same; the difference is that people get it. The design lens favors records the government already holds over yearly applications.

## Points decided with this memo

Bill protection's sunset (point 12).

## Consequences

- *A family of four in Detroit on SNAP* gets the credit each winter; under A they apply each fall and may miss it.
- *The fiscal effect* in the window is inside the booked line; the lasting cost is making the help permanent, about $9-10 billion a year from FY2040.
