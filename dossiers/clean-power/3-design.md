# 3. Design choices

Role: section 3 of the clean power dossier: the real forks, the options weighed, the recommendations and their consequences, and the decision memos Espen decides in one batch. Status: draft (step E-C1), **for decision**. Nothing here is decided until Espen answers; each memo and point is decided on its own, and none is adopted by silence. Every figure cites `evidence.json`; costs are on the general fund against the January 2029 law, FY2030-39 and FY2040, and a minus sign is a saving.

## The plan in one paragraph (as recommended)

Price carbon where the price does the most work and costs households least: power plants and heavy industry, on the legacy price path, with a border adjustment, and return most of it as a named Climate Dividend paid to every adult before the price reaches bills (memo 1). Start the clean-energy credits again from 2030 and step them down as a clean electricity standard (80% by 2035, 100% by 2040) and statutory vehicle standards take over (memos 2 and 3). Back heavy industry with contracts for difference. Build the grid with public lenders and a public builder of last resort (memo 4). Deliver energy help automatically to households the government already knows are struggling (memo 5). Prepare places and back insurance with a capped federal reinsurance facility (memo 6). Guarantee income, pensions and tax base to the workers and towns that lose fossil jobs (memo 7).

## The baseline in January 2029

Section 1, part F has the table. In short: the IRA's household, vehicle, wind and solar credits are closed to new buyers and projects [EV-CLP-224] [EV-CLP-225] [EV-CLP-220]; nuclear, geothermal and storage keep theirs to 2033 [EV-CLP-222]; EPA's greenhouse authority for vehicles and power plants has been rescinded, with lawsuits pending [EV-CLP-240] [EV-CLP-244]; the methane charge is delayed to 2034 [EV-CLP-232]; NFIP runs on short extensions [EV-CLP-608]; and the bipartisan permitting package's fate is open as of November 13, 2026 [EV-CLP-261]. The memos are written for both permitting cases (memo 4, part 2).

**Labeled exceptions to the 2029 frame.**
- *2027-28, under divided government (a Democratic Congress after the 2026 midterms [EV-CLP-265] and the current President).* NFIP's long reauthorization, LIHEAP's funding, the BRIC court order's implementation and the permitting fixes can be attempted in 2027-28; whatever passes moves into the baseline.
- *Preparation before 2029.* Draft the dividend's payment rail with the secure floor's (`DEC-2026-11-12-06`), the standard's credit registry, and the contracts office's model contracts.

## What each recommendation rests on

- **Memo 1 B** rests on measured effects of carbon prices (peer-reviewed: Murray and Rivers; Dechezleprêtre and others; Metcalf and Stock) [EV-CLP-302] [EV-CLP-334] [EV-CLP-337], Canada's split of work between its industrial and consumer prices (a think tank's projection reported by CBC) [EV-CLP-318], the repeal record (government sources) [EV-CLP-310] [EV-CLP-343], and the legacy model's receipts by source (a project estimate) [EV-CLP-714]. The choice to leave buildings to a standard is **judgment**.
- **Memo 1 A (dividend)** rests on Espen's decided payment rail (`DEC-2026-11-12-06`) and survey evidence on visibility [EV-CLP-319] [EV-CLP-321].
- **Memo 2 B** rests on the booked legacy design and its step-down (project estimates) [EV-CLP-702] [EV-CLP-716], with the IRA's cost growth (JCT, CBO) [EV-CLP-200] [EV-CLP-201] as the risk.
- **Memo 3 B** rests on the legacy model's run of the standard (a project estimate) [EV-CLP-708] [EV-CLP-723] and the queue data (LBNL) [EV-CLP-408]. The 80% level is **judgment** on that model.
- **Memo 4 B** rests on transmission-need studies (REPEAT; DOE; NERC) [EV-CLP-400] [EV-CLP-401] [EV-CLP-403], DOE's lending record [EV-CLP-427] and the public builders' statutes [EV-CLP-416] [EV-CLP-420]. The transmission authority's cost is a **project estimate**.
- **Memo 5 B** rests on reach data (CRS; HHS via NLIHC) [EV-CLP-039] [EV-CLP-038] and the design lens; no study of automatic delivery is in the register (**gap**).
- **Memo 6 A** rests on NIBS's mitigation estimates (one institute) [EV-CLP-610], the insurance research (Treasury FIO; NBER) [EV-CLP-143] [EV-CLP-140] and working backstops [EV-CLP-616] [EV-CLP-618].

## Shared rules this dossier relies on (`dossiers/shared-rules.md`)

- **Rule 1, what counts as income.** The Climate Dividend and the energy bill credit do not count as income for any federal program, under Espen's decided rule that payments that do not replace wages never count (`DEC-2026-11-12-06`, memo 7 part 1 B). Working answer: decided by the secure floor.
- **Rule 3, which immigrants are eligible.** Working answer: the dividend follows immigration's decided table for the plan's universal programs, residence (`DEC-2026-11-12-07`, memo 6 C); the transition guarantee follows employment records. Range: if Espen prefers the dividend to require a work-authorized Social Security number, as the 2020-21 payments did, fewer residents are paid and the cost falls slightly.
- **Rule 5, who verifies and pays.** The IRS pays the dividend on the child benefit's rail; the energy bill credit uses SNAP, SSI and LIHEAP enrollment records the states already hold (verify once).
- **Rule 6, conditions on help.** The only condition on households is none; the only condition on states is hazard-resistant codes for the insurance help (point 13).

## Points for a yes or no

Each is decided on its own; a "no" is recorded and the dossier is revised.

1. **Goals.** Adopt as the plan's targets net zero by 2050 and 61-66% below 2005 by 2035 [EV-CLP-116], stating plainly that no domestic package the project has modeled reaches the 2035 range (the legacy model's best is about 48% [EV-CLP-715]; independent current-policy projections are 26-44% [EV-CLP-112] [EV-CLP-113]). *Recommended: yes.*
2. **Road fuels stay outside the carbon price**, priced only by Moving America's fuel steps (D7) [EV-CLP-709]. *Yes.*
3. **The methane fee** (REV-C3): the IRA's charge reinstated from 2030 at $1,200 a ton of methane rising to $1,500, with a facility exemption for certified leak prevention, undoing the delay to 2034 [EV-CLP-232]. *Yes.*
4. **The border adjustment** (REV-C2) on imports of the covered goods, with export rebates, built to count against the EU's border charge [EV-CLP-333]. *Yes.*
5. **The 80/20 split**: 80% of the price returned as the dividend, 20% to the transition share, which pays memo 7's guarantee first [EV-CLP-700]. *Yes.*
6. **A dividend account with a permanent appropriation**, so a shutdown or a hostile Congress cannot stop a payment without changing the law (legacy drafting note). *Yes.*
7. **A cost review trigger for the credits**: if they run more than 25% above the booked path for two years, Treasury reports and the step-down comes forward unless Congress acts [EV-CLP-201]. *Yes.*
8. **Restore the national greenhouse inventory** as a statutory duty with a fixed release date, so OUT-05 is measured [EV-CLP-103]. *Yes.*
9. **A clean-heat standard from 2032** (CLI-4H): new heating equipment sold must meet a rising clean share, with the credits and Weatherization heat pumps paying for low-income replacements. *Recommended: yes, given memo 1 B leaves buildings unpriced.* Burden: on households replacing a furnace, the choice of equipment; on manufacturers and distributors, the sales share.
10. **Large new loads pay their own grid costs**: data centers and other loads above a threshold pay for the generation and wires they need, in every market including ERCOT [EV-CLP-024] [EV-CLP-027] [EV-CLP-257]. *Yes.*
11. **Raise TVA's $30 billion bond cap** (set in 1979) [EV-CLP-416] [EV-CLP-464], and extend Bonneville's and WAPA's borrowing for transmission. *Yes.*
12. **Bill protection's sunset**: the booked low- and moderate-income bill protection (CLI-5L) ends when the standard's payments and the clean grid bring bills below their 2029 level, not on a fixed date. *Yes.*
13. **Codes as a condition**: states adopt current hazard-resistant building codes within three years to keep the federal insurance help, and new federal rebuilding aid meets a forward-looking flood standard (CLI-8). *Yes.* Burden: on states and localities, code adoption and enforcement; only about a third of hazard-prone jurisdictions have adopted today [EV-CLP-613].
14. **Farms and forests as booked** (CLI-13A, 13F): continuing conservation payments and forest work [EV-CLP-702]. *Yes.*

## Fiscal effect (reported, not gated; `DEC-2026-11-10-05`)

**The booked basis.** The budget model books the legacy climate rows at about **$1.81 trillion over FY2030-39 and $165 billion in FY2040** on the general fund (case `mixed`, read from `legacy/sources/recovery-budget-audit.csv`):

| Group | Rows | FY2030-39 | FY2040 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fee and dividend accounting | CLI-2 (nets to zero in its own channel), CLI-2O, CX, CXP | $410 billion | $71.0 billion |
| Credits and industry | CLI-1, CLI-9 | $866 billion | $76.4 billion |
| Standards | CLI-3 (CLI-3E, 4E, 4H at zero) | $20 billion | $2.5 billion |
| Grid | CLI-10G (CLI-6 at zero) | $39 billion | $0 |
| Bills and add-ons | CLI-5, CLI-5L, CLI-5W | $224 billion | $1.7 billion |
| Adaptation | CLI-7, 7K, 7C, 7N | $196 billion | $9.8 billion |
| Farms and forests | CLI-13A, 13F | $55 billion | $3.5 billion |
| **Total** | | **$1,811 billion** | **$164.9 billion** |

Two things in this table are easy to miss. **The fee and dividend cost the general fund money**, about $0.41 trillion over ten years and $71 billion in FY2040, because the dividend returns 80% of the fee tax-free while the fee's offset against income and payroll taxes falls on the general fund (the convention Espen adopted, `DEC-2026-11-12-08`). And **the credits carry most of FY2040's cost**.

**The recommended set**, against the booking (project arithmetic; ranges summed line by line, so wide): about **+$0.11 trillion over FY2030-39 (range about −$0.06 to +$0.28 trillion) and about −$9 billion in FY2040 (range about −$20 to +$5 billion)**, from memo 1 B (about −$0.03 trillion; −$26 billion), memo 4 B (+$0.04-0.08 trillion of borrowing-eligible capital; about 0), memo 5 B (0; +$9-10 billion) and memo 7 B (+$0.08 trillion; +$6-10 billion). So the recommended set is about **$1.9 trillion over FY2030-39 and about $156 billion in FY2040**, about 3.6% of the plan-wide FY2040 general-fund shortfall of about $4,275 billion (`charter/PROGRESS.md`).

**What these figures leave out.**
- The credits' cost risk: the legacy model counts its credit set at about twice the booking [EV-CLP-710].
- The reinsurance facility's $75-150 billion contingent liability, disclosed, never scored [EV-CLP-718].
- Disaster appropriations outside CBO's baseline, about $15-25 billion a year, which are current-law exposure [EV-CLP-720].
- Any damages under the permitting package's permit-certainty section if it becomes law unamended [EV-CLP-255].
- Memo 1 B changes the O5 bridge's receipts (process emissions stay covered, so it is kept as booked); the booking step will rerun it.

**Is any of this mainly about money?** No memo here sets options that reach the same people at very different cost, except memo 2 part 1, where A costs roughly $0.2-0.35 trillion more than B for a little more pull on investment; that difference is stated in the memo.
