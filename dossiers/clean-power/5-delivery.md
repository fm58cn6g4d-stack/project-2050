# Clean, affordable power and a prepared country: delivery

Role: section 5 of the clean power dossier. It says who runs each provision of `4-specification.md`, through which existing agency, records and payment system, what capacity that needs, the timeline from enactment to full operation, how people get remedies, what states do, and how the plan reaches rural places. Status: draft (step E-C2, PR 1), at L2, on `DEC-2026-11-13-06` and `DEC-2026-11-13-07`. It applies Espen's rule to use existing machinery first and simplify where a system or design is more complex than its purpose needs (`DEC-2026-11-13-08`), alongside the design lens (`DEC-2026-11-09-13`). Dates are working choices unless the provision fixes them.

## 1. Who runs what, and through which existing system

Every job below runs through an agency, a set of records and a payment system that exist today. The column "Existing machinery" names it; "What is new" is what the act adds to it.

**What the act does not build.** No new federal agency, corporation or registry. Three choices in section 4 simplify the legacy design under `DEC-2026-11-13-08`, and each is a working choice the milestone review can test:
1. **The carbon price is charged on emissions EPA already measures** (REV-C1G.01), not upstream on fuel sales, which memo 1 B's narrower coverage would otherwise need: tracking which coal, gas and oil deliveries go to a covered facility would add a fuel-tracking system for every distributor.
2. **The standard uses the certificate systems the states built** (CLI-3A.01), not a federal registry.
3. **The builder of last resort is WAPA's existing program extended nationwide** (CLI-10.03), not a new federal transmission corporation. Memo 4 B described "a federal transmission authority, on TVA's model"; this delivers it through an agency that already lends for and partners on lines, with TVA and Bonneville building in their own regions. It changes the vehicle, not the decision. If the review or Espen prefers a separate entity, the provisions are unchanged except CLI-10.03's agency.

And two simplifications in how help reaches people:
4. **Bill protection is paid as part of the bill credit** (CLI-5L.01), so a household gets one credit, not two programs.
5. **The guarantee pays on records the employer already files** (CLI-11.01, .02) and on the worker's statement checked after the fact, with no caseworker review of each job (Espen: "my instinct would have led to unnecessary bureaucracy", `DEC-2026-11-13-06`).

## 2. Capacity

- **The IRS.** The dividend rides the child benefit's build (`dossiers/secure-floor/5-delivery.md`, part 2): a second payment type on the same file, quarterly. The carbon price adds a few thousand corporate excise filers who already file Form 720 for other excises or are large corporations under examination (judgment). The credits' systems ran in 2023-25 and need restarting, not building. The IRS's general rebuild is the legacy capacity line (CAP-7); the dividend's added running cost is small against the child benefit's $1.5-1.9 billion a year (judgment: under $0.2 billion a year) and is left to the booking step.
- **EPA.** It already measures the covered emissions. What it needs is staff the inventory and the reporting program lost after 2025 (`1-problem.md`, part C) and the rulemaking staff for the statute's deadlines. The legacy file prices the standard's administration and permitting staff at about $0.02 trillion, booked as CLI-3 [EV-CLP-724].
- **DOE.** The Loan Programs Office and the Grid Deployment Office carry the contracts, both tranches and the national-interest list. Their 2025 cancellations [EV-CLP-438] [EV-CLP-440] mean staff must be rebuilt. The second tranche's milestone gate (CLI-10.01) is also a capacity gate: money is not released faster than the offices can manage it.
- **WAPA.** Its transmission program manages $3.25 billion of borrowing today [EV-CLP-421]; it has mostly lent and partnered rather than built, so national reach as a builder needs construction staff and regional offices for the Eastern and Texas interconnections (judgment: a few hundred staff, paid from the lines' rates and the start-up inside CLI-10.01's range). The legacy direction warned that federal construction cannot assume regional workforce and delivery capacity (memo 4); the two-year filing window (CLI-10.03) means the builder acts only where no one else will, line by line.
- **States' LIHEAP offices.** The automatic credit triples the households served (memo 5: about 17% of eligible households today to about half [EV-CLP-038]) but removes the application interview for most of them. The added work is the yearly records match and the vendor payments, which states already run. Administration stays within LIHEAP's existing administrative allowance.
- **State unemployment agencies.** The guarantee's volume is small: about 8,000 covered layoffs a year (working assumption, `6-cost.md`, part 1). The agencies paid trade adjustment's wage insurance on the same model.
- **FEMA.** The formula floor (CLI-7K.01) is meant to cut the grant-writing work that kept small places out [EV-CLP-605] [EV-CLP-625]; FEMA's own staffing and independence are the legacy CLI-7 design, priced in CAP-7.

**If R2 is late.** The price and the first dividend move together: the price starts the first calendar quarter at least six months after enactment, and the first dividend is paid in the quarter before (working choice). The credits apply from the tax year of enactment.

**If the clean power and climate act is late or fails.** Nothing in R2 or R3 depends on it except bill protection's funding from the standard's payments (CLI-3A.02), which the transition share covers until the standard exists. Without the standard and the Clean Air Act finding, the credits' step-down (CLI-1A.02) would end subsidies without the rules that replace them; the trigger then reports it, and section 8 says what changes.

## 4. Remedies

- **The Climate Dividend.** The child benefit's notice and correction route (FAM-1.10): a person missing a payment confirms at a front door, and the correction takes effect the next quarter, with back payments. Appeal as for any IRS payment.
- **Covered facilities.** EPA's certified total can be disputed through EPA's existing reporting corrections; the tax is assessed and appealed as any excise.
- **The bill credit.** A household that should have been matched and was not applies to the state LIHEAP office as now, and gets the credit for that year. States' existing fair hearings apply.
- **The guarantee.** A worker left off the employer's file, or disputing an hourly figure, asks the state unemployment agency, which decides within 30 days (working choice) with the agency's existing appeal. An employer that fails to file the closure record pays the guarantee's cost for its workers (working choice: the WARN Act's own penalty structure).
- **Places.** A county disputing its tax-base figure takes it to the state, which certifies it; PILT's existing review applies.
- **Facilities and suppliers.** Court review of rules and orders as the Administrative Procedure Act and the Clean Air Act provide, within CLI-3A.04's shorter windows if case 2 applies.

## 5. States' roles

- **LIHEAP.** States run the automatic credit with their own benefit tables and vendor agreements, matching their own SNAP rolls and SSA's SSI data. No new state matching cost: the money is federal.
- **Weatherization.** As today, with heat pumps allowed.
- **The standard.** State standards stay; a supplier's credits count toward both. State commissions approve large-load tariffs (CLI-6A.03) and securitization bonds (CLI-11.07).
- **Codes.** Adopting current hazard-resistant codes within three years is the condition for the wind, wildfire and hail help and the reinsurance facility (CLI-8.01). It is a condition on new aid only.
- **Unemployment agencies.** Pay the guarantee as Labor's agents, as they paid trade adjustment.
- **Assessors.** Certify the lost tax base (CLI-11.06).

## 6. Rural delivery

Rural places are where the transition's costs and much of its building fall: coal counties, co-op service areas and wildfire country. Following the rural direction (`DEC-2026-11-09-26`; `dossiers/foundations/rural-delivery.md`, section 5), the plan looks first to USDA's local channels:
- **Rural co-ops' clean power.** USDA's Rural Utilities Service already lends most of what rural electric co-ops borrow, about $7 billion a year, at about zero or negative subsidy (`rural-delivery.md`, 4.3). The first grid tranche's loans (CLI-10G.01) and the credits' elective pay (CLI-1A.01) reach co-ops through RUS as well as DOE: a co-op applies to the lender it already uses (working choice). New ERA's obligated awards are paid out from day one (`rural-delivery.md`, 4.3).
- **The dividend's front door.** Rural Development offices are front doors for the dividend as for the child benefit (CLI-2A.02).
- **The bill credit.** Delivered fuels (propane, heating oil), common in rural homes, are paid through the same vendor agreements as utilities (CLI-5H.01).
- **Coal counties.** The guarantee's tax-base replacement (CLI-11.06) goes through PILT, which already pays most rural counties. The mitigation fund's formula floor (CLI-7K.01) reaches small and rural places without grant writers.
- **Wildfire.** The Forest Service runs the wildfire work (CLI-7K.01) through its local offices.

## 7. What delivery leaves open

- **The covered-facility count and the IRS's excise workload.** Studied in implementation; the 2027-28 check of the reporting program's coverage answers it.
- **WAPA's national reach.** Whether WAPA's program, extended, can build in the Eastern and Texas interconnections at the pace the list needs, or a separate entity is needed. The first two-year windows (about 2033) show it; it is a delivery question, not a change to the commitment.
- **How many households the automatic match reaches.** No study of automatic LIHEAP enrollment is in the register (memo 5); the first winter's counts answer it.
- **The guarantee's hourly reporting.** State wage records hold quarterly earnings, and only a few states record hours; the worker's monthly statement carries the hours. The published counts (CLI-11.05) show whether that works.
