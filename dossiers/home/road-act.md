# Current law: the ROAD to Housing Act (2026)

Role: the housing dossier's map of the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act, Public Law 119-101, the baseline every housing design choice now starts from. It lists the provisions that bear on the dossier, title by title, with what each does, when it takes effect and ends, whether money is attached, and which fork in `3-design.md` or agenda item it touches. Status: draft (stage C). Sections 2 and 3 link here; `3-design.md` says what the Act changes in each recommendation.

Sources: the enacted text as published by the Government Publishing Office (SRC-0438, grade A), and the Congressional Research Service's summaries of the enacted law (R49354), the Senate bill (R48732) and the House bill (R48849) (SRC-0437, 0435, 0436, grade C). Where a CRS summary and the text differ, the text governs. Dates follow from enactment on July 11, 2026.

## In brief

- **What it is.** H.R. 6644 became Public Law 119-101 without the President's signature on July 11, 2026 [EV-HOU-200]. It merges the Senate's ROAD to Housing Act of 2025 and the House's Housing for the 21st Century Act: 12 titles and 59 sections, passed 85-5 and 358-32 [EV-HOU-205] [EV-HOU-204].
- **No money.** It appropriates nothing, and its last section says no additional funds are authorized to carry it out [EV-HOU-201] [EV-HOU-202]. Its one dollar authorization is the Innovation Fund, $200 million a year for FY2027-2031 [EV-HOU-202] [EV-HOU-206]. Most new programs are discretionary for HUD, temporary and competitive [EV-HOU-203]. So the Act is mostly rules, permissions and empty program shells: it builds channels this plan can fill, but it pays for little itself.
- **Where it overlaps this dossier.**
  - Zoning rewards (HOU-6, fork 4): an Innovation Fund that pays localities for supply growth, and a CDBG reallocation ("Build Now") that moves money toward places whose building speeds up [EV-HOU-206] [EV-HOU-208].
  - The investor limit (HOU-7): a 15-year ban on large investors buying more single-family homes, with annual reporting and two rounds of GAO and HUD reports already built in [EV-HOU-222] [EV-HOU-224].
  - Public housing (HOU-5): the Rental Assistance Demonstration becomes permanent, with a higher cap [EV-HOU-231].
  - Vouchers (HOU-4): faster first inspections, including pre-inspection for landlords new to the program [EV-HOU-232].
  - Income rules: one narrow change to 42 U.S.C. 1437a, for disabled veterans in HUD-VASH [EV-HOU-233].
- **What it does not do.** It adds no rental assistance, no voucher awards and no emergency rent aid; it does not touch the Low-Income Housing Tax Credit, Housing First or treatment conditions; it adds no protection against landlords refusing vouchers, which federal law bars only in some assisted properties [EV-HOU-246]; it has nothing on voucher mobility or search help beyond lists of pre-inspected units [EV-HOU-232]; and it preempts no local zoning [EV-HOU-206] [EV-HOU-243]. (This is a reading of the full text and the CRS section-by-section summary [EV-HOU-205]; absence has no single anchor.)
- **HUD's load.** The legacy housing file said ROAD assigned "roughly 35 programs" to HUD with no new funding (`legacy/domains/domain-housing.md`, Dependencies). By this dossier's count from the table of contents, about 38 of the 59 sections give HUD a new program, pilot, rule, report or duty; the rest fall to USDA, Treasury, the bank regulators, FHFA, CFPB, GAO and the Interagency Council on Homelessness. The legacy figure holds as an order of magnitude. Neither count is evidence of workload; HUD capacity stays a section 5 question.

## The provisions, title by title

"Money" says whether funds are attached. "Touches" names the fork in `3-design.md` or the agenda item. Unless noted, a provision takes effect on enactment and has no end date. Sections that do not bear on the dossier are listed at the end.
