# Memo 5: Housing First

Role: decision memo for Espen, fork 5 of the housing design (`../3-design.md`). Status: awaiting Espen's decision. Nothing changes until Espen decides.

## The question

"Housing First" gives a homeless person a home without first requiring treatment or sobriety. Services are offered, not required. A July 2025 executive order turns federal policy against it and pushes treatment as a condition of help [EV-HOU-107]. What should the plan do?

## Why it matters

Chronic homelessness among single adults reached 155,750 in 2025, the highest on record [EV-HOU-016]. Many of these people have a disability, mental illness or addiction.

## What the evidence shows

- **Housing First keeps people housed.** In Canada's trial, people were stably housed 71% of the time, against 29% with usual services [EV-HOU-099]. A federal health agency review found homelessness down 88% compared with treatment-first programs [EV-HOU-101].
- **It is not an addiction treatment.** Effects on drug use are unclear [EV-HOU-100], and deaths did not differ [EV-HOU-103]. There is little evidence for people with active, severe addiction [EV-HOU-105].
- **In the U.S. its benefits exceeded its costs** in the studies reviewed [EV-HOU-102].

## The options

**A. Housing First as the federal rule.**
- *For people:* for example, a veteran sleeping outside gets an apartment this month, with a caseworker who offers help with drinking but doesn't require it.
- *Risk:* people who want a sober setting may not find one. Opponents will say it ignores addiction.

**B. Housing First, plus a recovery-housing choice (recommended).**
- As A, plus funded sober housing for people who want it. It is never a condition of help.
- The plan measures results for people with active addiction, the group the trials missed.
- *For people:* the same veteran gets an apartment. Someone else leaving rehab can choose a sober building instead.
- *Cost:* some added money for recovery housing and evaluation. Section 6 sizes it.

**C. Treatment as a condition (the 2025 order).**
- *For people:* people who won't or can't stay in treatment lose help, and many return to the street.
- *Risk:* the trials show treatment-first programs house fewer people [EV-HOU-101].

## Recommendation

**B.** The housing evidence is strong and consistent. The evidence does not support making treatment a condition, and it does not show Housing First solves addiction. B houses people first, respects those who want recovery housing, and fills the evidence gap.

## If you decide

Section 4 writes the rule and the recovery option. The care flagship carries the treatment capacity it depends on.
