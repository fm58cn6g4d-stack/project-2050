# Memo 5: Housing First

Role: decision memo for Espen, fork 5 of the housing design (`../3-design.md`). Status: awaiting Espen's decision. Nothing changes until Espen decides. Sources are named in plain text; the bracketed tags point to the evidence register (`../evidence.json`).

## The question

"Housing First" gives a homeless person a home without first requiring treatment or sobriety. Services are offered, not required. A July 2025 executive order turns federal policy against it and pushes treatment as a condition of help (White House executive order, July 2025) [EV-HOU-107]. What should the plan do?

**Current law.** The ROAD to Housing Act (2026) does not change this memo's options or recommendation. It neither writes the 2025 order into law nor reverses it. It does require a new federal plan to end homelessness by July 2027 (Congressional Research Service, 2026) [EV-HOU-238], which this dossier expects to follow the order; that is judgment. And for 2027-2030 it lets cities put more of one homelessness grant into shelter and outreach, but not if they clear people out without offering shelter or housing (ROAD to Housing Act, 2026) [EV-HOU-237].

## Why it matters

Chronic homelessness among single adults reached 155,750 in 2025, the highest on record (Congressional Research Service, homelessness overview, 2026) [EV-HOU-016]. These are people homeless for at least a year, or four times adding up to a year over three years (same source) [EV-HOU-016].

## What the evidence shows

- **Housing First keeps people housed.**
  - In Canada's trial, people were stably housed 71% of the time, against 29% with usual services (Aubry and others, At Home/Chez Soi trial, 2016) [EV-HOU-099].
  - A review for the federal Community Guide found homelessness down 88% compared with treatment-first programs (Peng and others, 2020) [EV-HOU-101].
- **It is not an addiction treatment.**
  - Effects on drug use are unclear (Baxter and others, University of Glasgow review, 2019) [EV-HOU-100].
  - Deaths did not differ (Lachaud and others, 2025) [EV-HOU-103].
  - There is little evidence for people with active, severe addiction (Kertesz and others, 2009) [EV-HOU-105].
- **In the U.S. its benefits exceeded its costs** in the studies reviewed (Jacob and others, Community Guide economic review, 2022) [EV-HOU-102].
- **Across whole communities the picture is weaker and mixed.**
  - More supportive housing went with somewhat less chronic homelessness (Byrne and others, 2014) [EV-HOU-175].
  - Each extra voucher for homeless veterans meant one fewer homeless veteran (Evans and others, 2019) [EV-HOU-176].
  - But in another study the link faded after the first year (Corinth, American Enterprise Institute, 2015) [EV-HOU-083].
  - A critic argues Housing First has not been shown to end homelessness community-wide (Eide, Manhattan Institute, 2020) [EV-HOU-106].
  - The trials show it houses the people it serves; whether it shrinks homelessness overall is less certain.

## The options

**A. Housing First as the federal rule.**
- *For people:* for example, a veteran sleeping outside gets an apartment this month, with a caseworker who offers help with drinking but doesn't require it. People housed this way stay housed far more often (Aubry and others, 2016) [EV-HOU-099].
- *Risk:* people who want a sober setting may not find one. Opponents will say it ignores addiction, and the trials do not show it reduces drug use (Baxter and others, 2019) [EV-HOU-100].

**B. Housing First, plus a recovery-housing choice (recommended).**
- As A, plus funded sober housing for people who want it. It is never a condition of help.
- The plan measures results for people with active addiction, the group the trials missed (Kertesz and others, 2009) [EV-HOU-105].
- *For people:* the same veteran gets an apartment. Someone else leaving rehab can choose a sober building instead.
- *Cost:* some added money for recovery housing and evaluation. Section 6 sizes it.
- *What the evidence doesn't show yet:* there is no evaluation of recovery housing in the register yet. That part rests on judgment, and the evaluation is part of the design.

**C. Treatment as a condition (the 2025 order).**
- *For people:* people who won't or can't stay in treatment can lose their help.
- *Risk:* the trials show treatment-first programs house fewer people (Peng and others, 2020) [EV-HOU-101].

## Recommendation

**B.** It rests on three findings:
1. The housing evidence is strong and consistent across trials and reviews (Aubry and others, 2016; Peng and others, 2020) [EV-HOU-099] [EV-HOU-101].
2. The evidence does not show Housing First solves addiction, and it is thin for people with active addiction (Baxter and others, 2019; Kertesz and others, 2009) [EV-HOU-100] [EV-HOU-105].
3. In the U.S. its benefits exceeded its costs (Jacob and others, 2022) [EV-HOU-102].

B houses people first, respects those who want recovery housing, and fills the evidence gap. The recovery-housing part rests on judgment.
