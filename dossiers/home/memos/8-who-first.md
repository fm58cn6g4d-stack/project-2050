# Memo 8: Who the guarantee reaches first

Role: decision memo for Espen, fork 8 of the housing design (`../3-design.md`), section 1's open question. Status: awaiting Espen's decision. Nothing changes until Espen decides. Sources are named in plain text; the bracketed tags point to the evidence register (`../evidence.json`).

**This memo also decides the pace of the million new vouchers (HOU-4A) and which group the $135 billion envelope (HOU-4D) reaches.** On October 30 you made both working choices rather than settled ones (`DEC-2026-10-30-02`). The recommendation keeps HOU-4A's pace as filed, 250,000 a year from 2030 to 2033, and, with memo 2's recommendation, points HOU-4D at the second group in line.

## The question

The new help phases in at 250,000 vouchers a year from 2030 to 2033, then continues under memo 2. Far more households need it than that. Who should be first in line?

**Pace.** A faster start would reach people without a home sooner, but even now only about three in five new voucher holders find a home within six months (Ellen, O'Regan and Strochak, Institute for Research on Poverty, 2024) [EV-HOU-092]. Vouchers that cannot be used help no one, so this memo keeps the plan's pace; that is this dossier's judgment from that evidence.

**Current law.** The ROAD to Housing Act (2026) does not change this memo's options or recommendation. It sets no order for who gets vouchers. It makes disabled veterans easier to qualify for the veterans' voucher program (ROAD to Housing Act, 2026) [EV-HOU-233], which helps reach homeless veterans.

## Who is waiting

- **People without a home:** 745,652 on a single night in 2025 (HUD count, reported by the Congressional Research Service, 2026) [EV-HOU-014]. That included nearly 150,000 children in 2024 (HUD, 2024 homelessness report to Congress) [EV-HOU-026].
- **The poorest renters with severe problems** (no help, paying over half their income in rent, or living in very bad conditions) (HUD, Worst Case Housing Needs report, 2025) [EV-HOU-186]:
  - 1.7 million families with children, with 3.3 million children in all;
  - 1.7 million older adults;
  - 1.8 million other households, mostly single individuals.
- **People with disabilities:** 1.16 million households with severe needs include someone under 62 with a disability (HUD, Worst Case Housing Needs report, 2025) [EV-HOU-032].
- **Everyone paying over 30% of income for rent:** over 21 million households (Census Bureau, American Community Survey, 2023 data) [EV-HOU-001].

## The options

**A. People without a home first.**
- *For people:* for example, a family living in a shelter gets a voucher in 2030 instead of waiting years.
- *Evidence:* the strongest trials are for this group.
  - Vouchers sharply reduced homelessness for homeless families (Family Options Study, Gubits and others, 2018) [EV-HOU-086].
  - Housing First kept homeless adults with mental illness housed (Aubry and others, Canada's At Home/Chez Soi trial, 2016) [EV-HOU-099].
- *Precedent:* Congress has targeted vouchers this way before: 70,000 Emergency Housing Vouchers for people homeless or at risk (Congressional Research Service, voucher program overview, 2023) [EV-HOU-188].

**B. Families with children first.**
- *For people:* a single father of two paying most of his income in rent gets help early.
- *Evidence:*
  - Moving children to lower-poverty areas young raised their adult earnings (Chetty, Hendren and Katz, Moving to Opportunity study, National Bureau of Economic Research, 2015) [EV-HOU-090].
  - People in families with children had the largest one-year rise in homelessness in 2024, 39% (HUD, 2024 homelessness report) [EV-HOU-026].

**C. People about to lose their home first.**
- *For people:* renters facing eviction get long-term help.
- *Evidence:* many of them may need only short help, not a long-term voucher.
  - In one trial, about $2,000 of one-time aid made households 81% less likely to become homeless within six months (MIT Press Reader, 2023, on Phillips and Sullivan's Santa Clara County trial) [EV-HOU-109].
  - Memo 6's fund serves them better.

**D. No priority: everyone eligible by need score or lottery.**
- *For people:* fair in form, but with 5.8 million households in severe need (HUD, 2025) [EV-HOU-186], most homeless families would wait for years. That last part is this dossier's arithmetic: 250,000 vouchers a year against 5.8 million households.

## Recommendation

**A and B together, in this order:**
1. People without a home, families with children first among them.
2. The poorest families with children with severe problems.
3. Other households among the poorest renters with severe problems, including older and disabled renters.

It rests on three findings:
1. The strongest trials are for people without a home (Family Options Study, 2018; Aubry and others, 2016) [EV-HOU-086] [EV-HOU-099].
2. Children gain most from early moves (Chetty, Hendren and Katz, 2015) [EV-HOU-090].
3. One-time help serves renters at imminent risk: about $2,000 once cut homelessness sharply in one trial (MIT Press Reader, 2023, on Phillips and Sullivan's trial) [EV-HOU-109]. That compares with about $15,200 a year for a voucher, the plan's planning figure (`legacy/domains/domain-housing.md`, HOU-4A); the comparison is this dossier's.

Under memo 2's recommendation, the million vouchers cover step 1 and the start of step 2; the $135 billion then enrolls the rest of step 2, the poorest families with children with severe problems, at up to 300,000 a year from 2034, so that every family in the group can get help from 2037. Step 3 follows only when the budget allows: under memo 2's recommendation it has no funded route in the 2030s, a change from the earlier draft, which phased all three steps in.

Renters about to lose their homes get help from memo 6's emergency fund, not this line. If you keep a one-time package instead (memo 6, A or B), they would have no national help after 2031; then this order should set aside a small share of vouchers for them.

Local housing agencies can keep local preferences under this order, but not rules based on how long someone has lived in the area (HUD voucher rules, 24 CFR 982.207) [EV-HOU-189].
