# Memo 8: Who the guarantee reaches first

Role: decision memo for Espen, fork 8 of the housing design (`../3-design.md`), section 1's open question. Status: awaiting Espen's decision. Nothing changes until Espen decides.

## The question

The new help phases in at 250,000 vouchers a year from 2030 to 2033. Far more households need it than that. Who should be first in line?

## Who is waiting

- **People without a home:** 745,652 on a single night in 2025 [EV-HOU-014], including nearly 150,000 children in 2024 [EV-HOU-026].
- **The poorest renters with severe problems** (no help, paying over half their income in rent, or living in very bad conditions) [EV-HOU-186]:
  - 1.7 million families with children, with 3.3 million children in all;
  - 1.7 million older adults;
  - 1.8 million other households, mostly people living alone.
- **People with disabilities:** 1.16 million households with severe needs include someone under 62 with a disability [EV-HOU-032].
- **Everyone paying over 30% of income for rent:** over 21 million households [EV-HOU-001].

## The options

**A. People without a home first.**
- *For people:* for example, a family living in a shelter gets a voucher in 2030 instead of waiting years.
- *Evidence:* the strongest trials are for this group. Vouchers sharply reduced homelessness for homeless families [EV-HOU-086].

**B. Families with children first.**
- *For people:* a single father of two paying most of his income in rent gets help early.
- *Evidence:* moving children to lower-poverty areas young raised their adult earnings [EV-HOU-090]. People in families with children had the largest one-year rise in homelessness in 2024, 39% [EV-HOU-026].

**C. People about to lose their home first.**
- *For people:* renters facing eviction get long-term help.
- *Evidence:* many of them may need only short help, not a long-term voucher. In one trial, about $2,000 of one-time aid made households 81% less likely to become homeless within six months [EV-HOU-109]. Memo 6's fund serves them better.

**D. No priority: everyone eligible by need score or lottery.**
- *For people:* fair in form, but with 5.8 million households in severe need [EV-HOU-186], most homeless families would wait for years.

## Recommendation

**A and B together, in this order:**
1. People without a home, families with children first among them.
2. The poorest families with children with severe problems.
3. Other households among the poorest renters with severe problems, including older and disabled renters.

Renters about to lose their homes get help from memo 6's emergency fund, not this line. Local housing agencies can keep local preferences under this order, but not rules based on how long someone has lived in the area [EV-HOU-189].
