# Memo 6: Emergency rent help

Role: decision memo for Espen, fork 6 of the housing design (`../3-design.md`). Status: awaiting Espen's decision. Nothing changes until Espen decides.

## The question

The plan has an $85 billion one-time emergency housing package for 2030-31 (HOU-1A). Should emergency help be a one-time package, or a standing fund that is always there when a renter hits a crisis?

## Why it matters

Small, fast help keeps people housed. In Chicago, people who called a help line when money happened to be available were 76% less likely to enter a shelter [EV-HOU-108]. In a California trial, about $2,000 of aid made households 81% less likely to become homeless within six months [EV-HOU-109]. In 2025, 23% of renters fell behind on rent at some point [EV-HOU-008].

## The options

**A. One-time $85 billion (as now planned).**
- Rent help plus money to fill gaps in building projects, spent in 2030-31.
- *For people:* a big wave of help in two years, then nothing.
- *Risk:* the last national program spent only about 6% of its first round in its first months [EV-HOU-150]. Rents have cooled since [EV-HOU-068], so the need in 2030 is uncertain. It never measured how much money went to the wrong people [EV-HOU-154] [EV-HOU-182].

**B. One-time $40 billion, rent help only.**
- *For people:* the same wave, smaller.
- *Risk:* the same start-up problems, at lower cost.

**C. A standing emergency-rent fund, plus a smaller one-time start (recommended).**
- A permanent fund gives one-time help to renters about to lose their homes: a month or two of rent, a deposit, legal help.
- Simple rules: renters can state their own income and hardship; landlords can apply; payment errors are measured from the first year.
- The building-gap money moves into the construction funds you already chose.
- *For people:* for example, a cook whose hours are cut in a recession in 2034 can get back rent paid before an eviction, not only someone who happened to struggle in 2030.
- *Cost or risk:* permanent spending, so it must be paid for from 2040 under the fiscal rule. It is likely small next to rental help; section 6 sizes it. It is a new option, not yet in the agenda.

## Recommendation

**C.** Prevention works and is cheap per family helped. A standing fund is there when need rises, and it avoids rebuilding a program from scratch each crisis. The evidence on the last national program is split: one evaluation credits it with fewer evictions [EV-HOU-152], while lottery studies found it helped people pay rent but did little to keep them from moving [EV-HOU-153]. That argues for building and testing a lasting program carefully.

## If you decide

Section 4 writes the fund and its delivery rules; section 6 sizes it. A right to a lawyer in eviction court stays a state and local choice the fund can pay for; the dossier does not propose a federal mandate (`../3-design.md`, "Resolved here without a memo", point 4).
