# Memo 4: Participation help as an on-ramp into the jobs the plan creates

Role: decision memo for Espen, fork 6 of the secure floor (`../README.md`, "Forks for S3"; `../3-design.md`). It carries out your November 11 direction that the floor be an on-ramp into care, infrastructure construction, trades, housing production and clean power. Status: **decided** (`DEC-2026-11-12-06`): **B**, as recommended, inside the $100 billion; point 10 as a proposal. Specified in `../4-specification.md` (LAB-10.01 to 10.05). Sources are named in plain text; bracketed tags point to `../evidence.json`. Costs marked "judgment" are working assumptions.

**Decision line.** Recommended: **B, paid on-ramps: participation support (LAB-10) recast as paid training into the plan's own jobs, with a training stipend, child care and transport, built with employers on the projects the plan pays for, inside the $100 billion envelope.** Cost: **$100 billion over FY2030-39, about $11-12 billion in FY2040**, enough for roughly 400,000 to 900,000 people over the decade (project arithmetic on program costs). Evidence: randomized trials of sector training; a matched study of apprenticeship.

**This memo decides what LAB-10 buys and how it connects people to work.** Unemployment insurance, the minimum wage, apprenticeship law (LAB-8) and national service stay with the work flagship.

## The question

You approved LAB-10 on October 26 as a $100 billion envelope over FY2030-39, led by the Labor Department through existing institutions, paying for transport, child care during training, accommodations, navigators and paid placements, "an offer, not a work condition" (`legacy/domains/domain-labor.md`, LAB-10) [EV-SEC-698]. You also said that a decent life should not depend on being able to work (governing decisions, October 26) [EV-SEC-678]. The envelope has no design. The question is what it should buy.

## Why it matters

- **The plan creates the jobs.** Care at a $20 floor (`DEC-2026-11-11-04`), housing production and repair (`DEC-2026-11-09-21`), transportation's repair and rail (`DEC-2026-11-10-08`) and the clean power buildout. Health care and social assistance add the most jobs of any sector to 2035, over 2.2 million (BLS projections) [EV-SEC-133]; an industry group puts construction's need at 349,000 new workers in 2026 (Associated Builders and Contractors, uncorroborated) [EV-SEC-145].
- **Low-income parents cannot train without income and child care** (Urban Institute, 2016) [EV-SEC-149].
- **Today's system is small.** WIOA's adult and dislocated-worker programs have about $2.3 billion a year (DOL, program year 2026; the dislocated-worker line includes the national reserve) [EV-SEC-638].
- **Child care is already solved for most trainees.** Care's decided guarantee makes child care free at or below 85% of state median income with no work or study test (`DEC-2026-11-11-03`), so LAB-10 needs to pay only for hours and settings the guarantee misses.

## What the evidence says, in five lines

1. **Training built with employers for specific jobs works** (randomized trials pooled): earnings gains of 11 to 40 percent that last, for Year Up, Project QUEST, WorkAdvance and Per Scholas (Katz, Roth, Hendra and Schaberg) [EV-SEC-143].
2. **Registered apprenticeship pays** (matched comparison): $5,839 more in the ninth year (Mathematica for DOL) [EV-SEC-144].
3. **It costs real money per person** (program evaluations): Project QUEST about $10,501 a participant (Elliott and Roder, 2017) [EV-SEC-636], WorkAdvance $6,400-8,300 (MDRC) [EV-SEC-637], Year Up $27,158 including $6,885 of stipends (HHS's Office of Planning, Research and Evaluation, 2022) [EV-SEC-635], the apprenticeship grants about $5,171 per added apprentice (DOL evaluation brief, 2022) [EV-SEC-640].
4. **Stipends help people finish, but no trial isolates them** (evaluation and review): Year Up paid up to $8,870 and 75 percent completed [EV-SEC-644]; a review of 11 studies finds incentives raise participation and completion more than earnings (What Works Centre for Local Economic Growth) [EV-SEC-645].
5. **Pre-apprenticeship with support brings new people into the trades** (peer-reviewed, not randomized): Oregon's highway program found child care and other supports raised completion, and more women entered (Kelly and others) [EV-SEC-643].

## Options

**A. The legacy envelope as written.**
- **What it does.** $100 billion through American Job Centers for navigators, transport, accommodations, child care during training and paid placements, offered to anyone who wants it [EV-SEC-698].
- *For people:* a parent can get help, but the help is not tied to any particular job.
- *Cost:* $100 billion over FY2030-39.
- *Evidence:* the general job-search and counseling services the envelope resembles have weaker evidence than sector training [EV-SEC-143].
- *Risk:* the money spreads thin across many services, and no placement count can be read from it [EV-SEC-698].

**B. Paid on-ramps into the plan's jobs (recommended).**
- **What it does.**
  - *Training tied to jobs the plan pays for.* Sector partnerships with employers in care, construction and repair, rail and transit, housing production and clean power, in the places the plan invests. Pre-apprenticeship leading into registered apprenticeship (LAB-8, the work flagship's).
  - *A training stipend,* up to about $9,000 a year, as Year Up pays [EV-SEC-644], so a parent can afford to train. It does not count against SNAP or rent (memo 7, part 1).
  - *Child care for the hours the guarantee misses* (evenings, weekends, before a slot opens) and *transport* to training and the first months of work.
  - *Places on the projects.* A share of labor hours on plan-funded construction goes to apprentices, as the Inflation Reduction Act's clean-energy credits require 15% for projects begun from 2024 (IRS guidance) [EV-SEC-641] [EV-SEC-642]. This is proposed to housing, transportation and clean power (point 10).
  - *An offer, never a condition.* No benefit depends on taking part [EV-SEC-698].
- *For people:* a single father on SNAP in a county getting a rail repair contract enters a ten-week pre-apprenticeship with a stipend and evening child care, then an apprenticeship on the project.
- *Cost:* $100 billion over FY2030-39 (the envelope). At about $11,000-25,000 a person for training, stipend and supports (judgment, from the program costs above), that serves roughly 400,000 to 900,000 people over the decade, about 40,000-90,000 a year (project arithmetic). About $11-12 billion in FY2040 if kept at the envelope's run rate (judgment).
- *Evidence strength:* randomized trials for the training model; matched comparison for apprenticeship; no trial of the whole package.
- *Political risk:* low. Apprenticeship and sector training have bipartisan support, and the project-hours share already exists in law for clean energy [EV-SEC-641].
- *Burden:* an application to a program, by choice.

**C. A right to paid training for anyone.**
- **What it does.** Every adult who enrolls in approved training gets income support for its length, as trade adjustment assistance pays displaced workers.
- *Cost:* about $250-300 billion over FY2030-39 (judgment: several hundred thousand people a year at a stipend and training cost).
- *Evidence:* trade assistance's evidence is split, with one study finding $50,000 more over ten years (Hyman) [EV-SEC-148] and another finding nothing or less (Mathematica for DOL) [EV-SEC-147].
- *Why not now:* it is three times the envelope, its demand is unknown, and much of it would duplicate AI-4's transition support (`agenda/ai`) [EV-SEC-129].

**D. Leave it to the work flagship.**
- **What it does.** The secure floor guarantees only that no benefit carries a work condition; training is designed with apprenticeships and unemployment insurance in flagship 5.
- *Why not:* the work flagship has not started, and the plan's care and construction jobs begin hiring in 2030-31. The floor's families are the ones who need the stipend and child care.

## Recommendation: B

- **It spends the envelope where the evidence is strongest:** training built with employers for specific jobs, which randomized trials show raises earnings for years [EV-SEC-143].
- **It connects the floor to the plan's own investments,** as your direction asked: the care, construction, rail, housing and clean power jobs, in the places they are built.
- **It removes the two barriers parents name:** income and child care while training [EV-SEC-149]. Care's guarantee already pays most child care; LAB-10 fills the hours it misses.
- **It keeps your principle:** an offer, never a condition.

**What would change this:** demand far above the envelope in the first years (toward a larger amount, reported to the financing settlement); the work flagship designing a training right (toward C, run there).

## If you decide

- **Under B:** section 4 writes LAB-10 as a program in the 2029 reconciliation bill, at $100 billion over FY2030-39, run by the Labor Department through state workforce boards and WIOA's centers. The project-hours share (point 10) goes to the housing, transportation and clean power dossiers as a proposal. The stipend is excluded from other programs' income (memo 7, part 1).
- **Seams:** LAB-8's apprenticeship slots and AI-4's training accounts are coordinated, so no one is paid twice [EV-SEC-698].
