# Memo 3: Income support beyond the child benefit: the earned income credit, SNAP and SSI

Role: decision memo for Espen, forks 4 and 5 of the secure floor (`../README.md`, "Forks for S3"; `../3-design.md`). Status: draft for decision (board row S3); nothing here is decided. Sources are named in plain text; bracketed tags point to `../evidence.json`. Costs marked "judgment" are working assumptions. Unemployment insurance and the minimum wage stay with the work flagship.

**Decision line.** Recommended: **part 1 B, a larger earned income credit for workers without children (the 2021 design, permanent), with the credit for families kept; part 2 B, restore SNAP's 2025 cuts and raise and index SSI's asset limits.** Cost: part 1 about **$0.15 trillion over FY2030-39, about $16 billion in FY2040**; part 2 about **$0.21-0.26 trillion over FY2030-39, about $27-33 billion in FY2040** (judgment, on CBO's and SSA's actuaries' scores). Evidence: randomized trials (split) for part 1; CBO's scores and the program record for part 2.

**This memo decides what the flagship carries beyond the child benefit.** None of these programs has a legacy design (`../legacy-research.md`, 5), so any item you choose becomes a new agenda item at L1.

## The question

The floor a family stands on in 2029 is more than the child credit: the earned income credit, SNAP and SSI make it up for millions of families. The 2025 law cut SNAP and left the earned income credit and SSI as they were (section 1, part B). Two questions:
1. **The earned income credit.** Leave it, enlarge the small credit for workers without children, or fold part of it into the child benefit, as Romney's Family Security Act would?
2. **SNAP and SSI.** Does the flagship undo the 2025 SNAP cuts, and does it fix SSI's limits, or does it leave them as they are?

## Why it matters

- **The credit for workers without children is tiny.** At most $664 in 2026, gone by $19,540, and only from age 25 to 64 (IRS) [EV-SEC-026] [EV-SEC-027].
- **SNAP is shrinking.** The 2025 law cuts it by almost $187 billion over ten years (CBO, via CRS) [EV-SEC-035]; its work rules alone remove about 2.4 million people in an average month (CBO, 2025) [EV-SEC-036]. Urban's model of the Senate bill found 22.3 million families losing some or all benefits [EV-SEC-633], and of the law as a whole, 3.4 million more people in deep poverty, 0.8 million of them children [EV-SEC-634].
- **SSI's asset limit has not moved since 1989**: $2,000, or $3,000 for a couple (SSA) [EV-SEC-042], and its income exclusions are not indexed [EV-SEC-043].
- **Each program already works.** In 2025 the earned income credit moved about 2.1 million children out of poverty and SNAP about 1.2 million (Census, 2026) [EV-SEC-024].

## What the evidence says, in five lines

1. **The earned income credit cuts poverty and helps children** (quasi-experimental, well established): $1,000 more cuts single-mother poverty by 8.4 points (Hoynes and Patel) [EV-SEC-249] and low birth weight by 2-3% (Hoynes, Miller and Simon) [EV-SEC-250].
2. **Its work effect is now contested** (both sides, section 2, part A.6): Schanzenbach and Strain find 3.0-3.3 points more employment per $1,000 [EV-SEC-253]; Kleven finds no robust effect outside 1993 [EV-SEC-252].
3. **A larger childless credit raised work in one trial and not in the other** (randomized): Paycheck Plus raised employment 2.6 points in New York [EV-SEC-254] and had no effect in Atlanta [EV-SEC-255].
4. **Work rules in benefits mostly remove eligible people** (quasi-experimental): Arkansas's Medicaid work requirement cut coverage with no gain in employment (Sommers and others, 2020) [EV-CAR-212].
5. **Food aid in childhood pays off for decades** (quasi-experimental): children in counties that got food stamps early were healthier as adults (Hoynes, Schanzenbach and Almond) [EV-SEC-232].

## Part 1. The earned income credit

**A. Leave it as it is.**
- *Cost:* none.
- *Consequence:* a 22-year-old warehouse worker earning $15,000 gets nothing, because the credit starts at 25 [EV-SEC-027]; a 30-year-old earning the same gets about $350 (project arithmetic on the 2026 parameters [EV-SEC-026]). The credit's 32.7% improper payment rate, mostly from confusion over which child qualifies, stays (IRS, 2025) [EV-SEC-258].

**B. Make the 2021 childless expansion permanent (recommended).**
- **What it does.** The credit for workers without children rises to about $1,500 (from $664), phases in and out at 15.3%, and starts at 19 rather than 25 and continues past 65 [EV-SEC-256]. The credit for families is unchanged.
- *Where tried:* 2021, when 4.3 million young workers got almost $900 on average (Tax Policy Center) [EV-SEC-257]; Paycheck Plus [EV-SEC-254] [EV-SEC-255].
- *For people:* both workers get about $1,000 (project arithmetic on the 2021 rates [EV-SEC-256]).
- *Cost:* about $0.15 trillion over FY2030-39 and about $16 billion in FY2040 (judgment).
  - *Benchmarks:* JCT put a permanent extension at $135 billion over FY2022-31 (via CRS, 2021) [EV-SEC-609]; the Tax Policy Center at $135.4 billion over FY2025-34, $14.0 billion in FY2026 [EV-SEC-610]. Grown at about 2% a year to the 2030s window, about $0.15 trillion (project arithmetic).
- *Evidence strength:* two randomized trials, split on work; well established on income.
- *Political risk:* low. A larger childless credit has had bipartisan support, and the 2021 law enacted it for a year [EV-SEC-256].

**C. Fold part of the credit into the child benefit (the Family Security Act's route).**
- **What it does.** The child-related part of the earned income credit is cut and its money moves into a child benefit, as Romney's plans did [EV-SEC-273] [EV-SEC-275].
- *Cost:* saves money against B.
- *Consequence:* the Tax Policy Center and Urban put Family Security Act 2.0 at 14% to 12.4% child poverty, against more for the 2021 credit [EV-SEC-615]; Niskanen found 1.1 million children lifted [EV-SEC-614]. With memo 1 A, families with children would gain less than the child benefit alone gives them, and the credit's proven effects on children would shrink.

**D. B, and separate the worker credit from the child credit.**
- **What it does.** A worker credit by earnings alone, and the child parts moved into the child benefit with no loss to any family.
- *Why not now:* it rewrites the main credit for working families, and the cost and losers cannot be shown without the household model. B, with point 9's simpler qualifying-child rules, gets most of the error reduction (section 3).

## Part 2. SNAP and SSI

| Option | What it does | FY2030-39 | FY2040 | Recommended |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. Carry none | Map SNAP and SSI, design nothing (the README's default) | 0 | 0 | |
| **B. Restore SNAP; fix SSI's limits** | Repeal the 2025 law's SNAP work-rule expansion, state benefit cost share and Thrifty Food Plan freeze; raise SSI's asset limits to $10,000 and $20,000, indexed (the SSI Savings Penalty Elimination Act's figures, which care's part B also uses [EV-CAR-478]), and index the $20 and $65 exclusions (point 8) | about $0.21-0.26 trillion | about $27-33 billion | **Yes** |
| C. B, and SSI at the poverty line | B, and the SSI benefit raised to 100% of the poverty line, as the SSI Restoration Act proposes | about $0.9-1.1 trillion | about $110-120 billion | |

- **The basis (judgment, on outside scores).**
  - *SNAP:* CBO scores the nutrition subtitle at almost $187 billion of savings over FY2025-34 [EV-SEC-035]. The cuts are fully in force by 2030, so restoring them costs about $0.2-0.25 trillion over FY2030-39 (project arithmetic, growing the later years of CBO's window). The administrative cost shift to states [EV-SEC-031] is restored too.
  - *SSI's asset limits:* SSA's actuaries put $10,000 and $20,000, indexed, at $8.9 billion over 2025-34 [EV-SEC-630]; about $0.01 trillion in the 2030s window.
  - *SSI at the poverty line:* the Roosevelt Institute's microsimulation puts the SSI Restoration Act at about $61 billion a year in 2024 dollars [EV-SEC-631], adding 2.58 million recipient households and cutting SSI household poverty by 17.46 points [EV-SEC-632]. Roosevelt is a progressive think tank; SSA's own letter on the bill is not yet in the register.
- *For people:* under B, a 55-year-old in a rural county without jobs keeps SNAP; a disabled woman can save $9,000 for a car without losing SSI. Under C, an SSI recipient's $994 a month [EV-SEC-041] rises to about $1,330 (the 2026 guideline of $15,960 over twelve [EV-SEC-532]; project arithmetic).
- *Political risk:* B reverses part of a 2025 law, which the reconciliation bill can do; the SNAP pieces are spending. C is a large new cost.

**Why B over C.** B undoes cuts the evidence says remove eligible people and fixes a limit frozen since 1989. C is the bigger poverty gain for disabled and older people but costs four times as much; with the plan's Social Security minimum benefit (memo 6) and care's home benefit, part of SSI's population gains anyway. C is a strong candidate for a later step once the financing settlement knows its room.

## Recommendation: part 1 B; part 2 B

- **Part 1 B gives low-paid workers without children a real credit** at modest cost, with one trial showing more work and one showing no harm, and a 2021 record of reaching young workers [EV-SEC-257]. C would take from the families memo 1 is helping.
- **Part 2 B undoes cuts that remove eligible people** and raises an asset limit that punishes saving. It costs about an eighth of memo 1 A.
- **What it costs in burden:** none new; B removes SNAP's added work-rule paperwork.

**What would change this:** a financing settlement with room for more (part 2 C); evidence that the 2025 SNAP time limit raised work without removing eligible people (toward keeping it); the household model showing part 1 D can be done without losers (toward D).

## If you decide

- **Under part 1 B:** section 4 writes the childless credit into the 2029 reconciliation bill; a new agenda item at L1.
- **Under part 2 B:** the SNAP repeal in the same bill (the agriculture committees' title), and the SSI changes there too. New agenda items at L1. Care's part B uses the same asset-limit figures [EV-CAR-478], so the two stay consistent.
- **Under A:** section 1's map stands; nothing is added.
