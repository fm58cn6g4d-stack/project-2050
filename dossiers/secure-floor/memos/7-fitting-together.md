# Memo 7: How the floor fits together: what counts as income and how fast help falls

Role: decision memo for Espen, fork 9 of the secure floor (`../README.md`, "Forks for S3"; `../3-design.md`), on shared rules 1 and 2, which this dossier owns (`../../shared-rules.md`). Status: **decided** (`DEC-2026-11-12-06`): part 1 **B** and part 2 **B**, as recommended. Specified in `../4-specification.md` (INC-5.01 to 5.03). Sources are named in plain text; bracketed tags point to `../evidence.json`. Rates are cents lost from each added dollar of earnings.

**Decision line.** Recommended: **part 1 B, the floor's new cash payments that do not replace wages (the child benefit, the Climate Dividend, training stipends, the refundable credits) never count as income or assets for any federal or federally funded program; wage-replacement benefits (paid leave, Social Security) count as they do today; part 2 B, a published ceiling of 65 cents per added dollar for the household model's typical families, counting every layer except housing's rent rule, met by design rules rather than a new statutory cap.** Cost: part 1 about **$1-3 billion a year of forgone offsets in housing and SNAP (judgment)**; part 2 **none by itself**. Evidence: program rules and the record of coordination at home and abroad; randomized trials on how smoothing affects work.

**This memo sets the rules other dossiers design within.** Housing and care have already set their layers (`DEC-2026-11-10-03`; `DEC-2026-11-11-03`); this memo does not change them.

## The question

Every program defines income its own way, and their phase-outs stack (section 1, part E). Two questions:
1. **What counts as income (rule 1).** Housing's working answer is that no new benefit raises rent, in a range of 0 to 30 cents a dollar (`../../shared-rules.md`, rule 1). Should the floor's new payments count for rent, SNAP, SSI and Medicaid?
2. **How fast help may fall (rule 2).** Should the plan set a ceiling on the combined rate, and how?

## Why it matters

- **A refundable credit already counts for nothing.** Section 6409 of the tax code keeps any refund or advance credit out of income, and out of resources for 12 months, for every federal program [EV-SEC-534]. A payment outside the tax code counts unless a statute excludes it.
- **The stack is steep for families taking several programs.** A single parent of two on SNAP and a voucher, in the earned income credit's phase-out, loses about 83 cents of each added dollar, about 53 without the voucher (section 1, part E.3; statutory rates) [EV-SEC-501] [EV-HOU-417] [EV-HOU-410].
- **But most families take one program or none.** CBO's average marginal rate for low- and moderate-income workers is 31%, and 62% of lower-income families get no means-tested transfer (CBO) [EV-HOU-401] [EV-SEC-539].

## What the evidence says, in five lines

1. **Smoothing a cliff changes behavior less than expected** (randomized): replacing SSDI's earnings cliff with a $1-for-$2 offset raised the share earning above the line but not average earnings, and cost more than it saved (SSA's Benefit Offset National Demonstration) [EV-SEC-546].
2. **Few wage earners bunch at kinks; the self-employed do** (quasi-experimental): at the earned income credit's kink (Saez) [EV-SEC-549] and the premium credit's poverty-line notch [EV-SEC-550].
3. **One income test can serve many programs** (international practice): Canada's federal and provincial benefits all use the prior year's family net income [EV-SEC-558].
4. **Merging benefits into one is a large delivery risk** (audit): the UK's Universal Credit [EV-SEC-556].
5. **Low tapers on a family benefit are workable** (international practice): Australia's family benefit falls by 20 cents, then 30 cents, a dollar [EV-SEC-559].

## Part 1. What counts as income

**A. Current law.** Tax-code payments are excluded [EV-SEC-534]; everything else counts unless a statute says otherwise.
- *Consequence:* memo 1's child benefit and memo 3's credits are excluded; LAB-10 stipends and any payment outside the tax code raise rent by up to 30 cents a dollar [EV-HOU-410] and cut SNAP.

**B. Exclude the floor's payments that do not replace wages (recommended).**
- **What it does.** One statutory rule, modeled on section 6409: the child benefit, the Climate Dividend, LAB-10 training stipends, the plan's refundable credits and the value of free or capped child care (as you decided for vouchers, `DEC-2026-11-10-03`) are not income or resources for any federal or federally funded program. Paid leave and Social Security, including the minimum benefit and caregiver credit, count as today, because they replace wages that would have counted.
- *For people:* a parent in public housing who starts a training stipend keeps the same rent; a parent on paid leave pays rent on the leave benefit, as on the wages it replaces.
- *Cost:* about $1-3 billion a year of forgone offsets (judgment: the stipends and the dividend's share among assisted renters and SNAP households; the child benefit is already excluded). Housing's dossier tests 0 to 30 cents; this sets it at 0 for these payments.
- *Burden:* none; it removes a reporting duty.

**C. Exclude everything new, including paid leave and Social Security increases.**
- *Consequence:* a family on paid leave would pay less rent than when working; Social Security increases would not count for SSI, which would make SSI's interaction with Social Security inconsistent. It costs more and treats wage-replacement unevenly.

## Part 2. How fast help may fall

What a single parent of two between 100% and 200% of poverty loses from each added dollar, on statutory rates (illustrative arithmetic, section 1, part E.3):

| Layer | Today (2026) | Under the plan, 2030-34 | Under the plan, from 2035 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Payroll tax | 7.65 | 7.65 | 7.65 |
| Federal income tax | 0 to 12 | 0 to 12 | 0 to 12 |
| Earned income credit phase-out | 21.06 [EV-SEC-501] | 21.06 | 21.06 |
| Child credit or benefit | 0 | 0 (memo 1 A phases out above $150,000) | 0 |
| SNAP | 24 [EV-HOU-417] | 24 | 24 |
| Premium credit, where it applies | about 11-16 [EV-SEC-514] | about 11-16 | 0 (the single plan, `DEC-2026-11-10-09`) |
| Child care | Varies by state | 0 at or below 85% of state median income; 7 above (`DEC-2026-11-11-03`) | Same |
| **Total, without housing** | **about 53-81** | **about 53-81** | **about 53-65** |
| Housing, if assisted | 30, at the next annual review [EV-HOU-410] | 30 | 30 |

**A. No ceiling.** Each dossier reports its own rate (today's interim rule).
- *Consequence:* nothing stops a later program from adding a layer in the same income range.

**B. A published ceiling of 65 cents, met by design rules (recommended).**
- **What it does.**
  - *The ceiling:* for the household model's typical families (a single parent with two children, a married couple with two, a single adult) between 50% and 300% of poverty, the combined rate from every federal layer, payroll and income tax included, stays at or below 65 cents. Housing's 30% rent rule is outside it, because housing reviews income only once a year and has set its own rule (`../../shared-rules.md`, rule 2).
  - *Design rules that meet it:* no new phase-out below 200% of poverty in any plan benefit; the floor's payments excluded (part 1 B); one income finding reused across programs, as HUD and Medicaid may already do [EV-SEC-510] [EV-SEC-562]; benefits held steady for a set period after a raise, as SNAP's transitional benefits and child care's continued eligibility do [EV-SEC-560] [EV-SEC-529].
  - *Monitoring:* the household model publishes the distribution of combined rates each year; a family type above the ceiling for two years triggers a review by the owning dossiers. No statutory cap and no new calculation for families.
- *Does the plan meet it?* From 2035, yes, at the edge (project arithmetic on section 1, part E.3): the single parent at about 53-65, depending on income tax; a married couple with two children at about 40, or about 65 in a state that gives them SNAP through its broad income option; a single adult at about 40-57 with memo 3's larger childless credit. In 2030-34 the premium credit adds 11-16 cents in its range, and the single plan ends that layer.
- *Why not 60:* the families at 60-65 are those taking SNAP and the earned income credit at once. Getting them under 60 means lowering one of those two rates where they overlap, a priced change for section 6, not a rule.
- *Cost:* none by itself.
- *Burden:* none on families; one annual report.

**C. A hard statutory cap.**
- **What it does.** A coordinating credit refunds any combined rate above, say, 50 cents.
- *Why not:* it needs every program's rate for every family each year, the kind of system the UK built with Universal Credit [EV-SEC-556], and the trial evidence says lower rates change work little [EV-SEC-546].

## Recommendation: part 1 B; part 2 B

- **Part 1 B gives the floor one rule:** what helps a family without replacing a wage never takes help away elsewhere. It extends the law that already protects the child credit.
- **Part 2 B sets a line the plan meets from 2035,** and keeps later programs from crossing it, without a new calculation for any family. It follows the design lens (`DEC-2026-11-09-13`): published counts and a review, not a rule enforced up front.
- **The evidence says the case is fairness and simplicity,** not much more work [EV-SEC-546]. That is why the recommendation is rules and monitoring, not a costly cap.

**What would change this:** a financing settlement with room to lower SNAP's or the earned income credit's rate where they overlap (toward a 60-cent line); the household model finding many families above 65 cents after 2035; housing choosing to count the floor's payments (part 1 would follow its rule).

## If you decide

- **Under part 1 B:** section 4 writes one exclusion section in the 2029 reconciliation bill, cited by every plan benefit; the housing dossier's range narrows to 0 for these payments.
- **Under part 2 B:** shared rule 2's note records the 65-cent ceiling and its design rules; each dossier's section 4 states its layer against it; the household model (`MOD-household`) reports it.
