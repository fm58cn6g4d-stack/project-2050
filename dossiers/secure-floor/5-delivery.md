# A secure floor for every household: delivery

Role: section 5 of the secure floor dossier. It says who runs each provision of `4-specification.md`, what capacity it needs, the timeline from enactment to full operation, how people get remedies, what states do, and how the floor reaches rural places. Status: reviewed (L2, milestone review `review-milestone.md`, #298; L3 after its fix steps and re-check), on `DEC-2026-11-12-06`. Dates are the decided ones; where a step's date is a working choice, it says so. What the IRS and SSA need is stated here for shared rule 5's owner, the democratic renewal foundation, which decides who verifies and answers for mistakes plan-wide.

**Agencies already doing the work.** Every job runs through a channel that exists. The new national jobs are three: records-based enrollment for the child benefit, the IRS's largest new build; SSA's paid leave office; and the investment board. The first and second are the delivery risk (part 2).

## 2. Capacity

- **The IRS.** It paid families of about 61 million children monthly in 2021, built within four months of the law [EV-SEC-107] [EV-SEC-108]. A permanent benefit needs more: monthly payment for every child, enrollment from records, the payee rule and the safe harbor. The build and running cost is about $1-2 billion a year (memo 2's judgment; section 6). IRS Direct File is gone [EV-SEC-063], so simple returns need a free route again (preparation, part 3). The legacy plan's CAP-7 capacity line carries the IRS's general rebuild; the child benefit's build is counted on its own line in section 6 so it is not lost if CAP-7 changes.
- **SSA.** SSA had about 52,100 staff in September 2025, about 6,500 fewer than a year before, and could not hire [EV-SEC-066]. The floor gives it paid leave (about 12-20 million claims a year at maturity, on the legacy claim-volume check, `legacy-research.md`, 3.2), front-door work for the child benefit, SSI's changes and the 2030 act's benefit changes. Paid leave's administration is paid from the leave fund (3% of benefits plus $3 billion of start-up, as booked); SSA's own staffing is a CAP-7 line. Closing a field office cut disability recipients nearby by 16% [EV-SEC-106], so the front doors depend on keeping offices open, not adding duties to fewer of them. *Sequence:* SSA builds paid leave first (benefits January 2031), then the 2030 act's benefit changes (from 2031); the child benefit's front-door duty is light (recording statements), and the IRS carries the build.
- **USDA Rural Development.** Rebuilt to about 5,000 field staff (`DEC-2026-11-10-02`); the front-door duty is recording statements, not deciding eligibility.
- **Labor.** LAB-10 is about $10-14 billion a year at maturity on the booked path, against WIOA's $2.3 billion [EV-SEC-638]: a fourfold-plus growth in training money through the same boards. The boards need two years to build sector partnerships at that scale (working assumption), which the booked four-year ramp allows.
- **States.** SNAP's restoration removes work for states (the time-limit screens, the cost-share calculation). No new state duty is created except the stipend's payment where a board uses a state fiscal agent.

**If R1 is late.** The child benefit's tax year moves with it: the benefit takes effect for the first tax year that begins at least six months after enactment, and payments begin within six months (working choice). SNAP's and SSI's dates move to the first fiscal or calendar year after enactment.

**If the 2030 act is late.** SS-11.01 is the piece that cannot wait: if the act has not passed by the end of 2031, a short bill carrying only SS-11.01 moves first (section 8).

## 4. Remedies

- **The child benefit.** A notice states what the records show, the amount, the payee and how to correct it. A correction at a front door takes effect the next month. A payee dispute goes to a short IRS administrative review with a decision within 60 days (working choice); payment continues to the current payee meanwhile. Appeal to the IRS Independent Office of Appeals and the Tax Court as for any credit. The safe harbor (FAM-1.06) means a low-income family's mistake never becomes a debt, and no payment is taken for an old one (FAM-1.07).
- **The credits.** As today, through the return and the Taxpayer Advocate.
- **SNAP and SSI.** Their existing fair hearings.
- **LAB-10.** Notice, appeal and accessible help, as the legacy rules set [EV-SEC-698]; declining or leaving never costs a benefit (LAB-10.04).
- **Paid leave.** SSA's reconsideration and hearing process; job restoration enforced by Labor and by a private right of action (FAM-3.07).
- **Social Security.** As today.
- **Data use.** A person may see which records enrolled them; a match outside FAM-1.08's purposes is unlawful, with the Privacy Act's remedies.

## 5. States' roles

- **SNAP.** States run it as today, with the 2025 law's added duties and cost share removed (INC-2.01).
- **Paid leave.** States that do as well keep their programs, file for the federal contribution and report (FAM-3.06). The thirteen state programs [EV-SEC-309] each decide whether to stay or join.
- **LAB-10.** State workforce boards run the on-ramps; states coordinate child care for the hours the guarantee misses with their child care agencies (care's guarantee is state-run, `DEC-2026-11-11-03`).
- **The territories.** The IRS pays Puerto Rico's families directly; the other territories pay under Treasury-approved monthly plans with full federal reimbursement, and the IRS pays where no plan is approved (`4-specification.md`, "Where particular groups fall"). SNAP's and SSI's changes reach only where those programs run: Guam and the Virgin Islands for SNAP, the Northern Mariana Islands for SSI [EV-SEC-703] [EV-SEC-704].
- **No new state matching cost** anywhere in the set.

## 6. Rural delivery

Rural Development's offices are a front door for the child benefit (FAM-1.10), as the cross-cutting rural direction asks (`dossiers/foundations/rural-delivery.md`; `DEC-2026-11-10-02`). In counties without an SSA field office, a Rural Development office or a VITA site within the county is the in-person route (working choice: the agencies publish the map). LAB-10's partnerships follow the plan's investment: rural road, bridge, water and power work, and rural housing repair, with transport support weighted to rural trainees' distances.

## 7. What delivery leaves open

- **How much of the matching can run by 2030.** Studied in implementation: the 2027-28 preparation match sizes it. If enrollment from records reaches fewer children at first, the benefit still pays every child on a return and every family that confirms through a front door.
- **The payee rule's disputes.** How many there will be is unknown; the review's 60 days is a working choice to revisit with the first year's counts.
- **SSA's staffing.** If SSA cannot staff paid leave by 2031, the start moves, the program does not shrink (memo 5).
- **The investment board's governance details.** Membership, terms and the index are working choices for the 2030 act, informed by SSA's score.
