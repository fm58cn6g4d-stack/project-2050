## Political and legal events

- **The Byrd rule strikes FAM-1.08's data-sharing authorities from R1.** The child benefit still pays every child on a return and every family that confirms at a front door; the authorities move to the 2030 act (regular order).
- **R1 slips.** The benefit takes effect for the first tax year at least six months after enactment (`5-delivery.md`, part 3).
- **The 2030 Social Security act stalls.** SS-11.01 moves first as a short bill by the end of 2031, so a depletion in the fourth quarter of 2032 [EV-SEC-400] never cuts benefits; the rest waits for 60 votes or the rules reform.
- **The Senate rules reform fails.** Social Security needs 60 votes; the bipartisan track (memo 6 D) becomes the route, with the act as the offer.
- **A court bars the investment board's holding of stocks.** SS-7.01's fallback carries the gap.
- **The 2027 or 2028 Trustees report widens the gap again** (it grew 0.60 points in a year [EV-SEC-403]). The package's share falls; SS-7.01 carries more.

## Left open (not decided)

- **The repayment cap** above 200% of poverty ($1,000 a child, working choice).
- **The investment board's share** of reserves (up to 20%, working choice) and **how the budget model records it** (`6-cost.md`, part 4).
- **SS-7.01's trigger level** (−0.25% of payroll in two reports, working choice).
- **The stipend's level** (up to $9,000 a year, working choice).
- **SSI in the territories** (a fork for Espen). SSI does not reach Puerto Rico, Guam, the Virgin Islands or American Samoa [EV-SEC-703]. *Alternatives:* leave it as today; extend SSI to all four, about $2.5-3 billion a year in the 2030s, almost all Puerto Rico, on SSA's actuaries' estimate [EV-SEC-705] grown to the decade (`6-cost.md`, part 2); or raise the territories' AABD grants. *What would make it matter:* Espen's answer; it reaches the poorest aged and disabled residents of the territories at modest cost.
- **SS-8's share** against **SS-7.01's start** (working choices): at a 20% share the investment closes little and the fallback carries most of the remaining gap (`6-cost.md`, part 4). A larger share, or the fallback starting before 2035, would change that.

## Hard stops

- **No benefit cut at depletion.** If neither the act nor SS-11.01 is law when the retirement fund runs short, the plan's first priority in Social Security is a stand-alone loan bill.
- **No work condition** on any benefit in the set (LAB-10.04; rule 6). A proposal to add one goes to Espen.
- **No data use for immigration enforcement** (FAM-1.08); if a law requires it, the matching stops and memo 2 A applies.
