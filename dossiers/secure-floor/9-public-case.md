# A secure floor for every household: the public case

Role: section 9 of the secure floor dossier: a plain-language brief, messages, hard questions and a can-say list, built only from sections 1-8 and never ahead of them. Status: draft (stage D, board row S4, PR 2), on `DEC-2026-11-12-06`. Every number traces to its section and evidence ID in the source table at the end. Sources are named in plain text before each tag. Project estimates say "on our estimates" or "about". Each promise is dated from when its provision starts. Espen's framing for the child benefit: a benefit for working and middle-class families.

## The brief

**A floor under every working and middle-class family.** Raising children costs more every year, and the help families get from Washington has not kept up. In 2025, 13.4% of American children were poor on the Census Bureau's fullest measure, about 9.7 million children (Census, 2026) [EV-SEC-019] [EV-SEC-020]. In 2021, the one year the child credit was paid monthly to almost every family, child poverty fell to 5.2%, the lowest on record (Census, 2022) [EV-SEC-202].

Project 2050's secure floor does four things, starting in 2030.

**A monthly child benefit for working and middle-class families,** from 2030. $300 a month for each child from 6 to 17 and $360 for each child under 6, in 2025 dollars and rising with prices, plus $2,400 in a baby's first month: the American Family Act, introduced in Congress in 2025 [EV-SEC-262]. Every family below about $150,000 for couples ($112,500 for single parents), adjusted for inflation, gets the full amount, however little the parents earn; it falls gradually above that [EV-SEC-263]. It is paid to the parent the child lives with, starting from the records the government already holds, so families do not have to apply.

**Help that holds when work is short or paid little,** from 2030. Workers without children get a real earned income credit, up to about $1,500 in 2021 dollars from age 19, as they did in 2021, paid with the tax refunds from 2031 [EV-SEC-256]. Food help cut by the 2025 law is restored. People on SSI can save up to $10,000 without losing it, and from 2031 SSI reaches older and disabled people in Puerto Rico, Guam, the Virgin Islands and American Samoa for the first time, where today the limit is $2,000, unchanged since 1989 (Social Security Administration) [EV-SEC-042]. Paid training into the jobs the plan creates, in care, construction, rail, housing and clean power, comes with a stipend and help with child care and transport, and it is always an offer, never a condition.

**Paid leave,** from 2031. Twelve weeks for a new child, a family member's serious illness or your own, paying 85% of the first part of your wages, run by Social Security, with your job back. A worker earning $60,000 pays $180 a year to start (project estimate) [EV-SEC-676].

**Social Security kept whole.** The retirement fund can pay full benefits only until late 2032 on current law, then 78% (Social Security Trustees, 2026) [EV-SEC-400]. The plan closes most of the gap by asking earnings above $250,000 and large investment incomes to pay in, and on our estimates a contribution increase of up to one point, split between workers and employers, is expected from the late 2030s to close the rest. It adds a stronger minimum benefit and credit for caregiving years, and, from the 2030 Social Security act, promises full benefits on time whatever happens.

**What it achieves, on our estimates.** Child poverty falls to about 9% (somewhere between 8% and 12%), about 3 million fewer poor children (section 6). The child benefit costs about $1.85 trillion over 2030-39; the whole floor on the general budget about $2.49 trillion over the decade and about $287 billion a year from 2040, before the plan's financing (section 6).

## The case in one paragraph

Families with children are working harder for less security. The secure floor pays every working and middle-class family a monthly child benefit, $300 to $360 a child, starting from records the government already has, so no family has to apply and no child is left out because a parent earned too little. It gives workers without children a real tax credit, restores food help, lets disabled people save, pays for training into the jobs the plan builds, guarantees twelve weeks of paid leave with your job back, and keeps Social Security paying in full. On our estimates it cuts child poverty by about a third.

## Messages

1. **For working and middle-class families.** Every family below about $150,000 for couples gets the full benefit; most families get far more than today. A couple with two school-age children earning $70,000 gets $600 a month instead of about $367 (2025 dollars) [EV-SEC-262].
2. **Every child counts, not only the ones whose parents earn enough.** Today 17 to 19 million children get less than the full credit because their parents earn too little (Tax Policy Center; Columbia University, 2025) [EV-SEC-011] [EV-SEC-601]. The secure floor ends that.
3. **Government that comes to you.** The benefit starts from a birth record or a tax return; a parent confirms a letter instead of filing an application, and can ask questions at a Social Security or USDA office.
4. **Monthly, because bills are monthly.** When the 2021 payments arrived, food shortages in families with children fell by about a quarter (Shafer and others, 2022) [EV-SEC-209].
5. **Leave without losing your job.** 66% of workers who needed leave and didn't take it couldn't afford to, and 45% feared losing their job (Labor Department survey, 2018) [EV-SEC-307].
6. **Social Security, paid in full, on time.** No cut at depletion, ever.

## Hard questions, plain answers

- **"Will people stop working?"** Studies of the 2021 payments found no effect on work (Ananat and others) [EV-SEC-212]. Economists who expect some parents to leave work still find child poverty falls substantially (Bastian; Corinth and others) [EV-SEC-219] [EV-SEC-218].
- **"Why do higher earners get less?"** It is a benefit for working and middle-class families. Couples above about $210,000 get somewhat less than today's credit, about $340 a year for a couple at $250,000 with two children in 2030 (section 7). The full benefit goes to everyone below about $150,000.
- **"How do you pay for it?"** The plan's financing is set in one place for the whole plan. The child benefit is about $1.85 trillion over ten years on our estimates; the floor's new pieces add about $0.5 trillion (section 6).
- **"Isn't a payroll contribution for leave a tax increase?"** It is $180 a year for a worker at $60,000 to start, for twelve weeks of paid leave; it can rise to at most $330 [EV-SEC-676].
- **"Is Social Security going to the stock market?"** Part of its reserves, in a broad index fund run by an independent board. Your benefit never depends on the market. On our estimates the investment closes only a small part of the gap, so a contribution increase of up to one point, split between workers and employers, is expected to start automatically in the late 2030s (section 6).

## Stories (composite illustrations, not real people)

- **Tasha, a single mother of two in Ohio, ages 3 and 8, earning $14,000.** Today she gets about $1,725 a year of child credit [EV-SEC-006]. From 2030 she gets $660 a month (2025 dollars) [EV-SEC-262], and her food help is not cut.
- **Marcus, 22, working in a warehouse for $15,000 a year.** Today he gets no earned income credit because he is under 25 [EV-SEC-027]. Under the plan he gets about $1,000 (section 7).
- **Ana, a home health aide earning $2,400 a month, having a baby in 2031.** She gets about $1,850 a month for twelve weeks and her job back [EV-SEC-340].

| Can say | Can't say |

| "On our estimates, child poverty falls to about 9%, about a third lower" | "Ends child poverty" or "cuts it in half" |
| "Every family below about $150,000 (couples) gets the full benefit" | "Every family gets more than today" (about 5.4 million families above about $210,000 get less, section 6) |
| "No family has to apply; it starts from records" | "Every child is paid automatically from day one" (records reach most; front doors and returns reach the rest) |
| "Paid leave from 2031" | "Paid leave from 2030" (contributions start in 2030, benefits in 2031) |
| "Full Social Security benefits on time" (from the 2030 act) | "Social Security is solved for 75 years" (no package score exists [EV-SEC-477]) |
