# Fair pay and real power at work: what would change our minds

Role: section 8 of the work dossier (flagship 5). For each major commitment in sections 3 to 7 (84 provisions in `provisions.json`), it gives the finding that would reverse or resize the choice, where that evidence would come from and when, the review trigger and who acts, and what we would do instead. It also covers the political and legal events that would force a fallback, what is left open, the hard stops, the options for the future labor economy that later work explores (`DEC-2026-11-17-96`), and when the evidence arrives. Status: **draft** (stage F), on `DEC-2026-11-17-01` to `-36`, `-95` and `-96`. Items still with Espen are covered on their recommendations and stay **provisional** (part 7). A change to a decided choice goes back to Espen by memo; the fallbacks below are the options the memos already weighed, unless a row says otherwise.

## How to read this section

- **Four questions for each commitment.** What would reverse or resize it; where that evidence comes from (a data series, a pilot, administrative records, a score or a court) and when; the review trigger and who acts; and what we would do instead.
- **Who acts, in two periods.** Before January 2029 the project acts: the dossier reopens and the choice goes to Espen by memo. From 2029 the plan's own machinery acts: the wage commission (LAB-4.04), BLS, the Secretary of Labor, the Board, the states, the agencies' Inspectors General and GAO, each as its provision says, and Congress where a change needs law. Most triggers start a published review, not an automatic change (`DEC-2026-11-09-13`). The exceptions are named: the recession pause (LAB-4.03), Extended Benefits (LAB-11.04), the quorum fallback (LAB-2.04) and the standby trigger (LAB-19.04, provisional).
- **Thresholds are working choices.** A trigger's level (two quarters, 30% take-up, half the gross raise) is set so it can be checked. It can change without reopening the decision it watches. Section 7, part 7, lists the same signs of failure; this section adds what each one would change.
- **Evidence.** Bracketed tags point to `evidence.json` or another dossier's register. "Judgment" marks a project view, with its basis stated.

## 6. The household result: how much of the raise a single adult keeps

**The finding** (`7-validation.md`, part 1B). On the marketplace in a state that has not expanded Medicaid, a full-time single adult whose pay rises from $7.25 to the third step ($12.50 in 2032) keeps about **39% of the raise under the plan, against 59% at 2029 law**. INC-1.01's larger childless credit and INC-2.02's SNAP deduction phase out over the same range, so each added dollar loses more to the two together. The premium the raise brings (about $874 a year) lowers both columns alike and explains none of the gap. From 2035 the single plan removes the premium and the single adult keeps 52-59% at full time. At no wage is the adult worse off under the plan than at 2029 law, except by paid leave's worker share (about $90 a year).

**What it would and would not change.**
- **It does not change the floor.** The floor raises the adult's net resources at every step under both laws. The question is how the income-support layers phase out, which is shared rule 2's, owned by the secure floor (INC-5.02), and stage G's to price.
- **It is not a breach of the 65-cent ceiling.** The ceiling is a marginal test for small raises; this is an average over a large raise. The secure floor's check finds every typical family at or below 65 cents at the margin except one married-couple case in 2030-34 (`dossiers/secure-floor/4-specification.md`, INC-5.02).
- **The review trigger is already met, so it goes to stage G now.** The trigger (a working choice): a typical family keeping less than half of a full-time raise from the old federal floor to the current step in the household model's yearly report. Today's run puts the single adult there for every year of 2030-34, so the review is not left to wait for a second report. The finding goes to the secure floor's owners and the financing settlement with this PR (For the board), not to Espen as a work question.

## Political and legal events

- **The Senate rules reform fails** (legacy D2). The labor act needs 60 votes; the legacy simulation gave it a 13% chance in the plan's first Congress [EV-WRK-712]. R1's parts still run: unemployment insurance's money and standards where Byrd-eligible, Extended Benefits, wage insurance, apprenticeship, the 14(c) grant and national service's fallback. The executive steps hold only while an administration keeps them (LAB-4.05, 2.06, 16.01). The fallbacks for a smaller labor act are memo 3 C (remedies and first contracts), memo 1 C ($15, then indexed) and memo 4 B (no fee title), each by Espen's decision. This is the flagship's main legislative risk (`5-delivery.md`, part 3).
- **The parliamentarian strikes the UI standards from R1.** They ride the labor act; on the working timeline that keeps January 1, 2031 (section 4).
- **The parliamentarian rejects a contingent date.** The credit (LAB-5.01) moves to the labor act, and the service fallback (LAB-9.06) to the authorization (judgment; Byrd eligibility is to check for both, section 4).
- **A recession during the steps.** The pause (LAB-4.03) delays the next step a year at a time; Extended Benefits switch on by rule.
- **A Court ruling on the board's remedies, first-contract arbitration or binding boards.** The act's design already assumes *Slaughter* and *Jarkesy* [EV-WRK-136] [EV-WRK-131]. A ruling against arbitration or binding boards goes back to Espen with the fallbacks in part 2.
- **A later Congress or president.** The contractor minimum wage, the board quorum and the General Counsel's memos are executive and can be reversed, as in 2025 [EV-WRK-038]. The rules in statute (card check, first contracts, the ABC test, the overtime method, the heat floor) can be repealed only by law, which is why they are in statute.
- **Displacement rising before the trigger can start** (about 2035). If memo 10 C is chosen, its trigger needs two years of records. Before then, the yearly BLS report (from 2031) and Labor's report to Congress are the response, and memo 10 D's tools are the AI-and-work topic's.

## Hard stops

- **No work condition on help** (shared rule 6, `DEC-2026-11-17-23`; LAB-10.04). Unemployment insurance keeps its insurance test by attestation; a proposal to require search or the reemployment session goes to Espen.
- **The cause of a job loss is never asked** (LAB-18.01, `-95`). Neither wage insurance nor the trigger asks whether trade, AI or anything else caused a loss, and AI exposure is never an eligibility test (LAB-19.03).
- **The floor never falls.** The pause delays a step; indexing never lowers the floor (LAB-4.02, 4.03).
- **No loss of services when 14(c) ends.** No one loses Medicaid coverage or day services because a certificate ends (LAB-4.09).
- **No immigration retaliation and no data use for enforcement.** A threat to report status is retaliation (LAB-7.06); complaint files stay behind immigration's firewall (IMM-4.11). If a law requires otherwise, the plan opposes it.
- **The right to discuss pay stays a right** whatever its average wage effect (`-09`).
