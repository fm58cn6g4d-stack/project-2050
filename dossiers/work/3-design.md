## How to read this section

- **One batched set.** Nine memos and sixteen yes-or-no points, so Espen can answer once. The memos follow the agenda's items, LAB-1 to LAB-9 with their options, plus unemployment insurance, which has no legacy row but is this flagship's (section 1, part G).
- **Each memo opens with a decision line** in the same form: the recommended option, its cost over FY2030-39 and in FY2040, and its evidence. `memos/README.md` repeats them in one table.
- **Legacy designs are options** (`DEC-2026-10-30-02`). LAB-4A's two-thirds target, LAB-5's credit, LAB-1A's override, LAB-3's two boards, LAB-7's doubled damages, LAB-8A's size and LAB-9D are each weighed against alternatives, and the legacy file is used as evidence, not as a default (`DEC-2026-11-13-15`). "Inherited assumptions, traced" says which, if any, Espen chose: none of the labor designs is Espen's for this plan.
- **Report, don't gate** (`DEC-2026-11-10-05`). Costs are reported for stage G and not trimmed to fit. Memo 1 part 2 and memo 8 part 2 are partly money questions, because their options reach the same people at different cost.
- **Design lens** (`DEC-2026-11-09-13`). Each option says what it asks of workers, employers and agencies. Memo 1 drops untested tiers and a credit; memo 7 uses records states already hold; memo 9 turns a placement quota into a reported target.
- **Time frame** (`DEC-2026-10-29-01`). Recommendations start from the January 2029 law; anything earlier is labeled preparation, and January 2029 executive steps are labeled as such.
- **Settled elsewhere, not reopened:** LAB-10's participation support and paid leave (secure floor, `DEC-2026-11-12-06`); the care wage floor, HLT-10 (`DEC-2026-11-11-04`); clean power's transition guarantee and its wage insurance (`DEC-2026-11-13-06`); E-Verify (`DEC-2026-11-12-07`, `-12`); agency capacity, CAP-7 (democratic renewal). ## Recommended without a memo: sixteen yes-or-no points

Each is decided on its own. None is adopted by silence. Costs are judgment unless a tag gives them.

1. **A heat standard in statute (LAB-6).** The labor bill directs OSHA to issue, within a year, a standard with California's elements (a written plan, water, shade, rest and acclimatization above heat triggers, indoor and outdoor), and sets a floor that state laws cannot lower, which overrides Florida's ban on local heat rules [EV-WRK-241].
   - *Why:* California's 2005 standard cut hot-day injuries 15-27% by industry (Fomenko, McInerney and Negrusa, NBER working paper, 2026) [EV-WRK-258]; OSHA's 2024 analysis, the Biden administration's, put costs at $7.8 billion a year against $9.18 billion of benefits [EV-WRK-255].
   - *January 2029, executive:* finish the rule if OSHA has not (legacy EXE-17) [EV-WRK-237].
   - *Cost:* none federal beyond OSHA staff (CAP-7). **Recommended: yes.** **Decided: yes (`DEC-2026-11-17-14`).**
2. **Home care workers stay under federal minimum wage and overtime law,** with the 2013 coverage written into statute if the 1975 exemptions return (care's point 2) [EV-WRK-225] [EV-CAR-616]. *Cost:* inside the care floor's range. **Recommended: yes.** **Decided: yes, with a change; final wording in `DEC-2026-11-17-30` (note, part f).**
3. **The overtime salary threshold in statute,** at the 2024 rule's $1,128 a week [EV-WRK-221], indexed every three years, against $684 today [EV-WRK-223]. *Why:* the 2024 rule was vacated [EV-WRK-222]; a statute cannot be. *Cost:* none federal. **Recommended: yes.** **Decided: yes, with a change: the 2024 method in statute, never below $1,128 (`DEC-2026-11-17-31`; note, part g).**
4. **Layoff notice that works:** 90 days for closings and mass layoffs, layoffs counted across an employer's sites, enforced by the Labor Department with penalties instead of private suits alone, as New Jersey's 2023 law does for notice [EV-WRK-384]. *Why:* only about 45% of displaced workers got written notice [EV-WRK-318]; no agency enforces WARN [EV-WRK-329]. **Recommended: yes.** **Decided: yes (`DEC-2026-11-17-17`).**
5. **No federal severance mandate in the first bill;** study New Jersey's one week per year of service [EV-WRK-384] first. **Recommended: yes (not now).** **Decided: yes, with a change, provisional: study, then recommend a mandate or a guarantee of state and local authority (`DEC-2026-11-17-18`).**
6. **Predictable schedules:** two weeks' notice of schedules and pay for last-minute changes for large retail, food-service and warehouse employers. *Why:* Fair Workweek laws raised two weeks' notice 13 points with no fall in hours (Schneider, Harknett and Arbelaez, *Science Advances*) [EV-WRK-409]; results depend on enforcement [EV-WRK-410]. **Recommended: yes.** **Decided: yes (`DEC-2026-11-17-19`).**
7. **Federal workers' bargaining restored:** revoke Executive Order 14251 in January 2029 and enact the Protect America's Workforce Act [EV-WRK-148] [EV-WRK-146]. **Recommended: yes.** **Decided: yes (`DEC-2026-11-17-20`).**
8. **January 2029 executive steps:** restore the federal contractor minimum wage (Executive Order 14026, revoked in 2025) [EV-WRK-038]; seat a board quorum; withdraw the General Counsel's 2026 memos [EV-WRK-128]. Labeled executive action, not law. **Recommended: yes.** **Decided: yes (`DEC-2026-11-17-21`).**

- **Tensions to watch.**
  - *Votes:* the labor bill is the hardest vote in the flagship (legacy estimate 13%) [EV-WRK-712]; memo 4 is separable, memo 3 C and memo 1 C are the fallbacks.

  - *Cost:* memo 8 and memo 7 are the largest new general-fund lines; memo 8 part 2 is a money question against C.
