# Memo 2: The tipped, youth and disability subminimum wages

Role: decision memo for Espen on the three federal wages below the floor (part of agenda LAB-4), answering section 2's question 3. Status: **proposed**, for Espen's decision. The legacy design (the tipped wage phased out over seven years; the youth wage and 14(c) certificates ended) is a working choice, not Espen's (`DEC-2026-10-30-02`; `legacy/domains/domain-labor.md`, LAB-4) [EV-WRK-700]. Sources are named in plain text; bracketed tags point to `../evidence.json`.

**Decision line.** Recommended: **B, end all three on one law's schedule: the tipped cash wage rises to the full floor over seven years; the youth wage ends with the first step; 14(c) certificates end over five years, with a funded move into supported jobs and a guarantee that no one loses Medicaid-funded day services in the transition.** Cost: about **$1-3 billion over FY2030-39 for the 14(c) transition (judgment), about zero in FY2040**; the tipped and youth changes are inside memo 1's indirect budget effect. Evidence: thin and contested on the tipped wage (one working paper and one descriptive comparison); a peer-reviewed two-state study and a GAO audit on 14(c); none on the youth wage.

## The question

Under current law in 2029, employers may pay tipped workers $2.13 an hour in cash if tips make up the rest, workers under 20 $4.25 for their first 90 days, and workers with disabilities under a 14(c) certificate any wage tied to productivity (Labor Department; CRS) [EV-WRK-005] [EV-WRK-006] [EV-WRK-007]. The Labor Department withdrew its 14(c) phase-out proposal in July 2025, saying it lacked the authority [EV-WRK-010]. So each subminimum ends only by statute.

## Why it matters

- **Tipped work.** Seven states already pay tipped workers the full minimum: Alaska, California, Minnesota, Montana, Nevada, Oregon and Washington (Economic Policy Institute) [EV-WRK-069].
- **14(c).** 642 employers held certificates on October 1, 2025, paying 35,534 workers below the minimum (Labor Department) [EV-WRK-008], down from about 424,000 in 2001 [EV-WRK-009]; 16 states have ended the certificates (GAO) [EV-WRK-011].
- **For a household.** A server in a $2.13 state takes home a wage that depends on the night's tips, with the employer owing a top-up that workers rarely check. Under B, the cash wage itself reaches the floor.

## What the evidence says, in four lines

1. **The tipped wage: thin on both sides.** Neumark and Yen find higher tipped wages cut tipped restaurant jobs slightly (elasticity about −0.08) without raising earnings much (working paper; authors long critical of large increases) [EV-WRK-067] [EV-WRK-068]. EPI finds tipped workers' poverty about 4 points higher in $2.13 states, a descriptive comparison (Economic Policy Institute, labor-aligned) [EV-WRK-069]. Neither studies full elimination.
2. **Recent changes are unevaluated.** Chicago is phasing out its tipped wage over five years [EV-WRK-072]; the District of Columbia's council slowed the voters' phase-out to 75% of the full wage by 2034 [EV-WRK-071].
3. **14(c): what travels with repeal matters.** New Hampshire's repeal was followed by higher employment of people with cognitive disabilities; Maryland's showed no change (Kakara, Bair and Venkataramani, peer-reviewed) [EV-WRK-074]. In Colorado and Oregon fewer than half of former 14(c) workers the states could track moved to other jobs, and 54-61% were in Medicaid-funded day services instead (GAO, 2025) [EV-WRK-073]. The Commission on Civil Rights majority recommended repeal with a planned phase-out and more supported employment (2020) [EV-WRK-075].
4. **The youth wage:** no study of its effect was found (section 2, A.5).

## Options

**A. The legacy set:** tipped wage phased out over seven years; youth wage and 14(c) ended, with no transition named.
- *Risk:* the GAO finding [EV-WRK-073]: ending certificates without supported employment moved few people into jobs.

**B. One schedule for all three, with a funded 14(c) transition (recommended).**
- *Tipped:* the cash wage rises in seven equal steps to the full floor; tips stay the worker's, and the tip credit ends with the last step.
- *Youth:* the $4.25 wage ends with memo 1's first step.
- *14(c):* no new certificates from enactment; existing ones end over five years, the Raise the Wage Act's pace [EV-WRK-043]; a five-year grant to state vocational rehabilitation and developmental disability agencies for supported and customized employment, and a rule that no one loses day services or Medicaid coverage because a certificate ends.
- *Cost:* the grant at about $0.2-0.6 billion a year for five years, $1-3 billion in all (judgment: a few thousand dollars a year of job support for about 35,000 workers and their providers' conversion) [EV-WRK-008]; nothing federal for tipped or youth wages beyond memo 1's indirect effect.
- *Burden:* employers stop tracking tips against a top-up; providers convert programs over five years with funding.

**C. A partial tipped step:** the cash wage rises to 70-75% of the floor (the District's 2034 path) [EV-WRK-071], with the tip credit kept for the rest; youth wage and 14(c) as in B.
- *What it buys:* less change for restaurants, given thin evidence; a compromise some cities have chosen.
- *What it costs:* keeps the top-up rule and its enforcement problem; tipped workers' pay stays partly on tips.

**D. Leave the subminimums to the states** (the 2029 baseline), with 14(c)'s transition grant only.
- *What it costs:* tipped workers keep $2.13 under federal law wherever their state has not set a higher cash wage.

## Recommendation: B

- **One wage for every worker** is the simplest rule to follow and enforce (`DEC-2026-11-09-13`). Seven years is long enough to watch restaurants' response; the wage commission (memo 1) reports on it each year.
- **For 14(c), the evidence says repeal works only with the help that travels with it** [EV-WRK-073] [EV-WRK-074], so the grant and the day-services guarantee are part of the recommendation, not extras.
- **This rests on judgment for the tipped wage,** because neither side's evidence is causal on full elimination. C is the fallback if the commission's reports show tipped jobs falling.

**What would change this:** a causal study of Chicago's or the District's changes showing large job loss (slow the tipped steps to C); evidence that supported employment does not reach former 14(c) workers (lengthen the phase-out).
