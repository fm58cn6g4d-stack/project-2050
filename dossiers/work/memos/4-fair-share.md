# Memo 4: Fair-share fees and state right-to-work laws

Role: decision memo for Espen on the PRO Act's override of state right-to-work laws (agenda LAB-1A against LAB-1B), answering section 2's question 5. Status: **decided** (`DEC-2026-11-17-05`): **A**, as recommended, as a separate title. The legacy file chose the override (LAB-1A) and called it "Espen's call" (`legacy/domains/domain-labor.md`, LAB-1); it is a working choice, not a decision of Espen's for this plan (`DEC-2026-10-30-02`). Sources are named in plain text; bracketed tags point to `../evidence.json`.

**Decision line.** Recommended: **A, allow fair-share fees in private-sector contracts notwithstanding state right-to-work laws, as the PRO Act does, written as a separate title so it can be dropped without reopening the rest of the bill.** Cost: **zero** to the federal budget. Evidence: every study agrees right-to-work laws lower unionization (quasi-experimental); the wage effect is contested; the growth claim rests on a weaker design from an advocate of the laws. This is a values choice the evidence informs but does not settle.

## The question

Federal labor law lets states ban contracts that require workers a union represents to pay a fee for that representation. Twenty-six states and Guam do; Michigan repealed its law in 2023 (National Conference of State Legislatures, 2026) [EV-WRK-141]. The union must still represent everyone in the unit, members or not. The PRO Act would let private-sector contracts require a fee anyway. Public-sector fees are barred by the Supreme Court's *Janus* decision (2018; not yet in the register), which a statute cannot change, so this memo is about private employers only.

## Why it matters

- **Density.** In right-to-work states a union bargains for workers who need not pay, which makes organizing and keeping a unit harder (the mechanism every study below measures through unionization).
- **For a household.** A nurse in a right-to-work state keeps about the fee (often 1-2% of pay; judgment, no figure in the register) and still gets the contract; her union has less to bargain and enforce it with.
- **Politics.** The legacy file judged the override the single provision most likely to cost frontline and Republican-leaning votes (`domain-labor.md`, LAB-1).

## What the evidence says, in four lines

1. **Right-to-work lowers unionization:** in Oklahoma (Eren and Ozbeklik, peer-reviewed, synthetic control) [EV-WRK-166] and in five states since 2011 (Fortin, Lemieux and Lloyd, working paper) [EV-WRK-165]. No study in the register finds otherwise.
2. **Wages: contested.** Fortin, Lemieux and Lloyd find lower wages [EV-WRK-165]; Eren and Ozbeklik find no effect on wages or employment [EV-WRK-166].
3. **Growth: one advocate's estimate.** The Mackinac Center finds 0.8 point faster income growth a year, from state growth regressions, not a causal design (Mackinac Center, free-market, 2013) [EV-WRK-187].
4. **Political voice.** Right-to-work laws lowered the Democratic presidential vote 3.5 points and turnout 2 points in border counties (Feigenbaum, Hertel-Fernandez and Williamson, 2018) [EV-WRK-188]. That is an effect on unions' political role, which some will count for the override and some against.

## Options

**A. The override (the PRO Act and the legacy LAB-1A; recommended).** Contracts may require a fair-share fee from represented workers, whatever state law says.
- *What it buys:* the end of the free-rider rule that every study ties to lower density [EV-WRK-165] [EV-WRK-166], so memo 3's rules work in every state.
- *What it costs:* votes; it overrides laws in 26 states.

**B. Leave state right-to-work laws (the legacy LAB-1B).**
- *What it buys:* the legacy file's judgment that this is the version most likely to pass with one or two defections to spare (`domain-labor.md`, "Survival at 51").
- *What it costs:* lower density in half the states persists; memo 3's gains are smaller there.

**C. Members-only bargaining where a state bans fees.** In right-to-work states, a union may choose to bargain for its members only, ending its duty to represent non-payers, without overriding state law.
- *What it buys:* removes the free-rider problem without a federal override.
- *What it costs:* it splits workplaces into covered and uncovered workers, the opposite of a single contract; untested in the U.S. private sector since the 1930s.

## Recommendation: A, as a separable title

- **On the plan's own goal,** collective bargaining coverage (OUT-06), the evidence points one way: right-to-work lowers it [EV-WRK-165] [EV-WRK-166].
- **The wage effect is contested,** so the case is about representation, not a proven raise.
- **Writing it as its own title** lets the bill drop it, if the count requires, without reopening memo 3. B is a reasonable choice: this is the one memo where the decision is mostly about values and votes.

**What would change this:** a vote count where the title costs the whole bill (B, with memo 3 intact).
