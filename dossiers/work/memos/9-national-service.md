# Memo 9: National service and job guarantees

Role: decision memo for Espen on national service (agenda LAB-9, options LAB-9A to 9D) and its overlap with a transitional job guarantee (AI-4F, home `agenda/ai`), answering section 2's question 11. Status: **decided** (`DEC-2026-11-17-13`): **C, provisional**. The scale is open: Espen asked for a target between C's 150,000 and A's about 250,000, possibly blended with apprenticeship or subsidized placements (`../notes/espen-questions-2026-11-17.md`, part e). The legacy file chose LAB-9D in its Decision Brief 2 (`legacy/governing/decision-brief-02-trims-scope-and-fiscal-rule.md`); under `DEC-2026-10-30-02` that is a working choice, not a decision for this plan. LAB-10's paid on-ramps are decided in the secure floor (`DEC-2026-11-12-06`). Sources are named in plain text; bracketed tags point to `../evidence.json`.

**Decision line.** Recommended: **C, the legacy LAB-9D with two changes: seek a bipartisan authorization first for about 150,000 more full-time service positions a year by 2034, built on AmeriCorps, with a living allowance, health coverage, child care where needed and an education award equal to the maximum Pell grant, usable for apprenticeships; if no authorization passes by the end of 2030, the reconciliation fallback funds the same scale as direct spending (not the universal offer); cross-regional and cross-class placement becomes a published target with a yearly report, not a placement quota. No separate job guarantee.** Cost: about **+$0.03-0.05 trillion over FY2030-39 and about +$5-6 billion in FY2040** (project arithmetic on AmeriCorps' own cost rules), against the legacy LAB-9A's $0.12 trillion. Evidence: a matched comparison of AmeriCorps members (civic effects, no earnings result); official grant rules for cost per member; one small job-guarantee pilot in Austria. The case is civic (OUT-10), not a labor-market one.

## The question

AmeriCorps' FY2026 appropriation is $1.254 billion, including $557.1 million for State and National programs and $180 million for the National Service Trust that pays education awards (Voices for National Service, from the enacted act) [EV-WRK-397]. In 2025 the administration ended grants and cut staff, and courts ordered funding restored in the states that sued (`legacy/domains/domain-labor.md`, "Where things stand"; no court record in the register). The legacy design is a paid service year open to every 18-to-26-year-old, with mixing written into the statute, at about $0.12 trillion over FY2030-39 (range $0.06-0.20 trillion), assuming about $35,000 a slot and 250,000 new slots by 2034 [EV-WRK-705]. The question is the scale, the vehicle and what service is for.

## Why it matters

- **What a slot costs now:** up to $25,200 a year in federal grant money per full-time position, with a minimum living allowance of $20,400 and a 24% local match for new grantees (Volunteer Florida's FY2026 notice of AmeriCorps rules) [EV-WRK-398], plus the education award, equal to the maximum Pell grant of $7,395 (Mapping Your Future, 2025) [EV-WRK-399]: about $32,600 federal per full-time year (project arithmetic), close to the legacy $35,000 [EV-WRK-705].
- **For a household.** A 19-year-old from a small town serves a year in a child care classroom in another state, earns a living allowance and health coverage, and leaves with $7,395 toward college or an apprenticeship.

## What the evidence says, in three lines

1. **Civic effects, not earnings:** AmeriCorps State and National members were more likely to choose public-service careers and NCCC members more connected to their communities than similar non-members; no short-term effect on education (Abt Associates for the national service agency, matched comparison) [EV-WRK-391]. No earnings result was found.
2. **Job training is a different program:** the evidence for raising earnings is sector training and apprenticeship (memo 8) [EV-SEC-143].
3. **Job guarantees:** Austria's pilot for the long-term unemployed in one town raised well-being and cut long-term unemployment, with no harm to other jobs (Kasy and Lehner, matched randomization) [EV-WRK-389]; it enrolled 107 people at about €237 a month each [EV-WRK-390]. One small town.

## Options

**A. The legacy LAB-9A: a universal offer** to every 18-to-26-year-old, mixing in statute, mandatory funding in reconciliation, ramping to 250,000 new slots by 2034.
- *Cost:* about $0.12 trillion (range $0.06-0.20 trillion) [EV-WRK-705].
- *What it buys:* durable money and a promise every young person can claim.
- *Risk:* the national service agency lost staff in 2025 and must rebuild before it can place 250,000 more a year (`domain-labor.md`, capacity note).

**B. The legacy LAB-9B: about 150,000 more slots through appropriations,** about $0.05 trillion (legacy estimate) [EV-WRK-709].
- *What it costs:* reversible every year, as 2025 showed.

**C. LAB-9D, changed (recommended).**
- *Scale:* about 150,000 more full-time positions a year by 2034, ramped with the agency's capacity.
- *Vehicle:* a bipartisan authorization first, which the legacy vote simulation rated far likelier to pass than the labor bill (77% against 13%, a legacy estimate) [EV-WRK-712]; if none passes by the end of 2030, the reconciliation bill funds the same scale as direct spending.
- *Mixing:* a published target share serving outside their home region and teams across income bands, reported each year with a review if it falls short, instead of a quota that every placement must meet (`DEC-2026-11-09-13`).
- *Placements:* the plan's own needs (child care and pre-K classrooms, disaster recovery, public health, election administration), with a path into memo 8's apprenticeships and the care workforce.
- *Labor standards:* no displacement of paid workers or filling of jobs vacated by layoffs or strikes (the AmeriCorps rule, made stricter, as the legacy file proposes).
- *Cost:* about $5 billion a year at full scale (project arithmetic: 150,000 times about $32,600 [EV-WRK-398] [EV-WRK-399], plus health and child care), about +$0.03-0.05 trillion over FY2030-39 with the ramp and about +$5-6 billion in FY2040.

**D. The legacy LAB-9C: the universal offer without mixing.**
- *What it costs:* the legacy file's own warning that service near home is "a jobs program with a flag on it" (`domain-labor.md`, LAB-9); the civic purpose is the evidence's only finding [EV-WRK-391].

**The job guarantee (AI-4F).** A funded position for anyone under 30 unemployed 16 weeks or more, about $0.6 trillion a decade at the legacy scenario's scale (`legacy/domains/domain-ai.md`, AI-4F). Recommended: **no separate program;** LAB-10's paid on-ramps (decided) serve people who need a paid placement, and service can take people out of work into a team. The Austrian evidence is too small to carry a national guarantee.

## Recommendation: C

- **Service is a civic program, and the evidence is civic** [EV-WRK-391]; C keeps mixing, which is the reason for it, without a quota on every placement.
- **A bipartisan route first** is the legacy judgment and the likeliest to last; the fallback makes the money durable if the vote fails, at a scale the agency can staff.
- **What it costs in burden:** none on participants beyond service; programs report placements they already record.

**What would change this:** a rebuilt agency placing 150,000 more well by 2033 (raise toward A); evidence of earnings gains from service (count it with memo 8).
