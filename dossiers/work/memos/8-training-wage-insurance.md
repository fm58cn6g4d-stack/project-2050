**Decision line.** Recommended: **part 1 C, one apprenticeship and sector-training line built with employers: grants per apprentice started and completed, through joint labor-management programs, community colleges and sector partnerships, in construction, energy, care and advanced manufacturing, with the instructor pay line folded in and a published target of 600,000 new apprentices a year by 2034; no training levy; part 2 B, a general wage insurance for workers displaced after three years in a job, paying half the hourly wage gap for the hours worked, up to $10,000 a year for two years, run through state unemployment offices, with clean power's larger guarantee taking precedence for the workers it covers.** Cost: about **+$0.04-0.07 trillion over FY2030-39 for part 1 and +$0.02-0.04 trillion for part 2, about +$6-10 billion in FY2040 together** (judgment), against the legacy LAB-8's $0.03 trillion and AI-4A's $0.12 trillion envelope, which covers part 2's benefit. Evidence: randomized trials of sector training and of the national workforce system; a matched evaluation of apprenticeship; a regression-discontinuity study of TAA's wage insurance; England's levy as a failure to avoid.

## The question

Registered apprenticeship had 703,475 active and 311,035 new apprentices in FY2025 (Labor Department) [EV-WRK-349]; about 42% finish within a year of schedule (GAO) [EV-WRK-350]. The main workforce grants are flat in dollars, so falling in real terms [EV-WRK-335] [EV-WRK-337]. TAA, the one program built for displaced workers, has certified no one since July 2022 (CRS) [EV-WRK-330]. Housing, transportation and clean power all assume a larger construction and energy workforce (shared rule 8), and care asks for a care line here (`dossiers/care/3d-design-workforce.md`, point 5). The question is how to fund training that works, and what displaced workers get when their new job pays less.

## Why it matters

- **Lost pay lasts.** Only 49% of long-tenured displaced workers back in full-time jobs earned as much as before (BLS, 2026) [EV-WRK-317]; high-tenure workers lose about 25% a year for years (Jacobson, LaLonde and Sullivan) [EV-WRK-321].
- **For a household.** A 45-year-old machinist laid off at $32 an hour takes a $24 job. Under part 2 B she gets $4 an hour for every hour she works, up to $10,000 a year, for two years, while she trains for a better one.

## What the evidence says, in five lines

1. **Sector training built with employers works:** earnings up 11-40% in pooled randomized trials (Katz, Roth, Hendra and Schaberg) [EV-SEC-143].
2. **Apprenticeship pays:** $5,839 more in the ninth year (Mathematica for the Labor Department, matched comparison) [EV-SEC-144]; grantees spent about $5,171 per apprentice (the Labor Department's American Apprenticeship Initiative evaluation) [EV-SEC-640].
3. **General vouchers through the workforce system show no near-term gain,** while job-search help raised earnings (Mathematica's national randomized evaluation) [EV-WRK-385] [EV-WRK-386].
4. **A levy is not a program:** England's apprenticeship levy was followed by a 26% fall in starts (National Audit Office, 2019) [EV-WRK-387].
5. **Wage insurance worked for older trade-displaced workers:** employment up 8-17 points and earnings up more than $18,000 over four years, paying for itself (Hyman, Kovak and Leive, regression discontinuity) [EV-WRK-365]; take-up was low [EV-WRK-332].

## Part 1. Apprenticeship and training

**A. The legacy LAB-8A:** one registered-apprenticeship expansion for construction (450,000 apprentices), energy and care, mandatory grants with completion bonuses, about $0.03 trillion (range $0.02-0.05 trillion), with no source sizing it [EV-WRK-704].

**B. A plus the legacy LAB-8B instructor pay line:** grants to pay technical instructors near industry wages, about +$0.01-0.02 trillion [EV-WRK-710].

**C. One employer-built line with a target (recommended).**
- *What it does:* grants per apprentice started and per completion, to joint labor-management programs, community colleges and sector partnerships that place trainees in paid jobs before training starts; four sectors named (construction, energy, care with a line for home care aides, nursing assistants and child care staff, and advanced manufacturing); the instructor line inside it; a target of 600,000 new apprentices a year by 2034, about double FY2025's 311,035 [EV-WRK-349] (a decided target; no evidence in the register shows it is feasible, and the register holds only the FY2025 count), with counts and completion rates published each year by sector and state.
- *Workforce money:* a share of WIOA training funds moves to sector programs, given the trial evidence [EV-WRK-386] [EV-SEC-143].
- *Cost:* about $4-7 billion a year at full scale (judgment: about 300,000 more starts at $10,000-15,000 each in grants and instructor pay, against $5,171 per apprentice in the federal initiative) [EV-SEC-640], ramping up: about +$0.04-0.07 trillion over FY2030-39. CBO scored the 2020 National Apprenticeship Act's authorizations at $3.1 billion over five years (CBO, 2020) [EV-WRK-396], a smaller program.
- *Burden:* on programs, start and completion reports they already file in the federal apprenticeship system.

**D. A training levy** on large employers' payrolls, on England's model. Not recommended: starts fell after England's [EV-WRK-387].

## Part 2. Wage insurance

**A. None beyond what is decided:** unemployment insurance (memo 7), LAB-10's on-ramps and clean power's guarantee for fossil-fuel workers.

**B. A general wage insurance on TAA's model, paid by the hour (recommended).**
- *Who:* any worker displaced from a job held three years or more, whatever the cause, so no one must prove trade or AI caused the loss (the legacy AI-4A principle) [EV-WRK-711].
- *What:* half the gap between the old and new hourly wage, for the hours worked, up to $10,000 a year for two years, for prior wages up to twice the median (clean power's cap); TAA paid half the gap up to $10,000 over two years for workers 50 and over [EV-WRK-331].
- *Delivery:* state unemployment offices, using the wage records they hold; enrollment offered at the unemployment claim, which is how to lift TAA's low take-up [EV-WRK-332].
- *Precedence:* workers covered by clean power's guarantee get that one (90% of prior hourly pay), not both (`DEC-2026-11-13-06`).
- *Cost:* about $2-4 billion a year (judgment: about 1.1 million long-tenured displaced a year [EV-WRK-315], about half reemployed at lower pay [EV-WRK-317], average payments of $4,000-6,000), about +$0.02-0.04 trillion over FY2030-39, inside AI-4A's legacy envelope of $0.12 trillion [EV-WRK-711].

**C. Clean power's design for everyone:** 90% of prior hourly pay for two years, capped at twice the median.
- *Cost:* several times B (judgment: about +$0.15-0.30 trillion over FY2030-39).
- *What it buys:* one rule for every displaced worker; but its 90% was set for whole-community closures, not ordinary turnover.

## Recommendation: part 1 C, part 2 B

- **Fund what the trials support:** training built with employers for real jobs, and apprenticeship, with a target and published counts so the plan can see whether supply grows as housing, transportation and clean power need.
- **Wage insurance is the best-evaluated help for displaced workers** [EV-WRK-365], and paying it by the hour through offices that already hold wage records keeps it simple.
- **What it costs in burden:** nothing new on workers; enrollment at the unemployment claim.

**What would change this:** completion rates falling as starts double (slow the target, fund mentors); evidence that wage insurance in younger, non-trade populations does not raise employment (narrow eligibility to older workers).
