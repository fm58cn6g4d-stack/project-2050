# Fair pay and real power at work: specification

Role: section 4 of the work dossier (flagship 5). It writes the decided designs as provisions: what each does, its parameters, who is covered, the vehicle, the agency, how it changes the January 2029 law and the evidence it rests on. Provision IDs and their fields are in `provisions.json`. Status: **draft** (stage F), on Espen's decisions `DEC-2026-11-17-01` to `-36` (the work batch and the follow-up note), `-95` (wage insurance, decided for now) and `-96` (the direction on the future labor economy), and on three standing rules: `DEC-2026-11-09-13` (guide, don't police), `DEC-2026-11-10-05` (report, don't gate) and `DEC-2026-11-10-07` (what needs an answer now). Espen approved starting it on November 17, 2026. Costs are reported for stage G and nothing is booked in the budget model; each provision's `cost_source` points to section 6 (`6-cost.md`). Delivery is section 5's.

## How to read this section

- **Status of each provision.**
  - *Decided:* Espen decided it, in a memo option or a yes-or-no point in section 3, cited.
  - *Decided for now:* memo 8 part 2, wage insurance (`DEC-2026-11-17-95`). It is the base, "not the plan's whole answer to a changing labor economy" (`-96`).
  - *Working choice:* a parameter this section sets so the draft is complete. It rests on judgment and can change without reopening a decision.
  - *Provisional, on the recommendation:* items still with Espen, drafted on the recommended option so the specification is complete, as the lead asked on November 18 relaying Espen ("keep proceeding through the steps and bring the decisions to me in the morning"). They are revised if Espen answers otherwise:
    - points 5 and 11, which Espen decided "yes, with a change" and left open for study (`DEC-2026-11-17-18`, `-24`); `-95` and `-96` do not settle them (LAB-14.01, LAB-17.01);
    - memo 10, the measurement of displacement and a standby displacement trigger, which `-96` sends to Espen in this section (`memos/10-displacement-measurement-trigger.md`; LAB-19.01 to 19.05, part E).
- **Commitments and working assumptions.** This section commits to:
  - the floor's schedule, its indexing, the pause and the commission;
  - the end of the three subminimums, with the 14(c) transition;
  - the organizing rules, card check, the penalties, the private right of action and the fair-share title;
  - the standards boards and the first board's deadline;
  - the heat standard, the enforcement rules, the coverage tests and both transparency rights;
  - the unemployment insurance standards and Extended Benefits;
  - wage insurance's eligibility, benefit and four rules;
  - the apprenticeship line and its target, and national service's scale, gate and fallback.

  Thresholds, penalty amounts, the wage-base schedule, dates after enactment and every cost are working assumptions, with ranges in section 3 and section 6 (`6-cost.md`).
- **Dates.** The plan begins in January 2029 (`DEC-2026-10-29-01`). The working timeline: executive steps from January 20, 2029; the 2029 reconciliation act (R1) by about mid-2029; the regular-order labor act signed in 2029, so the first wage step is January 1, 2030 (`DEC-2026-11-17-01`). Calendar dates are when people are paid; fiscal years are for money. Preparation in 2027-28 is labeled as such.
- **Evidence.** The last column lists the evidence IDs a provision rests on, in this dossier's register or another's. "Project arithmetic" and "judgment" mark the project's own figures; their basis is in section 3, "What the options cost".
- **Agenda homes.** The provisions sit under the agenda items the memos and points name (`agenda/agenda.csv`): LAB-1 to LAB-9 and LAB-11 as section 3 left them, and new rows for the points that had none: LAB-12 (wage-law coverage: home care and the overtime threshold, points 2 and 3), LAB-13 (layoff notice, point 4), LAB-14 (severance, point 5, provisional), LAB-15 (predictable schedules, point 6), LAB-16 (federal workers' bargaining, point 7), LAB-17 (codetermination, point 11, provisional), LAB-18 (wage insurance, memo 8 part 2) and LAB-19 (displacement measurement and the standby trigger, memo 10, provisional). **Why this split.**
- *Reconciliation carries money, not rules without a budget effect* (the Byrd rule). The wage mandate was barred from reconciliation by the 2021 parliamentarian ruling (`legacy/domains/domain-labor.md`, LAB-4), so the floor and every labor-law rule go to the labor act. Unemployment insurance's standards are conditions on employers' federal tax credit, which has a budget effect, so they are drafted for R1; any the parliamentarian strikes move to the labor act (working choice).
- *Contingent money.* Two R1 items depend on later events: the credit (LAB-5.01) pays only for wage steps the labor act enacts, and the service fallback (LAB-9.06) pays only if no authorization passes by December 31, 2030. A contingent effective date is ordinary in reconciliation, as the legacy credit's design assumed; Byrd eligibility is to check for both.
- *The budget model's rails.* The model books the legacy LAB-5, LAB-8 and LAB-9 rows on rail R2. Point 16 moves this flagship's money to R1; rebooking is stage G's.

**Quits for good cause: a working reading, pending Espen.** `-95` defines a displaced worker as "separated against one's will for a reason other than misconduct, on the state's own unemployment finding", and the note's eligible list names lack-of-work separations and discharges not for misconduct. Neither says what happens to a worker who quits and whom the state finds had good cause. Section 4 drafts a middle reading (LAB-18.03) and puts it to Espen:
- *In:* quits for good cause connected with the work or attributable to the employer, such as unpaid wages, a large pay cut or unsafe conditions. These are close to a separation against the worker's will, and many states already limit good cause to them [EV-WRK-415].
- *Out:* quits for personal good cause, such as following a spouse's move, even where LAB-11.01's compelling-reason rule (a working choice) makes them eligible for unemployment insurance [EV-WRK-417].
- *No new finding either way:* states already sort every separation into lack of work, a quit or a discharge [EV-WRK-414] [EV-WRK-416], so including or excluding quits uses the state's finding as `-95` requires.
- *Cost:* memo 8 B's $2-4 billion a year rests on the survey's layoff counts [EV-WRK-315], which include no quits. Work-connected quits add a small cost outside that basis (judgment; section 6 prices it at about $1.2 billion over FY2030-39, `6-cost.md`, part 4).

The alternatives are to exclude all quits, the literal reading of "against one's will", or to include every quit the state finds was for good cause.

## Where particular groups fall

Groups the provisions do not name, placed by working choices that follow from the decisions:
- **Public employees.** The wage floor, overtime and the heat standard reach state and local workers as the Fair Labor Standards Act and OSH Act already do; state public-sector bargaining is outside the labor act, and *Janus* bars public-sector fees (LAB-1A.01). Federal workers' bargaining is LAB-16.01.
- **Farmworkers.** The floor, the end of the youth wage and the heat standard reach them; the labor act's exclusion of agricultural workers (29 U.S.C. 152(3)) and wage law's farm overtime exemptions are not changed in the first bill. They gain the right to discuss pay (LAB-7.09), which the labor act does not give them. Their visas' wage rules follow the decided floors (immigration, shared rule 8). This is a gap for later work, not a decision.
- **Domestic workers.** Covered by the floor and, for those employed by agencies, by home care coverage (LAB-12.01); the labor act's exclusion of domestic service stays. They gain the pay-discussion right (LAB-7.09).
- **App-based and other contract workers.** Covered by unemployment insurance and the labor act unless they pass the ABC test (LAB-1.07, 11.02); under wage law, case by case on the statutory test (LAB-7.07).
- **People with disabilities.** 14(c) ends with a funded transition and no loss of day services (LAB-4.08, 4.09). Wage insurance, apprenticeship and service are open to them on the same terms.
- **Workers under 20.** The youth wage ends with the first step (LAB-4.06). Young entrants have no three-year job to lose, so wage insurance misses them; memo 10 asks Espen about that gap.
- **Immigrant workers.** Every provision covers workers whatever their status, as wage and safety law does today. Retaliation by immigration threat is retaliation (LAB-7.06), and immigration's deferred action and firewall apply (IMM-4.17, 4.11). Wage insurance and unemployment insurance follow immigration's eligibility table (shared rule 3, `DEC-2026-11-12-07`).
- **Rural places.** The floor bites harder where wages are lower; the commission measures rural counties first (LAB-4.04) [EV-WRK-405]. Wage insurance, unemployment insurance and apprenticeship reach rural workers through the state UI offices and American Job Centers; USDA's local channels hold no labor program (`dossiers/foundations/rural-delivery.md`), so section 5 weighs them as front doors only.
- **The territories.** Wage law applies in every territory as today. The floor's schedule in the territories is LAB-4.10, added in section 5 and revised in the milestone review's fix step 2 (`5-delivery.md`, part 5), provisional for Espen: on the recommendation, the national schedule capped at 75% of each territory's median in Puerto Rico, Guam, the Virgin Islands and the Northern Mariana Islands, because $17 in 2035 would sit at about 93% of Puerto Rico's median and 105% of the Northern Mariana Islands' [EV-WRK-455] [EV-WRK-460]; American Samoa on its own path in today's law. Puerto Rico and the Virgin Islands run federal-state unemployment insurance, so LAB-11 and LAB-18 reach them.
- **Tribal nations.** Tribal governments' own workers are outside the labor act's definition of employer in most circuits (to check in drafting). Unemployment and wage insurance reach tribal workers through state agencies. LAB-8's grants are open to tribal workforce programs.
