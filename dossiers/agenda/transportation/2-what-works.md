# Transportation: what works

Role: section 2 of the transportation dossier: the evidence on the tools section 3 weighs, at home and abroad, and the failures to avoid. Status: draft (stage C, step 7, PR 2). It is a conversion. The legacy files had no research section, so this one gathers what they relied on and adds outside evidence only where a choice in section 3 turns on it (`charter/STANDARDS.md`, "What needs an answer now").

## 1. Paying for roads

- **Indexing works and is common.**
  - **Variable rates.** Twenty-six states and D.C. now have gas taxes that change with prices or costs. Twenty-four still have fixed rates (Institute on Taxation and Economic Policy) [EV-MOV-123]. NCSL counts the same 26 states and D.C. [EV-MOV-124].
  - **Recent increases.** Thirty-five states and D.C. have raised gas taxes since 2013 [EV-MOV-124].
  - **What it means for the plan.** Indexing is no longer unusual; the federal rate's fixed cents are the exception.
- **People support repair-dedicated fuel taxes more than general ones.**
  - **2024 survey.** In the Mineta Transportation Institute's survey, 74% supported a 10-cent gas tax increase dedicated to maintenance. Only 35% supported one for "transportation" in general, and the two mileage fees drew 51% and 39% [EV-MOV-129].
  - **2025 survey.** The results were similar: 75% for the maintenance version [EV-MOV-134].
  - **Bearing on memo 1.** The steps are written as repair money (the "Fix-It-First" steps), and renewal is paid first.
- **Per-mile charges for electric cars are being tried, at small scale.**
  - **Oregon.** OReGO had 1,160 vehicles enrolled on June 30, 2026, at 2 cents a mile with a fuel-tax credit [EV-MOV-116]. A 2025 law phases in a mandatory charge for some vehicles from July 2027 to July 2028, with a flat-fee option. That law is summarized by Oregon DOT staff; the statute itself is not yet read [EV-MOV-117].
  - **Utah.** It had 9,703 enrolled vehicles in July 2025, up 45% in a year [EV-MOV-119], and projects program costs of 14.2% of revenue in FY2027 [EV-MOV-120].
  - **Collection cost.** It is the open risk. Oregon staff put the fuel tax's collection cost near 0.3% and the weight-mile tax's near 11%, and expect road usage charges to cost more at first [EV-MOV-118].
  - **Bearing on memo 1.** It supports reading the odometer at registration rather than building a new reporting system. The design's 10% ceiling on collection cost sits between Oregon's and Utah's experience.
- **Weight-distance taxes exist and have known weaknesses.**
  - **Oregon.** Its weight-mile tax covers trucks over 26,000 lb, about 277,000 of them. It raised about $252 million in FY2013, 15% of the state DOT's revenue [EV-MOV-121].
  - **Evasion.** A study commissioned by the state DOT estimated evasion at 5.09% to 11.33%, depending on the method [EV-MOV-122]. Collection cost runs about 11% [EV-MOV-118].
  - **Bearing on memo 2.** A federal charge built on records carriers already file (the interstate fuel-tax agreement) avoids a separate filing system. It still needs audits, and section 5 sizes them.

## 2. Building and repair

- **Adding lanes adds driving.**
  - **Duranton and Turner.** Driving on interstates rises about one-for-one with new lane-kilometers, which is why added lanes rarely relieve congestion for long (Duranton and Turner, American Economic Review, 2011) [EV-MOV-114].
  - **California's review.** It found short-run elasticities of 0.3 to 0.6 and long-run ones mostly 0.6 to just over 1.0, with a best estimate near 1.0 (Handy and Boarnet, for the California Air Resources Board, 2014) [EV-MOV-115].
  - **Bearing on memo 5.** It supports a lower federal share for new general-purpose lanes, the one condition memo 5 keeps.
- **Costs eat buying power.**
  - **FHWA's index.** Its highway construction cost index (2003 = 1) stood at 2.04 in mid-2021, peaked at 3.36 in late 2024 and was 3.13 in early 2026 [EV-MOV-132].
  - **Eno's estimate.** Measured from mid-2021, it puts the 2021 law's lost buying power at 32% [EV-MOV-133], matching BTS's estimate [EV-MOV-008].
  - **Bearing on memo 3.** Nominal levels lose value fast. Section 6 states whether the plan's figures are indexed (A-5 says they are not).
- **Grants stall between selection and signing.** GAO found a quarter of surveyed awardees without signed agreements [EV-MOV-009]. The legacy design answers with a 180-day agreement clock (division E). That is a delivery fix for section 5, not a design fork.

## 3. Safety

- **Proven countermeasures exist.**
  - **Roundabouts.** They cut fatal and injury crashes 82% where they replace two-way stops and 78% where they replace signals (FHWA) [EV-MOV-126].
  - **Road diets.** Fewer, safer lanes cut crashes 19% to 47% (FHWA) [EV-MOV-127].
  - **Caveat.** These are FHWA's summaries of its crash-modification research; the underlying studies are not yet in the register.
  - **Bearing on the design.** It supports limiting safety money to proven countermeasures where a state's targets regress, the Safe System call kept in section 3 (A-25).

## 4. Transit

- **Federal operating help.**
  - **Pandemic relief.** It filled the fare gap: $69.5 billion in three acts, raising the federal share of operating cost from 8% to 17% [EV-MOV-103].
  - **Commuter railroads.** GAO found that 17 of 31 were still spending relief money in FY2023, while their operating costs rose 28% in nominal terms and 9% in real terms [EV-MOV-128].
  - **What the register lacks.** No evaluation of the pre-1998 operating aid or of conditions on it (memo 4).
- **Construction cost is the binding problem for expansion.**
  - **The Transit Costs Project.** It ranks the U.S. the sixth-costliest of 59 countries for rail transit construction [EV-MOV-125]. It is one research group's analysis and not yet independently corroborated, so the dossier presents it as that group's finding.
  - **Bearing on memo 6 and section 4.** Gates and unit-cost review matter more than the size of the program.

## 5. Passenger rail at home and abroad

- **Europe.** The European Court of Auditors reviewed EU-funded high-speed lines [EV-MOV-130]. It found:
  - an average cost of €25 million a kilometer;
  - €25.1 billion of overruns at the line level;
  - trains running at 45% of design speed on average;
  - 3 of 7 lines below the passenger level that justifies high speed.

  The lesson for memo 6: choose corridors where demand justifies the speed, and pay by stage.
- **At home.**
  - **The pipeline.** The Corridor ID program selected 69 corridors in 2023 [EV-MOV-108].
  - **Federal review.** The administration is reviewing all of them, and FRA has lost 22% of its staff [EV-MOV-109].
  - **Long-distance routes.** FRA's long-distance study found no program to fund the routes it recommends [EV-MOV-111].
  - **The lesson for memo 6.** Money is not the only gap: so are federal staff and sponsors that are ready to build. The design's gates, regional commissions and readiness reserve address that.

## 6. Failures to avoid (from the legacy files)

- **The all-vehicle per-mile charge.** The legacy design's earlier version replaced fuel taxes with a per-mile charge on every vehicle. It hid a large increase, charged trucks like sedans and could not have been collected in time (`3-design.md`, memo 1).
- **NEVI.** The federal charging program spread $5 billion across 50 state plans under highway procedures. It obligated little before it was suspended, and part of it was rescinded [EV-MOV-014]. The design replaces it with national auctions paid for uptime (section 3, point 4).
- **Obligation cliffs.** Money obligated in the year it is enacted piles up and then drops [EV-MOV-217]. The design ramps it over FY2029-31.

| Added lanes add driving about one-for-one | Memo 5: keep the lower federal share for new lanes |
