# Transportation: the problem

Role: section 1 of the transportation dossier: what is wrong with how the country pays for and runs its roads, transit and passenger rail, for whom, and how large. Status: draft (stage C, step 7, PR 2). It is a conversion: the legacy design (`legacy/domains/transportation-moving-america-act-2031-draft-2.md`) stated most of these facts without sources. Each is now traced to its source or dropped, and the legacy figures superseded by newer data are noted.

Outcomes this section bears on:
- OUT-05, clean, affordable power and a prepared country: transportation is the largest source of emissions;
- OUT-11, the fiscal tests: the trust fund's gap is the plan's to close or to fund.

`charter/outcomes.md` has no transportation outcome; section 3, point 7, asks Espen whether to add one.

## In short

- **The money runs out in 2028.** The trust fund that pays for highways and transit is short in 2027 for transit and in 2028 for highways. Most of the fuel taxes that feed it expire on September 30, 2028 [EV-MOV-002] [EV-MOV-016].
- **Repair falls behind.** Roads and bridges wear out faster than they are rebuilt, the Interstate's rebuilding is unfunded, and construction costs ate a third of the 2021 law's added money [EV-MOV-005] [EV-MOV-010] [EV-MOV-008].
- **Deaths stay high.** About 36,600 people died on the roads in 2025 [EV-MOV-007].
- **Transit and rail have no stable footing.** Transit's fare revenue is a quarter below 2019 while its costs are up, and pandemic aid is spent [EV-MOV-100] [EV-MOV-103]. Passenger rail has no dedicated revenue and a pipeline of corridors that is early and under federal review [EV-MOV-108] [EV-MOV-109].
- **It costs households and the climate.** Transportation is a household's second-largest expense and the largest source of U.S. greenhouse emissions [EV-MOV-013] [EV-MOV-011].

## 1. The money runs out in 2028

- **Taxes.** The federal gasoline excise is 18.3 cents a gallon and diesel 24.3 cents. Current law cuts both to 4.3 cents after September 30, 2028 [EV-MOV-016]. It also stops crediting fuel and truck taxes to the Highway Trust Fund for taxes received after that date, with a short tail to July 1, 2029 for earlier liability [EV-MOV-004].
- **Shortfall.** Even if the taxes continue, CBO's June 2026 baseline projects a cumulative shortfall:
  - from 2027 in the transit account ($2,921 million);
  - from 2028 in the highway account ($11,914 million);
  - reaching $45,189 million and $86,010 million by 2031 [EV-MOV-002].

  Under the House committee's bill the 2031 shortfalls grow to $99.5 billion and $48.2 billion [EV-MOV-015]. These are CBO's projections (analysis); section 2 adds an independent projection.
- **Programs.** The 2021 authorization, extended by the Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act, 2027, expires on December 11, 2026 [EV-MOV-001]. The House committee approved a five-year bill, H.R. 8870 (BUILD America 250), 62 to 2 on May 22, 2026 [EV-MOV-003]. It provides $470.7 billion of contract authority over 2027-2031 and authorizes $109 billion more [EV-MOV-001].
  - It has not passed the House, and the Senate has no bill (`3-design.md`, "The baseline in 2029").
- **Who it hurts** (judgment, not yet evidenced). A lapse would stop reimbursements to states and transit agencies for work under way, so contractors and workers would feel it first, and riders and drivers later, as projects slow.

The legacy design described this cliff in the same terms. Its FY2031 shortfalls ($99.5B and $48.2B) match CBO's [EV-MOV-015]. Its FY2030 figure ($108.0B) is not carried, since no choice turns on it.

## 2. Repair falls behind

- **The backlog.** FHWA's latest Conditions and Performance report (25th edition, 2018 data) estimates a highway and bridge investment backlog of $1.1 trillion, $852 billion of it on existing assets [EV-MOV-005].
  - Doing all cost-beneficial work would take $151.1 billion a year from all governments, 31.3% more than the $115.1 billion a year spent in 2014-18 [EV-MOV-005].
  - The share of federal-aid pavement miles with poor ride quality rose from 15.8% to 22.6% between 2008 and 2018. Weighted by traffic, it rose from 14.6% to 15.2% [EV-MOV-017].
- **Bridges.** In the 2026 National Bridge Inventory, 41,148 of 624,939 bridges were in poor condition [EV-MOV-006], down from 41,685 a year earlier [EV-MOV-018]. The legacy design's "41,677 poor bridges" was a 2025 count from a secondary source; the inventory itself replaces it.
- **The Interstate.** The National Academies estimated in 2018 that renewing and modernizing it would take $45-70 billion a year over 20 years, against a recent $20-25 billion [EV-MOV-010]. No federal program is dedicated to it (`3-design.md`, memo 3).
- **Buying power.** BTS estimates that rising highway construction costs cut the value of the 2021 law's funding by 32% over FY2022-25, with costs up 63% from FY2021 to FY2025 [EV-MOV-008]. This is the second Trump administration's analysis; section 2 adds an independent cost measure.
- **Who it hurts.** Drivers and freight users pay for rough roads in vehicle wear and time; the register does not yet hold an estimate of that cost, and no choice in section 3 turns on it.

## 3. Deaths stay high

NHTSA estimates 36,640 traffic deaths in 2025, 6.7% fewer than the 39,254 in 2024. The death rate was 1.10 per 100 million vehicle miles, down from 1.19, and pedestrian deaths fell an estimated 8% [EV-MOV-007]. These are early estimates, to be replaced by the final 2025 count (FARS) when NHTSA publishes it.

**Who it hurts.** Pedestrians and people on local streets. Most public road miles are local: counties, towns and cities own 3,127,662 of the 4,208,454 miles of public road, about three-quarters, by this dossier's sum of FHWA's 2024 table [EV-MOV-012]. Local governments get federal safety and repair money mostly through their states.

## 4. Money is slow to reach the ground

- **Unsigned agreements.** A GAO survey in 2024-25 found that 23% of surveyed awardees (49 of 213) of fiscal year 2022 discretionary grants under the 2021 law had no signed grant agreement [EV-MOV-009].
- **Changed projects.** DOT's own analysis of fiscal year 2019 grants found that 31 of 32 projects changed between selection and signing [EV-MOV-009].

## 5. Transit has lost its footing

- **Fares and costs.** National Transit Database figures, in nominal dollars [EV-MOV-100]:
  - transit's operating expenses rose from $50.7 billion in 2019 to $70.1 billion in 2025;
  - fare revenue fell from $16.1 billion to $12.0 billion over the same years.

  In 2025 dollars, CRS puts 2024 fare revenue at $11.8 billion against $20.2 billion in 2019 [EV-MOV-102].
- **Riders.** Boardings were 8.05 billion in 2025 against 9.88 billion in 2019 [EV-MOV-101]. Late-2025 ridership was 81% of late 2019, in an APTA figure that CRS repeats [EV-MOV-102].
- **The federal share.** Pandemic relief gave transit $69.5 billion. The federal share of transit operating costs rose from 8% in 2019 to 17% in 2024 [EV-MOV-103]; that money is spent.
  - In GAO's review of commuter railroads, 17 of 31 were still using relief money in FY2023 [EV-MOV-128].
  - Federal law has not let large systems use formula money to run service since 1998 (`3-design.md`, memo 4).
- **Repair.** The federal Conditions and Performance report estimates a transit reinvestment backlog of $101 billion in 2018 [EV-MOV-104]. Reaching good repair would take $20.3 billion a year against $13.5 billion spent [EV-MOV-104]. CRS restates the backlog as $127.8 billion in 2025 dollars [EV-MOV-105], the figure the legacy design used. The data are from 2018: a re-check, not a blocker.
- **Building.** An independent count found 47 projects on FTA's capital grants dashboard in November 2026, with one new grant agreement since January 20, 2025 and 30 of the 47 delayed, by a median of 12 months (The Transport Politic, 2026) [EV-MOV-112] [EV-MOV-113]. Its delay count is one analyst's calculation, not FTA's.
- **Cost.** U.S. rail transit is the sixth-costliest of 59 countries to build, with New York 8 to 12 times a low-cost baseline (Transit Costs Project) [EV-MOV-125].
- **Who it hurts** (judgment). Riders who have no car and depend on frequent service lose most when systems cut service to close deficits. The count of households without a car is in "Evidence still needed".

## 6. Passenger rail has no footing of its own

- **Amtrak.** It carried 34.5 million trips in FY2025, with an adjusted operating loss of $598.4 million and 87% cost recovery, by its own measure [EV-MOV-106]. Counting everything, Eno puts the FY2025 operating loss at $1.76 billion [EV-MOV-107].
- **The corridor pipeline.**
  - In December 2023 FRA selected 69 corridors in 44 states for its Corridor Identification program [EV-MOV-108]:
    - 15 upgrades;
    - 47 new or extended routes;
    - 7 high-speed lines.
  - In September 2026 FRA's administrator said the agency is "looking at all of the corridors", and FRA's staff is down 22% (Trains, 2026) [EV-MOV-109]. The current administration's review is a risk to the pipeline the plan builds on.
  - The legacy file's reading that no corridor had reached the last planning step by March 2026 [EV-MOV-225] is still to be traced to FRA.
- **Long-distance service.** FRA's 2025 study of daily long-distance service identified routes that would add about 23,200 route miles and reach 61 more metro areas and 39 million more people [EV-MOV-110]. It found "no sustained financial support or program" to build or run them [EV-MOV-111].
- **Cost abroad.** The European Court of Auditors found European high-speed lines averaging €25 million a kilometer, with large overruns and some lines carrying too few passengers [EV-MOV-130]. Building high-speed rail is not cheap anywhere; section 2 draws the lessons.

## 7. It costs households and the climate

- **Households.** Transportation was 16.5% of average household spending in 2025, second only to housing at 33.9% [EV-MOV-013]. Households without a car, or with one they can barely afford, have the fewest choices; the register does not yet hold a count of them (below).
- **Climate.** Transportation was the largest source of U.S. greenhouse emissions in 2022, at 28.4% [EV-MOV-011].
- **Charging.** The federal charging program (NEVI) had obligated more than $526 million by early 2025, with contracts for nearly 4,000 fast-charging ports at 990 locations. Congress rescinded just over $503 million of it in January 2026 [EV-MOV-014].
