# Memo 3: How large, and how the money is split

Role: decision memo for Espen, fork 3 of the transportation design (`../3-design.md`). Status: decided by Espen: A, as recommended (November 10, 2026; `DEC-2026-11-10-08`). One of six in the batch (`README.md`). It goes to Espen because the choice is mainly about money (`charter/STANDARDS.md`, "Fiscal rule in dossiers"). Sources are named in plain text; the bracketed tags point to the evidence register (`../evidence.json`). "The plan's model" is the legacy Moving America design's own arithmetic (2026), a project estimate.

## In short

The plan as filed authorizes $936.57 billion for FY2032-36. The legacy design sized it under a self-imposed ceiling on general-fund spending, 0.206% of GDP. That rule is gone: dossiers now report their cost and the plan is balanced as a whole. So, should the program stay this size, or grow where the ceiling held it back?

**Recommendation: keep the size and the split (A)**, publish the old ceiling only as a comparison, and let the financing settlement decide whether the plan-wide margin can afford option B.

## The question

How large should the 2031 bill be, and how should new fuel-tax money be split between highways, transit and rail?

**What you already chose.** Option A is the plan as filed (Draft 2's calls A-4, A-7, A-8, A-17, A-18 and A-23).

## What the old ceiling was

The 0.206% figure restates what surface transportation drew from the general fund in its peak year, FY2026 (the American Rail Competitiveness Act draft, 2026) [EV-MOV-213]:
- $60.4 billion of rail and transit advance appropriations and the trust-fund transfer;
- plus the yearly rail and transit grants.

No law or CBO rule sets it [EV-MOV-213]. The legacy design used it in three ways:
- as a trigger in the bill;
- to count the general-fund grants against it;
- to start Interstate and Bridge Renewal's new money at zero in FY2032, a year with "no room" (Moving America Draft 2, 2026) [EV-MOV-212].

## The options

**A. Keep the size and the split (as chosen; recommended).**
- $936.57 billion over FY2032-36. Users pay $545.84 billion and the general fund $390.73 billion, or 0.180% of GDP a year, falling to 0.026% by FY2042-46 (the plan's model) [EV-MOV-200].
- New fuel-tax money goes 35% to highways, 50% to transit and 15% to rail through FY2036, then 45 / 40 / 15.
- The bill keeps a capped $25 billion backstop for accounts that run short when price pauses delay the fuel-tax steps (the plan's model) [EV-MOV-219].
- The ceiling becomes a published comparison, not a trigger.

**B. Resize where the ceiling held the program back.**
- Interstate and Bridge Renewal starts with new money in FY2032 instead of ramping from zero. Each $2 billion a year adds about 0.005 points of GDP to the draw (the plan's model) [EV-MOV-212].
- *For people:* bridges and Interstate segments rebuilt sooner.
- *Risk:* more general-fund cost in FY2032-36, for the settlement to balance.

**C. Smaller: the first fallback tier.**
- The legacy design's first fallback tier cuts the 2031 bill to $865.02 billion and the average draw from 0.180% to 0.147% of GDP (the plan's model) [EV-MOV-226].
- *Risk:* it cuts expansion first, mostly rail and transit.

**On the split.** The split does not change the total draw; it decides which account pays for itself first. Keeping the first split forever would leave transit with $5.9 billion it cannot spend while highway repair is only 80% user-paid in FY2042-46. Moving it in FY2037, as chosen, strands nothing (the plan's model) [EV-MOV-211]. The memo sees no reason to change it.

## Recommendation

**A.** It rests on the plan's model and on judgment.
- **Option A is already the design's central case.** Its cost is reported to the settlement, under the project's rule that dossiers report costs rather than shrink to a ceiling.
- **Option B is a real choice for later.** It belongs to the settlement once the rest of the plan is priced, because it is mainly about money and the evidence on Interstate need is the same either way.
- **What changes in the bill.** Section 108 keeps the backstop and the yearly report, and drops the 0.206% trigger language.

**What it does not settle.** Whether the plan as a whole can carry transportation's draw. Section 6 reports transportation's yearly cost and its permanent cost from FY2040 for the settlement.
