# Memo 1: Who pays for road repair

Role: decision memo for Espen, fork 1 of the transportation design (`../3-design.md`). Status: for decision (one of six in this batch, `README.md`). Sources are named in plain text; the bracketed tags point to the evidence register (`../evidence.json`). The figures marked "the plan's model" are the legacy Moving America design's own arithmetic (2026), a project estimate with no demand response.

## In short

Federal fuel taxes have not gone up since 1993, and electric cars pay none. The plan as filed fixes that with three pieces:
- fuel taxes that rise with prices, plus ten small steps;
- a per-mile charge on electric cars, at what an efficient gas car pays;
- a later "fossil premium" on gasoline and diesel that electric cars never pay.

**Recommendation: keep it (A).** The alternatives either cost the general fund much more or leave the long-run draw higher.

## The question

How should drivers pay to keep roads and bridges in repair from 2029 on: higher fuel taxes, a charge on electric cars, the general fund, or a mix?

**What you already chose, and what this would change.** The plan as filed (MR1, MR2, MR3; Draft 2's calls A-9, A-10, A-11 and A-28) is option A. Nothing changes if you keep it.

## What is wrong today

- The federal gasoline excise is 18.3 cents a gallon and diesel 24.3 cents, unchanged since 1993 (Internal Revenue Code, 26 U.S.C. 4081) [EV-MOV-016] [EV-MOV-202]. Their real value has fallen with prices, while cars use less fuel and electric cars use none.
- Current law cuts both to 4.3 cents after September 30, 2028 (same law) [EV-MOV-016], and stops crediting them to the trust fund (26 U.S.C. 9503) [EV-MOV-004].

## What the evidence says

- **States.** Twenty-six states and D.C. already have gas taxes that change with prices or costs (Institute on Taxation and Economic Policy, 2025; NCSL, 2025) [EV-MOV-123] [EV-MOV-124].
- **Public support.** It depends on the purpose. In the Mineta Transportation Institute's surveys, 74% (2024) and 75% (2025) supported a 10-cent increase dedicated to maintenance, against 35% for one for transportation in general [EV-MOV-129] [EV-MOV-134].
- **Per-mile charges for electric cars.** They run in Oregon and Utah at small scale, 1,160 and 9,703 vehicles (Oregon DOT, 2026; Utah DOT, 2025) [EV-MOV-116] [EV-MOV-119]. Early collection costs are high: Utah projects 14.2% of revenue (Utah DOT) [EV-MOV-120]. That is why option A reads the odometer at registration rather than building a new system.

## The options

**A. Index, ten steps, an electric-car charge and a later premium (as chosen; recommended).**
- **Fuel taxes.**
  - Made permanent and indexed to prices from October 2029.
  - Ten steps of 3 cents (in 2029 dollars), one a year.
  - A step is deferred, not lost, in any year gas averages over $4.00 or rose over 20%.
- **Electric cars** pay per mile at what a 40-mpg gas car pays in fuel tax.
  - The odometer is read at registration; no location data.
  - Plug-in hybrids pay 30% of the car rate.
- **The premium.** From FY2040 to FY2044, five more 2-cent steps on gasoline and diesel only.
- *For drivers:*
  - A 25-mpg car driven 12,000 miles pays $88 a year in federal fuel tax today, $142 in FY2032 and $225 in FY2036 (the plan's model, 2026) [EV-MOV-201].
  - In 2029 dollars the gas tax ends near what the 1993 rate was worth (the plan's model) [EV-MOV-202].
  - An electric car pays $77 a year in FY2031 and $141 in FY2036, the same as a 40-mpg hybrid (the plan's model) [EV-MOV-204].
- *For the budget:* the general fund pays 0.180% of GDP a year over FY2032-36, falling to 0.026% by FY2042-46 (the plan's model) [EV-MOV-200].
- *Risk:*
  - A visible tax increase for most drivers.
  - Three price pauses would delay about $53.3 billion of receipts, which is why the design keeps a capped $25 billion backstop (the plan's model) [EV-MOV-219].

**B. The same, without the premium.**
- *For drivers:* gasoline stops rising after the tenth step.
- *For the budget:* the FY2042-46 draw is 0.065% of GDP instead of 0.026% (the plan's model) [EV-MOV-206].
- *Risk:* lower than A's, because the premium is enacted in 2029 and starts only in FY2040, so it can be repealed before it bites. Its receipts are an upper bound anyway, since the model assumes no change in driving [EV-MOV-206].

**C. Indexing only, plus the electric-car charge.**
- *For drivers:* the smallest increase.
- *For the budget:* the general fund pays 0.267% of GDP over FY2032-36. Highway repair is only 62% paid by users in FY2042-46 (the plan's model) [EV-MOV-203].

**D. Smaller steps (2 cents).**
- *For the budget:* 0.209% of GDP, with highway repair 85% user-paid in FY2042-46 (the plan's model) [EV-MOV-203].

**Not offered: a per-mile charge on every car in place of fuel taxes.** The earlier legacy design tried it. It hid a large increase, charged a heavy truck the same per mile as a sedan, and could not have been collected in time (Draft 2's review of the earlier design). The charge is kept for electric cars only.

## Recommendation

**A.** It rests on judgment and the plan's model, not on outside evidence:
- **Fairness.** The steps make road users, not the general fund, pay for repair. The premium makes fossil fuel pay more than road use costs from 2040, while electric cars never pay it.
- **Choosing B instead.** B is the reasonable choice if you would rather leave the premium to a later Congress: it changes nothing before FY2040.
- **Pegging electric cars to a 25-mpg car instead of 40** would raise only about $5.7 billion more over five years. It would also charge electric cars more per mile than an efficient gas car, so the memo does not recommend it (the plan's model) [EV-MOV-205].

**What it does not settle.** Fuel taxes take a larger share of income from lower-income households. Whether the plan as a whole leaves them ahead is the fairness test (D3), run by the household model across all flagships in the financing settlement. Section 7 reports transportation's part.

**Evidence still needed.** The fuel-tax burden by income group, from the household model (section 7).
