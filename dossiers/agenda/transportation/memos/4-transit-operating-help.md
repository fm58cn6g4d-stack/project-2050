# Memo 4: Federal help to run transit

Role: decision memo for Espen, fork 4 of the transportation design (`../3-design.md`). Status: for decision (one of six in this batch, `README.md`). Sources are named in plain text; the bracketed tags point to the evidence register (`../evidence.json`). "The plan's model" is the legacy design's own arithmetic (2026), a project estimate. Facts on fares, ridership and agency budgets are in section 1.

## In short

Since 1998, federal money has paid to build and repair transit in large cities, but not to run it. Pandemic aid filled the gap left by lost fares, and that aid is spent. The plan as filed adds $35 billion over five years to help large systems keep service running, with conditions.

**Recommendation: keep the money, and keep the conditions that protect riders, but drop the ones that mostly add paperwork (D).**

## What is wrong today

- **Fares.** Fare revenue fell from $16.1 billion in 2019 to $12.0 billion in 2025, while operating costs rose from $50.7 billion to $70.1 billion (National Transit Database, nominal dollars) [EV-MOV-100].
- **Relief.** Pandemic relief of $69.5 billion raised the federal share of operating costs from 8% to 17% (Congressional Research Service, 2026) [EV-MOV-103], and it is spent.

## The question

Should federal money help pay to operate transit in large cities, and if so, how much and on what conditions?

**What you already chose.** Option B is the plan as filed (Draft 2's call A-3). Draft 2 records it as decided by Espen. Under the project's October 30 rule (`DEC-2026-10-30-02`) it is weighed again here.

## The options

**A. No operating help.**
- *For the budget:* the general-fund draw falls to 0.164% of GDP (the plan's model, 2026) [EV-MOV-210].
- *Risk:* systems facing deficits cut service, or use repair money to run trains, which grows the repair backlog.

**B. A $35 billion tier with conditions (as chosen).**
- $6.0 billion rising to $8.0 billion a year over FY2032-36, for urbanized areas of 200,000 people or more (Moving America Draft 2, 2026) [EV-MOV-220].
- **The money is shared out by:**
  - 50% hours of service;
  - 30% trips;
  - 20% low-income residents.
- No area receives more than 25% of its operating cost net of fares. In its first year it covers 11.9% of net operating cost nationally (Moving America Draft 2, 2026) [EV-MOV-220].
- **Conditions.** An area must:
  1. keep state and local operating support at least at its three-year average, rising with prices (maintenance of effort; the transit division's draft text, 2026) [EV-MOV-221];
  2. run at least 95% of its FY2028-30 service hours (a service floor);
  3. not be under an FTA direction to fix the condition of its assets, under the plan's new repair rule (49 U.S.C. 5326(e) as the plan amends it);
  4. not raise the share of its formula money spent as "preventive maintenance" above its FY2027-29 average;
  5. publish a plan if its cost per service hour outruns public-sector wages by more than 1.5 points for three years (the transit division's draft text, 2026) [EV-MOV-221].
- *For the budget:* the average draw stays at 0.180% of GDP (the plan's model) [EV-MOV-200].

**C. Larger: $70 billion.**
- *For people:* room for service increases, not just keeping service.
- *For the budget:* the average draw rises to 0.196% of GDP, with a peak year of 0.223% (the plan's model) [EV-MOV-210].

**D. B's money with lighter conditions (recommended).**
- **Keep conditions 1, 2 and 3.**
  - Maintenance of effort stops federal money from replacing state money.
  - The service floor makes the money reach riders.
  - The repair rule keeps operating help from rewarding a system that lets its assets decay, which is the operating gap's other face.
- **Replace conditions 4 and 5 with a yearly published report** of preventive-maintenance spending and cost per service hour, with a review if an area's figures move sharply.
- *Burden it removes:* two tests an agency must track and certify each year.
- *What it gives up:* a hard stop on shifting capital money to operations, and on cost growth.

## Recommendation

**D.** It follows the design lens (`DEC-2026-11-09-13`): prefer published counts with a review trigger over tests enforced up front. Conditions 1 and 2 protect riders directly. Conditions 4 and 5 target agency accounting and costs, which public reporting can watch at lower burden. This rests on judgment, not on an outside evaluation: no study of the 1998 rule's conditions is in the register.

**What it does not settle.** Whether $35 billion is enough. It is sized as a share of operating cost, not to any single system's gap. Section 7 tests it against the reported gaps of the largest systems.
