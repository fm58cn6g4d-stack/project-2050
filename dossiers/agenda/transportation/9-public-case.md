# Transportation: the public case

Role: section 9 of the transportation dossier: a plain-language brief, messages, hard questions and a can-say list, built only from sections 1-8 and never ahead of them. Status: draft (stage C, step 7, PR 3), on `DEC-2026-11-10-08`. Every number traces to its section and evidence ID in the source table at the end. Sources are named in plain text before each tag.

## The brief

**Fix it first, then build.**

America's roads, bridges, buses and trains are paid for by a federal gas tax that hasn't gone up since 1993. It is 18.3 cents a gallon, worth less every year, and electric cars pay none of it (the Internal Revenue Code) [EV-MOV-016]. Under current law, most of it expires after September 30, 2028 (the Internal Revenue Code) [EV-MOV-016]. Even if it doesn't, the trust fund runs short for transit in 2027 and for highways in 2028 (Congressional Budget Office, 2026) [EV-MOV-002].

Meanwhile:
- **Bridges.** 41,148 bridges are in poor condition (Federal Highway Administration, 2026) [EV-MOV-006].
- **Deaths.** 36,640 people died on our roads in 2025 (National Highway Traffic Safety Administration, 2026) [EV-MOV-007].
- **Transit.** Fare revenue is a quarter below 2019 while costs have risen, and the pandemic aid that bridged the gap is spent (National Transit Database; Congressional Research Service, 2026) [EV-MOV-100] [EV-MOV-103].

**What the plan does.** Four laws, from 2029:
1. **Keep the money flowing.** Extend today's fuel taxes for one year, so the trust fund doesn't hit a cliff in 2028. This is a bipartisan step in 2027-28.
2. **Make users pay for repair** (2029).
   - The gas tax rises with prices, plus ten small 3-cent steps that pause when gas prices spike.
   - Electric cars pay by the mile, at what an efficient gas car pays, read from the odometer at registration. No tracking device, and by law no location data (the full privacy rules start in 2031; see the hard questions).
3. **Make the heaviest trucks pay for the damage they do** (2030).
   - A charge by weight and miles replaces a flat $550 tax that hasn't changed since 1984 (the Internal Revenue Code, 26 U.S.C. 4481) [EV-MOV-209].
   - Railroads and air travelers pay small taxes toward passenger rail.
4. **Fix it first, then build** (2031). $937 billion over 2032-36 (the plan's model) [EV-MOV-200] for:
   - repair first;
   - a program to rebuild the Interstate and its bridges;
   - help to keep transit running in big cities, with rules that protect riders;
   - the largest passenger rail program the country has had, paid out only as projects prove they are ready.

**What it costs.**
- Users pay most of it. The general fund pays about 0.18% of GDP a year in 2032-36, falling to about 0.03% by the mid-2040s (the plan's model) [EV-MOV-200].
- For a typical gas car (25 miles per gallon, 12,000 miles a year), federal fuel tax goes from $88 a year today to $225 in 2036 (the plan's model) [EV-MOV-201]. That is about 37 cents a day by 2036.
- An electric car pays $141 in 2036, the same as a 40-mpg hybrid (the plan's model) [EV-MOV-204].

**What the plan is honest about.**
- These figures assume people don't drive less when fuel taxes rise, so revenue is an upper bound (the plan's model) [EV-MOV-206].
- Few rail corridors are ready to build, and the current administration is reviewing all of them (Trains, 2026) [EV-MOV-109]. Money will wait at the gates rather than be spent badly.
- The plan narrows the Interstate's repair gap; it does not close it (National Academies, 2018) [EV-MOV-010].

## The case in one paragraph

We've let the roads and rails run on a 1993 tax that loses value every year and lapses in 2028. The plan has drivers pay a little more for repair, a few cents at a time, pausing when gas prices spike. Electric cars pay their fair share without anyone tracking where they go. The heaviest trucks pay for the wear they cause. Repair comes first, then we build, and big projects get paid only as they prove they're ready.

## Hard questions, plain answers

- **"Isn't a gas tax increase a tax on working people?"** It costs a typical driver about $137 a year more by 2036 [EV-MOV-201], or about 37 cents a day. It pauses when prices spike, and every cent goes to repair first. The plan's fairness test is run across the whole plan.
- **"Will the government track my car?"** No. The charge reads only the odometer, and the readings are private tax information from the first day. The 2031 law adds an outright ban on collecting location data and deletes readings after three years; the plan would rather pass those rules before the charge starts in October 2030, in a standalone privacy bill.
- **"Why should my taxes pay for city transit?"** Most of the money goes to roads. Transit's operating help is a capped share, and cities and states have to keep paying their part.
- **"Isn't high-speed rail a boondoggle?"** Badly planned rail can be, as Europe's auditors found [EV-MOV-130]. That is why the plan pays in stages, after independent review, with local money committed first.
- **"What if Congress doesn't pass a highway bill before 2029?"** Then the plan writes one itself in 2029-30 (section 3, point 1).

## Stories (composite illustrations, not real people)

- **A county road engineer** has a bridge rated poor and no money to replace it. Under the plan, repair money comes first, and the state publishes which bridges are falling behind.
- **A nurse who takes the bus to a night shift.** Her route survives because the city kept its own funding to qualify for the operating help.
- **An owner-operator** pays by weight and miles on the records he already files, instead of a flat fee that ignores how much his truck wears the road.
