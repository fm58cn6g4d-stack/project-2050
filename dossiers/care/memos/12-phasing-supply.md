# Memo 12: How fast, and how supply is built

Role: decision memo for Espen, fork 12 of the care dossier (part C; `../3c-design-child-care.md`). Status: **for Espen, not decided.** Sources are named in plain text; bracketed tags point to `../evidence.json`. Costs marked "judgment" are working assumptions.

**This memo decides when families can count on the guarantee (memo 10) and pre-K (memo 11), and how providers are paid so that slots exist when they do.** Workers' pay, the deepest supply constraint, is part D's. This memo's payment rules decide whether the money reaches the slots.

## The question

- **The supply gap.** Care costs families a great deal, yet programs lose money on infants and pay staff $16.82 an hour (BLS, 2026) [EV-CAR-516].
- **The design rule.** The legacy design rule "supply with demand" led the legacy file to phase: pre-K and a lowest-income tier first, the full 7% cap from 2032 (FAM-2A). The full cap from 2030 is FAM-2B. Both are working choices (`DEC-2026-10-30-02`). The principle is settled.
- **The question.** Phase, start at once, or let each state start when ready? And how should providers be paid?

## What the evidence says, in four lines

1. **Price cuts ahead of supply produced waitlists.**
   - *What happened.* Canada halved fees in a year, and spaces came at about half the target pace. Waitlists rose from 19.1 to 30.8 percent of children not in care (Auditor General, 2025; C.D. Howe, 2026) [EV-CAR-538] [EV-CAR-539].
   - *Foreseen.* Canada's budget officer saw it coming [EV-CAR-540].
2. **Quebec's fast build-out came with mostly "fair" quality** (Institut de la statistique du Québec, 2003) [EV-CAR-536] and worse child outcomes [EV-CAR-531].
3. **Paying for supply works in the short run.** ARPA's $23.975 billion of operating grants paid providers directly until 2023 (CRS) [EV-CAR-509]. The 2024 rule's pay-by-enrollment was rescinded as costly to states [EV-CAR-510].
4. **Wages keep staff.** DC's pay fund is linked to turnover of 37 against 51 percent (Urban Institute, 2025; program evaluation) [EV-CAR-550]. The DoD system tied pay to training [EV-CAR-551].

## The options

**A. Phased behind supply, with providers paid for capacity (recommended; FAM-2A, updated).**
- *2030-31 school year:*
  - free care for every child under 5 at or below 85% of state median income;
  - free pre-K for 4-year-olds everywhere, and for 3-year-olds where states are ready;
  - facilities grants (point 7);
  - providers paid by enrollment, monthly and in advance, at rates set by a cost-of-care model, not market surveys;
  - operating grants for infant rooms and for areas with few slots (the ARPA model, made permanent).
- *July 2032:* the full guarantee, with no family paying above 7% of income.
- *2033-34:* 3-year-olds everywhere.
- *Where tried:* the legacy FAM-2A; New Mexico's expansion in steps (2019-22, then 2025) [EV-CAR-548]; Washington's planned steps [EV-CAR-549]; ARPA's grants [EV-CAR-509].
- *For people:* families at or below 85% of state median income get free care in the first year. The family at 150% waits until 2032 for the cap, by which time more rooms exist.
- *Cost:* about $1.6 trillion over FY2030-39 with memos 10 B and 11 A (range $1.1-2.4 trillion; judgment), rising from about $40 billion in 2030 to about $170 billion in 2033.
- *Evidence strength:* international practice (Canada's failure mode) and program evaluation (ARPA, DC). The phasing dates are judgment.
- *Political risk:* "free for some first" invites the charge that the middle class waits. The two years are short, and the first step reaches about half of families with young children (judgment).
- *Burden:* providers report enrollment monthly, as most already do.

**B. The full guarantee from 2030 (FAM-2B).**
- *What it is:* memo 10's full cap and memo 11's pre-K for 3s and 4s from the 2030-31 school year.
- *Where tried:* Canada's fee cut, in effect [EV-CAR-538].
- *For people:* everyone is promised it at once. In much of the country there are not enough rooms or staff to keep the promise for two or three years.
- *Cost:* about $0.15-0.25 trillion more over FY2030-34 on paper (judgment). Much of it would show up as higher prices rather than more care.
- *Political risk:* the clearest promise. Waitlists in the first years become the story.

**C. Each state starts when it shows the slots.**
- *What it is:* federal money for the full guarantee flows to a state once it certifies enough licensed capacity and staff. Until then the state gets facilities and operating grants only.
- *Where tried:* the CCWFA's certification that a state serves every family at or below 85% before expanding is a version of it [EV-CAR-580].
- *For people:* families' access depends on their state's pace. The slowest states may be those that least want the program.
- *Cost:* lower in the 2030s and uncertain, about $1.0-1.4 trillion over FY2030-39 (judgment).
- *Political risk:* a state that does not want the program can delay it indefinitely, BBB's opt-out problem [EV-CAR-587].
- *Burden:* a certification process the design lens counsels against.

## Consequences at a glance

| | **A. Phased** | B. All at once | C. State readiness |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Free care at or below 85% | 2030 | 2030 | When the state certifies |
| 7% cap for everyone | 2032 | 2030 | When the state certifies |
| FY2030-39 federal cost (with memos 10 B and 11 A) | about $1.6 trillion | about $1.75-1.85 trillion | about $1.0-1.4 trillion |
| Waitlist and price risk | Moderate | High | Low, but access varies |
| Fit with the legacy | FAM-2A | FAM-2B | New |

## Recommendation: A

- **It follows the settled principle, supply with demand,** with the U.S. tools that built supply: operating grants and pay by enrollment [EV-CAR-509] [EV-CAR-510].
- **It reaches the families with least first,** and all families within two years.
- **It avoids the failure Canada and Quebec show** [EV-CAR-538] [EV-CAR-536] without leaving access to a state's choice (C).
- **Evidence strength:** international practice and program evaluation; the dates are judgment.

**What would change this:**
- part D's workforce plan shows staff cannot grow fast enough by 2032 (move the cap to 2033);
- a 2027-28 deal funds supply early (move it earlier).
