# Memo 15: Early educators' pay

Role: decision memo for Espen, fork 15 of the care dossier (part D, the care workforce; `../3d-design-workforce.md`). Status: **decided by Espen, November 11, 2026: B** (`DEC-2026-11-11-04`); cost lines re-audited in step 8: parity is a ratio on part C's base and counts no baseline wage growth, and it is now at the recommended child care share, 90% for three years then 80% (`../3d-design-workforce.md`, "What is and isn't counted"). Parity by 2038 rather than 2036 is memo 17's option 3. Sources are named in plain text; bracketed tags point to the evidence register (`../evidence.json`). Costs marked "judgment" are this dossier's working assumptions; their basis is in `../3d-design-workforce.md`, "What the options cost", and part C's `../3c-design-child-care.md`.

**This memo decides what the plan pays the people who teach and care for children from birth to school entry,** in centers, family child care homes, Head Start and pre-K, under part C's guarantee (memo 10) and pre-K (memo 11). It is a separate fork from memo 14 because the benchmark is different: early educators are compared with elementary teachers, not with nursing assistants.

## The question

- **Today.**
  - *Child care workers:* about a million jobs at a median of $16.82 an hour (BLS, 2026) [EV-CAR-516] [EV-CAR-612].
  - *All early educators:* on a wider count, a median of $13.07 an hour, against $31.80 for elementary and middle school teachers (Center for the Study of Child Care Employment, 2024) [EV-CAR-629].
  - *Pay tracks public funding:* about $53,200 in school-based pre-K, $35,819 in Head Start and $29,570 in child care (Markowitz and Bassok, 2022 data) [EV-CAR-521].
- **What part C left here.**
  - *Child care:* priced at today's wages, with parity shown as a separate line of about $45 billion a year at maturity (`../3c-design-child-care.md`).
  - *Pre-K:* priced with parity, because every pre-K design weighed builds it in [EV-CAR-566].
- **The legacy design.** FAM-2A put "wage parity for care workers" in its first step, 2030-31 (`legacy/domains/domain-family.md`). It is an agent's working choice, weighed here.

## What the evidence says, in four lines

1. **Pay keeps educators.** DC's pay equity fund raised salaries to about $51,000-75,000; participating centers had 37 percent turnover against 51 percent at comparable centers without it (Urban Institute, 2025; program evaluation, with self-selection) [EV-CAR-550].
2. **Pay tied to training lifted quality** in the military's child care system (RAND, 1998) [EV-CAR-551].
3. **Pre-K at higher pay pulls staff and space from infant care.** New York City's pre-K cut capacity for children under 2 at private centers by 2,700 seats, all in high-poverty areas (Brown, 2024; quasi-experimental) [EV-CAR-569].
4. **Parity programs are fragile when funded year to year.** DC's fund was cut and is funded only through FY2027 (DC Fiscal Policy Institute, 2026) [EV-CAR-630].

## The options

**A. Parity with elementary teachers from the guarantee's start (2032).**
- *What it is:* the Child Care for Working Families Act's rule. Payment rates must give every staff member a living wage, and lead teachers pay equivalent to elementary educators with similar credentials [EV-CAR-581]. It applies to all settings, including family child care homes, from July 2032, when memo 10's full guarantee starts.
- *Where tried:* DC's fund, at about this level [EV-CAR-550]; public pre-K in schools [EV-CAR-521].
- *For workers:* a lead teacher with a bachelor's degree in an infant room earns what a first-grade teacher in the district earns, from 2032.
- *Cost:* about $45 billion a year at maturity on top of memo 10 B (range $30-65 billion), about $0.4 trillion over FY2030-39 and about $60 billion in FY2040 at 90% federal (part C's judgment; no independent model is in the register); at the recommended child care share, about $0.35 trillion and $53 billion.
- *Evidence strength:* program evaluation (DC) and the military system. The cost is judgment.
- *Political risk:*
  - The cost lands with the guarantee itself, in the same years as health's busiest transition.
  - Parity for workers without degrees invites the "daycare paid like teachers" attack. A pay scale by credential answers part of it.
- *Burden:* providers report pay by role, through the rate system.
- *Supply:* parity overnight requires degree-holders the pipeline does not yet have (part C, point 6).

**B. Memo 14's floor at once, then a salary scale by role and credential that reaches parity by 2036 (recommended).**
- *What it is:*
  - *From the 2030-31 school year:* every child care and pre-K staff member is paid at least memo 14's floor ($20 in 2026 dollars, if chosen).
  - *A published salary scale:* by role (assistant, teacher, lead teacher, director) and credential, in every state's rates, rising in steps to parity with elementary teachers by the 2036-37 school year.
  - *The same scale in every setting:* pre-K and infant rooms, centers and family homes, so pre-K does not draw staff from infant care.
  - *The pathway:* part C's point 6 pays for credentials (tuition, paid release time), and each credential moves a worker up the scale.
- *Where tried:* DC's fund uses a scale by role and credential [EV-CAR-550]; the military system tied pay steps to training [EV-CAR-551].
- *For workers:* an assistant earning $16.82 gets $20 in 2030. A lead teacher's pay rises each year until it matches the district's elementary teachers in 2036.
- *Cost:* about $25 billion in 2033 (range $15-35 billion), about $0.22 trillion over FY2030-39 and about $53 billion in FY2040 at the recommended child care share, the same end point as A (judgment; at 90%, $0.25 trillion and $60 billion).
- *Evidence strength:* as A for pay's effect; the phasing follows the supply evidence [EV-CAR-569] and part C's credential point.
- *Political risk:*
  - Lower early cost than A.
  - Advocates who want parity at once will call 2036 too slow.
  - A scale in law is easier to defend each budget cycle than a supplement.
- *Burden:* as A.

**C. Memo 14's floor only.**
- *What it is:* child care staff get the general care floor; pre-K keeps the parity part C priced; no scale for child care.
- *Cost:* about $10 billion a year (range $5-15 billion), about $0.07 trillion over FY2030-39 at the recommended child care share (judgment).
- *For workers:* a floor, but the infant-room teacher still earns far less than the pre-K teacher down the hall.
- *Evidence strength:* the floor's evidence (memo 14). The gap with pre-K is the New York City risk [EV-CAR-569].
- *Political risk:* low cost. Child care advocates and part C's quality goals are not met.

**D. A DC-style wage supplement paid outside the rates.**
- *What it is:* federal grants to states to pay educators a supplement on top of provider wages, at A's or B's level.
- *Where tried:* DC's fund [EV-CAR-550].
- *For workers:* the same pay as A or B, delivered as a separate payment.
- *Cost:* as A or B, by level, but by yearly appropriation.
- *Political risk:* the most exposed. DC's fund was cut and is unfunded after FY2027 [EV-CAR-630].
- *Burden:* a second payment system beside the rates.

**Fiscal rule** (`DEC-2026-11-10-05`): A and B reach the same end at different early cost, so the choice is partly about money and comes to you. The cost is reported to the settlement.

## Recommendation: B

- **It reaches parity**, the legacy direction and the Child Care for Working Families Act's standard [EV-CAR-581], on the same schedule as the people trained to meet it (part C, point 6).
- **It closes the pre-K gap from the first year,** by paying infant rooms and pre-K on one scale, which avoids New York City's drain on infant care [EV-CAR-569].
- **It writes pay into rates,** not into a supplement that can be cut [EV-CAR-630].
- **Evidence strength: moderate.** Program evaluations of pay and retention; the cost of parity is judgment.

**What would change this:**
- an independent cost model of parity far from part C's $45 billion;
- the financing settlement cannot carry the scale (C, with the scale later);
- evidence that credentials do not improve classroom quality (a scale by role and experience rather than credential).
