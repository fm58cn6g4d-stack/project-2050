# Memo 13: Who runs it, and Head Start's place

Role: decision memo for Espen, fork 13 of the care dossier (part C; `../3c-design-child-care.md`). Status: **for Espen, not decided.** Sources are named in plain text; bracketed tags point to `../evidence.json`.

**This memo decides which government runs the guarantee and pre-K, what happens in a state that declines, and what becomes of Head Start.** Section 5 (delivery) builds on the answer.

## The question

- **Today.** States run child care subsidies through the block grant, and HHS's Administration for Children and Families oversees them.
- **Head Start.** It runs federal-to-local: HHS funds local grantees directly, outside the states, with health, family and nutrition services. It is funded for 715,873 children and pregnant women (FY2024) [EV-CAR-512] at $12.4 billion (FY2026) [EV-CAR-504].
- **The opt-out problem.** BBB's score assumed a third or more of children would live in states that did not take part (CBO, via CRFB) [EV-CAR-587], the largest weakness of state-run designs.
- **The legacy.** It names HHS's ACF and lets localities apply directly if a state opts out (FAM-2D).

## What the evidence says, in four lines

1. **State-run entitlements are exposed to state choices and budgets.** BBB assumed wide non-participation [EV-CAR-587]. Washington paused its own expansion in 2025 [EV-CAR-549].
2. **Head Start's long-run results are positive**, though its test gains fade (Deming, 2009; Bailey, Sun and Timpe, 2021; Head Start Impact Study, 2012) [EV-CAR-562] [EV-CAR-563] [EV-CAR-560].
3. **About a third of Head Start's children would otherwise attend other public preschools** (Kline and Walters, 2016) [EV-CAR-561], so how Head Start fits with universal pre-K matters for cost.
4. **Head Start's federal-to-local line has protected it in 2025-26.** The immigrant-eligibility directive was enjoined [EV-CAR-513]. Its FY2026 funding rose slightly [EV-CAR-504].

## The options

**A. States run it under a federal floor, HHS runs it directly where a state declines, and Head Start stays federal-to-local and joins at the guarantee's rates (recommended).**
- *What it is:*
  - States' child care agencies run the guarantee and pre-K under federal rules on eligibility, rates and standards.
  - If a state declines or fails, HHS contracts directly with localities, tribes or providers there, paying the same rates from the federal share, as the Affordable Care Act's federal marketplace does for states that decline, and as FAM-2D's direct applications by localities would.
  - Head Start keeps its grantees, standards and comprehensive services. It is paid the guarantee's and pre-K's per-child rates for its children, plus its own grant for the extra services.
  - Early Head Start grows inside the guarantee.
- *Where tried:* the CCWFA's and BBB's state structure [EV-CAR-580]; the Affordable Care Act's federal marketplace fallback; Head Start since 1965.
- *For people:* a family in a state that refuses the program still gets it, through HHS's contractors. Head Start families keep their program, now funded for the full day.
- *Evidence strength:* program experience (the ACA fallback, Head Start). The fallback for child care is untested and is judgment.
- *Political risk:* a federal fallback in a refusing state will be called federal overreach. It also needs HHS capacity that has been cut in 2025-26 (part C's section 5 will size it).
- *Burden:* none new on families.

**B. As A, with Head Start's money moved into the states' systems.**
- *What it is:* Head Start's grants become part of each state's allotment. States may keep local grantees.
- *Where tried:* proposed in past Republican budgets, never enacted.
- *For people:* one system. Head Start's comprehensive services survive only where states keep them.
- *Evidence strength:* none for the change itself. Head Start's long-run results come from its federal-to-local form [EV-CAR-562] [EV-CAR-563], and state-run programs are exposed to state budgets [EV-CAR-549] (program experience).
- *Political risk:* Head Start's advocates and grantees oppose it strongly. It hands the program to states at the moment some have sought to restrict it.

**C. A federal program paying providers directly in every state.**
- *What it is:* HHS sets rates, enrolls providers and pays them, as Medicare pays doctors. States license and inspect.
- *Where tried:* DoD's child care system [EV-CAR-551]; Medicare.
- *For people:* the same program everywhere.
- *Political risk:* the largest federal build and the biggest fight with states. It needs a new federal payment system in time for 2030.

## Consequences at a glance

| | **A. States, federal fallback, Head Start kept** | B. Head Start into the states | C. Federal program |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Children in a refusing state | Covered through HHS | Covered through HHS | Covered |
| Head Start | Kept, full-day funded | State choice | Kept as a provider |
| Federal capacity needed | Moderate | Moderate | Large |
| Ready by 2030 | Yes, through existing agencies | Yes | Doubtful |

## Recommendation: A

- **It builds on agencies that exist** (state lead agencies, HHS's ACF, Head Start's grantees), so the 2030 start is realistic.
- **The fallback closes BBB's opt-out hole** [EV-CAR-587].
- **It keeps Head Start's record and services** [EV-CAR-562] [EV-CAR-563], and paying it the common rate avoids double-funding the third of children who would attend other preschools [EV-CAR-561].
- **Evidence strength:** program experience; the child care fallback is judgment.

**What would change this:** HHS's 2029 capacity is too thin to run a fallback; then the fallback runs through tribes and large cities first, and section 5 says how.
