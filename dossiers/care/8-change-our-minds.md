# Care you can count on, part A (health): what would change our minds

Role: section 8 of the care dossier, health part. For each decided choice, the finding that would change it and what we would do instead; the political and legal events that would force a fallback; and when the evidence arrives. Status: draft (stage D, step 4, PR 2), on `DEC-2026-11-10-09`, `-11` and `-12`. A change to a decided choice goes back to Espen; the triggers below say when to ask.

## Political and legal events

- **The Senate rules reform fails** (D2; `dossiers/scenario/4-2029-plan.md`). The plan's money moves to a later reconciliation act, and the entitlement's rights, provider rules and endpoint wait for 60 votes (section 5). Restoration and the Advantage fix still pass in the 2029 reconciliation act.
- **A 2027-28 deal restores the enhanced credits** (`3-design.md`). HLT-1.04 shrinks; nothing else changes.
- **States' payment challenged.** The Supreme Court held in 2012 that threatening states' existing Medicaid funds to force the ACA's expansion was coercive (*NFIB v. Sebelius*) [EV-CAR-351] [EV-CAR-352]. That holding is about conditions on grants. HLT-7.17 is written instead as a direct obligation, the instrument Congress used for Medicare Part D's state contribution (42 U.S.C. 1396u-5(c)) [EV-CAR-353] [EV-CAR-354] [EV-CAR-355], which states have paid since 2006 and which has never been decided on the merits. The risk is that a court reads its collection by offset against federal payments as the same leverage *NFIB* barred, or holds that Congress cannot require states to pay at all. A condition on a new grant was weighed and not chosen now: no grant the plan creates before part B is worth more to a state than the payment it would ask, so states would decline it. If the payment fails, the fallback above (hold harmless) applies; once part B decides long-term care, its new money is the natural condition. The legal review at L4 confirms the drafting.
- **A challenge to the end of duplicative coverage.** Congress's commerce power covers regulating health insurance sold across state lines, and the employer contribution is a tax under the taxing power the same 2012 case relied on. Both need a legal review at L4; neither changes a choice now.
- **A later Congress votes to delay a wave.** The stages are automatic so that delay needs a vote (memo 1). If a delay passes, the protections still hold: no one loses coverage before the plan covers them (HLT-7.04).
- **Abortion coverage blocks passage.** Espen decided the plan covers it and ends Hyde in general (`DEC-2026-11-10-11`). If votes fail on it, the choice goes back to Espen; the dossier does not drop it.

## Hard stops

The design no longer holds, and goes back to Espen in full, if:
- the settlement cannot finance the plan at any endpoint date the Senate will pass;
- wave 1 shows enrollment from records cannot reach most people without applications;
- supply falls (closures, clinicians leaving) faster than global budgets and the fund can hold.
