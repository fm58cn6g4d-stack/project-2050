# Foundation: democratic renewal

Role: the democratic renewal foundation (`charter/PLAN.md` 3.1): "a democracy and a government that work for the 21st century". Status: **draft, stage F, step 9 (sections 1 to 9)**. Sections 1 and 2 are research only. Section 3's thirteen memos and 50 yes-or-no points are **decided** (`DEC-2026-11-17-40` to `-99`, November 17, 2026), each on its own: as recommended except memo 9 part 2, changed; memo 9 part 1 and the eight Discuss points were settled on the follow-up note (`-97`, `-98`), its other recommendations became directions for section 4 and memo 13 takes the staged path (`-99`), and several answers carry Espen's terms (`3-design.md`, "Decided"; questions answered in `notes/espen-questions-2026-11-17.md`). Level: the decided items are at **L2** in `agenda/agenda.csv`, with DEM-17 moved to L2 by section 4 and two rows added for decided items that had none (CAP-19, shared rule 5; CRT-20, civil legal aid); directions (DEM-3, DEM-4, DEM-9, CAP-10) and the pointers stay at **L1**. Section 4, the specification, is drafted (139 provisions in `provisions.json`; Espen's go, November 17). Section 5, delivery, is drafted, with four provisional fixes to section 4 that let provisions run (fix 1 amends a sentence of decided memo 10 B). Section 6, cost, is drafted: this foundation's booked rows are $507.2 billion over FY2030-39 and $50.0 billion in FY2040 (8.3% of the FY2040 plan-wide gap), of which memo 10's delivery contingency is $0 to $72.9 billion and $0 to $4.7 billion; the unbooked lines are about $19.4 billion and $2.5 billion, about $12.5 billion and $1.6 billion net of CRT-P1's room. Section 7, validation, is drafted: the models run on this branch (budget rebuild, section 6's scenario, the vote simulator, the capacity funding model, a new hiring-pace check and the immigration model), outside benchmarks, what the models miss, the outcomes moved, objections answered and every provision checked; it finds the booked CAP-7 path runs about two years ahead of the hiring the design assumes (at the working pace, on the booking's basis, about $17 billion less outlay over FY2030-39 and about $16 billion less IRS enforcement revenue, about zero net, no change in FY2040; restated in the milestone review's fix step 1; memo 10's contingency is a separate stage G question) and adds one provisional question. Section 8, what would change our minds, is drafted: for each major commitment the finding that would reverse or resize it, where it comes from and when, the review trigger and who acts, with one new provisional question (the 13-Justice option's review point). Section 9, the public case, is drafted: a plain-language brief, messages, hard questions, what a court could strike and what would stand, the five provisional items (decision-sheet D1 to D5) shown as provisional, a can-say / can't-say list and a source table tracing every number to sections 1 to 8. L3 follows the milestone review. Nothing is booked; section 6 prices the four unbooked lines for stage G (the immigration court's structure, the library line, statute tracking and legal aid), IMM-1's later tier-3 rebooking (about −$4.3 billion over FY2036-39) and the two delivery contingencies' overlap.

**What it covers** (`charter/PLAN.md` 3.1, exactly): "The voting floor, money in politics, ethics, courts, participation, long-run institutional reform, and a government that delivers: agency capacity, payment systems, and remedies when delivery fails. Each flagship's delivery section states its own needs; this foundation owns the cross-cutting state capacity." Stage F sizes that capacity "to the specified flagships" (`charter/PLAN.md` 6, stage F).
