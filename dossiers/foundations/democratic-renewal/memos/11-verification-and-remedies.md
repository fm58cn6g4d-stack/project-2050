# Memo 11: Shared rule 5, who verifies, pays and answers for mistakes

Role: decision memo for Espen, fork 11 of the democratic renewal foundation (`../3-design.md`). Status: **awaiting Espen's decision**. Sources are named in plain text; bracketed tags point to `../evidence.json`. It answers the hand-off three flagships wrote to this foundation (`dossiers/shared-rules.md`, rule 5; `dossiers/secure-floor/5-delivery.md`, line 3; `dossiers/immigration/5-delivery.md`, line 3) and housing's request for a statutory basis for record sharing (`dossiers/home/5-delivery.md`, line 384; CAP-13). Answers section 2's question 12.

**Decision line.** Recommended: **B, one plan-wide rule for every program the plan creates or changes: (1) eligibility from records the government already holds, with the person's own statement accepted where records are silent; (2) a statute letting the plan's programs share the records they need, with each use logged, a right to see what was used, and no use for immigration or criminal enforcement; (3) checks by sample after payment, not documents at the door; (4) no money recovered without a person's review, a notice, a hardship rule and a cap of 10% of a monthly payment, written in law; (5) payment continues while an appeal is pending; (6) the government, not the person, carries an error it made.** Cost: small; the agencies' share is inside part G's lines, and the data-sharing build is inside the cross-cutting state and local line. Evidence: randomized and quasi-experimental studies of burden and verification, official audits of improper payments, and two national scandals abroad.

## The question

Each flagship pays people through the IRS, SSA, CMS, HUD, USDA and the states. Who checks eligibility, how, and who bears a mistake?

## What the evidence says, in six lines

1. **Data, not portals, is the bottleneck:** agencies attribute about 73% of FY2021-24 overpayments ($556.6 billion of $765.8 billion) to failure to access data that already existed (CRS on Do Not Pay) [EV-DEM-291]; a Do Not Pay match neither stops a payment nor proves ineligibility [EV-DEM-292].
2. **Records-based delivery reaches most people fast and misses people outside the records:** Medicaid's automatic renewals ran from 99% to 3% by state, and household-level errors wrongly cut off about 500,000 people (KFF) [EV-DEM-319]; nearly 3 million eligible people had not claimed $4.7 billion of 2021 rebate credits (TIGTA) [EV-DEM-293].
3. **Burden decides take-up:** on-demand SNAP interviews raised approvals 6 points in a Los Angeles randomized trial [EV-DEM-318]; help applying raised older adults' SNAP enrollment from 6% to 18% [EV-DEM-119].
4. **Verification cuts both ways:** stricter biometric checks in Jharkhand reduced leakage but cut 1.5 to 2 million legitimate beneficiaries off at some point [EV-DEM-286]; a phased smartcard rollout in Andhra Pradesh cut corruption without reducing access [EV-DEM-287] (one research team).
5. **Automated recovery without a human check is the failure to avoid:** Australia's Robodebt cost A$2.4 billion in repayments and compensation [EV-DEM-303]; the Dutch childcare inquiry found an "all or nothing" approach with no hardship clause [EV-DEM-304], and the cabinet resigned [EV-DEM-305].
6. **Remedies that rest on an administrative setting move with each administration:** SSA's default withholding went from 100% to 10% in 2024, then 50% in 2025 (CRS; NBC News) [EV-DEM-301] [EV-DEM-302]. The constitutional floor is thin: a prior hearing for welfare (*Goldberg*) [EV-DEM-306], a balancing test for disability (*Mathews*) [EV-DEM-307].

## The options

**A. Each program sets its own rules (today).**
- *For:* no new law; agencies know their programs.
- *Against:* a person in three programs proves the same facts three times; recovery rules change with each administration [EV-DEM-302].

**B. One plan-wide rule, records first (recommended).** The six parts in the decision line.
- *For:* uses records the government holds, the design lens's first preference (`DEC-2026-11-09-13`), and writes the protections Robodebt and the Dutch case lacked into law [EV-DEM-303] [EV-DEM-304]; fixes the data access that caused most overpayments [EV-DEM-291].
- *Against:* a records statute is a large privacy grant; sample-based checks accept some improper payments before they are caught.
- *Burden:* lower for people; for agencies, data agreements and sampling instead of document review.

**C. Documentary proof at the door.** Every applicant shows papers before payment.
- *For:* fewer payments to ineligible people up front.
- *Against:* falls hardest on eligible people with the least paperwork [EV-DEM-121] [EV-DEM-286]; costs more to run.

**D. B plus a federal benefits ombudsman** with power to order an agency to correct a case, on the Taxpayer Advocate model, which gave full or partial relief in 78.2% of its closed cases in FY2023 [EV-DEM-298] but slowed to 110 days a case as the IRS slowed [EV-DEM-299].
- *For:* a remedy outside the agency that made the mistake.
- *Against:* a new office; the UK ombudsman's record is self-reported [EV-DEM-300]; the flagships already route complaints to program ombudsmen (care's Beneficiary Ombudsman) and appeals.

## What it means for people

- *A parent getting the monthly child benefit who also gets SNAP and Medicaid:* under B, her income is checked once from records and reused; under C she brings pay stubs to each office.
- *A retiree overpaid by mistake:* under B, a person reviews the case, the repayment is capped at 10% of her benefit, and if the error was SSA's she does not repay; under A, the cap is whatever this year's notice says.

## Recommendation: B

- It puts the work on records and the risk of the government's own errors on the government, which the evidence on burden and the scandals abroad support [EV-DEM-318] [EV-DEM-303].
- **What would change this:** sample audits showing improper payments well above today's programs (then add targeted checks for the riskiest cases, not documents for everyone).
