## The question

Every flagship's delivery leans on CAP-7 or CAP-7S, and none has a settled agency share (section 1, G.6 and G.7). How much does each agency get, and what sets the pace at which it is spent?

## Why it matters

- **The agencies lost staff:** a net −258,108 federal employees from January 20, 2025 to September 2026 (OPM, second Trump administration data) [EV-DEM-082]; the IRS fell from 94,095 to 70,829 FTE in a year [EV-DEM-088]; SSA's full-time permanent staff from 56,645 to 48,530 [EV-DEM-105]; HUD's from about 8,800 to about 6,300 [EV-DEM-096].
- **Money does not buy staff quickly:** with Inflation Reduction Act money in hand, the IRS lost more revenue agents than it hired in the first half of FY2023 (TIGTA) [EV-DEM-277]. In January-June 2025, large agencies hired 65,953 people while 134,122 left (GAO) [EV-DEM-085]: hiring at scale takes time even in a normal year.
- **Some capacity pays for itself:** CBO estimated $40 billion more for IRS enforcement raises about $103 billion [EV-DEM-270], and audits at the top return more than $12 per $1 with deterrence (Boning and others) [EV-DEM-273]. Nothing in the register prices the return on SSA, CMS or HUD staff (section 2, G.7).

## Part 1: size

**A. Keep the October 26 sizes and spend to them.** Booked as it stands; agencies share by the bottom-up table and the rest is allocated as needs appear.
- *For:* no change; a margin for needs nobody has written down.
- *Against:* an unallocated balance is the kind the legacy review warned against ("a named contingency of a stated size is better", line 120).

**B. Allocate the bottom-up table inside the ceilings; name the rest a delivery contingency (recommended).** About $43 billion of surge and about $30 billion of sustained money on the booked basis, released by OMB only against a written need from a delivery section or a readiness report, published.
- *For:* each flagship gets a settled line (the gap section 1 found); the margin covers the review's high case ($103.3 billion and $355.1 billion) [EV-DEM-328] without being spent by default.
- *Against:* the contingency can still be spent; OMB's release power is the kind a hostile administration misuses (then statute names its purposes).

**C. Lower the ceilings to the bottom-up midpoint.** About $55 billion and $250 billion.
- *For:* about $43 billion and $30 billion less than booked over FY2030-39.
- *Against:* no room if the high case comes true; rebooking is a tier-3 change for stage G.

## Part 2: pace

**A. All money available on enactment; agencies hire as they can** (the legacy October 21 direction, `legacy/domains/domain-capacity.md`, line 110).
- *Against:* availability is not hiring [EV-DEM-277]; money with no plan to spend it invites rescission.

**B. Available on enactment and until spent, released against a published hiring path, with hiring authority in R1 (recommended).** Each agency publishes its hiring path; its next tranche follows its hiring, as housing proposes for HUD (a quarterly count and a one-year wait below 75% of path; `dossiers/home/5-delivery.md`, line 171). The first bill carries direct-hire authority for delivery jobs and lets former staff return without pay penalties (CAP-11). A program whose agency is behind starts later; it does not shrink (the secure floor's rule for paid leave, `dossiers/secure-floor/5-delivery.md`, line 87). The working path is the legacy review's midpoint: sustained money of $10.4 billion in FY2030 rising to $26.8 billion by FY2034 and $31.9 billion by FY2039 [EV-DEM-328].
- *For:* the pace matches what can be hired; money stays available, so a slow year does not lose it.
- *Against:* a published path is a target critics will score; delay becomes visible.

**C. Statutory staffing floors by agency** (the legacy CAP-7 design, line 112).
- *Against:* the legacy file's own caution: a floor "does not itself supply budget authority or secure operations through a funding lapse" (line 155).

**D. Contractors first to buy speed.**
- *Against:* the 2013 HealthCare.gov launch failed without testing and had to be recovered [EV-CAR-311] [EV-CAR-312]; contractors do not replace the decisions only staff can make.

## Recommendation: part 1 B, part 2 B

- Every flagship gets a line, the margin is named, and the pace follows hiring, which is the constraint the evidence shows [EV-DEM-277] [EV-DEM-085].
- **What would change this:** the other dossiers (education, international, labor, rights) stating needs above the legacy figures (then the contingency covers them first); a hiring record far faster than the path (then release faster).
