**Decision line.** Recommended: **A with a fact-finder: a binding code of conduct for the Justices, with gift, travel, income and amicus-funding disclosure; recusal decided by the Court with published reasons; and an inspector general for the judiciary who investigates complaints and reports findings to the Court and to Congress, with no power to sanction or to overrule a recusal.** Cost: about $5 million over five years on CBO's score of the closest bill. Evidence: the Court's unenforced 2023 code, a newspaper count of conflicts the Chief Justice acknowledged, the commission's view, polling.

## The question

The Supreme Court's first code (November 2023) has no enforcement mechanism, and the Judicial Conduct and Disability Act does not reach the Justices [EV-DEM-040] [EV-DEM-046]. Who enforces a code, and how far can Congress go?

## What the evidence says

- **Conflicts happen without checks:** the *Wall Street Journal* found 131 federal judges sat in 685 matters involving companies they or their families held stock in (2010-2018); the Chief Justice called for better training and conflict-checking software [EV-DEM-229].
- **The commission found a binding disciplinary framework for the Justices raises difficulties, but an advisory code is a positive step** [EV-DEM-248]. No study compares compliance under enforced and unenforced codes.
- **89% favor a strict ethics code** (Marquette, via SCOTUSblog) [EV-DEM-063].

## The options

**A. Binding code, self-decided recusal with published reasons, enforcement by disclosure, referral and appropriations (legacy CRT-2A)**, here with an inspector general who investigates and reports (recommended).
- *For:* disclosure rules sit on settled ground: Congress has applied 28 U.S.C. 455 and the Ethics in Government Act to the Justices for decades (`legacy/domains/domain-courts.md`, line 75); an investigator who only reports does not touch the Court's decisions.
- *Against:* no outside sanction; a Justice can ignore a finding.

**B. An outside panel of judges reviews recusal decisions (CRT-2B; the SCERT Act as introduced).**
- *For:* someone other than the Justice decides.
- *Against:* outside override of a recusal is "the likely point of invalidation" (line 82).

**C. An Office of Investigative Counsel reporting to Congress (CRT-2C; H.R. 7692).**
- *For:* investigations independent of the Court.
- *Against:* an agent of Congress investigating Justices invites a separation-of-powers fight; higher legal risk.

**D. Disclosure and gift rules only (CRT-2D).** *For:* lowest risk. *Against:* drops the binding code.

## What it means for people

- *A party whose case is heard by a Justice with a stake:* under A, the Justice's reasons for sitting are published and an investigator can report the facts; under B, a panel could remove the Justice from the case.
