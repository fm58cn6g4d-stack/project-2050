# Memo 5: The election regulators after *Slaughter*

Role: decision memo for Espen, fork 5 of the democratic renewal foundation (`../3-design.md`). Status: **decided** (`DEC-2026-11-17-48`, November 17, 2026): **B**, as recommended. The text below is the memo as sent; section 4 carries the decided design. Sources are named in plain text; bracketed tags point to `../evidence.json`. Agenda rows: INT-7 (the FEC); the EAC's structure under DEM-1 and DEM-11 (all L2). Answers section 2's question 5. The general template for independent agencies is EXE-9, homed in executive power (wave three); this memo applies it to the two election agencies only.

**Decision line.** Recommended: **B, a five-member FEC and a five-member EAC with staggered terms, no more than two members of one party, a quorum of three that a vacancy strategy cannot defeat, and a citizen suit when the FEC fails to act on a complaint within 120 days; with standing staff authority to keep the EAC's core functions running without a quorum.** No removal protection is relied on. Cost: about $0.9 billion over FY2030-39 for both agencies' added staff (legacy review). Evidence: CRS's deadlock counts, two views on whether deadlock is a fault, the 2025-26 loss of both agencies' quorums, and the *Slaughter* holding.

## The question

Both election agencies stopped working in 2025-26. The FEC had two of six commissioners in March 2026, and most actions need four votes [EV-DEM-031]; its chair said it went without a quorum through a record-spending midterm [EV-DEM-032]. The EAC has had no commissioners since July 9, 2026, so it cannot adopt voting system guidelines or change the federal form [EV-DEM-020]. After *Trump v. Slaughter* (2026), for-cause protection of commissioners is unconstitutional [EV-DEM-037], and CRS judges other commissions' protections "on precarious legal footing" [EV-DEM-054]. How should the two be built so they work without relying on removal protection?

## What the evidence says

- **The FEC deadlocks on a growing share of cases:** about 13% of closed enforcement matters in 2008-09 and 24.4% in 2014 (CRS) [EV-DEM-209]; counts for 2016 run from 14% to 30% by who counts [EV-DEM-211].
- **Whether deadlock is a fault is contested:** CRS says it may reflect Congress's intent but in some cases stops enforcement [EV-DEM-210]; a former Republican chairman calls deadlocks the exception, and beneficial [EV-DEM-211].
- **Abroad, weak enforcement bodies are a main obstacle** to political-finance rules (International IDEA) [EV-DEM-213].

## The options

**A. Six members as today, with quorum fixes.** Holdover members count toward a quorum; acting authority for the staff director on routine matters.
- *For:* least change; keeps the bipartisan veto both parties have relied on.
- *Against:* the 2026 quorum loss came from vacancies and removals, which these fixes only partly answer; deadlocks continue [EV-DEM-209].

**B. Five members, no more than two of one party, quorum of three, a citizen suit (recommended).** The legacy INT-7's first route ("an odd-numbered commission", `legacy/domains/domain-integrity.md`, line 96), built as that file asks: staggered terms, quorum rules a vacancy strategy cannot defeat, and appropriations and reporting conditions, not removal protection. If the FEC does not act on a complaint within 120 days, the complainant may sue the violator directly. The EAC gets the same five-member form, and its executive director may keep the federal form and certified guidelines in force and run DEM-11's formula while it lacks a quorum.
- *For:* breaks the 3-3 tie by structure; the citizen suit keeps enforcement alive if the agency is captured or emptied.
- *Against:* the fifth member (an independent) becomes the decisive seat, and who appoints that seat is the political fight; after *Slaughter*, a President can remove members at will, so independence is only partly restorable by statute (the legacy file's own limit, line 98).

**C. A separate enforcement bureau with a single director** on a fixed term, acting on complaints when the commission deadlocks (INT-7's second route).
- *For:* enforcement no longer waits for a vote.
- *Against:* a single director removable at will after *Slaughter* is the least insulated design of all.

**D. Move civil enforcement to the courts:** a private right of action for any violation, with the FEC limited to disclosure and guidance.
- *For:* does not depend on any commission.
- *Against:* enforcement by whoever can afford to sue; inconsistent rulings across circuits.

## What it means for people

- *A voter who reports an illegal coordinated ad:* under B, the FEC must act within 120 days or the voter's group may sue; today, a 3-3 vote ends it.
- *A county clerk waiting for certified voting machines:* under B the EAC's staff keep certification running without a quorum; today, nothing moves.
