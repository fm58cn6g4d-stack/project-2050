# Memo 9: The immigration court (CRT-5), and civil counsel beyond it

Role: decision memo for Espen, fork 9 of the democratic renewal foundation (`../3-design.md`). Status: **decided** (`DEC-2026-11-17-55` to `-57`, `-97`, November 17, 2026). Part 1 (`-97`, resolving `-55`): **A′**, the follow-up note's part e: an independent immigration court on the bankruptcy-court model, its judges appointed by the courts of appeals for 14-year terms, plus faster federal-court review (restored habeas and de novo review of law and mixed questions) (`../notes/espen-questions-2026-11-17.md`, part e). Part 2: **B with a change**: the added judges split between regular-term and temporary hires, each sized to the need, so the IMM-1 saving is smaller than −$5.5 billion (a later tier-3 change). Part 3: **B**, as recommended, with the public defender shortage addressed separately (part f). The text below is the memo as sent; section 4 carries the decided design. Sources are named in plain text; bracketed tags point to `../evidence.json` and, for immigration facts, to `dossiers/immigration/evidence.json`. Agenda rows: CRT-5 (5A to 5C) (L1). Answers section 2's question 8. **Settled already:** the immigration flagship's requirements for the court (capacity, timelines, notice, independence, and counsel option B: children, people with serious mental illness and everyone detained) are decided (`DEC-2026-11-12-07`; `dossiers/immigration/memos/4-asylum-courts.md`, part 2) and are not reopened; this memo designs the court that meets them.

**Decision line.** Recommended: **part 1 A, an Article I immigration court outside the executive on the Tax Court model (trial, appellate and administrative divisions), judges on fixed 15-year terms, restored Article III review of questions of law, and an express fallback that folds the court into the judicial branch if its structure is struck; the corps starts at the immigration model's about 1,670 judges; part 2 B, the judges added to clear the backlog are appointed on terms that end once it clears; part 3 B, civil legal aid grows through the Legal Services Corporation on a schedule tied to provider capacity, with no new federal right to counsel now.** Cost: the court's own structure about $3-10 billion over FY2030-39 (judgment, from the immigration milestone review); the judges and counsel are already booked in IMM-1. Evidence: CRS histories of Article I courts, the 2026 House bill, quasi-experimental evidence on counsel, and New York's capacity record.

## The question

The court the plan creates must decide 3.3 million pending cases [EV-IMM-100] with a corps that fell from 735 judges at the end of FY2024 to 557 at the end of December 2025 (CRS) [EV-IMM-104], then rose to 753 on board by September 30, 2026 after 312 hires in FY2026, counting reemployed annuitants and not the 36 temporary judges (EOIR) [EV-IMM-103], under a law that caps it at 800 from November 1, 2028 [EV-IMM-105]. What form does it take, and does the plan fund counsel in civil cases outside it?

## Part 1: the court's form

**A. Article I court plus Article III review (legacy CRT-5A; recommended).** The Real Courts, Rule of Law Act of 2026 as the base [EV-IMM-113]: a court that is not an agency of the executive, with Tax Court-style divisions; the Tax Court began as an executive board and became an Article I court in 1969, with 19 judges on 15-year terms (CRS) [EV-DEM-244]. Restored habeas and de novo review of law and mixed questions. A severable fallback into Article III if *Slaughter*'s reasoning reaches the judges' tenure (`legacy/domains/domain-courts.md`, line 104).
- *For:* takes adjudication out of the prosecutor's department; public-rights doctrine covers immigration, so a non-Article III forum is permissible (line 104); Congress creates such courts "for expertise, efficiency and control" (CRS) [EV-DEM-245].
- *Against:* for-cause tenure is the exposed point after *Slaughter*; no outcome evaluation of such a conversion exists [EV-DEM-244].

**B. Article III review only, adjudication stays in the Justice Department (CRT-5B).**
- *For:* cheapest; no new court.
- *Against:* judges stay removable by the Attorney General, which fails the flagship's independence requirement (immigration memo 4, part 2, item 4).

**C. Immigration adjudication moves into Article III (CRT-5C).**
- *For:* clearest independence.
- *Against:* 700 to 1,000 Senate confirmations (legacy, line 116) and life tenure for a corps the backlog sizes temporarily; one President could seat it all.

## Part 2: the judges added for the backlog

The immigration model needs about 1,670 judges, 920 above the 753 on board on September 30, 2026 [EV-IMM-103], at about $1.5 million a team, $13.2 billion over FY2030-39 (booked in IMM-3.08; `dossiers/immigration/6-cost.md`, line 56). After the backlog clears in FY2035, receipts need about 730 judges.
- **A. All judges on regular 15-year terms.** The surplus leaves by attrition, as booked.
- **B. The increment on terms that end when the backlog clears (recommended).** Saves about $5.5 billion over FY2036-39 and $1.1 billion in FY2040 on the immigration model (same line, a proposal it sends here). *Against:* shorter terms attract fewer experienced lawyers; a judge whose term ends is a judge with less independence late in it.

## Part 3: civil counsel beyond CRT-5 (section 2's question 8)

- **The gap:** low-income Americans get no or not enough help for 92% of their substantial civil legal problems (Legal Services Corporation's survey; it has a stake) [EV-DEM-240].
- **A right works only with lawyers to fill it:** New York's eviction right to counsel cut possessory judgments by 32.1 points among represented tenants (Cassidy and Currie, an upper bound) [EV-DEM-242], but representation stayed under 50% for three years and fell to 30% by March 2025 as funding and provider staff fell behind (New York City Comptroller) [EV-DEM-243].
- **A. Nothing beyond CRT-5 and the flagships' own legal aid** (housing's HOU-13.02 to .04).
- **B. Grow civil legal aid through the Legal Services Corporation, by a schedule tied to provider hiring, no new right now (recommended).** Sized in section 6 against provider capacity: the register holds no LSC funding series (Evidence still needed).
- **C. A federal right to counsel in eviction cases.** *Against:* the New York record shows the right outrunning the lawyers [EV-DEM-243].

## What it means for people

- *An asylum seeker in a backlog:* under A, the judge is not the prosecutor's employee, and a legal error can be reviewed by a federal court.
- *A tenant facing eviction:* under part 3 B, more legal aid lawyers in her county as providers can hire; no right she cannot use.

## Recommendation: part 1 A, part 2 B, part 3 B

- A meets every requirement the flagship decided, on a model Congress has used before [EV-DEM-244].
