# Memo 12: Protecting the civil service, and keeping payments flowing when funding lapses

Role: decision memo for Espen, fork 12 of the democratic renewal foundation (`../3-design.md`). Status: **awaiting Espen's decision**. Sources are named in plain text; bracketed tags point to `../evidence.json`. Agenda rows: CAP-8, CAP-9, CAP-4 (all L1). Answers section 2's question 13. **Homed elsewhere and read as a seam:** EXE-17 (rescinding Schedule Policy/Career by order) and EXE-18 (payment continuity in a lapse), both in executive power (wave three, `legacy/domains/domain-executive-power.md`).

**Decision line.** Recommended: **part 1 B, a statute that repeals Schedule Policy/Career, bars any schedule that strips career employees' appeal rights, restores the 2024 protections, and pairs them with a faster process for removing poor performers (the CAP-9 trade); part 2 B, a narrow automatic continuation in law: when appropriations lapse, benefit payments, payroll for staff who must work, and grants already awarded continue at the prior year's rate until a new law passes; no general automatic continuing resolution.** Cost: about zero for part 1; part 2 scores as new direct spending under CBO's convention but spends nothing a lapse would not later repay. Evidence: cross-national and quasi-experimental studies of merit hiring and patronage, the 2025-26 funding gaps, and CRS on automatic continuing resolutions.

## The question

Should career protections and payment continuity be written in statute, where a later President cannot undo them by order?

## Why it matters

- **Schedule Policy/Career is in force:** OPM's final rule of February 6, 2026 rescinds the 2024 appeal rights [EV-DEM-086], for about 50,000 positions by OPM's estimate [EV-DEM-087].
- **Funding stopped three times in seven months:** a 42-day government-wide gap from October 1, 2025, a 3-day gap from January 31, 2026, and a 75-day DHS gap [EV-DEM-127]; about 670,000 or more employees were furloughed in 2025 [EV-DEM-128].

## What the evidence says

- **Merit hiring goes with less corruption and better results:** across 52 countries, meritocratic recruitment predicts less corruption (observational) [EV-DEM-308]; when Brazilian mayors replaced municipal staff, school test scores fell (regression discontinuity) [EV-DEM-309]; British colonial governors appointed through patronage raised less revenue (quasi-experimental) [EV-DEM-310].
- **At-will employment in the states is contested:** Georgia's reform drew pessimism and rising resignations [EV-DEM-311] [EV-DEM-312]; Texas managers reported more responsive staff but mixed effects on fairness [EV-DEM-313]. In federal records for 1997-2019 there are almost no political cycles in the career service, and contracts run by politically misaligned officers had overruns 8% above the mean (Spenkuch, Teso and Xu) [EV-DEM-314].
- **Automatic continuing resolutions:** the one ever enacted, the 2013 Pay Our Military Act, ran 17 days; opponents argue a permanent one would slow appropriations and freeze spending; CBO scored such proposals as new direct spending (CRS) [EV-DEM-315]. In the states, 23% of budgets were late in 1988-2007 [EV-DEM-316].

## Part 1: the civil service

**A. Repeal and prohibit only (legacy CAP-8).** Statute bars the schedule; EXE-17 rescinds it on day one.
- *For:* simple; drafted against the actual rule text (`legacy/domains/domain-capacity.md`, line 165).
- *Against:* a defense with no answer on performance "loses the argument in public" (line 171).

**B. Repeal and prohibit, plus a faster process for poor performers (CAP-8 with CAP-9; recommended).** Protections against political removal, in exchange for a management system that can act on poor performance with due process.
- *For:* the trade the legacy file names; the evidence ties merit protection to results [EV-DEM-308] [EV-DEM-309].
- *Against:* unions will resist the performance half; its design is the hard part.

**C. Executive rescission only (EXE-17).** *Against:* reversible by the next order, which is how the schedule came back.

## Part 2: funding continuity

**A. A general automatic continuing resolution at prior-year levels, with penalties on Congress** (no recess, no other floor business until appropriations pass; legacy CAP-4, line 79).
- *For:* ends shutdowns; the penalty keeps pressure on Congress, not programs.
- *Against:* rewards whoever prefers flat funding; the opponents' case in CRS's account [EV-DEM-315].

**B. A narrow automatic continuation (recommended).** Benefit payments, payroll for staff who must work during a lapse, and grants already awarded continue at the prior year's rate; everything else lapses as today.
- *For:* protects the people the flagships pay, which is the failure the 2025-26 gaps tested; keeps the pressure on regular appropriations for everything else.
- *Against:* scores as direct spending under CBO's convention [EV-DEM-315]; overlaps EXE-18, which executive power designs.

**C. No statutory change;** EXE-18's executive design handles payments.
- *Against:* depends on the administration's willingness, which 2025-26 showed is not assured [EV-DEM-127].

## What it means for people

- *An SSA claims specialist:* under part 1 A or B, she cannot be moved into an at-will schedule for her views; under B, a poor performer in her office can be removed faster, with due process.
- *A family on SNAP or SSI during a funding fight:* under part 2 B, payments continue; under C, they depend on the administration's reading of the law.

## Recommendation: part 1 B, part 2 B

- Statute is the only instrument a later order cannot reverse; the narrow continuation protects payments without freezing the budget.
- **What would change this:** EXE-18's executive-power design covering payments fully (then part 2 C, with the statute held in reserve).
