**Decision line.** Recommended: **A, an ownership ban for members of Congress, their spouses and dependents, and the President, Vice President, Cabinet and other Senate-confirmed officials, met by divesting into broad funds or a qualified blind trust within 180 days, with penalties that apply automatically: the gain is paid back, plus a fine of 10% of the trade's value, assessed from the disclosure itself and published** (the 180 days and the 10% are this memo's judgment, for section 4 to test). Willful violations go to the Justice Department. Cost: near zero; CBO found no direct spending and a $3 million revenue loss over 2024-2034 for the Senate's ETHICS Act. Evidence: contested studies of members' trading returns, the STOCK Act's compliance record, and the record of ethics bodies without enforcement.

## The question

What does the plan ban, for whom, and who enforces it?

## Why it matters

- **The House passed a narrower bill on July 22, 2026, 232-198:** no new purchases, seven days' notice before a sale, members keep what they own [EV-DEM-034] [EV-DEM-035]. A Senate committee reported a broader ban (S. 1498) in 2025 [EV-DEM-034]. Unless enacted, both die in January 2027; if a weaker version is enacted in 2027-28, the 2029 bill amends it (legacy INT-1, `legacy/domains/domain-integrity.md`, line 50).
- **Rules without enforcement do not bind:** by January 2023, 78 members had failed to report trades properly, and the usual penalty is $200 or waived (Business Insider's count) [EV-DEM-220]; state ethics commissions show no measurable effect on corruption (an observational panel) [EV-DEM-227].

## What the evidence says

- **Whether members beat the market is contested.** Senators' purchases beat the market in 1993-98 (Ziobrowski and others) [EV-DEM-215]; later studies find no edge for the average member (Eggers and Hainmueller; Belmont and others) [EV-DEM-216] [EV-DEM-217]; members who rise to leadership outperform matched peers by 47 points a year afterward (Wei and Zhou, working paper) [EV-DEM-219]. A ban's case rests on trust and on the leadership finding, not on average returns.
- **Enforcement capacity is thin:** the Office of Government Ethics recorded 11 new criminal conflict-of-interest prosecutions in 2024 [EV-DEM-226], and its director was removed in February 2025 [EV-DEM-038].

## The options

**A. Ownership ban, Congress and senior executive, automatic penalties (recommended; legacy INT-1A with the enforcement added).**
- *For:* "members of Congress cannot own stocks" needs no explanation (legacy, line 198); automatic penalties do not depend on a committee's vote or a prosecutor's interest; covering the executive reaches the clearest conflicts.
- *Against:* forced sales cost members' families taxes (CBO's revenue line, below); covering the President and Vice President invites a separation-of-powers challenge.

**B. Trading ban only, the July 2026 House shape (INT-1B).**
- *For:* passed the House once; easier.
- *Against:* members keep existing holdings, the part critics said falls short [EV-DEM-035].

**C. Congress only (INT-1C).**
- *For:* drops the hardest fight.
- *Against:* leaves out the officials with the most direct power over markets.

**Enforcement (applies to A, B or C):** (i) the chambers' ethics committees as today; (ii) **automatic, published penalties computed from disclosures, collected by the chamber's ethics office, with willful cases referred to the Justice Department (recommended)**; (iii) a new independent ethics agency for both branches (stronger, but each House judges its own members under the Constitution, so penalties on members would still need the chamber).

## What it means for people

- *A member who owns bank stock and sits on the banking committee:* under A she sells or uses a blind trust within 180 days; under B she keeps it.
- *A Cabinet secretary:* covered under A and B as recommended here, not under C.

## Recommendation: A, with automatic penalties

- It covers what the House bill leaves out and fixes the STOCK Act's enforcement failure [EV-DEM-220].
- *Burden:* on covered officials, a one-time sale or trust; on the public, none.
- **What would change this:** a court ruling that the President cannot be covered by statute (then the rest stands by severability).
