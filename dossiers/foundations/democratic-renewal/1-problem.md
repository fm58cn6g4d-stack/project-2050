# Democratic renewal: the problem

Role: section 1 of the democratic renewal foundation. It sets out what is wrong, for whom and how large, and how the law will stand in January 2029 (`DEC-2026-10-29-01`), for the foundation's seven parts (`README.md`, "Scope"):
- A. the voting floor and election administration;
- B. money in politics;
- C. ethics and integrity;
- D. courts;
- E. participation and civic life;
- F. long-run institutional reform;
- G. a government that delivers: agency capacity, payment systems, and remedies when delivery fails. G.6 gathers the state-capacity needs the flagships have already specified.

Status: **draft** (stage F, step 1). **Research only: it recommends nothing and makes no design choice.** Espen allowed stage F groundwork on that condition (November 16). Every figure cites the register (`evidence.json`, `EV-DEM-001` on) or another dossier's register where it already holds the fact. Each entry there is labelled data or analysis and graded (`DEC-2026-10-28-07`). Figures from the second Trump administration's agencies are that administration's own data or claims, labeled as such; its analysis is presented as its view (`DEC-2026-10-30-01`). The legacy record is weighed as evidence, not as a default (`DEC-2026-11-13-15`): its figures are restated with file and line in "Inherited assumptions, traced", and where this section's sources differ, the section says so. Claims still without evidence are listed at the end and are not made here.

Outcomes this section frames (`charter/outcomes.md`):
- OUT-09, a democracy people can influence, and a government that delivers (all parts). Its measures are registration and turnout gaps, representation, benefit delivery times and error rates, access to remedies, and trust;
- OUT-10, connection: civic participation and service (part E);
- through delivery, every flagship's outcome (OUT-01 to OUT-07), because each flagship's promises run through the agencies in part G.

## In brief

- **About one eligible citizen in four was not registered in 2024, and the gaps follow age, education, income and race.**
  - In November 2024, 73.6% of voting-age citizens were registered and 65.3% voted [EV-DEM-001]. About 28.0 million were not registered, a floor, because many did not answer the question [EV-DEM-002].
  - Adults 18-29 were underrepresented among voters by 4.4 points, people with only a high school credential by 5.6 points [EV-DEM-003] [EV-DEM-004], and Hispanic citizens by 3.1 points [EV-DEM-005].
  - The disability turnout gap is 7.3 points on validated turnout, against 4.9 on self-report (one working paper) [EV-DEM-006].
- **The federal voting-rights floor has narrowed to almost nothing.**
  - Preclearance has been inoperable since *Shelby County* (2013) [EV-DEM-011].
  - *Louisiana v. Callais* (April 29, 2026) limits Section 2 to cases with a strong inference of intentional discrimination [EV-DEM-007]. CRS and the Brennan Center expect fewer successful cases and challenges to existing districts; the Manhattan Institute argued the old framework was the problem [EV-DEM-008] [EV-DEM-009] [EV-DEM-010].
  - Since the 2024 election, 22 states have enacted 47 restrictive voting laws and 32 states 61 expansive ones [EV-DEM-012].
- **The federal election agencies cannot act.** The Election Assistance Commission has had no commissioners since July 9, 2026 [EV-DEM-020]. The FEC had two of six seats filled in March 2026, and most actions need four votes [EV-DEM-031].
- **Money moves outside the limits.** Independent expenditures reported to the FEC were $4.4 billion in 2023-24 [EV-DEM-023]. Dark money was $1.4 billion (OpenSecrets) to more than $1.9 billion (Brennan Center), by method [EV-DEM-026] [EV-DEM-025]. *NRSC v. FEC* (June 30, 2026) ended the limits on party coordinated spending [EV-DEM-027].
- **The checks inside the executive were removed or weakened.** A court held the firing of 17 inspectors general unlawful but did not reinstate them [EV-DEM-036]. *Trump v. Slaughter* (June 29, 2026) overruled *Humphrey's Executor* [EV-DEM-037]. The Supreme Court's 2023 code has no enforcement mechanism [EV-DEM-040].
- **Courts.** The Judicial Conference asks for 71 judgeships [EV-DEM-041]; the 2024 JUDGES Act was vetoed [EV-DEM-044]. *CASA*, *Loper Bright*, *Corner Post* and *Slaughter* reset remedies, deference and agency structure [EV-DEM-047] [EV-DEM-051] [EV-DEM-052] [EV-DEM-053]. 34% approve of the Supreme Court (September 2026) [EV-DEM-062].
- **Trust is near its floor.** 17% trust the government in Washington to do what is right most of the time (Pew, September 2025) [EV-DEM-065].
- **The federal workforce shrank by about a tenth, and the agencies the flagships depend on lost the most.**
  - OPM counts a net change of −258,108 federal employees since January 20, 2025 (data as of September 2026; second Trump administration data) [EV-DEM-082].
  - IRS staff fell from 94,095 to 70,829 FTE (FY2025 to FY2026) [EV-DEM-088]; HUD's from about 8,800 to about 6,300 [EV-DEM-096]; SSA's full-time permanent staff from 56,645 (FY2024) to 48,530 (FY2026), SSA's own table [EV-DEM-105]; USAID's from about 13,600 to 241 [EV-DEM-099].
- **Delivery already fails in measurable ways.** About $186 billion of improper payments in FY2025 [EV-DEM-113]. Callers to SSA waited an average 108.6 minutes for a callback, and about 25 million calls ended without service in FY2025 [EV-DEM-106]. Veterans wait 2.4 years on the appeals hearing docket [EV-DEM-123]. Federal customer satisfaction fell for the first time in five years [EV-DEM-129].
- **Funding itself became unreliable.** A 42-day government-wide funding gap from October 1, 2025, a 3-day gap from January 31, 2026, and a 75-day gap at DHS [EV-DEM-127]. GAO found Impoundment Control Act violations five times by April 2026 [EV-DEM-132]. GAO and OMB disagree on pocket rescissions, and courts have not settled it [EV-DEM-133] [EV-DEM-135] [EV-DEM-134].
- **States carry more of the load from 2026-28.** The federal share of SNAP administration fell from 50% to 25% on October 1, 2026 [EV-DEM-136]. States with high SNAP error rates pay part of benefits from FY2028 [EV-DEM-137]. Medicaid work requirements start by January 1, 2027 [EV-DEM-139].
- **Every flagship already hands capacity to this foundation** (G.6): CAP-7 and CAP-7S carry the IRS, SSA, CMS, HUD, FEMA and Labor rebuilds the flagships assume, shared rule 5 (who verifies and answers for mistakes) is owned here, and the immigration court's form and judge count are set here. **No flagship's needs have a settled agency share** (G.7).

### A.2 The federal floor after *Shelby County* and *Callais*

- **Preclearance.** *Shelby County v. Holder* (2013) struck the coverage formula, which left preclearance inoperable. Section 2's nationwide ban stands [EV-DEM-011].
- ***Callais*.** On April 29, 2026 the Court held that Section 2 is violated only on evidence supporting a strong inference that a state intentionally drew districts to give minority voters less opportunity because of race [EV-DEM-007]. The legacy file describes the same holding from the syllabus (`domain-democracy.md`, line 84).
- **Its effect is contested.**
  - CRS: existing majority-minority districts drawn to comply with Section 2 may be challenged as racial gerrymanders, and some legislatures changed maps for 2026 [EV-DEM-008].
  - The Brennan Center: three changes to the *Gingles* framework that together make Section 2 cases much harder to win [EV-DEM-009].
  - The Manhattan Institute, before the decision: the problem lay with *Gingles*, not the Act, which disavows proportional representation [EV-DEM-010].
  - Both views are analysis. The holding itself is data.
- **What it means for 2029.** The holding stands unless Congress amends Section 2 or the Court revisits it. The legacy DEM-1 answers it in Title III, with "no guarantee of judicial survival" (`domain-democracy.md`, line 84). Weighing that answer is for section 3.

### A.3 State laws and federal proof-of-citizenship efforts

- **States moved both ways.** Since the 2024 election, 47 restrictive laws in 22 states and 61 expansive laws in 32 states (Brennan Center tracker, October 2026) [EV-DEM-012]. The count is an advocate's classification; the laws are data.
- **The March 2025 executive order.** A federal court permanently enjoined the EAC in October 2025 from requiring documentary proof of citizenship on the federal form under section 2(a) of Executive Order 14,248 [EV-DEM-013]. CRS reports earlier preliminary rulings in D.C. and Massachusetts finding the citizenship provisions likely beyond the President's power [EV-DEM-014].
- **The SAVE America Act.** The House passed it 218-213 on February 11, 2026, requiring documentary proof of citizenship to register and photo ID to vote [EV-DEM-015]. The Senate voted 48-50 against attaching it in June 2026, with 60 needed [EV-DEM-016]. Unless enacted, it dies with the 119th Congress.
- **The Justice Department's voter-roll suits.** It has sued 29 states and D.C. besides Michigan for unredacted voter rolls; as of June 2026 no court had found for it, several suits were dismissed, and the Sixth Circuit ruled against it in Michigan [EV-DEM-021]. This is a news count (Votebeat); a CRS or court-list count is still needed.

### A.4 Who runs elections, and with what

- **Money.** HAVA election security grants were $55 million in FY2024, $15 million in FY2025 and $45 million in FY2026 [EV-DEM-017]. The legacy DEM-11's "$1-4B a year" is the legacy author's own estimate "from the scale of HAVA appropriations", with no source (`domain-democracy.md`, line 220). No source in the register sizes what a federal floor would cost counties (Evidence still needed).
- **People.** More than 770,000 poll workers served in 2024, most aged 61 or older, and 47.9% of jurisdictions had significant trouble recruiting them [EV-DEM-019]. In 2026, 32% of local election officials reported threats, harassment or abuse; satisfaction with federal support fell from 52% to 45% [EV-DEM-022].
- **Ballots that do not count.** States rejected 584,463 mail ballots (1.2% of those returned) and about 25.1% of provisional ballots in 2024 [EV-DEM-018].
- **The federal agency.** The EAC has had no commissioners since July 9, 2026, when they were removed, so it cannot adopt voting system guidelines or change the federal form [EV-DEM-020]. After *Slaughter* [EV-DEM-037], its structure is a design question for section 3, with the legacy EXE-9 template.

## Part C. Ethics and integrity

- **Stock trading.** The House passed H.R. 7008 on July 22, 2026, barring new purchases by members, spouses and dependents and requiring 7 days' notice before sales; a Senate committee reported S. 1498 in December 2025 [EV-DEM-034]. The vote was 232-198, and critics said it falls short of a ban because members keep what they own [EV-DEM-035]. The legacy file has the same vote and treats it as the "floor the Democratic version must hold" (`domain-integrity.md`, lines 48-50). As of this writing nothing is enacted; bills not enacted die in January 2027.
- **Inspectors general.** In September 2025 a district court held that firing 17 inspectors general without 30 days' notice to Congress violated the Inspector General Act, but declined to reinstate the eight plaintiffs [EV-DEM-036]. The notice-and-reasons design the legacy EXE-5 proposes is the law already broken here (`domain-executive-power.md`, lines 81-85); what enforces it is the question.
- **Removal after *Slaughter*.** For-cause protection of FTC commissioners is unconstitutional; *Humphrey's Executor* is overruled [EV-DEM-037]. CRS judges that other commissions' protections, including the NLRB, FERC and the Federal Reserve, "appear to be on precarious legal footing" [EV-DEM-054] (one publisher's analysis; no independent view found).
- **The ethics office.** The President removed the Director of the Office of Government Ethics in February 2025, confirmed the previous December to a position that typically runs five years [EV-DEM-038].
- **Emoluments.** The first-term suits ended without a merits ruling: after the 2021 change of administration the Court vacated and ordered dismissal as moot [EV-DEM-039]. That is the gap the legacy INT-4's private cause of action is meant to fill (`domain-integrity.md`, line 72).
- **The Supreme Court.** Its first Code of Conduct (November 13, 2023) has no enforcement mechanism, and the Judicial Conduct and Disability Act does not reach the Justices [EV-DEM-040] [EV-DEM-046].

## Part D. Courts

### D.1 Judges and caseloads

- The Judicial Conference asked in March 2025 for 2 appeals and 69 district judgeships [EV-DEM-041]. Weighted filings exceed 500 a judgeship in 20 of the 25 districts it wants enlarged; filings have grown 30% since 1990 and judgeships 4% since 1991 [EV-DEM-042]. The district median was 411 weighted filings a judgeship (year to September 2024), against the Conference's usual threshold of 430 [EV-DEM-043].
- President Biden vetoed the JUDGES Act on December 23, 2024 [EV-DEM-044]. House Judiciary ordered reported a 2025 version with 65 permanent and 1 temporary district judgeships over staggered years [EV-DEM-045]; its later fate is unchecked.
- The legacy CRT-1's 66 seats follow the 2024 bill's lineage (`domain-courts.md`, line 65). The Conference's current request is 71 [EV-DEM-041].

## Part E. Participation and civic life

- **Trust.** Pew: 17% trust the government in Washington to do what is right just about always or most of the time (September 2025), down from 22% [EV-DEM-065]. Gallup asks differently: 45% trust the federal government on international problems and 38% on domestic ones, and trust in the three branches runs from 32% to 49% [EV-DEM-066]. Both series move with party control (context of EV-DEM-066); local government is trusted far more (`dossiers/foundations/rural-delivery.md`, "In brief", item 4).
- **Volunteering.** 28.3% of people 16 and older volunteered through an organization in 2022-23, up from 23.2% in 2021 but 1.7 points below the pre-pandemic rate [EV-DEM-067]; 54.2% helped neighbors [EV-DEM-068]. National service (LAB-9) is the work flagship's; this foundation's civic rows (DEM-16 to DEM-18) are small by comparison.
- **The citizen initiative.** At least eight 2026 ballot measures addressed the initiative process, the most since 2000, seven of them adding restrictions [EV-DEM-069]; legislators filed more than 90 bills in 2026 to change it [EV-DEM-070]. The November 2026 results are not yet in the register. The legacy DEM-19 is a state track because no federal hook exists (`domain-democracy.md`, line 289).
- **Libraries.** A federal court permanently enjoined the dismantling of the Institute of Museum and Library Services in November 2025 [EV-DEM-071], and the administration withdrew its appeal in April 2026 (the library association's report) [EV-DEM-072]. The legacy file cites a different case's April 2026 settlement (`domain-democracy.md`, line 246); both are noted, and the court record is still to be read.

## Part F. Long-run institutional reform

### F.1 The Senate's rules (D2)

- **The record.** Senate cloture motions filed per Congress rose from 71 in the 107th (2001-02) to 328 in the 116th, 336 in the 117th and 266 in the 118th; 301 have been filed so far in the 119th [EV-DEM-073]. The counts include nominations.
- **Why it decides this foundation.** Nearly every item homed here needs a floor vote outside reconciliation: the legacy files tag 11 of the 14 representation and elections items, all of CRT-1 to CRT-15 and INT-1 to INT-16 as needing D2's rules path (`domain-democracy.md`, line 305; `domain-courts.md`, line 220; `domain-integrity.md`, line 176). The legacy decision is F, graduated cloture, in week one of 2029, with the talking filibuster as the opening offer and a democracy carve-out as the fallback (`legacy/governing/project-2050-governing-decisions.md`, D2).
- **What changed since.** The scenario found no Merkley-Daines "Block 1": the October 2026 proposal is one package needing 67 votes, with no post-election vote found (`dossiers/scenario/4-2029-plan.md`, D2 row). Whether a rules change happens in 2027 or waits for 2029 is the scenario's question 4 for Espen (`dossiers/scenario/3-framework-2027-28.md`). **This section does not reopen D2.**

### F.2 The House

The House has had 435 members since 1913, apart from a temporary 437 [EV-DEM-074]. After the 2020 census the average district had 761,169 people, from 990,837 in Delaware to 542,704 in Montana [EV-DEM-075]. The legacy DEM-3 figure of "about 760,000 now" agrees (`domain-democracy.md`, line 130).

**Redistricting (DEM-2).** The facts DEM-2's memo will need are in the scenario dossier: the 2025-26 mid-decade redraws, *Callais*'s effect on them, the bipartisan bills to ban mid-decade redistricting (CRS counts about 70 since the mid-2000s) and the 2031-pens model (`dossiers/scenario/memo-defense-baseline-and-dem2.md`, choice b; `redistricting-2030-model.md`). DEM-2's structure goes to this dossier's memo set ("Directions for later stages"); section 1 adds nothing to it.

### F.3 The Electoral College

- The National Popular Vote compact counts 19 jurisdictions with 222 electoral votes and needs 48 more [EV-DEM-076]. This is the compact's own organization; an independent count is still needed. It matches the legacy file (`domain-democracy.md`, line 196).
- The Electoral Count Reform Act of 2022 (Division P of P.L. 117-328) is in force [EV-DEM-080]; it raised the objection threshold to one-fifth of each chamber and made the Vice President's role administrative [EV-DEM-081].

### F.4 Article V

- A convention needs applications from 34 state legislatures, and no official list of applications exists (CRS, 2017) [EV-DEM-077].
- Convention of States counts 20 states for its application, Kansas the 20th in 2026 [EV-DEM-078], as the legacy file says (`domain-amendments.md`, line 37). The aggregation question the legacy AMD-9 raises (`domain-amendments.md`, lines 88-101) is exactly where these counts diverge.

### G.3 Systems and payments

- **The rails exist.** Treasury's Bureau of the Fiscal Service made 87.8% of all federal payments in FY2024: nearly 1.3 billion payments, $5.4 trillion, 96.9% electronic [EV-DEM-116]. The legacy "over 1.4 billion" is not in a primary document. Every flagship's payment runs on these rails or the IRS's and SSA's (G.6).
- **The systems underneath are old.** The government spends more than $100 billion a year on IT, mostly on existing systems, and runs critical missions on decades-old technology (GAO High-Risk, 2025) [EV-DEM-111]. Of 38 high-risk areas, 20 are there partly for skills gaps or too few staff [EV-DEM-110].

### G.4 Errors, take-up and burden

- **Improper payments.** Agencies reported about $186 billion for FY2025 across 64 programs, about $24 billion more than the year before; GAO says the total misses some programs [EV-DEM-113]. The agency figures are the agencies' own method.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Medicaid | $37.4 billion | 6.1% | [EV-DEM-114] |
| Earned Income Tax Credit | $21.1 billion | 32.7% | [EV-DEM-114] [EV-DEM-115] |

  GAO names Medicare, Medicaid, unemployment insurance and the EITC among the programs behind about 80% of improper payments [EV-DEM-112]. An improper payment is not fraud: it includes payments with missing documentation and underpayments (context of EV-DEM-113).
- **Under-reach is as large a failure as over-payment.** 78% of eligible taxpayers claimed the EITC in tax year 2021 (81% of dollars) [EV-DEM-117]; SNAP reached 88% of eligible people in FY2022, its highest on record [EV-DEM-118].
- **Burden moves take-up.**
  - In a randomized trial with 30,000 older people likely eligible for SNAP, 6% enrolled with no help, 11% with information and 18% with information plus help applying [EV-DEM-119].
  - Closing SSA field offices cut disability recipients nearby by 16%, while applications fell 10% [EV-DEM-120].
  - Herd and Moynihan argue that learning, compliance and psychological costs reduce take-up and fall hardest on the disadvantaged [EV-DEM-121] (analysis, corroborated by the two studies above).
  - This is the evidence base for the project's design lens, "guide, don't police" (`DEC-2026-11-09-13`).

### G.5 Remedies when delivery fails, and the money that stops

- **Appeals queues.** The Board of Veterans' Appeals ended FY2024 with 200,805 appeals pending after deciding a record 116,192 [EV-DEM-122]; hearing-docket waits are 2.4 years, though pending appeals fell each year from FY2023 to FY2025 [EV-DEM-123]. SSA's hearings took 270 days in FY2026 [EV-DEM-104]; the number pending is still to be read.
- **When a system breaks at scale.** Labor's standard for prompt first unemployment payments is 87% [EV-DEM-124]. In the pandemic, GAO estimated unemployment fraud at $100-135 billion, 11-15% of benefits [EV-DEM-125]; Labor's inspector general estimated at least $191 billion of improper payments [EV-DEM-126]. The two measure different things (fraud against improper payments).
- **Shutdowns.** A government-wide gap of 42 days from October 1, 2025, a 3-day gap from January 31, 2026, and a 75-day DHS gap from February 14 to April 30, 2026 [EV-DEM-127]; around 670,000 or more federal employees were furloughed in 2025 [EV-DEM-128]. The legacy EXE-18 (payment continuity) is homed in `agenda/executive-power`; its premise, that a payment can stop for want of an appropriation or of staff, is what these gaps tested.
- **Impoundment.** GAO found Impoundment Control Act violations five times in the second Trump term by April 2026, with four findings of no violation and two mixed [EV-DEM-132]. After the July 2025 rescissions act, the President sent a pocket rescission of nearly $5 billion of foreign aid in August 2025 [EV-DEM-143]. GAO concluded that a September 2026 special message cannot withhold funds past the fiscal year's end [EV-DEM-133]; OMB says it is in full compliance [EV-DEM-135]; CRS says courts have not decided the merits [EV-DEM-134]. Both views are given; the legacy EXE-3 (a cause of action for the intended recipient) is homed elsewhere.
