# Research note: USDA's rural agencies as delivery channels

Role: cross-cutting research for board row R1. Espen wrote on November 9, 2026 that USDA Rural Development, the Rural Utilities Service and the Rural Housing Service "are some of the most effective direct govt programs the fed govt has ... trusted locally often because they are staffed and run locally and have direct and immediate results", and asked how Project 2050 could "supercharge them", weighing bigger programs and broader eligibility ("not just buyers over 62, etc"). This note describes what the three agencies run, what the evidence says about their results and local trust, what limits them, and the options for scaling each channel, then lists what each dossier can take from it. Status: draft (stage C). It decides nothing. Housing's memo 13 (row 4j) is the first reader; other dossiers draw on section 5 when their turn comes.

**Sources.** The note cites library sources directly, SRC-2100 to SRC-2178 in `library/catalog.csv`, plus earlier rows where noted (SRC-1898, SRC-1899, SRC-1912, SRC-1913, SRC-2000, SRC-2001). It has no `EV-` register. GAO's site, usda.gov, rd.usda.gov and most news and advocate sites block downloads from agent sessions; those were read through search tools, and their catalog rows say so, so the reviewer should check those quotations against the originals. USDA's budget justifications were saved from the content host that serves usda.gov files (catalog notes say which).

**How to read the evidence.** Program counts, dollar levels and rules are data. Claims about effects are analysis. Most program data for 2025 and 2026 come from the second Trump administration's own budget documents and releases, which this project treats with extra skepticism for their analysis (`DEC-2026-10-30-01`); their counts of loans and dollars are used as records, their reasons for cuts as that administration's view. Figures labeled **judgment** are this note's estimates (grade D), not evidence. The plan starts in January 2029 (`DEC-2026-10-29-01`), so every option is dated from 2029 on.

## In brief

1. **A large lender with a thin and shrinking staff.** Rural Development holds a $225.6 billion loan portfolio at the end of FY2025 [SRC-2146] and runs about 90 programs [SRC-2172] through state offices and more than 400 offices in all [SRC-2158]. Its staff fell from over 6,000 before the 2012-13 buyouts to about 5,000 [SRC-2127], to 4,455 full-time equivalents in FY2025, and to an estimated 3,058 in FY2026 [SRC-2130]. Between January and June 2025 it lost 1,745 of its 4,910 employees, 36%, the highest rate of any major USDA program agency [SRC-2143].
2. **Congress keeps funding it; the agency now leaves money unspent.** In FY2026, Section 502 Direct lent $571 million to 2,115 families out of $1 billion provided [SRC-1899] [SRC-2126]; advocates count about $180 million that expired [SRC-2155] [SRC-2156]. In FY2025 the rental preservation demonstration used about $1.1 million of $34 million, and Section 515 lending $8 million of $60 million [SRC-2131]. No source separates the causes: the 43-day shutdown, staff losses, the February 2026 rule changes for Section 502 and a new senior-approval step [SRC-2112] [SRC-2169] [SRC-2146].
3. **The evidence of results is mostly records, not causal studies.** The 1930s electrification loans raised farm output and were nearly all repaid [SRC-2161]. Studies of USDA broadband loans find gains mainly near cities, with wide uncertainty [SRC-2159] [SRC-2160]. No causal evaluation was found for Section 502, Section 504, water grants, community facilities or business guarantees; the business-guarantee job figures come from a lenders' group and show only correlation [SRC-2101]. Delinquency on Section 502 Direct is high: nearly a fifth of loans in March 2025 [SRC-2000].
4. **Local trust is plausible but unmeasured.** No survey asks about Rural Development itself. Americans trust local government (65%) far more than the federal government on domestic problems (38%) [SRC-2162], but one survey finds rural and urban residents trust local government about equally [SRC-2163]. USDA was the top-scoring federal agency for customer satisfaction in 2025 and fell sharply in 2026 [SRC-2164]. The strongest structural point is that RD's own staff take and decide applications in state and local offices, where HUD's public housing works through more than 3,000 local housing agencies (Congressional Research Service) [SRC-1898] [SRC-0302].
5. **Rules limit reach.** Seven statutory definitions of "rural" set limits from 5,000 to 50,000 people [SRC-2111]. Section 504 grants go only to owners 62 and over with very low incomes, up to $10,000 in a lifetime [SRC-1913]. Section 502 Direct is limited to incomes at or below 80% of area median, and its loan limit was cut in February 2026 from 80% to 60% of HUD's limits [SRC-2123] [SRC-2169].
6. **The rural rental stock is draining away.** About 12,000 Section 515 properties with about 380,000 apartments remain (March 2026), and 621 properties left between 2021 and 2026 [SRC-2154]. GAO found over 90% of properties could leave between 2028 and 2050 [SRC-2148]. Appropriations acts have let rent help continue after a mortgage ends since FY2024, and the ROAD to Housing Act (July 2026) made that permanent [SRC-0437]; under the earlier authority only eight such contracts had closed by December 2025 [SRC-2131].
7. **Scaling is mainly a staffing and systems problem, not a money problem.** Several channels cost little in budget terms: Section 502 guarantees, community facility direct loans and electric loans run at about zero or negative subsidy [SRC-2125]. What binds is staff, a mainframe-era loan system [SRC-2146] and the administration's stated plan to keep 2026's lower staffing and end some loan origination [SRC-2130].
8. **What this points to** (options, for each dossier's decision). Rebuild the field staff first (to roughly 5,000-6,000, about $0.3-0.5 billion a year, judgment); replace the loan systems; then scale the channels with proven take-up and simple rules: Section 502 Direct with nonprofit packagers, Section 504 opened to all low-income owners regardless of age, rent help after every 515 mortgage ends plus a preservation fund, competitive community-facility and water grants restored, and electric lending for rural clean power. One plain rural test across programs is worth weighing; the evidence on who it would add is thin.

## 1. What exists

### 1.1 Structure and staff

Rural Development is a USDA mission area with three agencies: the Rural Housing Service (RHS), the Rural Utilities Service (RUS) and the Rural Business-Cooperative Service (RBCS) [SRC-2146]. A national office in Washington and St. Louis sets policy and runs centralized servicing; state offices led by presidentially appointed state directors take and process direct loan and grant applications (CRS) [SRC-1898]. USDA counted more than 400 offices in June 2026 [SRC-2158] (its history page lists 47 state and territorial offices); GAO counted more than 400 offices at the end of FY2012 too [SRC-2149].

| Year | Staff | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before FY2012-13 buyouts | Over 6,000 staff years | USDA budget notes (Obama administration) [SRC-2127] |
| FY2012 | About 5,100 employees: about 1,400 headquarters operations, 3,700 field | GAO [SRC-2149] |
| September 2022 | 4,616 permanent full-time: 334 headquarters, 4,282 field | USDA budget notes (Biden administration) [SRC-2128] |
| FY2024 / FY2025 | 4,411 / 4,455 full-time equivalents | USDA budget notes, FY2027 [SRC-2130] |
| January-June 2025 | 1,745 of 4,910 employees left (36%) | USDA Inspector General [SRC-2143] |
| FY2026 (estimate) | 3,058 full-time equivalents | USDA budget notes, FY2027 [SRC-2130] |

The headquarters share depends on whether St. Louis counts as headquarters: on USDA's tables RD is about 95% field, on GAO's 2012 count about 72% (this note's arithmetic). Either way it is a field agency.

**2025-2026 changes (second Trump administration).**
- Most departures came through the deferred resignation program [SRC-2143]. USDA's own FY2027 budget notes say "RD is at historically low staffing levels compared to its loan and grant portfolio", that "Some RD loan and grant origination activities will be terminated", and that RD will operate while keeping "the reduced staffing levels realized in 2026" [SRC-2130]. RD's Under Secretary told a farm paper that RD had 3,100 staff after a cut of 1,500 and was hiring an engineer and a public information officer for every state [SRC-2173].
- The FY2026 request had already proposed cutting RD by 1,503 full-time equivalents, 32% (CRS) [SRC-2104]. USDA's July 2025 reorganization moved most Washington staff to five hubs and named no change specific to RD [SRC-2141] [SRC-2114]. RD's June 2026 plan centralizes "loan origination, processing, and servicing functions under one centralized national framework" while keeping state program staff in place [SRC-2158]. That moves RD toward the central model of FHA, away from the local underwriting Espen values; the release gives no evidence for its stated rationale.
- Effects on the ground are reported, not measured. A former Under Secretary (Biden administration, a critic) testified that Mississippi lost 90% of its RD staff [SRC-2101]; a non-random advocate survey found a median state staff cut of 46% [SRC-2157]; a rural owners' trade group reported six staff nationally reviewing preservation deals [SRC-2174]. The reorganization memo said cuts to "front-line facing positions will be minimized" [SRC-2141]; USDA's own tables show field permanent staff falling from 4,205 in FY2025 to 2,879 planned for FY2026 [SRC-2130].

**Systems.** RD runs five loan and grant accounting and servicing systems plus a funds-control system; the single-family servicing system, LoanServ, "is a commercial mainframe product" [SRC-2146]. The FY2025 financial audit found a new material weakness: a projected error of more than $2 billion in $55.8 billion of open obligations, and a new rule that senior leadership approve grants and loans before funds are allotted [SRC-2146]. RD plans to merge its systems into one platform, starting with business guarantees [SRC-2130] [SRC-2173]. USDA lists an Inspector General review of RHS's loan-application software project among its open audits [SRC-2131]; its recommendations are withheld from the public version. The ROAD to Housing Act directs GAO to report on RHS technology by July 2027 and permits, without funding, more staff and better systems (CRS) [SRC-0437].

### 1.2 How the money flows

- **Annual appropriations.** For loans, each year's act sets a loan level and pays the expected cost, the credit subsidy; grants are appropriated directly (CRS) [SRC-1898] [SRC-2112]. RD's leaders described it as delivering "over $40 billion annually" in 2025 [SRC-2101]; the Under Secretary put FY2026 at nearly $25 billion [SRC-2172].
- **Subsidy rates decide how far a dollar goes.** For FY2026, OMB estimates Section 502 Direct at about 13% (each $1 of appropriation supports about $7.70 of loans), Section 502 guarantees at about −0.8% (fees exceed expected losses), community facility direct loans at about −3.5%, Section 515 at about 30%, and electric loans at about −1% to −4% [SRC-2125]. Water and waste loans run at about 4.8% [SRC-2125] ($47.6 million of subsidy for $1 billion of loans in FY2026) [SRC-2116]. Values read from OMB's tables should be checked against the PDF (catalog note).
- **State allocations, pooling and expiry.** A regulation sets each state's share by formula, holds a national reserve, and "pools" money a state has not used, usually near August 1 [SRC-2100]. Most loan subsidy is one-year money unless the act says otherwise; Section 502 Direct's FY2026 money was split between FY2026 and FY2027 [SRC-1899] [SRC-2126].
- **Earmarks.** Community facility grants are 99% earmarked, "leaving little for USDA to allocate competitively" [SRC-2112]; in FY2026, 44% of water and waste grants went to earmarks [SRC-2116].
- **Authority.** RHS housing programs rest on the permanent Housing Act of 1949. The other programs rest on farm bills: the 2018 farm bill was extended through FY2026 [SRC-2106], and the 2025 reconciliation law did not reauthorize RD programs [SRC-2113]. A 2026 farm bill passed the House and the Senate Agriculture Committee but was not enacted as of November 2026 [SRC-2107]. Most RD programs keep running on appropriations when authority lapses [SRC-2113]. ReConnect has no specific statutory authority [SRC-2105].

### 1.6 What counts as rural

Congress has written "seven rural definitions that primarily use population thresholds" (CRS) [SRC-2111]:

| Programs | Limit |
| --- | --- |
| Telecommunications loans | 5,000 |
| Water and waste direct loans and grants | 10,000 (guarantees 50,000 since 2018) |
| Community facilities; electric (plus existing service areas); broadband direct loans and grants; ReConnect by rule | 20,000 |
| Housing: up to 2,500; to 10,000 if "rural in character"; to 20,000 outside metro areas with a "serious lack of mortgage credit"; places that were rural before the 2020 census keep eligibility up to 35,000 | 2,500-35,000 |
| Business programs (B&I, REAP) | 50,000 |

"Rural in character" is applied as a density screen of 1,000 people per square mile [SRC-2111]. CRS notes that grandfathering keeps help in places but spreads "limited resources" and treats similar towns differently [SRC-2111]. No government count of how many people live in housing-eligible areas was found; the widely repeated "97% of land, 109 million people" comes from lender marketing and predates the 2020 census. GAO last studied the housing definition in 2004 [SRC-2152].

## 2. Evidence of results and local trust

### 2.1 Results

| Channel | Best evidence | What it shows | Strength |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Electric loans, 1930s | Kitchens and Fishback (NBER, later Journal of Economic History) [SRC-2161] | Loans raised farm output and productivity, with smaller effects outside farming; "nearly all of the loans were fully repaid" | Causal, historical |
| Electric loans, 1990s | GAO [SRC-2122] [SRC-2121] | More than $1.7 billion written off on a few generation cooperatives' nuclear and coal debts; a further ~$3 billion at risk on one bankruptcy | Records |
| Broadband loans | Kandilov and Renkow (2010, 2020; same authors) [SRC-2159] [SRC-2160] | Positive effects driven by communities closest to cities; the main loan program's benefit-cost ratio 1.98-2.99, with intervals wide enough that costs may exceed benefits | Causal, one team |
| ReConnect | GAO 2026 [SRC-2119]; Inspector General [SRC-2136] [SRC-2137] | Spending slow: 69% of FY2020 awards spent by April 2026, 1% of FY2025 awards; no buildout reported; a mapping error overstated unserved homes in two approved applications ($17.8 million); no program-specific goals yet [SRC-2153] | Records |
| Section 502 Direct | Petach (2024) [SRC-2001]; ProPublica [SRC-2000] | States with more 502 loans have higher ownership, especially Black and Hispanic households (correlation); nearly a fifth of 159,208 direct loans delinquent, March 2025 | Correlation; records |
| Self-help (523) | Old USDA material (about 2000; not catalogued) | Claims of lower delinquency than other 502 borrowers; families self-select. No recent data found | Weak |
| Section 515 | GAO [SRC-2148]; An (2026) [SRC-2170] | 60% of properties with maturing mortgages left in 2014-17; nonprofit owners 30-40% less likely to convert to market rents (observational) | Records; correlation |
| B&I | Lenders' group study cited in testimony [SRC-2101]; Inspector General 2003 [SRC-2140] | "A positive correlation" with jobs; about $1.1 billion of losses paid through 2001 | Correlation; records |
| REAP | Inspector General [SRC-2139] | Energy savings reported by USDA are projections; an audit found energy produced or saved "was not accurately reported" | Records |
| Water, community facilities | — | No evaluation found | — |

**Reading.** Rural Development has one strong causal success, historical electrification, and many programs that reach people at scale with records but no counterfactual. It has also had large losses where it lent to big, risky borrowers (1990s power plants, early business guarantees). The record does not show that its programs work better than alternatives; it shows they reach rural people other channels often miss, at low budget cost for the loan programs. This note found no study comparing how fast help arrives through RD and through HUD or states.

### 2.2 Speed

USDA reports its median eligibility decision for Section 502 Direct fell from 84 to 64 days in FY2026 (administration release) [SRC-1899]. The Inspector General found 10 of 12 sampled Illinois rental properties still had unresolved physical deficiencies 294 to 831 days after notice [SRC-2134]. RD's FY2025 performance measures were not published [SRC-2146]. There is no RD-wide time series on processing.

### 2.3 Local trust

- **No direct measure.** Pew's recent agency favorability surveys do not include USDA (checked through 2026). No survey of trust in Rural Development was found.
- **Proxies.** 65% of Americans trust local government to handle local problems, 59% state government, 38% the federal government on domestic problems (Gallup, 2025) [SRC-2162]. A university survey finds "nearly identical shares of urban and rural residents express high trust" in local government [SRC-2163], so a rural trust premium should not be assumed. USDA was 2025's top-scoring agency in the American Customer Satisfaction Index and had the second-largest fall in 2026; the index's researcher links the fall to staff cuts, a correlational reading [SRC-2164].
- **Structure.** RD's own employees, in state and area offices, take and process applications (CRS) [SRC-1898]. Advocates argue this field structure helps it reach rural residents, a view CRS reports without endorsing [SRC-1898]. HUD's public housing runs through more than 3,000 local public housing agencies (CRS) [SRC-0302]. RD's June 2026 centralization moves away from this model [SRC-2158].

**Reading.** Espen's view that RD is trusted because it is local is consistent with the proxies but not tested. The design question this leaves for each dossier is practical: keep decisions in state offices, with staff who know the place, and measure satisfaction and speed by state so the claim can be checked.

## 3. Limits

### 3.1 Staff, backlogs and systems

- Staffing is about a third below FY2025 and, by USDA's own plan, will stay there [SRC-2130]. Engineers matter for water and community facility projects; a former Under Secretary testified that 16 remained in the state system in 2025 [SRC-2101].
- In FY2025 and FY2026 several programs obligated well below their appropriations (section 1.3) [SRC-2131] [SRC-1899]. House members asked USDA to report staffing and processing times [SRC-2124]; the Under Secretary blamed the shutdown [SRC-2173]. Both views stand; no source isolates the cause.
- Old, separate systems, a projected $2 billion error in open obligations, a new approval layer and withheld findings on the housing application software [SRC-2146] [SRC-2131].
- Oversight is light rather than alarming. The Inspector General's 2025 list of USDA management challenges has no RD-specific item [SRC-2144]; its FY2026 plan includes a review of RD's oversight of loan-servicing contractors [SRC-2147]. GAO found in 2016 that the 502 guarantee program was not fully consistent with OMB's standards for credit programs, and GAO has since closed several of its 11 recommendations as implemented [SRC-2150]; an Inspector General audit found errors in half the sampled appraisal reviews [SRC-2133].

### 3.2 Program rules that block reach

| Rule | Effect | Burden it adds (design lens) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Section 504 grants only at 62 and over [SRC-1912] | Younger very low-income owners, including disabled owners and families with children, get only loans | An age test is simple; the cost is who it excludes |
| 504 income ≤50% of median; $10,000 lifetime grant; lien on larger loans [SRC-1913] | A roof or furnace often costs more; owners just above 50% are out (ROAD opened loans to low income) | A lifetime cap needs records across decades |
| 502 Direct: ≤80% of median; "unable to obtain credit elsewhere"; 60% loan limit since February 2026 [SRC-1898] [SRC-2169] | The limit makes the program unusable where homes cost more (advocates say in over 20% of rural counties [SRC-2173]) | The credit-elsewhere test and two director reviews add paperwork and delay |
| Seven population limits, 5,000 to 50,000 [SRC-2111] | A town can qualify for a water loan but not a telecom loan; growing towns lose eligibility at each census | Applicants and staff must check a different map for each program |
| "Rural in character" and "serious lack of mortgage credit" [SRC-2111] | Judgment calls applied as density screens | Periodic reviews and appeals |
| Colonias set-asides tied to metro size [SRC-2151] | GAO: nearly 60% of colonias will likely lose eligibility for some targeted set-asides as border metros grow | — |

### 3.3 Money that expires or never flows

- One-year money, pooled each August and lapsing at year-end, rewards states with ready pipelines [SRC-2100].
- Earmarks now take nearly all community facility grants and 44% of water grants [SRC-2112] [SRC-2116], so competitive money for the neediest towns has nearly vanished.
- Obligated is not spent: no New ERA money had been disbursed by February 2026 [SRC-2165]; ReConnect spending lags awards by years [SRC-2119].

### 3.4 Section 515's maturing mortgages

- **The stock.** 12,014 properties and 379,894 apartments housing about 546,000 people, March 2026 (Housing Assistance Council, an advocate that works on 515 preservation) [SRC-2154]; USDA and the Inspector General count 12,150 to about 12,600 properties [SRC-2134] [SRC-2131].
- **The losses.** 621 properties and 14,928 units left June 2021 to March 2026; most left early (prepayment, foreclosure), not at maturity, and "Data on the reason for property exits does not exist" [SRC-2154].
- **What is coming.** GAO: "between 2028 and 2050, over 90 percent" of properties could exit, about 13,000 properties with 407,000 units [SRC-2148]. USDA projected about 80 properties a year maturing through 2028, then about 550 a year to 2040 [SRC-2175], and about 27% of units could exit by 2034 [SRC-2176]. The heaviest years fall inside the plan's window.
- **What changed.** Until 2024, rent help ended when a mortgage was paid off. Appropriations acts from FY2024 let USDA keep paying it ("decoupling"), up to 5,000 units in FY2026 [SRC-2126], and the ROAD to Housing Act made decoupling and the preservation demonstration permanent [SRC-0437]. Uptake is small: eight contracts with 157 units closed and 18 applications with 415 units under review, December 2025; USDA says 547 of 911 units eligible to leave in 2025 were preserved [SRC-2131].
- **Capital.** USDA projects preservation needs "exceeding $30 billion between December 2025 and 2050 ($1.2 billion per year)" [SRC-2131], against $30 million for the preservation program and $50 million for 515 loans in FY2026 [SRC-2126].

## 5. For each dossier

Each item is a starting point for that dossier to weigh; none is decided.

- **Housing (row 4j, memo 13).** RHS is the plan's rural delivery channel for buying (502 Direct and guaranteed, memo 9), repair (504, memo 11), self-help (523), farmworker housing (514/516) and the rural rental stock (515 and 521). It should also cover tribal areas, which this note did not research: RHS runs a Native American single-family relending demonstration that the FY2027 request would not fund [SRC-2131] [SRC-2125]. The memo should weigh: dropping 504's age-62 grant limit and widening income limits; 502 Direct at scale with the 80% loan limit restored; rent help after every 515 mortgage plus a preservation fund before the 2028-2040 peak; and the staff each needs. Sections 1.3, 3.2, 3.4 and 4.2.
- **Transportation.** RD has no transit program. Community facility loans and grants buy vehicles and first-responder equipment [SRC-2131], and business guarantees can finance freight facilities. Rural transit runs through the Federal Transit Administration's rural formula grants, which this note did not research. RD's state offices are a channel for small-town road, bridge and safety projects only if Congress gives them a program.
- **Clean power (flagship 4).** RUS electric loans ($7 billion a year), New ERA and PACE, and REAP are the main rural clean-power channels: rural electric cooperatives already borrow from RUS for most of their lending needs, and the agency has a long record with them. The dossier should weigh a second New ERA-scale round from 2029, REAP grants restored, and the 1990s lesson on lending for big plants. Sections 1.4, 1.5, 4.3, 4.4.
- **Broadband.** ReConnect and telecom loans overlap with BEAD and FCC programs; GAO finds little actual overlap but no national strategy [SRC-2118] [SRC-2120]. After BEAD builds out, RUS's role is upkeep, upgrades and affordability in places BEAD missed. ReConnect needs statutory authority and goals [SRC-2105] [SRC-2153].
- **Water.** RUS water and waste programs lend directly to systems of 10,000 or fewer, where state revolving funds reach small systems less (CRS) [SRC-2103] [SRC-2109]. A clean water or climate-resilience program should use both: SRFs for larger systems, RUS for small ones, with engineers and technical help funded.
- **Care (flagship 2).** Community facility direct loans already finance rural hospitals, clinics, nursing homes and child care centers at near-zero subsidy [SRC-2131] [SRC-2125]; Distance Learning and Telemedicine grants fund rural telehealth. The care dossier can route rural child care and clinic construction through CF, with competitive grants restored.
- **Economic development and fair work.** B&I, REAP, cooperative grants, value-added grants and relending reach rural firms; the evidence is correlation only. Pair any expansion with an evaluation.
- **Democratic renewal (government that delivers).** RD's field network is a test case for the foundation's state-capacity work: staffing levels, systems replacement and published speed measures are cross-cutting asks.
