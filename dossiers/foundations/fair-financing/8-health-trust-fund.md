# Stage G step 10: the health contribution as a trust fund

Role: re-checks the settlement and the fiscal-path memos against Espen's decision that the health contribution pays only for the health system (`DEC-2026-11-25-12`), redrafts the rules that set its rate, and puts the forks to Espen as memos G26-G28 ([`memos/G26-G28-health-fund.md`](memos/G26-G28-health-fund.md)). Status: **analysis, November 25, 2026; its memos are decided (G26 A, G27 A, G28 A, `DEC-2026-11-25-30` to `-32`) and booked in stage G step 11** (`2-settlement.md` part 8), which restated parts 3 and 4 below. Parts 1-2 are step 10's reading. Figures: [`notes/health-trust-fund-results.md`](notes/health-trust-fund-results.md), generated by `models/bookings/health_trust_fund.py --write` on the booked case `mixed`. Nominal $B by federal fiscal year; project arithmetic on exploratory models, not official scores.

**The decision (Espen, verbatim):** "The health contribution may only be used to pay for the health system's expenses. To the extent it results in "paying down the debt" it must be by reducing the amount the health system requires general fund transfers/outlays. Do not treat the health contribution as a general purpose broad based tax whose revenue is fungible across all parts of the federal budget. It is the replacement for the Medicare HI payroll tax and it should behave like it (essentially a trust fund)"

## In plain terms

**The rule, as `DEC-2026-11-25-12` records it:**
- The rate is set against the health system's costs.
- Its revenue never exceeds what health spends.
- A higher rate can help the general fund and the debt only by replacing general-fund transfers to health, never by paying for anything outside health.

Each item below is judged on those three tests.

1. **The account already exists in law: the plan's fund** (HLT-7.22). The contributions, the five health streams and the states' payments go into it, and the general fund covers any shortfall (`DEC-2026-11-15-29`).
2. **From 2035 the contribution pays only for health.** The fund runs short every year, by $538B in FY2040 and $1,524B in FY2056 with long-term care in the plan. So every contribution dollar is spent on health, and a higher rate only replaces part of the general fund's transfer. That holds up to a ceiling where the contribution would cover all of the fund's new cost: **6.20% in FY2040 (the lowest year after FY2035's 5.36%), 6.94% in FY2050 and 7.35% in FY2056** on this account.
3. **Before 2035 it does not.** Over FY2030-34 the fund's sources exceed its new spending by **$816B**, while the contribution collects $1,301B.
   - $425B of the surplus is credited to HI, which is already a trust fund.
   - The other **$391B** reaches the general fund's figures.
   - The booking keeps $250B of that as a reserve and counts the rest against the rest of the plan's spending (G6 C). That pays for things outside health, so it **breaks the rule**.
4. **The 3.80% passes the tests in amount, but was not set against health's costs.** It is 3.25% for the fold-in of the 2 points (G2 C) plus 0.55 points (about $123B in FY2040). Those 0.55 points were "the fallback" that closed the whole budget (`2-settlement.md` part 2). They are allowed, because they replace part of health's transfer, but they need restating as the contribution's share of health's cost (G26).
5. **Rate rises for the debt path are allowed, below the ceiling.** G19 B's "R last", G23 A's no-VAT fallback (4.52%) and G24 A (5.03%), D (4.30%) and the 4.81% comparison (from FY2035, against FY2035's 5.36%) all stay below the ceiling in every year. They only cut health's general-fund draw, so they pass.
   - G23 C's and D's health-contribution mixes (6.31% and 7.43% from FY2040) cross the ceiling on this account. C crosses in FY2040 only; D crosses in every year to FY2056. Neither is recommended.
   - On the fund's whole spending, the baseline's included, no rate in the memos crosses.
   - What the rule adds is a **ceiling in law**, certified by the fund's trustees (G28).
6. **Step-downs on a general-fund or debt surplus pass the tests,** because a lower rate only raises health's general-fund draw. This covers G16 B's second leg, G18 D′ and G20 B's return order. They don't set the rate against health's costs, though, and a rate that rises for the debt and falls on the debt's surplus is a general-fund lever in practice. G27 re-bases them on the fund's own account, at no cost on the central estimate.
7. **Health grows faster than the contribution's base.** Health spending grows at GDP plus a point under D2, and long-term care grows faster. The contribution's AGI base and the other dedicated sources grow about with GDP.
   - If a fund rule fixes the contribution's share of costs, the rate drifts up after FY2040 to about **4.1% by FY2056**.
   - If it fixes the general fund's share, the rate reaches **4.7%**.
   - Holding 3.80% means the general fund pays all of the drift.
   - No formula avoids this choice (G27).
8. **In short, only the rest of G6 C breaks among the decided rules.** Two options nobody recommended also break: G23 C's and D's contribution-only plugs, which cross the ceiling (point 5).
   - Restate: the settlement's basis for 3.80% and its "rate each year would need" line.
   - Re-base on the fund: G16 B's second leg, G18 D′, G20 B's order.
   - Add: a ceiling.
   - Survive: everything else, G13 and G25 included.

## Part 1. The health system's own account, FY2030-56

Built from the booked case's own lines (`health_trust_fund.py`, part 1 of the note).
- **Inflows:**
  - the unified contribution (HCU with S06 and S03), less the HI revenue it replaces (HCW, HCA with S02, HCP, HCH);
  - the employer contribution (HEC, HEX);
  - HX, which `DEC-2026-11-15-20`'s definition of health's need credits to health;
  - the five health streams (HS1-HS4 with the stack cap S04, H1a);
  - HI's early H1a and HLT-6 Part A credit;
  - the states' long-term care payment (S05).
- **Outflows:** HLT-1, -2, -4, -6, -7 (the worker credit included) and -8, plus long-term care in the single plan from 2035 (HLT-9 to -11, `DEC-2026-11-11-02`).
- **Basis:** all figures are increments to CBO's baseline. The plan's law commits the general revenue the baseline already spends on health, and today's HI tax, to the fund (`../health-financing.md` part 2.1). The account doesn't repeat them.

- **Does the contribution ever exceed health's expenses?** Not in any year: it is a fraction of the fund's new spending alone.
- **Do the fund's sources exceed its expenses?** Yes in FY2030-34, by $816B. In those years the five streams run from FY2030, and the contribution steps in with the waves, ahead of the spending.
  - $425B of the surplus is H1a's and HLT-6's credit to HI. That money already sits in a trust fund and folds into the plan's fund in 2035.
  - The other **$391B** reaches the general fund's figures, and the booking spends it elsewhere beyond G6 C's $250B reserve.[^gf]
- **The general-fund transfer the fund needs:**
  - beyond the baseline's: $538B in FY2040 (0.99% of GDP), $1,060B in FY2050 and $1,524B in FY2056 (1.59%);
  - without long-term care: $181B, $477B and $773B.
  - The FY2035-39 transfer, $2.55T in all, carries the transition: the hospital fund, worker help, the worker credit at its peak.
- **The rate at which the contribution would pay all of the fund's new cost:** 6.20% in FY2040 and 7.35% in FY2056. This is the ceiling Espen's second sentence allows.

[^gf]: Step 7 and G6 read health's first call (`health_booking.first_call()`): −$660B over FY2030-34. That figure excludes HI's credit, as this one does. It also counts only health's parts of HLT-4, without the long-term care bridge (about $212B more spending here), and precedes the settlement's S03 and S04 (about $50B less revenue net). On step 7's reading, $410B is counted elsewhere; on the whole fund's, $141B.

**What the account leaves out** (the fable consult's points, checked; none changes a conclusion below):
- **HI's own shortfall.** At the 2035 fold-in, the fund takes on Part A's current-law shortfall, about 0.56% of HI payroll over 75 years (`2-settlement.md` part 6, item 4). The settlement ended the general fund's grant to HI on the reasoning that a grant to the plan's fund is "the general fund paying itself". With a segregated fund that reasoning no longer holds. The shortfall belongs in the general fund's defined share, explicitly. That is about $79-85B in FY2040 (the risk row) and is inside the baseline's committed revenue in this account.
- **HX's split** (G29 A with R2, decided, `DEC-2026-11-26-03`: the OASDI part, 28% of HX and of HEX, sourced, is out of the fund's sources and goes to Social Security's trust funds from FY2031, as current law does). By law, the income-tax part of the tax on former premiums goes to the general fund, and the payroll part to Social Security and HI (`../health-financing.md` part 2.1). Crediting it to health, as `DEC-2026-11-15-20` defined the first call, is a general-fund transfer under another name: net of HEX, about $358B in FY2040. Its Social Security part can't be health's at all. Treated as general-fund money, it raises the transfer by that amount and moves no rate under G27 A.
- **Interest on a reserve** is an intragovernmental credit, netted out of the unified budget: about $10B a year on $250B.
- **The baseline's general revenue** is committed "growing with the plan's costs" (`../health-financing.md` part 2.1). That makes it a cost share already, which G27 A and B extend.

## Part 3. The rules redrafted: the rate set by the fund's own account

**The rule in two parts:**
- **Destination:** every receipt goes into the plan's fund and is spent only on the health system.
- **Determination:** the base rate is set from the fund's own position: its costs, its dedicated sources and a general-fund share fixed in the health law. No general-fund or debt finding enters it.

**The general fund's share** was the fork (G27; decided A, `DEC-2026-11-25-31`). **Restated in stage G step 11, after #493 (CLI-1's step-down) and I1 A**, which lower every version's FY2056 debt by about 1.6 points; figures from `notes/health-trust-fund-results.md` parts 2 and 4. Each version is set so the rate is 3.80% in FY2040 (G26 A):

| | FY2040 | FY2045 | FY2050 | FY2056 | General-fund gap FY2050 / FY2056 | Debt FY2056 (no goal) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A as booked: the fund's rule, quarter-point steps** (band on the contribution's share, tested a year ahead: G27.1a A, `DEC-2026-11-26-01`; the $250B standing reserve: G29 R2, `-03`) | 3.80% | 4.05% | 4.05% | **4.05%** (3.80% to FY2042; 4.30% in FY2046-47 and FY2052-55) | −133 / −189 | **129.3%** |
| A, the smooth share (no steps) | 3.80% | 4.04% | 4.10% | 4.17% | −150 / −240 | 129.6% |
| B. The general fund pays a fixed share | 3.80% | 4.31% | 4.50% | 4.88% | −283 / −547 | 126.8% |
| C. R fixed in law at 3.80%, the general fund the residual (step 7's arithmetic) | 3.80% | 3.80% | 3.80% | 3.80% | −238 / −223 | 131.6% |

All columns are on the fund's new spending (step 10's table mixed in the whole-spending rates, the reviewer's note on #502; on the whole spending A's smooth rate is 4.13% in FY2056 and B's 4.73%). Before #493 and I1 the debt column read 131.2%, 128.9% and 133.1% for A, B and C.

- **A** spreads health's faster growth: the contribution's share holds, and the general fund absorbs the drift in the other sources (the employer contribution, the streams). With G13 kept, a lost wealth tax still lands on R.
- **B** puts all of the drift on R, so the general fund's share is predictable. It also puts D2's overrun partly on the general fund unless G13 overrides.
- **C** holds take-home but leaves R most fungible at the margin, and the general fund pays all of health's growth. The rate moves only by a new law.

**How the rate moves (all three):**
- **No annual reset.** An AGI base falls in a recession, so a yearly formula would raise R in a slump.
- **The fund's trustees** project the fund over ten years each year, as HI's trustees do.
- **The rate steps a quarter point, at most once a year,** when the projected reserve leaves a corridor of 15-25% of a year's outlays (the fable consult's design). Up if below, down if above. **As booked (step 11):** the decision doesn't say whose outlays or which projected year; the booking reads the band on the contribution's own share of the fund's cost and tests one year ahead (G27.1a; Espen chose this reading, A, `DEC-2026-11-26-01`). On the fund's new spending the rate would reach 4.80%, and on its whole spending 5.30%, building reserves of $1.9T and $3.9T by FY2056 (the note's part 4a). On the booked reading the rate holds 3.80% to FY2042, then moves back and forth between 4.05% and 4.30% (up in FY2043 and FY2046, down in FY2048, up in FY2052, down in FY2056). At a fixed rate the contribution falls behind its share as health outgrows its base, a quarter point (about $70-110B a year) refills the reserve, and the band is only about two steps wide.
- **The step-down trigger is the fund's own certified reserve above the corridor, never a general-fund surplus.** It replaces G16 B's second leg and G18 D′.
- **G13's overrun steps stay.** They are the up-side of the same test, sized on the fund alone.
- **The 3.25% floor stays** (G16, G18), as a floor on the contribution's share.

**The early surplus:**
- The fund keeps all of it: HI's $425B as HI's balance at the fold-in, and the $391B that the booking had on the general fund.
- $250B of the $391B is a standing contingency reserve, the bridge G13 already relies on.
- The rest, $141B, pays FY2035's transition and lowers the general fund's transfer then.
- Debt held by the public is unchanged. Gross debt and debt subject to the limit rise by the reserve while it is held (G21's concern).

## Method and sources

- `models/bookings/health_trust_fund.py` (`--write` regenerates the note) reads `models/tools/recovery-budget/model.py`'s `build_case('mixed', …)` with the settlement booked. The rate's yield per point is `budget_scan.PER_POINT_2040` grown on HCU's path, as `settlement_booking.py` uses it. The take-home test is `budget_scan.take_home`. Debt with the rate moved adds the rate change × the per-point yield as an adjustment to the booked case.
- The account's scope follows `DEC-2026-11-15-20` (health's rows and HX) and `DEC-2026-11-11-02` (long-term care in the single plan from 2035). The fund's sources are `../health-financing.md` part 2.1 and the ledger's descriptions (`models/data/inputs/ledger-revenue.csv`: HS1-HS4 and H1a "paid into the plan's fund").
- Consulted: a fable-model subagent on the accounting and the redraft. Its claims were checked against the files. G6's original recommendation, the HX split, the HI grant's reasoning, G13's netting and the worker credit inside HLT-7 are confirmed. Its +0.67 points for the wealth tax ignored the stack cap: it is 0.49.
- Linear yield per point overstates the rate's yield at 4.5% and above, where avoidance rises (S03's range is 10-25%).
