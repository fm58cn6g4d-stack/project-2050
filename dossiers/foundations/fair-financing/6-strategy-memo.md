# Strategy memo: rates, the debt limit, the Fed and the dollar

Role: the strategy memo Espen asked for at the stage G boundary review (`DEC-2026-11-24-17`), part of the fiscal path review (stage G step 8c). It covers interest rates and debt dynamics, the debt limit, the Federal Reserve's independence and the dollar's reserve role, and how a government taking office in January 2029 would explain and manage them while delivering visible improvements early. It is written for Espen and as the base for a later public brief. Status: **draft, November 24, 2026. Analysis, not a decision:** its forks are memos G20 to G22 ([`5-budget-process.md`](5-budget-process.md)). Figures for the plan are step 8a's and 8b's ([`4-fiscal-path.md`](4-fiscal-path.md)); market and legal facts are as of November 24, 2026.

**How to read the evidence here.** Each claim is tagged as data (a measured fact, a law, a court's holding) or analysis (a projection or interpretation) in the register. Analysis by the second Trump administration is presented as that administration's view (`DEC-2026-10-30-01`). The dollar's reserve role is treated as a tail risk with the review's stress of +100 to +200 basis points, not as a story about decline.

## In plain terms

1. **Most of what's left of the deficit is interest.** On the booked plan the FY2040 deficit is 5.1% of GDP, and 4.6 points of that is interest on debt already run up (`4-fiscal-path.md` part 1.2). So the plan's fiscal health turns on interest rates more than on any program.
2. **Rates are higher than the plan's baseline assumed.** CBO's February 2026 baseline had the 10-year Treasury rate at 4.3% by late 2027 (CBO, 2026) [EV-FIN-072]. On November 23, 2026 it was 4.92% (Treasury) [EV-FIN-071]. The Federal Reserve raised its rate in September 2026 [EV-FIN-068], with inflation at 3.6% (Fed minutes) [EV-FIN-070]. If rates stayed there, the plan's central reading would drift toward its stress case. That is a reason to plan for the stress, not a forecast.
3. **The dollar's special role lowers US borrowing costs, and it is slowly eroding, not collapsing.** The dollar's share of world reserves was 56.7% in mid-2026 (IMF) [EV-FIN-075]. A shock that cost the US part of that advantage is the tail the review stresses at +100 to +200 basis points: FY2056 debt would be 157-186% of GDP on the booked path rather than 133%.
4. **The Fed's independence held in court, narrowly.** In June 2026 the Supreme Court refused, 5-4, to let the President's removal of Governor Lisa Cook take effect, and said "the appearance of independence is key" to the Fed's design [EV-FIN-065]. The same day it overruled what was left of *Humphrey's Executor* for other agencies, leaving the Fed as the exception [EV-FIN-064]. A 2029 government's best support for the Fed is a credible fiscal path and a public hands-off rule.
5. **The debt limit should go** (memo G21): repeal it in 2029, under the Senate's decided rule, and never rely on the Fourteenth Amendment or a platinum coin.
6. **How to explain it:** we pay for what we build, and the plan's first term runs a general-fund surplus while families get the child benefit, child care and lower health costs first. Interest is the price of past borrowing, and the debt rule stops it growing. We don't lean on the Fed, and we don't hold the country's credit hostage.

## Part 1. Interest rates and debt dynamics

**The arithmetic.** Debt as a share of GDP stays flat when the primary balance (taxes less non-interest spending) equals about debt × (r − g), where r is the average interest rate on the debt and g is nominal growth (`4-fiscal-path.md` part 1.3). At 117% of GDP, each point by which r exceeds g needs about 1.2% of GDP of primary surplus just to stand still.

**Where the plan's numbers sit.**
- The booked path's own r − g is **+0.44** over FY2036-56, on CBO's February 2026 rates (`4-fiscal-path.md` part 1.1).
- CBO projected net interest more than doubling, from $1.0 trillion in 2026 to $2.1 trillion in 2036, with the average rate on debt rising from 3.4% to 3.9%. Its baseline debt reaches 175% of GDP by 2056 (CBO, 2026) [EV-FIN-072].
- Higher debt raises rates by about 2 to 3 basis points per point of debt to GDP (a CBO working paper, 2019) [EV-FIN-076], or about 3 basis points, mostly through the term premium (a Dallas Fed working paper, 2025) [EV-FIN-077]. Step 8a's dynamic layer uses that range, and its symmetric reading puts the plan's FY2056 debt at about 147% once the feedback is run on both paths (`4-fiscal-path.md` part 2.3).
- Olivier Blanchard argued in 2019 that safe rates below growth are "more the historical norm than the exception", which would make debt cheaper to carry (Peterson Institute, 2019) [EV-FIN-082]. The rise in rates since then qualifies that view. The review's central reading doesn't assume it; its favorable reading (r − g of −0.5) shows what it would be worth.

**What has happened since CBO's baseline.** These are data, and they describe a moment; whether they persist is not known.
- The Fed cut its target range to 3.50-3.75% in December 2025 [EV-FIN-069], then raised it to 3.75-4.00% in September 2026 [EV-FIN-068].
- PCE inflation rose to 3.6% in September 2026 (Fed minutes) [EV-FIN-070].
- The 10-year Treasury yield was 4.92% on November 23, 2026, and the 30-year 5.29% (Treasury) [EV-FIN-071]. That is above CBO's projected 4.3% for late 2027 [EV-FIN-072].
- The Fed's minutes describe the rise in yields as mostly a response to strong economic data, and Treasury market functioning as "orderly" [EV-FIN-070]. So this is not a market in distress. It is a higher-rate world than CBO assumed.

**What it means for the plan** (analysis, the review's own reading).
- The average rate on the debt moves slowly, because most debt was borrowed at older rates and rolls over over years. A 10-year yield about 0.6 points above CBO's path, if it lasted, would carry the plan part of the way toward 8a's +100 basis-point stress over the next decade.
- That stress, from FY2035, takes FY2056 debt on the booked path to about 157%, and raises the share of GDP each debt goal needs from 1.35-1.54% to 2.0-2.2% (`4-fiscal-path.md` part 1.5).
- The 2029 plan should be built so that it doesn't fail if rates stay where they are:
  - the hard rule (no credit for hoped-for growth);
  - the debt-path rule's rate watch, which starts the return procedure early (memo G20);
  - no permanent program financed by a rate assumption.

**Debt management** (a judgment, not sourced here). Treasury chooses how much to borrow short and long. Borrowing longer locks in today's rates and makes the budget less exposed to a later shock, at the price of a term premium now. A 2029 Treasury should review the maturity mix against the rate stress in its first quarterly refunding, and say publicly that it is doing so for resilience, not to steer rates. That is a Treasury decision, and the plan doesn't price it.

## Part 2. The dollar's reserve role, as a tail risk

**The facts.**
- The dollar's share of the world's allocated foreign exchange reserves was 56.70% in the second quarter of 2026, down from 57.18% the quarter before (IMF) [EV-FIN-075].
- At the end of 2024 it was 57.8%. Gold, at record prices, passed the euro as a share of reserves at market value, 20% to 16% (European Central Bank, 2025, on IMF data; the ECB has a stake in the euro's role) [EV-FIN-079].
- In April 2025, after the tariff announcements, the dollar fell while Treasury yields rose, "the opposite of what usually happens in a risk-off environment". The 10-year yield rose almost 50 basis points in a week, the third-largest weekly rise since 1986 (European Central Bank, 2025) [EV-FIN-074]. Markets then stabilized.

**What the advantage is worth** (analysis, contested):
- One model estimates that losing reserve status would raise US real rates by about 90 basis points (Jiang, Krishnamurthy, Lustig and Richmond, National Bureau of Economic Research working paper, 2026) [EV-FIN-078]. That is a model counterfactual, not an observation.
- A Federal Reserve Board staff paper finds that the special convenience of Treasury bonds has already "turned negative" at medium and long maturities, while the dollar itself keeps its advantage [EV-FIN-080]. Part of the premium that used to lower US borrowing costs may already be gone.
- CBO warns that expectations of higher inflation "could erode confidence in the U.S. dollar as the dominant international reserve currency", and that a fiscal crisis would raise rates abruptly, though it predicts neither (CBO, 2026) [EV-FIN-073].

**How the plan treats it.** As the stress, not the forecast. The review's +100 and +200 basis-point shocks from FY2035 (`4-fiscal-path.md` part 1.5) bracket what the reserve-status literature implies, and the plan's answer is mechanical:
- the debt-path rule's rate watch starts a return plan;
- the ladder's deferred steps take effect;
- nothing is cut by formula (memo G20).

The plan doesn't claim to protect the dollar's role by itself. What a government can do is avoid the things that the April 2025 episode and the rating agencies point to: unpredictable policy, threats to the Fed, and fights over paying the country's bills.

## Part 3. The Federal Reserve's independence

**The law, as of November 2026.**
- Fed governors serve 14-year terms and may be removed only "for cause" (12 U.S.C. 242, as the Court restated it) [EV-FIN-065].
- In May 2025 the Supreme Court called the Fed "a uniquely structured, quasi-private entity" in the tradition of the First and Second Banks of the United States (*Trump v. Wilcox*) [EV-FIN-063].
- **_Trump v. Slaughter_** (decided June 29, 2026) overruled what remained of *Humphrey's Executor*, so for-cause protection no longer shields most independent agencies. The Court said its opinion "should not be read" to decide the Fed's case [EV-FIN-064].
- **_Trump v. Cook_** (decided the same day, 5-4) denied the government's application to let Governor Cook's removal take effect. She was the first governor fired in the Fed's 111-year history. The Court said the test of cause is whether it implies "an unfitness for the place", not a wish for a more congenial replacement [EV-FIN-065]. It was an emergency-docket ruling, not a final judgment. Reports since say the President has begun a new removal process; that isn't in the register yet.

**The events.**
- In January 2026 Chair Powell disclosed that the Justice Department had served the Fed with grand jury subpoenas threatening an indictment over his testimony. He called it a consequence of setting rates on the Fed's own assessment rather than the President's preferences. That is his own account, from an interested party [EV-FIN-066].
- Kevin Warsh was confirmed as chair in May 2026 [EV-FIN-067].
- Treasury's own borrowing advisory committee, the dealers and investors who buy the debt, told Treasury in July 2025 that the FOMC's independence "remains of paramount importance" to a well-functioning Treasury market. That view comes from an interested party, published by the second Trump administration's Treasury [EV-FIN-081].

**What a 2029 government should do.** These are recommendations for Espen to note, not forks.
1. **A public hands-off rule from day one:** the President and Treasury don't comment on rate decisions, and the administration doesn't seek a governor's removal except for cause found through notice and a hearing, the standard the Court set out in *Cook* [EV-FIN-065].
2. **Appoint for competence across the spectrum,** and fill vacancies promptly, so the Board's makeup isn't a campaign issue.
3. **Put the Cook standard into statute:** define "cause" as inefficiency, neglect of duty or malfeasance, with notice, a hearing and judicial review. Do the same for the heads of the principal statistical agencies, whose numbers the debt rule reads. The removal of the BLS commissioner in August 2025, which former BLS officials said undermines the credibility of federal statistics, is the reason [EV-FIN-061] [EV-FIN-062]. This belongs with democratic renewal's next touch, which owns the plan's institutional reforms, and costs nothing.
4. **The best protection is fiscal.** A central bank is under most pressure when a government needs low rates to carry its debt. The debt-path rule and a credible path are what make the Fed's independence cheap to respect.

## Part 4. The debt limit

The recommendation is memo G21: repeal the limit in 2029, with a delegated suspension as the fallback, and never rely on the constitutional arguments. In short:
- The limit, $41.1 trillion since the 2025 law [EV-FIN-016], is likely to be reached again in FY2027 [EV-FIN-017]. It governs paying bills Congress already incurred.
- GAO puts the borrowing cost of the 2011-2023 impasses at about $107-161 million, plus harder-to-measure costs, and calls the process "an unnecessary risk of U.S. default" [EV-FIN-025] [EV-FIN-026]. S&P and Fitch both cited the standoffs when they downgraded the US [EV-FIN-028] [EV-FIN-029].
- Under the Senate's decided graduated cloture (legacy D2), repeal can pass by regular order.
- What to seek in 2027-28 (for example a raise sized to last into 2030) stays with board question 0a.

## Part 5. How a 2029 government explains and manages it

**Deliver first, visibly.** The plan's sequence already fits (`-18`):
- **What families see first:** the child benefit, child care and pre-K, and lower health costs.
- **Who pays early:** the top end and corporations.
- **What the budget shows:** a general-fund surplus over FY2030-34 from health's early financing (−$589B over those years; `4-fiscal-path.md` part 4, step 8b, #484).
- So the first term can say, truthfully, that it is paying for what it builds and running the general fund ahead, while families feel the gains.

**Lock in the rules early, while there is a majority.** In the first Congress:
1. the debt-path rule (G20);
2. the revenue ladder with its dates (G19), cancellable by finding;
3. repeal of the debt limit (G21);
4. statutory protection for the Fed's governors and the statistical agencies' heads (part 3 above).

Each is cheap now and hard later. The UK's September 2022 "mini-budget", announced without an independent forecast [EV-FIN-038], was followed by a gilt-market crisis the Bank of England had to stop [EV-FIN-039]. The lesson for the US is simple: every major fiscal law goes to Congress with CBO's score.

**Say what is true about debt.**
- *What to say:* "Interest on past borrowing is now one of the largest lines in the budget. Our plan pays for every new program and holds debt steady as a share of the economy from 2040, under a rule anyone can check. If things go better than expected, part of the gain goes to paying debt down and part comes back to you."
- *What not to say:* that debt will fall below 100% (on CBO's rates it holds at about 117%); that rates will come down; or anything about what the Fed should do.

**If rates spike (the tail):**
- **Don't** cut programs in a panic, and don't lean on the Fed.
- **The rule's rate watch** starts a return plan with a guaranteed vote. The ladder's deferred steps take effect.
- **Treasury** reviews its borrowing mix and speaks once, plainly.
- **The Fed** has tools for market functioning that are its own to use: the Bank of England's 2022 purchases are the precedent [EV-FIN-039].
- **The message:** the plan was built for this case, and it is in law already.

**If a recession comes:** the rule stands aside on a measured test, and the stabilizers run (memo G20). CBO estimates the stabilizers added about 0.3% of potential GDP a year to deficits on average over 1976-2025 (CBO, 2026) [EV-FIN-059]. That is the economy's own shock absorber, and the plan doesn't fight it.
