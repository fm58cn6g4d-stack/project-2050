# Stage G, step 4: the settlement draft

Role: the recommended financing package for the whole plan, the one place where the plan is balanced (`DEC-2026-11-10-05`; `charter/PLAN.md` section 6, stage G; `1-audit.md` part E, step G4). It takes health's share from the top-end and corporate rows first (`DEC-2026-11-15-20`), then gives way on other spending or refinances it (`DEC-2026-11-15-04`), and closes FY2040 and every later year on the general fund. Status: **draft, November 20, 2026; every fork provisional, drafted on the recommendations** (Espen's stage G method). Nothing here is decided or booked: no file under `models/data/inputs/` changes, and G7 books what Espen decides. The forks are memos G1-G9 in [`memos/`](memos/README.md).

Units and sources:
- Federal fiscal years, case `mixed`, nominal $B. A positive gap is a shortfall.
- **The base is #467's corrected model** (branch `claude/stage-g-2a-model-fixes` at `7db1d56`): $615.6B short in FY2040 and $3,998.5B over FY2030-39, with H1a counted once and D3 on the booked package.
- Every package is priced by `models/tools/financing-protection/packages.py` on the budget model's own `build_case`, and on D3 (scenario Q-H7 with each option's edits). The risk table is from `screen.py`. Full output is in [`notes/settlement-results.md`](notes/settlement-results.md), regenerated by the commands in its header.
- Project arithmetic on the project's exploratory models, not official scores.

## In plain terms

1. **The plan is about 1.1% of GDP short each year from FY2040, and the shortfall grows.** Health's share of other flagships' revenue is $532B of the $616B. The rest of the plan accounts for $84B. The gap grows after 2045 because the national health budget lets health grow a point faster than the economy (D2), and long-term care grows faster still.
2. **The top end can't close it.** Health's $532B takes the most durable top-end and corporate rows (part 1). The rest of the top-end stack is already at or past the rates where more tax raises little: 68-73% on top wages, and over 100% on the wealthiest holders' capital income before the cap (memo G4).
3. **The recommended package** (part 2) does six things:
   - fixes four pricing items;
   - caps the wealth tax against the income taxes on the same return (G4);
   - ends a $300 adult credit that was never adopted, and has the states pay their full current share of long-term care, as they already do for health (G1);
   - makes the education fund solvent at 1.7 points (G7);
   - drops the 2 points on income above $20,000/$40,000, so that 88.7% of workers come out ahead on health's take-home test (G2);
   - **closes the rest with a broad value-added tax**: 4.75% from health's January 2035 endpoint, stepping to 5.25%, 5.6% and 6.3% by FY2051 as health's costs grow (G3, G8).
4. **With it, every year from FY2040 to FY2056 closes** (−$11.4B in FY2040). Debt falls to 117.7% of GDP in FY2040 against the baseline's 132.7%, and to 134.0% in FY2056 against 180.2%. Every fifth of households below the top comes out ahead on D3 on average, and in every single year from FY2033 to FY2039. The fourth fifth is about $4,100 a year ahead on the decade average, $2,000 in its thinnest year (FY2033) and $9,650 by FY2039. Social Security is unchanged.
5. **D3's revenue-mix test passes on its decided window, but thins in mature years.** The top 1% and corporations bear 57% of new general-fund revenue over FY2030-39; the rule is more than half. In FY2040 alone it is 50%, and in FY2056 46%, because a VAT falls broadly. Memo G3 asks Espen about it (point G3.2).
6. **The two big risks are D2 and the wealth tax.** Without both, the package is $388B short in FY2040, about 3.7 VAT points. Memo G5 recommends a contingent backstop in law rather than taxing for them now.
7. **The real choice is the VAT.** It is a new kind of federal tax, and it costs lower-income households more as a share of income than the 2 points it partly replaces. The alternatives are folding the 2 points into the health contribution (G2 C), which roughly halves the VAT but leaves a quarter of workers behind on health costs, or cuts on the scale of whole programs (G1 D).

## Method and sources

- Gap and debt: `models/tools/recovery-budget/model.py` `build_case`, case `mixed`, with each option as an explicit, labeled adjustment ("Exploratory: ...") or a `build_case` argument. Revenue options enter as outlay-equivalent adjustments, which moves the general-fund and unified balances correctly; that is not a classification. The education rate and the HI grant are set for one screen and restored (tested: `test_inputs_untouched`).
- D3: `models/tools/household/d3.py` and `provisions.py` on the measured keys, scenario Q-H7, with each option's edits (`packages.Household`).
- Option sizes: the stack note's cap menu (`../notes/stack-2026-11-16.md` part 4); `health-household-contribution.md` sections 2 and 8 (the take-home rates and the cost per quarter point); care memo 17 (option 7); the flagships' own memos for G1 B (a Standard-tier sweep of every booked dossier, checked against the cited lines); CBO's VAT option via `legacy/sources/revenue-channels-and-full-menu.md` (line 112) and `legacy/domains/domain-revenue.md` ("Other choices", REV-CX for the offset convention).
- Health's first call: `models/tools/health_booking.py` `first_call()`.
- Tests: `python3 models/tools/financing-protection/test_packages.py`, after the common rebuild. They check that the screens reproduce the committed gap and D3, that each option has the size its source states, that the recommended package closes FY2040 to FY2056, and that no adopted input changes.
