"Other sources" are the employer contribution (HEC, HEX), HX and the five health streams (HS1-HS4 with S04, H1a, and HI's early H1a and HLT-6 Part A credit). "Health spending" is HLT-1, -2, -4, -6, -7 and -8 (`DEC-2026-11-15-20`'s scope); long-term care is HLT-9 to -11, in the single plan from 2035 (`DEC-2026-11-11-02`), with the states' payment (S05). "Balance" is sources less spending: a minus is the general-fund transfer the fund needs; a plus is health money the booking spends elsewhere.

## 2. The rate the fund needs, by how the general fund's share is defined

Each rule is set so the rate is the booked 3.80% in FY2040; FY2035-39 stay at 3.80% (the transition years, met by the fund's reserve, part 3). A: the contribution pays a fixed 29.1% of the fund's new spending (Medicare Part B's model, premiums at 25% of cost). B: the general fund pays a fixed 13.3% of it. C: the rate fixed in law, the general fund the residual (the booking). Each cell: the general fund's transfer ($B) / the rate.

## 3. The fund's reserve if FY2030-34's surplus stays in it

- Of FY2030-34's surplus, 425.4 is HI's credit of H1a and HLT-6's Part A share, which HI already holds and which folds into the fund in 2035.
- The rest, which reaches the general fund's figures, built over FY2030-34: 391.0. Held as the fund's standing reserve: 250.0 (G6 C's amount). Drawn to lower the general fund's transfer: FY2035 141.0.
- Unified debt is unchanged: the money is the same; only the split between the general fund and the fund moves. The general fund's own FY2030-34 position is 391.0 worse, and its FY2035-39 transfer 141.0 smaller.
- Interest the fund would be credited on its reserve is an intragovernmental payment, netted out of the unified budget; it is not modeled.
