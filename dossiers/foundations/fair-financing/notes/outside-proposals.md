# Outside deficit-reduction proposals, screened against the plan

Role: a screened table of published federal deficit-reduction proposals (CBO, CRFB, Peterson Solutions Initiative, Congressional Progressive Caucus, Simpson-Bowles, Domenici-Rivlin), for the plan's fiscal review. It tests each one against four rules and against what the plan already books.
Status: research note, November 24, 2026; built by a Standard-tier subagent, checked by the step's author (stage G step 8b): "What fits" corrected for three rows already in the plan or in conflict with it.

**Re-scored in step 9 for G19's sources only** ([`revision-results.md`](revision-results.md) part 1; [`../7-batch3-revision.md`](../7-batch3-revision.md) part 4.1): itemized-deduction limits (about $95B in FY2040, from 123-312), the larger carbon fee (about $15B net of its dividend, from 111-147) and the AGI surtax (about $190B). **Every other row below is still CBO's December 2024 figure on pre-2025 law, unscored on current law,** and enters no recommendation.

## What this answers and how to read it

- **The need.** The plan starts in 2029. Its debt goals need about 1.35-1.54% of GDP of extra deficit reduction from FY2036, about $730-830B in FY2040, on top of the settlement (`../4-fiscal-path.md`, "In plain terms" item 3; `debt-gap-results.md`). The settlement as booked already closes the general-fund gap in FY2040 (−$6.0B; `../2-settlement.md` part 2).
- **The four rules.**
  - (a) No VAT or national consumption tax.
  - (b) No new taxes on the middle class in the first years (about FY2029-2034). Broad revenue is allowed later.
  - (c) Social Security and Medicare benefits and financing are out of scope: no benefit, retirement-age, payroll-cap or Medicare eligibility changes.
  - (d) Options the plan already includes are marked "already in plan".
- **Screen results used.**
  - *fits*: passes (a)-(c), not in the plan, no other bar found.
  - *fits (to confirm)*: passes, but an overlap or who-bears question is open. Counted separately in "What fits".
  - *fails a / b / c*: the rule it breaks.
  - *already in plan*: the plan books it or a larger version (the booked row is named).
  - *later only*: a broad levy that reaches the middle class, so it could start no earlier than about FY2035. Not additive with each other (same households).
  - *fits rules; plan scan set aside*: passes (a)-(c), but `../3-budget-scan.md` already weighed the area and declined it for a reason that is not one of the four rules.
- **Scaling to FY2040 (CBO rows).** CBO's table gives each option's 2034 effect (the last year of its window). I multiplied that year by the growth from FY2034 to FY2040 in the plan's adopted baseline (`models/data/fiscal-baseline.csv`): nominal GDP ×1.249 (43,373 to 54,167) for taxes and mandatory spending; ×1.17 for defense and nondefense discretionary (baseline outlays FY2034 to FY2040: defense 1,051 to 1,232, nondefense 1,096 to 1,282). A range is the low and high alternatives. This is rough: CBO priced the options on its June 2024 baseline, before the 2025 reconciliation law, so every tax row is **to confirm** against the current law. Timing options (advertising amortization, LIFO) do not scale this way.
- **Scaling to FY2040 (non-CBO rows).** CRFB's 10-year totals: total ÷ 10 × 1.40 (FY2040 GDP over a FY2031 mid-window GDP of about 38,800 on the same baseline), window to confirm. The Progressive Caucus's revenue target is a fixed 4.4% of GDP a year, so its FY2040 level is 4.4% × 54,167, split by CRFB's shares. Peterson plan rows and the two older plans are not scaled.
- **Savings are deficit reduction, positive = reduces the deficit,** before interest savings, as CBO reports them. Options overlap and don't sum.

## What's set aside and why

- **Fails (a), the VAT or a consumption tax:** CBO 72 (362-549), Peterson's PPI plan (a VAT), AAF's destination-based cash-flow tax (consumption-type; to confirm against rule a), Domenici-Rivlin's debt reduction sales tax.
- **Fails (b):** CBO 55, tax on VA disability payments (44). It targets one group and can't be deferred.
- **Fails (c), Social Security and Medicare:**
  - Social Security: CBO 62, 20, 21, 27 and the Social Security part of Simpson-Bowles, Domenici-Rivlin and every Peterson plan.
  - Medicare: CBO 7, 8, 15; CRFB's site-neutral and MA items; the Progressive Caucus's drug-negotiation lines. The plan has already booked MA overpayments (HLT-6) and replaces Medicare in 2035 (`../3-budget-scan.md` part B).
  - CRFB's indexing freeze for mandatory programs.
- **Already in plan:** CBO 65 (G7, $50.1B), 64 (G1, $201.4B at 7 points), 47 (C18), 51 (G6), 56 (HX), 28 and 31-35 (G10, −$101.4B), 1 (S09, −$5.0B); CBO 4 and 6 (Medicaid, folded into the single health plan and S05); the Progressive Caucus's corporate and top-earner lines; CPC's war-funding line (no baseline to cut); CRFB program integrity (G8) and caps (G10). The plan's pricing is sometimes below CBO's scaled figure: CBO 28 scales to about 196 against the plan's −101.4 (and 123.2 for its C3 variant), so a deeper defense cut is a choice the plan declined (G10).
- **Fits the rules but the plan has already weighed and declined:** CBO 23 (VA means test), 42 (NDD cuts), 52 (private activity bonds), and federal pay and pension options. Reason: protected earned benefits or booked designs (`../3-budget-scan.md` part B).
- **Whole plans from Peterson and the older commissions cannot be adopted as packages.** Every one rests on changes to Social Security or Medicare. Their useful content is the lever list in each row.

**Simpson-Bowles (December 2010).** The National Commission on Fiscal Responsibility and Reform proposed about $4.1T of deficit reduction over FY2012-20 including about $0.7T of interest: discretionary caps ($1.66T, the largest piece), tax reform that eliminated income tax expenditures to lower rates (about $0.8T of the revenue, with a 15-cent gas tax and chained CPI adding about $0.2T), health savings, and Social Security changes that included chained CPI and a taxable maximum covering 90% of wages. Its final vote did not reach the 14 of 18 needed (the vote count is from memory, not read: to confirm). Source: the report as reproduced by SSA, [The Moment of Truth](https://www.ssa.gov/history/reports/ObamaFiscal/TheMomentofTruth12_1_2010.pdf), and [TPC's summary](https://taxpolicycenter.org/briefing-book/moment-truth-report-national-commission-fiscal-responsibility-and-reform-december-2010). For this plan, the discretionary-cap and base-broadening ideas are echoed (G10, C39), the Social Security pieces are out of scope, and its approach of zero-basing tax expenditures to cut rates is the opposite of the plan's choice to keep rates higher on the top.

**Domenici-Rivlin (November 2010).** The Bipartisan Policy Center's Debt Reduction Task Force proposed about $6T of savings by 2020 (CNN; to confirm), aiming at debt of 60% of GDP and a balanced primary budget by 2020. It cut income tax rates to two (15% and 27%) and the corporate rate to 27%, broadened the base, and added a 6.5% debt reduction sales tax that TPC says is "structured similarly to a broad-based value-added tax". It froze domestic discretionary spending for four years and defense for five (saving $1.0T and $1.1T), changed Medicare and Medicaid, and raised the Social Security tax base to 90% of wages. It fails rule (a) on the sales tax and rule (c) on Social Security and Medicare. Sources: [TPC summary](https://taxpolicycenter.org/briefing-book/debt-reduction-task-force-restoring-americas-future-bipartisan-policy-center-november), [Rivlin at Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/solving-the-fiscal-crisis-comparing-the-plans-to-reduce-the-budget-deficit) and [CNN](https://money.cnn.com/2010/11/17/news/economy/debt_commission_rivlin_domenici/).
