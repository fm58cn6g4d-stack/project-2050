# Memo G6: Health's early surplus

Role: decision memo for Espen, fork 6 of stage G's batch ([`README.md`](README.md); [`../2-settlement.md`](../2-settlement.md) part 3; `../1-audit.md` part C, item 2). Status: **provisional, drafted on the recommendation, November 20, 2026.** Not decided, not booked. Nominal $B. Health's first call is from `models/tools/health_booking.py` (`first_call()`) on #467's corrected model; the package figures are from `models/tools/financing-protection/packages.py`.

## The question

The new health streams start in FY2030 (`DEC-2026-11-16-02`), but most of health's spending starts at the January 2035 endpoint. So over **FY2030-34 health's own revenue runs about $660B ahead of its booked need.** The audit had $883B; #467's correction, which moved H1a's early years to Medicare's hospital fund, lowered it. From FY2035 the position reverses: health's first call is +$2,783B over FY2035-39.

You decided that health's contributions are paid into the plan's fund, with the general fund covering any shortfall (`DEC-2026-11-15-29`). The budget model books all of this on the general fund, so the early surplus currently makes the rest of the plan's 2030s look cheaper.

The question: does the early surplus stay in the plan's fund as a reserve, or is it counted against the rest of the plan's spending in the 2030s?

**What it does not change:** FY2040 and later years, so the fiscal rule's test is unaffected. Debt is also unaffected either way: a reserve in the plan's fund is held in Treasury securities, so the federal government's total borrowing is the same.

## The options

| | What | The decade's general-fund figure (recommended package) | What it protects |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A (recommended)** | **Hold it as the plan's fund reserve,** about $660B plus interest by the endpoint | **+$1,111B short** (the rest of the plan's own position) | Health's first years after the endpoint, and a D2 shortfall (memo G5) |
| B | Count it against the rest of the plan, as the model books it | +$451B short | Nothing specific |
| C | Hold a reserve of one year of the plan's administrative and transition risk (about $250B); count the rest | about +$701B short | Part of both |

**A, a reserve (recommended).**
- It matches what you decided: the contributions are the plan's, paid into its fund.
- It gives the plan's fund about two to three years of cover if D2's national health budget misses its path (about $250B a year in FY2040). Memo G5's backstop needs that time to phase in.
- *The cost:* the general-fund figure for FY2030-39 reads as about $1,111B short instead of $451B. The rule tests FY2040 onward, so nothing fails. The debt guardrail is read on the unified budget, where debt is the same.
- *Burden:* none. The fund's trustees report the reserve each year.

**B, count it against the rest of the plan.**
- The decade looks $660B better.
- But the money is spent on other flagships in the 2030s, and the plan's fund starts the endpoint with nothing behind it.

**C, split.** A middle reserve, sized to the first year's risk. It leaves less cover for D2.

## Recommendation

**A.** Hold the early surplus in the plan's fund as a reserve, and show the decade's general-fund figure both ways in every report.

**What would change this:** if the debt path in the 2030s mattered more than health's first years, then B. But debt is the same under both, so B mostly changes how the decade reads.
