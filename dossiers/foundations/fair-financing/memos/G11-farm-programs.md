# Memo G11: Farm subsidies

Role: decision memo for Espen, fork 11 of stage G, from step 4b's whole-budget scan ([`../3-budget-scan.md`](../3-budget-scan.md) part C, items C5 and C6). Status: **provisional, drafted on the recommendation, November 20, 2026. A new fork.** Not decided, not booked. Nominal $B on case `mixed`, priced by `models/tools/financing-protection/budget_scan.py`. Project arithmetic on exploratory models, not official scores.

## The question

Farm subsidies are about $34B a year: crop insurance (about $15-16B) and the commodity programs (about $15-17B) (CBO's February 2026 account file).
- **The 2025 law raised them.** CBO scored its farm title at **+$53.060B for the commodity programs and +$5.980B for crop insurance** over FY2025-34 (CBO, through the Congressional Research Service, 2025) [EV-FIN-002].
- **The project's legacy menu also carries a crop insurance reform, never decided:** about $0.05T over the decade (the project's revenue file) [EV-FIN-014].

Each item is decided on its own, yes or no.

## The options

| | What | FY2040 | FY2056 | FY2030-39 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **G11.1 (recommended: yes)** | Reverse the 2025 law's commodity and crop-insurance increases in the 2029 farm bill: reference prices and the crop-insurance changes back to the 2018 farm bill's levels | −6.5 (range 3-10) | −8.9 | about −55 |
| **G11.2 (recommended: yes)** | Crop insurance reform: premium subsidies cut toward 40% for the costliest policies; the insurers' expense reimbursement and guaranteed return capped | −5.0 (range 3-10) | −6.9 | about −44 |
| Together | | **−11.5** | **−15.8** | **−98.7** |

**G11.1, the 2025 increases reversed.**
- *Who loses:* farm operations, mostly the larger ones, which grow most of the covered crops. Their payments go back to roughly 2024's levels.
- *Why the range is wide:* commodity payments depend on crop prices; when prices are high, the 2025 law's higher reference prices pay little.
- *What it doesn't touch:* the plan's own conditioned farm payments (CLI-13A, about $0.03T over the decade) stay.

**G11.2, crop insurance reform.**
- *Who loses:* crop insurance companies, through their reimbursements, and the farms with the largest policies. A smaller farm keeps most of its subsidy. CRFB puts the premium-share piece at about $35B over FY2026-35 (via a search extract), at the top of the range.
- *Delivery:* the Risk Management Agency's existing contracts with the insurers. No new system is needed.
- *Overlap with G11.1:* the 2025 law's crop-insurance increase was mostly higher premium subsidies, which this also cuts. Taken together, the two may overlap by up to about $0.6B a year, inside the ranges above.

**Ad hoc farm aid** (the $12B of "bridge" payments in December 2025 and the emergency aid before it) isn't in CBO's baseline, so not repeating it saves nothing on paper. The plan shouldn't repeat it.

**The sugar program** costs consumers $2.5-3.5B a year in higher prices, by the outside studies GAO cites, with almost no budget cost (GAO, 2023) [EV-FIN-011]. Reforming it would lower grocery prices but close no gap, so it is noted here and not priced.

## Recommendation

**Yes to both.** About $11.5B a year by FY2040: small against the gap, but it comes from the 2025 law's additions and from insurers' margins, not from families' budgets.

**What would change this:** the politics.
- Farm states weigh heavily in the Senate races the scenario dossier tracks for 2028 and 2030.
- If the 2029 farm bill needs farm-state votes, G11.2 alone is the smaller ask: insurers rather than farmers bear most of it.
