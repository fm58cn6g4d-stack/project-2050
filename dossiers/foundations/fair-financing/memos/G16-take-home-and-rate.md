# Memo G16: The take-home goal and the health contribution's rate

Role: decision memo for Espen, stage G step 8b, the fiscal path review ([`../4-fiscal-path.md`](../4-fiscal-path.md); `DEC-2026-11-24-14` to `-18`). Status: **provisional, drafted November 24, 2026. Not decided; nothing booked.** Central r − g (CBO's rates), the conventional score; nominal $B or % of GDP. Project arithmetic on exploratory models, not official scores. Decided on its own, not as a package.

## The question

You carried the take-home test into this review as a goal (`DEC-2026-11-24-12`). At the booked base rate R of 3.80%, 69.0% of workers come out ahead, against the 85-90% target (`DEC-2026-11-15-02`). Lowering R raises take-home, but it costs money the debt goal (G15) also needs (part 3). How should R move?

| R | Workers ahead (FY2040) | Money needed elsewhere in FY2040 (no goal) | On G15 B |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3.80% (booked) | 69.0% | 0 | $461B |
| 3.25% | 76.2% | $123B | $584B |
| 2.95% | 80% | $190B | $651B |
| 2.57% | 85% | $276B | $737B |

## The options

| | What | Take-home | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | Hold R at 3.80% | 69.0% | none |
| **B (recommended)** | **Hold 3.80% to FY2040; then lower R by a trigger, a quarter point at a time toward 3.25%**, only as certified savings appear beyond what the debt goal needs: health spending below D2's path (WAD-H1), or a general-fund surplus above the goal's path (G18 D′) | 69.0%, rising toward 76.2% if the savings come | none up front; each quarter point returned is about $56B in FY2040 terms, paid only from certified money |
| C | Lower R to 3.25% from 2035 by law | 76.2% | $123B a year in FY2040, $236B in FY2056, all of it to be found |
| D | Reach the 85% target: R at about 2.57% | 85% | $276B a year in FY2040, $528B in FY2056; with G15 B, about $737B a year of other money |

**A** is safe but leaves 31% of workers behind on health costs. Most of them are the group just above the exemption: 81.5% of those at 250-400% of poverty come out ahead.

**B** ties take-home to results, as G8 D does. If health reform works as designed (a quarter point a year below D2's cap is about $77B in FY2040), two steps can be paid for by FY2042-44.

**C and D** buy take-home now with money the plan doesn't have. Together with any debt goal, they need broad revenue on the same middle class the take-home test protects (G19). That defeats the purpose.

## Recommendation

**B.** Hold 3.80% through FY2040. Then step R down a quarter point at a time, toward 3.25% (76.2% of workers ahead), only when the trustees certify health spending below D2's path or a surplus beyond the debt goal's path. The 85% target is reached only if health reform delivers well beyond its design (about half a point a year below D2's cap).

**What would change this:** if take-home matters more to you than the debt goal, then C with G15 D.

## Question for you

**G16.** Hold the health contribution's base rate at 3.80% to FY2040, then lower it toward 3.25% by a trigger on certified savings (B, recommended)? Or hold it (A), lower it to 3.25% by law (C), or aim for the 85% target now (D)?
