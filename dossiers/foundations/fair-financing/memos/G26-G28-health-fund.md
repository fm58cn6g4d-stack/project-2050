# Memos G26-G28: The health contribution as a trust fund

Role: decision memos for Espen, stage G step 10 ([`../8-health-trust-fund.md`](../8-health-trust-fund.md); figures in [`../notes/health-trust-fund-results.md`](../notes/health-trust-fund-results.md)). They follow from your decision that the health contribution pays only for the health system and behaves like the HI tax it replaces (`DEC-2026-11-25-12`). Status: **decided November 25, 2026** (G26 A, G27 A with all three points, G28 A: `DEC-2026-11-25-30` to `-32`) **and booked in stage G step 11** (`../2-settlement.md` part 8). The figures below are as drafted, before the booking; the booked ones are in `../notes/health-trust-fund-results.md`. **G27.1a, decided (A, `DEC-2026-11-26-01`):** the decision set the band at "15-25% of a year's spending" without naming whose spending or which projected year is tested; Espen chose the contribution's own share of the fund's cost, tested one year ahead, the reading booked in step 11. **I2 and G29, decided** ([`G29-hx-social-security-part.md`](G29-hx-social-security-part.md); `DEC-2026-11-26-02`, `-03`): HX, the tax on former premiums, includes Social Security's payroll tax, which can't be health money. Sourced, it is 28% of HX and of HEX alike, about $100B a year in FY2040, not the step-11 estimate of $290B. It is out of the fund's sources, on the general fund through FY2044, and Social Security's from FY2045 (G29 C). The fund keeps a $250B standing reserve and draws the rest of its FY2030-34 surplus, $62.7B, against the FY2035 transfer (G29 R2). **As booked, the rate** is 3.80% through FY2042, 4.05% in FY2043-45, 4.30% in FY2046-47, 4.05% in FY2048-51, 4.30% in FY2052-55 and 4.05% in FY2056. It moves back and forth because at a fixed rate the contribution falls behind its share as health outgrows its base, a quarter point (about $70-110B a year) refills the reserve, and the band is only about two steps wide. The general fund's gap from FY2040 doesn't depend on it: the law fixes the general fund's share. Nominal $B; project arithmetic on exploratory models, not official scores. Each memo is decided on its own, not as a package.

## What your rule changes, in short

- **The money already goes to health after 2035.** From FY2035 the health fund runs short every year ($538B in FY2040, $1,524B in FY2056), so every dollar of the contribution is spent on health.
- **Before 2035 it does not.** Health's sources run $391B ahead of its new spending over FY2030-34 on the general fund's side, and the plan spends most of that elsewhere.
- **The rate passes your tests, but it was chosen the wrong way.** 3.80% was the lowest rate that closed the *whole* budget. Several memos also move it when the general fund or the debt path does better or worse.
  - Your rule allows a higher rate whenever it only replaces part of the general fund's transfer to health. That holds up to about 6.2% in FY2040 and 7.35% in FY2056, where the contribution would pay all of health's new cost.
  - What the rule needs is a rate set against health's costs, and a ceiling.
- **Health grows faster than the economy, so someone pays the difference.** D2 lets health grow a point faster than GDP, and long-term care grows faster still. The contribution's base grows about with GDP. Either the rate drifts up after 2040, or the general fund pays more each year. These memos ask which.

## G26. What the 3.80% stands for

The booked rate is 3.25% to fold in the 2 points (G2 C) plus 0.55 points ($123B in FY2040) that closed the whole budget. Under your rule the 0.55 points need a health reason: they are the share of health's cost the contribution pays.

| | The rate from 2035 | What it means | Cost to the general fund, FY2040 | Workers ahead (take-home test, FY2040) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A (recommended)** | **3.80%, re-founded as the fund's rate**: the contribution pays about 29% of the fund's new cost (about 15% of its whole cost, counting the contribution alone) | Every booked number stays. The general fund still pays $538B into the fund in FY2040 | none | 69.0% |
| B | 3.25%, the fold alone | The general fund pays $123B more into health in FY2040 | +$123B a year (more later) | 76.2% |
| C | Higher, for example 4.52%: the contribution takes a larger share of health's cost now, so the general fund pays less | Allowed: the fund still needs a transfer, and the rise only replaces part of it. It is G23 A's no-VAT fallback, taken now | −$161B | 56.8% |

**Recommendation: A.** It keeps the settlement's numbers and gives the rate a health basis. B is better for take-home, but its $123B a year would fall on the VAT you chose in G24 B. That is about 2.5 more points at G24's $48B a point, and somewhat less with the rebate narrowed. C buys debt reduction now at a take-home cost of 12 points. G28 keeps it available later, as a fallback.

**G26.** Re-found the 3.80% rate as the share of the health fund's cost the contribution pays (A, recommended)? Or 3.25% with the general fund paying more (B), or a higher share now (C)?

## G27. How the rate moves after 2040

This is the trust-fund rule itself: who pays for health growing faster than the economy.

| | Rule | Rate FY2045 / FY2050 / FY2056 (on the fund's whole cost) | General fund vs the booking, FY2050 / FY2056 (on its new cost, at slightly higher rates) | Debt FY2056 (no goal; same) | Workers ahead at the later rate (indication only, FY2040's incomes) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A (recommended)** | **The contribution pays a fixed share of the fund's cost**, as Medicare's premiums pay 25% of Part B; the general fund pays the rest | 4.01% / 4.09% / 4.13% | $101B / $158B better | 131.2% | about 64% |
| B | The general fund pays a fixed share; the contribution carries the rest | 4.15% / 4.38% / 4.68% | $205B / $422B better | 128.9% | about 57% |
| C | The rate fixed at 3.80% in law; the general fund pays whatever the fund lacks (today's booking) | 3.80% throughout | no change | 133.1% | 69% |

**For each:**
- **A** shares health's growth: the rate rises about a third of a point by FY2056. *Against:* take-home slips after 2040.
- **B** makes the general fund's bill predictable. *Against:* the rate rises almost a point, and take-home falls further.
- **C** protects take-home. *Against:* the general fund's transfer nearly triples, and the rate moves only when Congress changes it. That makes the contribution least like a trust fund: each extra dollar of it simply lowers the general fund's bill.

**Recommendation: A**, with three points, each yes or no:
1. **How the rate moves. Recommended: yes.**
   - The fund's trustees project the fund ten years ahead each year, as HI's trustees do.
   - The rate steps a quarter point, at most once a year, only when the fund's projected reserve leaves a band of 15-25% of a year's spending: up if below, down if above.
   - No yearly reset, which would raise the rate in a recession.
   - Never below 3.25%.
   - The fund's own trigger reads only the fund, such as health spending below D2's cap. It replaces G16 B's second leg and G18 D′, which read the general fund.
   - Those two passed your tests, since a lower rate only raises health's draw. But together with rises for the debt path, they would make the rate a general-fund lever.
   - A debt-path change to the rate goes through G28 instead: a change to the general fund's share, by law.
   - G13's backstop stays as the upward side. Note that the wealth tax's loss is about 0.49 points on the fund alone, not 0.46 and then 0.01, because the defense surplus no longer counts. The wealth tax must also be paid to the fund by statute.
2. **The early surplus stays in the fund. Recommended: yes.**
   - All of FY2030-34's surplus stays: HI keeps its $425B, and the fund keeps the $391B the booking had on the general fund.
   - $250B of the $391B is a standing reserve (G6 C's amount, now the fund's). The rest, $141B, lowers the general fund's FY2035 transfer.
   - Debt held by the public doesn't change. The general fund's own FY2030-34 figure is $391B worse. This replaces the rest of G6 C.
3. **The general fund's share is written in the health law. Recommended: yes.**
   - It covers the general revenue already spent on health (committed "growing with the plan's costs").
   - It covers HI's inherited shortfall explicitly.
   - It covers health's first call on the top-end and corporate rows (`DEC-2026-11-15-20`), which your rule allows.

**G27.** Set the rate from the health fund's own account, with the contribution paying a fixed share of the fund's cost (A, recommended), with its three points? Or fix the general fund's share instead (B), or hold the rate in law with the general fund paying the rest (C)?

## G28. The health contribution and the debt plan

Under your rule the contribution can still help the debt, but only by replacing part of the general fund's transfer to health. Three of batch 4's paths use it that way, and all three stay below the ceiling, so they pass:
- G23 A's no-VAT fallback: 4.52% from FY2040;
- G24 A: 5.03%;
- G24 D: 4.30% with a small VAT.

Two paths that aren't recommended cross the ceiling on this account, so they don't pass: G23 C with the contribution as its plug (6.31% in FY2040) and G23 D (7.43%).

| | What | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A (recommended)** | **The contribution stays on the debt plan's menu, but only as a change to the general fund's share in the health law, under a ceiling.** The fund's trustees certify each year that the contribution doesn't exceed the fund's cost (6.20% in FY2040, 7.35% in FY2056 on today's figures). No debt-path trigger moves the rate directly, and G20 B's return order drops its health-contribution step | None against batch 4: G23 A's fallback and G24's options stand |
| B | **Off the menu entirely.** The rate moves only by the fund's own rule (G27), never for the debt | Without a VAT, another broad tax: on goal A $161B in FY2040 and $243B in FY2050, an AGI surtax of about 3.7 points instead of 2; on goal B $276B and $415B, about 5 points |

**Recommendation: A.** It is what your rule says: the contribution may pay down debt by taking over part of health's general-fund bill, never by paying for anything else. The ceiling and the share in law keep that true. B is stricter than your rule and costs a larger surtax if no VAT passes.

With G24 B decided, the debt plan uses the VAT, not the contribution. So G28 matters only as the fallback if the VAT isn't enacted in 2033-34. Under A, the fallback is G23 A's route on goal B: the contribution at about 5.0% (G24 A's 5.03%), below the ceiling. Under B, it is an AGI surtax of about 5 points.

**G28.** Keep the health contribution available to the debt plan only as a smaller general-fund share of health's cost, by law and under a certified ceiling (A, recommended)? Or take it off the debt plan's menu entirely (B)?
