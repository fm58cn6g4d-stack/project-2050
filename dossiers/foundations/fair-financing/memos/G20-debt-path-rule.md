# Memo G20: The debt-path rule

Role: decision memo for Espen, stage G step 8c, the fiscal path review ([`../5-budget-process.md`](../5-budget-process.md) parts 1-4; `DEC-2026-11-24-14` to `-18`). Status: **provisional, drafted November 24, 2026. Not decided; nothing booked; nothing new priced.** Figures for the plan are step 8a's and 8b's ([`../4-fiscal-path.md`](../4-fiscal-path.md); memos G15-G19, #484, in review). Project arithmetic on exploratory models, not official scores. Decided on its own, not as a package.

## The question

Memo G15 B would hold debt flat at about 117% of GDP from FY2040 and step it toward 100% "as results allow". Memo G19 B would enact later broad revenue in the first Congress, with steps that can be cancelled if they aren't needed. G16 B and G18 D′ would lower the health contribution only on a surplus beyond the debt goal's path. All four depend on one rule: what counts as "results", who decides, what happens when debt runs high, and what happens in a recession. What form should that rule take?

**Your constraints:** a populist start with no early middle-class revenue (`DEC-2026-11-24-18`), no VAT, Social Security and Medicare out of scope, and the review's hard rule (a goal is met on the conventional score; dynamic and "works as designed" savings count only once they are measured).

**What the record shows** (part 2 of the review):
- The one US rule that cut spending by formula against deficit targets, Gramm-Rudman-Hollings (1985), was missed every year it ran, on budget scholar Allen Schick's account (Brookings testimony, 1997) [EV-FIN-053]. Its deficit was measured in a single August snapshot, so spending could be moved outside it and one-time asset sales could meet a target (Congressional Research Service, 2011) [EV-FIN-052]. The 1990 Budget Enforcement Act, which controlled new laws instead of the deficit, did better on the same account [EV-FIN-053] [EV-FIN-083].
- The statutory PAYGO sequester (2010) has never been ordered; Congress has excluded or wiped about $12 trillion from its five-year scorecard (CRS, 2026) [EV-FIN-055].
- The IMF's staff find deficit caps "potentially procyclical", find that rules without good escape clauses are "put in abeyance" after shocks, and find that estimating the cycle "opens avenues to circumventing the rule" (IMF Staff Discussion Note 18/04, 2018) [EV-FIN-045] [EV-FIN-046]. Sweden's government says an annual target "would need to be pro-cyclical" (Government Offices of Sweden, 2026) [EV-FIN-034].
- Scheduled tax rises are often delayed: the medical device tax was suspended twice and repealed (IRS) [EV-FIN-058].

## The options

| | Form | When debt beats the path | When it runs above | Recession |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | **Report and explain**, as Sweden does: a path in law, a published gap each year, and the President's budget explains any miss | nothing automatic | nothing automatic | explained |
| **B (recommended)** | **A measured debt path with a cancellable revenue ladder** | half the lead lowers the path for good; the other half can defer a tax step or lower the health contribution | deferred steps take effect; the President sends a plan to return within five years, with a guaranteed vote; **no automatic cuts** | the rule stands aside on a measured unemployment test; restarts from where debt is |
| C | **B plus automatic tax rises when behind:** if no plan passes within a year, the health contribution rises a quarter point a year | as B | as B, then automatic rises | stands aside |
| D | **A Swiss-style spending ceiling:** spending capped at receipts adjusted for the cycle | room rises | spending is cut to the ceiling | adjusted by an estimate |

**How B works** (the review's part 3):
- **The measure** is debt held by the public as a share of GDP at the end of each fiscal year: Treasury's and the Commerce Department's measured figures, not a forecast.
- **The path** from FY2040 is the FY2039 level, about 117%, held flat. It only ever moves down, toward 100%.
- **The ladder** is G19 B's revenue steps, enacted in the first Congress with their dates, on by default, so they count in the plan's score and a later Congress must vote to stop one.
- **The test** runs each October. A step not yet started is deferred a year if measured debt has run at least 1 point below the path for two years *and* a five-year projection on CBO's assumptions shows the path still met without it.
- **The escape clause:** a recession, on Claudia Sahm's measured trigger (a rise of 0.5 points in the three-month average unemployment rate above its low in the previous year; Hamilton Project, 2019) [EV-FIN-060], or an emergency declared by joint resolution. While it applies, no step starts and the automatic stabilizers run in full.
- **The rate watch:** if the average rate on Treasury debt runs 0.75 points above the path's assumption for a year, the return procedure starts early. It never raises a tax or cuts a program by itself.
- **The certifiers:** the trustees already named in G13 and G8, on published data, with CBO's reading published the same week. If the two disagree, nothing is given back. Executive officers must certify a trigger that executes the law (*Bowsher v. Synar*, 1986) [EV-FIN-054].
- **Investment** counts like any other borrowing. There is no carve-out (memo G22, point 3).

**What each does on the review's numbers** (central reading: CBO's rates, r − g about +0.44):
- **B:** the ladder's $430-460B in FY2040 is all needed to hold debt flat. Nothing is deferred, and debt stays at about 117%. If rates run half a point below growth, holding flat costs nothing; half the ladder then goes to the path, which is enough to get debt under 100% by FY2055 (that goal needs 0.36% of GDP on that reading), and the other half comes back to households. If rates run 1.5 points above growth, the ladder is 1.3-1.6 points of GDP short, and the return plan starts.
- **A:** the same money, but G19's steps would need a vote to cancel and G15's step toward 100% has no mechanism.
- **C:** as B, but if Congress doesn't act, the health contribution rises by formula. It is also G13's backstop instrument (if D2 misses), so take-home could fall well below 69% with both running.
- **D:** cuts by formula, which you asked the review to avoid, and it rests on an estimated cycle.

**For each:**
- **A** is simple and leaves Congress free. *Against:* Sweden's version works because its politics honors it; ours has 17 overrides of scheduled Medicare cuts behind it (CRS, 2015) [EV-FIN-057].
- **B** keeps the ladder's money real without cutting by formula, and gives a lawful off switch, so nobody needs a waiver. *Against:* it can't stop a later Congress from repealing the ladder, and on CBO's rates it moves nothing toward 100%.
- **C** has more force. *Against:* it raises middle-class taxes by formula outside a recession, which defeats the populist start.
- **D** is the strongest on paper. *Against:* it cuts by formula, and the IMF warns that cyclical estimates invite circumvention [EV-FIN-046].

**Not offered:** a Gramm-Rudman-style sequester.

## Three points inside B, each yes or no

1. **The split.** When results beat the path, half lowers the path and half may be returned. **Recommended: yes.** All of it to the path reaches 100% faster and returns nothing; all of it returned leaves debt at about 117% for good. Half and half honors both your "ideally below 100%" and the take-home goal (`DEC-2026-11-24-12`).
2. **The order of what is returned:** first defer a tax step not yet started, then step the health contribution down a quarter point (never below 3.25%), then suspend a step already in force. **Recommended: yes.** A tax not raised keeps the populist start. If take-home matters more to you, the health contribution can go first.
3. **After a recession, restart from where debt stands** rather than claw the jump back. **Recommended: yes.** The alternative is a glide back that recovers half the jump over ten years, as Switzerland repays emergency spending over six (Swiss Federal Finance Administration) [EV-FIN-032]. It is more disciplined, but it taxes in the recovery.

## Recommendation

**B, with points 1 to 3 as recommended.** It is the rule that G15 B and G19 B already assume. It has no automatic cuts for a hostile Congress to use, and it gives money back only once results are measured.

**Be clear about what it buys.** On CBO's rates it holds debt at about 117%. Getting below that depends on results beating CBO's assumptions. The review sends the lead a note so that G15 B and G19 B say this too.

**What would change this:**
- If you want the rule to bite harder, then **C**, at a cost to take-home.
- If you would rather not enact the ladder early, then **A**, with G19 voted on later.
- If you accept automatic spending limits, then **D**.

## Question for you

**G20.** Adopt a debt-path rule that measures debt against a path from FY2040, enacts G19's revenue steps early with each one cancellable by a measured finding, makes no automatic cuts, and stands aside in a recession (B, recommended), with its three points (the split, the order, the restart)? Or report and explain (A), add automatic tax rises when behind (C), or a spending ceiling (D)?
