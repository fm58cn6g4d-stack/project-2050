# Memo G23: The debt goal, made more aggressive

Role: decision memo for Espen, stage G step 9, batch 4 ([`../7-batch3-revision.md`](../7-batch3-revision.md) part 3). It redrafts memo G15 B on Espen's direction (`DEC-2026-11-25-01`). Status: **provisional, drafted November 25, 2026. Not decided; nothing booked.** Central r − g (CBO's rates), the conventional score, nominal $B or % of GDP. Project arithmetic on exploratory models, not official scores ([`../notes/revision-results.md`](../notes/revision-results.md) part 2). Decided on its own, not as a package.

## The question

You chose G15 B, debt held flat at about 117% of GDP from FY2040, and asked for it to be "a little more aggressive on debt", with the aim "to try to keep rates below growth on average" (`DEC-2026-11-25-01`). You also allowed a VAT or other broad measures after the plan's first few years. How far should debt fall?

**The base has moved a little.** The clean-energy credits' cost after FY2040 was overstated by about $80B a year (memo [G25](G25-cli1-after-the-fix.md)). Corrected, with G17 B's hold, debt on the booked plan is 117.7% of GDP in FY2040 and 131.4% in FY2056. G19 B's first steps in FY2037-39 then lower the level "held flat" from 116.9% to 116.1%.

## The options

Each is met on the conventional score, with no "works as designed" or dynamic savings (D4 as amended, `DEC-2026-11-25-11`). Debt rises in none of them after FY2040.

**On rates against growth.** CBO's working paper finds each point of debt adds about 2-3 basis points to interest rates in the long run [EV-FIN-076]. On the conventional score that feedback isn't counted, so rates run about half a point above growth in every option. The last column shows what lower debt does to that gap on CBO's estimate. It's the risk each goal buys down, not a saving.

**If rates or growth surprise:**
- *Rates half a point below growth:* B costs nothing, C 0.31% of GDP.
- *Rates 1.5 points above growth:* B needs 2.38% of GDP, C 2.79%.
- *A shock of +100 basis points from FY2035:* if the plan doesn't respond, debt in FY2056 is 139% under A, 132% under B and 121% under C. The debt-path rule's rate watch starts the return plan early (G20 B).

**For each:**
- **A.** *For:* the cheapest. *Against:* it isn't more aggressive, and rates stay above growth.
- **B.** *For:* a little more aggressive, as you asked: about $85B more than A in FY2040 and $2.4T more interest saved by FY2056. It brings rates to about growth, and it can be paid for without raising the health contribution and with a VAT under 5% (memo [G24](G24-later-revenue-with-a-vat.md)). Under G20 B, half of any good luck goes to the path, so if rates or health costs beat CBO's assumptions, B moves toward C on its own. *Against:* it stops at 110%, short of "ideally below 100%".
- **C.** *For:* 100% by FY2056, and rates just below growth on CBO's estimate. *Against:* about $250B a year more than B from FY2040, most of it a larger VAT (about 9.4% with its rebate, against 4.75%). About 41% of workers come out ahead on the take-home test, against 51% under B.
- **D.** *For:* the fastest. *Against:* its money starts in FY2033, inside the first years you want kept clear.

## Recommendation

**B: debt falling to 110% of GDP by FY2056.** The measured path is about a quarter point a year from FY2039's peak (113.8%). It is the debt-path rule's path (G20 B), measured on real debt each October. Half of any lead lowers the path for good, so B is a floor that results can push toward C.

**What would change this:**
- If getting rates below growth matters more than about $250B a year more from FY2040, then **C**.
- If the VAT can't be enacted, then **A**: G24's mix 1 then pays for it with the health contribution at about 4.5%.

## Question for you

**G23.** Commit the plan to debt falling to 110% of GDP by FY2056, on a measured path from FY2040 (B, recommended)? Or held flat at about 116% (A), falling to 100% by FY2056 (C), or under 100% by FY2050 (D)?
