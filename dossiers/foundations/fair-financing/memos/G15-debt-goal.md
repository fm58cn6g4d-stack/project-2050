# Memo G15: The debt goal

Role: decision memo for Espen, stage G step 8b, the fiscal path review ([`../4-fiscal-path.md`](../4-fiscal-path.md); `DEC-2026-11-24-14` to `-18`). Status: **provisional, drafted November 24, 2026. Not decided; nothing booked.** Central r − g (CBO's rates), the conventional score; nominal $B or % of GDP. Project arithmetic on exploratory models, not official scores. Decided on its own, not as a package.

## The question

You asked for debt "ideally below 100%", faster than the booked path (`DEC-2026-11-24-15`). The booked settlement pays for the plan's own programs, but current law's deficits remain. Debt held by the public is 117.8% of GDP in FY2040 and 133.1% in FY2056, rising about a point a year. Most of what is left is interest on debt already run up (part 1.2). Which debt goal should the plan commit to?

## The options

Each is met on the conventional score with no "works as designed" or dynamic savings (the hard rule). "Other money" is what must come from cuts, phasing and revenue other than the health contribution, with R held at 3.80% (part 3).

| | Goal | Extra deficit reduction | FY2040 | FY2056 | Debt FY2056 | If rates run 1 point higher (r − g +1.5) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | **Under 100% of GDP by the mid-2050s** (FY2055) | 1.35% of GDP from FY2036, phased in from FY2032 | $732B | $1,293B | 99.6% | 2.46% of GDP |
| **B (recommended)** | **Debt held flat as a share of GDP from FY2040**, at about 117%, then stepped toward 100% by a debt-path rule as results allow | 0.85% of GDP in FY2040, rising to 1.03% by FY2056 (the primary balance that holds the ratio) | $461B | $982B | 116.9% | about 2.1-2.4% |
| C | **Under 100% by FY2050** | 1.48% of GDP from FY2036 | $803B | $1,418B | 96.3% | 2.59% |
| D | **No new goal:** keep the booked path and the FY2056 surplus (G8 C) | none | 0 | 0 | 133.1% | debt 169% |

"Debt falling from the mid-2030s" is priced in part 1. Smoothly it costs 1.54% of GDP from FY2036, more than A or C, because it has to cover the FY2036-39 hump (part 4). It isn't offered on its own: B is its affordable form.

**A, under 100% by the mid-2050s.**
- *For:* it reaches your "ideally below 100%". Interest saved over FY2030-56 is about $11.5T.
- *Against:* $486B already in FY2035, the plan's first term after the endpoint. With no VAT, no middle-class revenue early (`-18`) and Social Security and Medicare out of scope, that money in the mid-2030s can only come from cutting the plan's own programs or from broad revenue years early. Starting later (FY2036-40) raises it to 1.66% of GDP. On the dynamic layer's symmetric reading it needs more: 1.52% central, 2.47% pessimistic.

**B, debt held flat from FY2040 (recommended).**
- *For:* it is the largest goal that fits your constraints. Nothing is asked before FY2040, so the populist start holds. It stops the ratio's climb at about 117%, against current law's 180% in FY2056. At r − g of −0.5 it costs nothing.
- *Against:* it doesn't reach 100% on its own. It still needs about $430B a year of new money from FY2040, after the outside fix and phasing (part 6), and most of that can only be broad revenue (G19). A rate shock of 100-200 basis points would double the cost.
- *The step toward 100%:* a debt-path rule (8c's budget-process part) would lower the target ratio each cycle when measured results allow: rates below the plan's assumption, receipts above the score, or certified health savings below D2 (WAD-H1). The plan would then move toward A without committing the money up front.

**C, under 100% by FY2050.** The fastest. It costs only about 0.1 points of GDP more than A, but the money has to start in the early 2030s, which collides with the populist start.

**D, no new goal.**
- *For:* nothing more to find, and take-home can rise instead (G16).
- *Against:* debt keeps rising about a point a year. At r − g +1.5 it reaches 169% by FY2056. The plan's long-term sustainability then rests on interest rates staying low.

## Recommendation

**B: commit to holding debt flat as a share of GDP from FY2040, met on the conventional score, with a debt-path rule (8c) that steps the target toward 100% as measured results allow.** It is the goal the plan can promise without a VAT, without early middle-class taxes and without touching Social Security or Medicare. It still delivers most of the difference between the plan and current law.

**What would change this:**
- If you would accept broad revenue from the mid-2030s, or deeper phasing of the plan's own programs, then **A**.
- If reaching 100% sooner matters more than the populist start, then **C**.
- If you would rather spend the room on take-home (G16), then **D**.
