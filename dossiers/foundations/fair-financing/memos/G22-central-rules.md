# Memo G22: The central rules (D3, D4, D4-B)

Role: decision memo for Espen, stage G step 8c, the fiscal path review ([`../5-budget-process.md`](../5-budget-process.md) part 6; `DEC-2026-11-24-14`, direction 3). Status: **decided November 25, 2026: yes to all four points** (`DEC-2026-11-25-08` to `-11`; G22.2 in chat after a reminder of the guardrail). D4's rewrite and D3's new test years are written as proposal text in `../5-budget-process.md` part 6; `legacy/governing/` stays read-only, so the rules' home is the decision log and that text. Four points, each a yes or no, and each decided on its own. Changing a central rule is a tier-3 decision; nothing here changes `legacy/governing/` or `charter/` until you decide it.

## The question

You allowed the review to examine the project's central rules and to propose changes where they serve "long-term sustainable progressivism" (`DEC-2026-11-24-14`). The rules as you decided them (legacy D3 and D4):
- **D3:** every income group below the top comes out ahead on net, and new general-fund revenue comes mostly from the top 1% and corporations.
- **D4-B, the fiscal arc:**
  - permanent programs paid for at maturity (FY2040 and every later year);
  - time-limited investments borrowed inside an envelope of up to 0.5% of GDP to 2034, tapering to zero by 2040;
  - guardrails against CBO's current-law path, with no test on the plan's own debt;
  - no dynamic revenue counted.

On the booked settlement, every fifth below the top comes out ahead (the fourth fifth by about $5,525 a household a year over FY2030-39), and the revenue-mix test reads 65% (`../2-settlement.md` part 3).

The review found four places where the rules and the new debt goal don't fit. Each point below changes one of them. Not proposed: loosening "paid for at maturity" or replacing D3's principle. Those two are what make the plan's progressivism last. An unpaid permanent program is the easiest for a successor to cut, and a plan whose middle class falls behind loses the voters it needs.

## The points

**G22.1. Test D3 in the mature years too. Recommended: yes.**
- *Why:* D3 is tested on FY2030-39. G19 B's later broad revenue starts in FY2037 and is mostly felt from FY2040, so the decade's test never sees it. That is about 0.8% of GDP from the late 2030s (`../4-fiscal-path.md` part 6).
- *What changes:* the same quintile test also runs in FY2040 and FY2050, with everything counted, including G19's steps at their default. If any fifth below the top falls behind, the steps' design changes before they are enacted: the carbon dividend goes first, and the surtax's threshold rises.
- *The revenue-mix half* (mostly from the top) stays a test of the decade only. Broad revenue can't pass it by design, and the decade is where the populist start lives. The memo says so rather than hiding it.
- *Not done here:* the review prices nothing new, so it can't yet say whether the fourth fifth stays ahead in FY2040 with the ladder in. The next booking runs that test.
- *If no:* the later middle-class revenue is checked against nothing.

**G22.2. Replace D4's current-law guardrail with the debt-path rule. Recommended: yes, if you choose G15 B and G20 B.**
- *Why:* "no test on the plan's own debt" was your reading when the plan paid only for itself. A debt goal is a test on the plan's own debt.
- *The old guardrail is now meaningless:* the booked plan is 47 points of GDP below current law's 180% in FY2056 (`../4-fiscal-path.md` part 1).
- *If no:* keep both, and report the old guardrail beside the new rule.

**G22.3. No standing carve-out for investment after FY2039; room earned instead. Recommended: yes.**
- *What stays:* D4-B's envelope stays as decided through FY2039.
- *After that:* a new investment envelope only by a new law, only for investments with a written return case, and only while measured debt is at or below the path, inside it.
- *Why:* a permanent "investment" exemption is the easiest part of a rule to game. The IMF's staff find that rules invite creative accounting and off-budget operations (IMF, 2018) [EV-FIN-044]. Germany's 2025 reform put a €500 billion fund outside its brake and excluded defense above 1% of GDP (European Commission, 2025) [EV-FIN-049]. The Bundesbank warns that "the broader the definition of investment, the more likely" its protection "would prove ineffective" (Deutsche Bundesbank, 2025) [EV-FIN-050].
- *If no:* a standing envelope, for example 0.25% of GDP, outside the path. It is more room for public investment, but it is a line the bond market won't net out.

**G22.4. Write the review's hard rule into D4. Recommended: yes.**
- *What changes:* D4's "no dynamic revenue is counted" becomes "no dynamic or 'works as designed' saving counts toward a test up front; measured results count through the debt rule's findings and the triggers that read them" (G16, G18, G20).
- *Why:* that is how the review already works (`../4-fiscal-path.md` part 2.3). Writing it in keeps a later review from counting hoped-for growth to meet a goal.
- *If no:* the hard rule stays a convention of this review.

## Recommendation

**Yes to all four** (G22.2 only with G15 B and G20 B). Together they keep D3's promise in the years when the plan asks more of the middle class, make the debt goal the plan's real fiscal test, and close the two easiest routes for a successor to game it: an investment label, or a growth forecast.
