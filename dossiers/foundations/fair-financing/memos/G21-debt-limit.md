# Memo G21: The debt limit

Role: decision memo for Espen, stage G step 8c, the fiscal path review ([`../5-budget-process.md`](../5-budget-process.md) part 5; `DEC-2026-11-24-16`, budget process). Status: **provisional, drafted November 24, 2026. Not decided; nothing booked.** A recommendation for 2029. What to seek in 2027-28 stays with board question 0a. Decided on its own, not as a package. It is kept apart from the debt-path rule (memo G20) on purpose.

## The question

The debt limit caps how much Treasury may borrow to pay for laws Congress has already passed. It doesn't limit new spending or tax cuts. It only decides whether the bills already run up get paid.
- The 2025 reconciliation law raised it by $5.0 trillion, to $41.1 trillion (Congressional Research Service, 2026) [EV-FIN-016].
- CRS reads recent projections as reaching it again sometime in FY2027 [EV-FIN-017].
- Congress has changed it 104 times since the Second World War [EV-FIN-018].

**What standoffs have cost:**
- GAO puts the immediate borrowing costs of the 2011-2023 impasses at roughly $107-161 million, with harder-to-measure long-term costs on top. It says the process "creates an unnecessary risk of U.S. default" (Government Accountability Office, 2026) [EV-FIN-025] [EV-FIN-026].
- Around the 2011 fight the S&P 500 fell about 17% and consumer confidence 22%. That is the Obama administration Treasury's account, written during the 2013 fight, so it is an interested party (Treasury, 2013) [EV-FIN-027].
- S&P cut the US rating in 2011, citing "political brinksmanship" (S&P's release, 2011) [EV-FIN-028]. Fitch cut it in 2023, citing "repeated debt-limit political standoffs" (Fitch Ratings, 2023) [EV-FIN-029].

Under the Senate rule you decided (graduated cloture from the first week of 2029, legacy D2), a change can pass by regular order after a fixed delay. Reconciliation isn't needed. What should the plan do with the limit?

## The options

| | What | For | Against |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A (recommended)** | **Repeal it.** Treasury borrows what enacted law requires. The debt-path rule (G20) becomes the anchor | Ends default threats for good. GAO recommends replacing the process with one that links debt decisions to spending and revenue decisions [EV-FIN-026] | "Democrats abolished the debt limit" is an easy attack, and a later Congress could restore it |
| B | **Tie it to the budget resolution** (a "Gephardt rule" in statute): adopting the budget deems the matching limit passed | Keeps a recorded decision on borrowing, when the budget is set | The House's version triggered in only 17 of its 33 years, because it was suspended or no budget passed (CRS, 2023) [EV-FIN-021]. It was repealed in 2023 [EV-FIN-020]. The Senate has no equivalent |
| C | **A delegated suspension**, the 2011 model: the President certifies the need; the limit rises unless Congress disapproves, and the President can veto that | Keeps a vote. In 2011 the Senate rejected disapproval 45-52 (CRS) [EV-FIN-022] | Still a vote the opposition can use for messaging, and a hostile Congress and President together could block it |
| D | **The constitutional arguments:** the Fourteenth Amendment's public debt clause, or a platinum coin | Available without Congress | The Supreme Court has construed the clause once, and how it applies to the limit is unclear (CRS, 2021) [EV-FIN-023]. Previous administrations and many lawyers have rejected the argument [EV-FIN-019]. In 2013 the Treasury and the Federal Reserve rejected the coin [EV-FIN-024]. Debt issued under a contested authority could shake the very market the plan relies on |

**Denmark** keeps a ceiling but set it, with all major parties' support, far above its debt so it never enters day-to-day politics. From 1993 to 2010 debt never came within Dkr 140 billion of it (Peterson Institute, 2011; dated) [EV-FIN-030]. That is a fallback wording for A if repeal itself becomes the attack line: keep a number in the law, set far above any projected debt.

## Recommendation

**A: repeal the limit in 2029, with C as the fallback** if the votes for repeal aren't there. **Never rely on D.** Say so publicly before 2029, so markets never price the chance of a contested default.
- The plan's own discipline is the debt-path rule and D4, which decide what is borrowed. The limit decides only whether the bills already run up get paid.
- If a dollar-amount raise is needed before repeal passes, the first reconciliation bill can carry one. Reconciliation can raise the limit by an amount; that is how the 2025 law did it [EV-FIN-016].

**What would change this:**
- If you want a recorded borrowing vote kept, then **C**.
- If you want it tied to the budget, then **B**, knowing how often the House's version was suspended.

## Question for you

**G21.** Repeal the statutory debt limit in 2029 and let the debt-path rule anchor borrowing, with a delegated suspension as the fallback and no reliance on the constitutional arguments (A, recommended)? Or tie it to the budget resolution (B), adopt the delegated suspension itself (C), or keep the constitutional arguments as the plan (D)?
