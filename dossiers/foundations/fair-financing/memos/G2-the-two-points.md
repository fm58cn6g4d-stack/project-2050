# Memo G2: The 2 points on income above $20,000/$40,000

Role: decision memo for Espen, fork 2 of stage G's batch ([`README.md`](README.md); [`../2-settlement.md`](../2-settlement.md) part 2; `../1-audit.md` part C, item 7). Status: **provisional, drafted on the recommendation, November 20, 2026.** Not decided, not booked. Nominal $B on case `mixed`, priced by `models/tools/financing-protection/packages.py`. Take-home figures are from `../../health-household-contribution.md` sections 2 and 8, with #467's intervals. Project arithmetic on exploratory models, not official scores.

## The question

The plan books an income contribution of 2 points on AGI above $20,000 single and $40,000 joint. It starts in 2035 and reaches the full 2 points in 2044. It raises **$216.3B in FY2040 and $636.8B in FY2056.**

When you set the health contribution at 2.25% (`DEC-2026-11-15-11`), the 88.7% of workers coming out ahead on the take-home test held "only if stage G drops or folds in the booked 2 points". With them, **70.1%** come out ahead, against your target of 85-90%. The 2 points fall on the same pay as the health contribution, but without its exemption below 250% of poverty.

## The options

| | What | FY2040 gap | FY2056 gap | Workers ahead (take-home) | The VAT then needed (memo G3) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A (recommended)** | Drop the 2 points | **+216.3** | +636.8 | **88.7%** | 4.75% from the 2035 endpoint, stepping to 6.3% by FY2051 |
| B | Keep them as booked | 0 | 0 | 70.1% | about 2.0 points lower in FY2040, 3.4 lower in FY2056 |
| C | Fold them into the health contribution: drop them and raise its base rate from 2.25% to 3.25% from 2035 | −7.7 | +207.7 | 75.6% | 2.75%, stepping to 4.0% |
| D | Drop them and raise the base rate to 2.5% | +160.3 | +529.5 | 85.9% | about 0.5 points lower than A |

**A, drop the 2 points (recommended).**
- *Take-home:* 88.7% of workers ahead, the middle of your 85-90% range. 100% of workers below 200% of poverty are ahead, and 98.6% of retirees and others.
- *Money:* $216B a year in FY2040, rising to $637B, has to come from elsewhere. In this package that is about 2 points of the VAT.
- *The honest trade-off:* the take-home test counts health costs only. A VAT raises prices, which the test doesn't count. In FY2040, two VAT points cost a household in the bottom fifth about $860 a year, against about $30 for the 2 points it replaces. In the fourth fifth they cost about $2,280, against $1,640 (rising to $2,730 at the full rate). So dropping the 2 points and paying with a VAT moves some of the burden down the income scale, even though the health test improves. D3 still has every fifth below the top ahead on average (memo G3).

**B, keep them.**
- *Take-home:* 70.1%. About 30% of workers would pay more in visible health costs than they do today.
- *Money:* no change. The plan needs about 2 fewer VAT points.

**C, fold them into the health contribution.**
- *Take-home:* 75.6%. The health contribution's exemption protects low earners that the 2 points don't, so more workers come out ahead than in B for about the same money in FY2040.
- *Money:* about the same as B in FY2040. Later it raises less, because the 2 points grow to their full rate in 2044 and this does not.
- *Visibility:* "a 3.25% health contribution" in place of 2.25%.
- *D3:* the best of the four for the bottom three fifths (Q1 +$14,267 against +$13,896 under A). It also raises the revenue-mix test's share (60% over FY2030-39 and 54% in FY2040, against A's 57% and 50%; memo G3).

**D, drop them and set the base rate at 2.5%.**
- *Take-home:* 85.9%, just inside your range. You chose 2.25% partly for the margin: at 2.5%, "one uncertain assumption" could put the result under 85% (`health-household-contribution.md`).
- *Money:* it recovers about $56B of the $216B in FY2040.
- *It reopens* `DEC-2026-11-15-11`, so it is listed for completeness.

## Recommendation

**A.** You set 85-90% of workers ahead as the test for health's financing, and dropping the 2 points is the only option that keeps it with a margin.

**What would change this:** if you weigh the cash that low-income households pay overall above the health test, then **C**. It puts fewer dollars on the bottom fifth than a VAT does, at the cost of a quarter of workers paying more in health costs than today.

## Question for you

**G2.** Drop the booked 2 points on AGI above $20,000/$40,000 (A, recommended), keep them (B), fold them into the health contribution at 3.25% (C), or drop them and set the contribution at 2.5% (D)?
