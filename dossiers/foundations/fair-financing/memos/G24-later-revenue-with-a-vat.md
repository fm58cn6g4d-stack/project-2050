## The question

You chose G19 B: from FY2037, limits on itemized deductions and a larger carbon fee, an AGI surtax from FY2040, the health contribution last. Then you allowed a VAT after the first few years. Should a VAT take part of the load, and from when?

**First, G19 B raises less than step 8b said.** Re-scored on the 2025 law, its steps raise about $328B in FY2040 ($235-413B), not $430-460B:
- Deduction limits are worth about $95B, not $123-312B. The 2017 law's larger standard deduction is now permanent, so fewer people itemize.
- A larger carbon fee raises about $15B for the deficit once its dividend keeps the bottom two fifths whole, not $111-147B. Step 8b had used CBO's economy-wide option, most of which the plan already prices.
- The AGI surtax (2 points above $100K/$200K) holds up, at about $190B.

So even holding debt flat needs about $130-170B more in FY2040, and goal B about $230-280B more.

**Second, where that remainder can come from.** The top end is used. The health contribution (R) is spoken for: G16 B holds it at 3.80% to FY2040 and then steps it down on certified savings, and G13 raises it if health spending misses D2's cap. That leaves a VAT.

## The VAT's design

- **A broad VAT,** on CBO's broad base less the care the plan pays for: about $90B a point in FY2040, net of the income and payroll offset.
- **A rebate for every adult of $150 a point** (2040 dollars, indexed): the VAT on the first $15,000 of each adult's spending. It is paid with the child benefit, Social Security and SSI, or as a refundable credit, with no application and no income test. At 4.75% it is about $710 an adult in FY2040. It costs about $42B a point, so each point nets about $48B.
- **Retirees on Social Security and SSI** are also protected if prices rise with the VAT, because their benefits rise with prices.

**In every option, every fifth below the top stays ahead in FY2040 and FY2050** (D3, as amended by G22.1). The mature-year reading is approximate: the household model runs to FY2039.

**Timing:** each two years the VAT waits adds about 0.4 points to its rate: 4.37% from FY2033, 4.75% from FY2035, 5.21% from FY2037.

**For each:**
- **A.** *For:* the best on the take-home test, and for low-income workers, because the health contribution exempts income up to 138% of poverty. No VAT. *Against:* it reverses G16 B, which holds R at 3.80% to FY2040 and then lowers it, and it stacks on G13's backstop, which also raises R. Workers would face R above 5%.
- **B.** *For:* it keeps G16 B intact, so R can still step down as health savings are certified. It spreads the load over consumption, including retirees' and the wealthy's, which the take-home test doesn't see. The rate stays under 5%. *Against:* about 2 points fewer workers ahead on the take-home test than A, and 6 points fewer low-income workers. A VAT is new national machinery (states' sales taxes run beside it).
- **C.** *For:* one instrument. *Against:* a VAT near 10%, and the take-home test falls to 42%.
- **D.** *For:* a smaller VAT (3%). *Against:* it also raises R from FY2040 against G16 B, for little gain on take-home.

## Two points, each yes or no

1. **The rebate: $150 a point for every adult. Recommended: yes.** A smaller rebate ($75) would net more from each point and lower the rate needed, but low-income workers would come out further behind. A larger one costs more than half the VAT.
2. **Enact the VAT in the 2033-34 Congress, to start in FY2035. Recommended: yes.** The ladder's other steps are enacted in the first Congress, as G20 B provides. A VAT enacted in 2029 would be on the ballot in 2030 and 2032, which your reason for waiting ("toxic until dems prove they can deliver consistently") argues against. *If no:* enact it in 2029 with its FY2035 start, cancellable by the rule like the other steps. It is more certain to happen, and it is in the score from the start.

## Recommendation

**B, with points 1 and 2 as recommended.** G19 B's deduction limits and AGI surtax stay as decided. The larger carbon fee drops out as a revenue step: it raises little once its dividend is paid, and stays a climate question. A VAT of about 4.75% with a $150-a-point rebate starts in FY2035 and pays for the rest, enacted in the second term. The health contribution stays on G16 B's path. If you choose G23 C instead, the same mix needs a VAT of about 9.4%.

**What would change this:**
- If keeping the take-home test highest matters more than G16 B's step-down, then **A**.
- If no VAT should be enacted at all, then **A** with G23 A.
