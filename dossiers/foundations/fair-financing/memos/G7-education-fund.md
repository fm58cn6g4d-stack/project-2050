# Memo G7: The education fund's rate

Role: decision memo for Espen, fork 7 of stage G's batch ([`README.md`](README.md); [`../2-settlement.md`](../2-settlement.md) part 3; `../1-audit.md` part C, item 28). Status: **provisional, drafted on the recommendation, November 20, 2026.** Not decided, not booked. Nominal $B on case `mixed`, priced by `models/tools/financing-protection/packages.py`. Project arithmetic on exploratory models, not official scores.

## The question

The education fund (EDU-9B) pays for the plan's schools and early-learning commitments: pre-K and the child care share, the teacher pay floor, the paraprofessional program and the Title I and IDEA top-ups. It is financed by a payroll contribution on all pay, with no cap, phased in over 2031-35, plus a general-fund share of 30% of its outlays.

At the booked **1.4 points the fund runs out of money in 2035**, its first full year. It is $254B in the red in FY2040 and $1.5T by FY2056. The general-fund gap doesn't show this, because the fund's shortfall sits outside the general fund. But a fund in the red can't legally pay its bills, so the booked package doesn't actually work as written.

The question: how to make the fund solvent.

## The options

| | What | General-fund gap, FY2040 | FY2056 | FY2030-39 | Fund | Households (D3, Q4, $ a year) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A (recommended)** | Rate to **1.7 points**, general-fund share unchanged at 30% | **+7.5** | +14.0 | −46.4 | Solvent to 2104; reserve $106B in FY2040 | **−289** |
| B | Rate to 1.65 points | +6.3 | +11.7 | −38.7 | Solvent, with a thin reserve ($46B in FY2040, about five weeks of outlays) | about −240 |
| C | Keep 1.4 points; the general fund tops the fund up to a one-to-two-year reserve (the "reserve corridor" case) | +45.0 | +40.8 | +599.4 | Solvent | 0 |
| D | Keep 1.4 points as booked | 0 | 0 | 0 | **Out of money in 2035** | 0 |

**Why A raises the general-fund gap a little:** the contribution is deductible, so a higher rate lowers income-tax receipts by 12.5% of the extra contribution. Over the decade A lowers the gap, because before 2035 the general fund fills whatever the contribution doesn't cover.

**A, 1.7 points (recommended).**
- *Who pays:* workers, through a further 0.3 points of pay (about $150 a year on $50,000). On D3, about −$290 a year for the fourth fifth on the decade average, and about −$650 for the top fifth.
- *What it buys:* the fund pays its bills every year. Its reserve grows over the decades, so the fund's own statute (the reserve corridor's rule) can lower the rate later, once the reserve passes two years of outlays.
- *Burden:* none added. It is the same contribution at a different rate, withheld as now.

**B, 1.65 points.** It is solvent on the central estimate, but with about five weeks of outlays in reserve in FY2040. One weak year of pay growth would put it back in the red.

**C, the general fund tops it up.**
- It keeps workers' rate at 1.4 points.
- It adds $45B a year to the general-fund gap from FY2040, and $599B over the decade while the reserve is built. That is about 0.5 more points of memo G3's VAT.
- It moves education's cost onto the general fund, which the settlement is trying to close.

**D, as booked.** It is not a real option: the fund can't pay benefits it has no money for. It is listed so the cost of doing nothing is visible.

## Recommendation

**A, 1.7 points.** It is the cheapest way to make the fund solvent with a real cushion, and it keeps education's financing dedicated and visible.

**What would change this:** if Espen wants no further charge on workers' pay, then C. It costs about $45B a year more on the general fund.
