# A new consensus on immigration: delivery

Role: section 5 of the immigration dossier. It covers who runs each provision of `4-specification.md`, the capacity each agency needs, the timeline, remedies, states' roles and rural delivery. Status: reviewed (L2; `review-milestone.md`), with the review's fix steps applied; L3 after the re-check. On `DEC-2026-11-12-07` and `-12`. Costs of the capacity named here are in `6-cost.md`. Shared rule 5 (who verifies and pays) is owned by democratic renewal; this section states immigration's needs for it.

## 2. Capacity

**USCIS.**
- *The workload* (working assumption, memo 1): about 8-10 million filings over the decade, most in the first three years after filing opens, with 6-8 million green cards [EV-IMM-603] [EV-IMM-609].
- *The base it starts from:* 12.8 million pending cases in mid-2026 [EV-IMM-140], completions falling [EV-IMM-141], and numbers lost for want of staff [EV-IMM-123].
- *The backlog:* the net backlog of 7.7 million [EV-IMM-140] clears in about 5.5 years at the backlog money's pace, about $0.7 billion a year [EV-IMM-575]. About 1.4 million cases are left when the money ends after four to five years (`immigration_model.uscis()`), so the six-month target (IMM-6.03) needs the money for about a year longer; section 6 funds that year in memo 7's line.
- *The hiring plan:* hiring starts on R1's enactment, with multi-year money available at once (IMM-6.01). That gives about 15 months of hiring before filing opens, which is memo 7's lead time in practice. The legacy plan counted about 12,000 hires (Bill Book 15, `legacy-research.md`, section 1). This section sizes the surge from memo 7's bottom-up table (about $17-27 billion over FY2030-39) rather than a head count. The lighter records-first form (IMM-1.06, 1.07) is what makes that money enough: most cases are a records check, a biometrics visit and a decision, not a document review.
- *Sequencing:*
  - notices to known people go first;
  - tier B by year of arrival, earliest first, so the longest-settled are decided first;
  - registration on filing, because the work permit is the receipt.
- *Asylum officers:* about 800 staff for 75,000 cases a year at DHS's 2022 estimate [EV-IMM-302], scaled to 250,000-350,000 decisions a year while the backlog clears (memo 4); the legacy capacity review's 1,500 officers (range 1,000-2,500) is the same order [EV-IMM-574].

**The records match.**
- *What it answers:* the IRS and SSA return, for a named person and given years, whether records show residence: a filing, posted earnings or a W-2. They return yes or no, never the return itself.
- *How it is tested:* on a sample of DACA files in 2027-28, before launch.
- *What it cannot reach:* people with no filings and no posted earnings; the sworn-statement route covers them. The share of the unauthorized population with ITIN filings or posted earnings is not in the register (section 3, "Evidence still needed"), so the statement route's volume is a working assumption to study in implementation.

**The interior enforcement component and the restructure.**
- *Personnel, contracts and money* transfer by function: removal operations to the new component, HSI's investigators to their own, and case-management contracts to a service run apart from arrests.
- *Training first:* officers are re-credentialed under the new rules and trained in them before the transition date.
- *Why the transition date falls in 2031:* registration has to be open and USCIS's surge under way first, so the reorganization does not draw on the same DHS management attention in 2030.
- *What runs meanwhile:* from signature, ICE applies the priorities, the cap, the standards and protected places; only its organization waits.

**Detention to the cap.**
- *The starting point:* about 65,000-73,000 held in 2026 [EV-IMM-171] [EV-IMM-172]. The base account sustains 41,500 beds [EV-IMM-362].
- *October 1, 2029:* the reconciliation money lapses [EV-IMM-035]. FY2030 appropriations fund 30,000 on average and case management for about 150,000 people at a time (range 100,000-250,000; `6-cost.md`, part 1).
- *Preparation:* a census of detention contracts that end with the reconciliation money, and case-management contracts ready to start on October 1, 2029.
- *The release path:* people released are placed in case management with a hearing date. The priorities decide who stays held.

**E-Verify.**
- *Volume:* checks roughly double at full coverage, from about half of new hires [EV-IMM-398], about 35 million more a year (project arithmetic on [EV-IMM-191]).
- *Contest capacity:* SSA's and USCIS's staff must answer contests in ten working days (IMM-4.14), or cases close as authorized. That rule puts the cost of delay on the government, not the worker.
- *Before the first tranche,* every registered person's status and number are in the system (IMM-4.13, condition (c)).

**The courts.**
- *The backlog:* 3.3 million pending cases [EV-IMM-100].
- *What shrinks it:* legalization closes the long-settled residents' cases on filing (IMM-1.10), and officers decide asylum first (IMM-3.01). Together they shrink the backlog before more judges arrive.
- *Judges:* `tools/immigration_model.py` (`eoir()`) puts the corps that clears the backlog by FY2035 at about 1,670 judges in all, **about 920 more than the 753 on board** [EV-IMM-103] (range about 860-2,400 in all, set mainly by completions a judge: CRS's average is about 510 [EV-IMM-641], range 450-750).
  - *The ramp:* about 250 net hires a year from FY2030, as the Manhattan Institute's estimate assumes [EV-IMM-646], plus replacements for attrition of about 5.6% a year (CRS counts 26 departures a year [EV-IMM-643]). EOIR hired 312 in FY2026 [EV-IMM-103], so the pace has been done. The corps reaches about 1,670 by FY2033 and shrinks by attrition after the backlog clears in FY2035, to about 1,250 by FY2040.
  - *The assumptions:* about 35% of pending cases close on filing for status (IMM-1.10); receipts fall by about 30% as officers decide first (IMM-3.01); new judges decide half a year of cases in their first year.
  - *A slower ramp:* at 150 hires a year, EOIR's FY2024 request [EV-IMM-644], the backlog clears about FY2037, two years late. With the 800-judge cap kept, it does not clear within fifteen years, which is why the repeal matters.
  - *Teams, not only judges:* each judge comes with clerks, staff attorneys, interpreters and space, about $1.5 million a team a year [EV-IMM-644] [EV-IMM-645]; EOIR's FY2024 request paired 150 judge teams with 750 attorneys and support staff [EV-IMM-644]. Courtroom space is the slowest part to build, so the 2027-28 preparation includes a space plan.
  - The final number is set with democratic renewal.
- *Counsel:* for everyone detained, at a 30,000 cap, a bounded caseload. For children it runs through ORR's existing legal-services contracts, extended to appointed counsel.

## 4. Remedies

| Who | What they can do | Provisions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A person arrested before filing who appears eligible | Show eligibility on a statement; no removal, and release unless a security or public-safety priority, until a decision or a year after filing opens | IMM-1.10 |
| An applicant refused status | An administrative appeal, then review in federal court; the work permit continues until the appeal is decided | IMM-1.05, 1.10 |
| A person whose records show the wrong years | Correct the record on the notice, by statement, at any front door | IMM-1.06, 1.07, 6.04 |
| A person held in breach of the standards | Sue to enforce them; the inspector's reports are public | IMM-4.04, 4.09 |
| A person arrested at a protected place or outside the priorities without a written reason | The arrest's record is reviewable; evidence from a breach is excludable in removal proceedings (working choice) | IMM-4.03, 4.07 |
| A worker wrongly flagged by E-Verify | Contest in ten days; no adverse action; confirmation if the agencies do not answer in ten days; lost wages on a claim; a charge to DOJ's Immigrant and Employee Rights Section | IMM-4.14 |
| A worker retaliated against for a complaint | Deferred action and a work permit; penalties on the employer | IMM-4.17 |
| A person whose data was misused for enforcement | A private right of action and penalties | IMM-1.36, 4.11 |
| A person ordered removed without proper notice | A right to reopen | IMM-3.10 |

## 5. States' roles

- **Eligibility.** States run SNAP, TANF and Medicaid under the table's rows (IMM-1.22, 1.27); the registered status is on the qualified list, so states have no new status to verify. Emergency Medicaid and state options for people without status continue until the single plan covers them in 2031.
- **Delegated enforcement.** States and localities that keep 287(g) agreements are bound by the priorities, standards, protected places and identification (IMM-4.10). A state may decline any agreement.
- **State E-Verify laws.** Twenty-two states require E-Verify for some employers [EV-IMM-190]. The federal mandate preempts state rules that are stricter in timing or scope than IMM-4.12, so employers face one rule, and the error-correction protections (IMM-4.14) apply everywhere (working choice).
- **State and local costs.** The National Academies found immigrants' long-run fiscal effect positive federally and negative at the state and local level [EV-IMM-222]. The plan's universal programs are federal, which limits the shift to states; the table's restorations follow the federal shares of each program.

## 6. Rural delivery

Rural delivery looks first at USDA's local channels (`DEC-2026-11-09-26`; `dossiers/foundations/rural-delivery.md`).
- **Front doors.** USDA Rural Development's state and area offices, more than 400 in all [SRC-2158], take statements and confirmations for the registry and pass them to USCIS the same day (IMM-6.04), as they do for the secure floor's payments (`DEC-2026-11-10-02`). They decide nothing about status. Farmworkers in tier A's work route live in exactly the places these offices serve.
- **Biometrics** are taken at USCIS centers, which are mostly urban, and by mobile visits scheduled through the same offices and through DOJ-recognized nonprofits.
- **The limit.** Rural Development lost about 36% of its staff in the first half of 2025 [SRC-2143], to an estimated 3,058 full-time equivalents in FY2026 [SRC-2130]. A front-door role adds work to a thin field staff. It depends on the field staff's rebuild that the rural note weighs (about $0.3-0.5 billion a year, judgment, `rural-delivery.md`), which this dossier does not fund. Until then the SSA field offices and the nonprofits carry most of it.
- **Farmworker housing** (USDA 514/516) follows rental help's rule (IMM-1.34), proposed to housing.

## 7. What delivery leaves open

- **The statement route's volume:** study in implementation, from the first year's filings, with the yearly fraud audit (IMM-6.06).
- **The restructure's internal design** (offices, regions, chains of command) below the four lines IMM-4.01 sets: the act and DHS, with the rules fixed in statute.
- **The court's form** (CRT-5): democratic renewal's.
- **The work routes' ceilings** after three years (IMM-2.11): set from the Labor Department's counts.
