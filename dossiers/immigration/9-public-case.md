## The brief

**An immigration system that is humane, orderly and lawful, and that people can trust.**

About 14 million people lived here without legal status in 2023 (Pew Research Center) [EV-IMM-002], and 45% of them had been here 20 years or more (Migration Policy Institute) [EV-IMM-005]. 5.3 million American children live with a parent who has no status (Migration Policy Institute) [EV-IMM-007]. The courts have 3.3 million cases waiting (Justice Department, 2026) [EV-IMM-100], and the immigration agency had 12.8 million applications pending in 2026 (Niskanen Center, from agency data) [EV-IMM-140]. In July 2026, 70.6% of the 65,765 people held in immigration detention had no criminal conviction (TRAC, from ICE data) [EV-IMM-171].

Project 2050's immigration plan does five things.

**Earned legal status for people who have built their lives here,** from late 2030.
- People who have lived here ten years or more get a green card, after fingerprints and a criminal check.
- So do Dreamers, anyone who has held temporary protection or DACA, and farm, care and construction workers with a work history, if they were here by January 1 of the year the law passes.
- Everyone else here on the day the law is signed can register, work legally and be protected from deportation while they reach ten years.
- Proof of residence comes from tax and earnings records the government already holds. There is no penalty, and no fee for families below twice the poverty line.
- People with legal or registered status, a filing receipt included, join the national health plan when it starts in 2031; people with no status keep emergency care, as today.
- Long-settled residents deported or pushed out since 2025, with three years here and a citizen or green-card-holder spouse, parent or child, can apply from abroad.

**A legal system that works,** from 2030-31.
- More family and employment green cards, the numbers from the 2024 bipartisan Senate bill, made permanent [EV-IMM-535].
- Spouses and children of permanent residents no longer wait in line.
- Portable visas for care and construction workers, paid at least the plan's wage floors, with a green card after three years. Immigrants are 30% of direct care workers [EV-CAR-614] and 34% of construction trades workers [EV-HOU-055].

**Fast, fair asylum decisions,** from the act.
- Trained asylum officers decide claims within six months.
- A surge at the border is met with more officers and a 90-day track, not a ban on asking for protection.
- At least 50,000 refugees are admitted a year; 7,730 were admitted in the first nine months of FY2026 (CRS) [EV-IMM-052].
- Children, people with serious mental illness and everyone detained get a lawyer.
- About 900 more immigration judges, with their staff, so the courts' backlog of 3.3 million cases clears in about six years, on our estimates (section 5). The final number is set with the plan for the courts.

**Enforcement that respects rights,** from the act's signature, with a new agency structure in 2031.
- ICE is replaced, and the department's immigration agencies are restructured.
- Enforcement focuses by law on threats to security, serious crime and recent unlawful entry.
- No more than 30,000 people are held on average, under standards they can enforce in court.
- Case management, not a cell, for everyone else: more than 99% of people in the full-service program who had a hearing appeared in court (Government Accountability Office) [EV-IMM-354].
- No arrests at schools, churches or hospitals without a warrant or an emergency, and officers show who they are.

**One fair rule at work,** with E-Verify phased in from about mid-2031, and never before October of the year after the law passes.
- Every employer checks new hires with E-Verify, in steps by employer size.
- The first step comes only when an independent inspector confirms the new legal status is up and running: sign-up open everywhere, work permits issued and recorded on time, and newly legal workers cleared by E-Verify as reliably as green-card holders. Until then the check repeats every 90 days, so it doesn't fall on the people the law just legalized.
- A worker wrongly flagged gets ten days to fix it, keeps the job meanwhile, and is paid for lost wages.
- Workers who report wage theft or unsafe work are protected from immigration threats.

**What it costs, on our estimates.** About $0.18 trillion over 2030-39 on the federal budget, about $28.8 billion a year from 2040, before the plan's financing (section 6). The largest pieces are health coverage for lawful newcomers, status itself and ending the wait for help for the newly legal. Social Security's funds gained from legalization in the 2013 Senate bill's score (Social Security's actuaries) [EV-IMM-604]; the E-Verify mandate costs them about $42 billion over ten years on our estimates.

## The case in one paragraph

Millions of people have lived here for decades, raising American children and doing the work the country runs on, with no way to become legal. This plan gives them earned status after a check, through the registry already in the law, with its date made to roll forward [EV-IMM-590]. It fixes the legal system so families and workers are not stuck in decades-long lines. It decides asylum claims in months, not years. It replaces ICE with enforcement focused by law on real threats, with limits on detention and protections people can enforce. And it phases in workplace checks only after legal status is running, with protections for workers who are wrongly flagged.

## Messages

1. **Earned, not given.** Ten years here, fingerprints and a criminal check. People with serious crimes are barred [EV-IMM-623].
2. **Order the country can see.** Clear cutoffs: the day the law is signed for registration, January 1 of that year for the fastest green cards; checks at work; decisions in months; a surge met with capacity.
3. **Government that already knows you.** Tax and earnings records prove residence, so no one hunts for twenty years of rent receipts [EV-IMM-639].
4. **Families first.** 5.3 million citizen children have a parent who could gain status [EV-IMM-007].
5. **Workers the plan needs.** Care and construction depend on immigrant workers [EV-CAR-614] [EV-HOU-055]; the plan keeps them here and pays them the plan's wage floors.
6. **Enforcement that respects rights,** and that a future administration cannot simply switch off, because it is in law.

## Hard questions, plain answers

- **"Isn't this amnesty?"** It is earned status: ten years' residence, fingerprints and a criminal check, under the registry already in immigration law, whose date has been stuck at 1972 since 1986 [EV-IMM-590] [EV-IMM-591].
- **"Won't it bring more people?"** No one who arrives after the law is signed can register, and only people here by January 1 of that year can get the fastest green cards. People who arrive while Congress debates can register, but wait ten years for a green card. After the 1986 legalization, long-run unauthorized inflows from Mexico did not change (Orrenius and Zavodny) [EV-IMM-233].
- **"What does it cost?"** About $0.18 trillion over ten years on our estimates. For legal status itself, outside scores of similar bills find a small first-decade cost (CBO, 2021) [EV-IMM-600].
- **"Will E-Verify flag me by mistake?"** In the government's last full study, 0.2% of citizens were wrongly flagged at first (Westat for USCIS, FY2009) [EV-IMM-391]. Under this plan you get ten days to fix it, you can't be fired meanwhile, the government must answer in ten days or you are confirmed, and lost wages are paid.
- **"Does replacing ICE mean no enforcement?"** No. A new agency enforces the law, focused on security, serious crime and recent unlawful entry, under rules in statute. Criminal investigators get their own agency.
- **"Will my information be used to deport my family?"** No. The law bars using registration or program records for immigration enforcement, with penalties, except under a criminal warrant for a named person.

## Stories (composite illustrations, not real people)

- **A cook in Houston who arrived in 2012** files in late 2030. His tax filings show his years here. He gives fingerprints, pays nothing because his family is below twice the poverty line, and gets a green card on approval.
- **His neighbor, who arrived in 2022,** registers the same month. She works legally on her filing receipt and can apply for a green card when she reaches ten years.
- **A home health aide recruited from abroad** comes on the care visa at the plan's wage floor. When her agency cuts her hours she moves to another, and after three years she is a permanent resident.
- **A family that crosses during a surge** is screened, placed with a case manager and decided by an asylum officer in about three months.

| A green card for people with ten years here, after a check | That everyone will apply: we assume about 70% |
| About $0.18 trillion over ten years, on our estimates | That legalization "pays for itself" on the federal budget: on our estimates the status title is a small cost and the set a larger one |
| E-Verify no earlier than October of the year after the law passes, and only once an inspector confirms legal status is running | A fixed E-Verify start date: it depends on the inspector's check, repeated every 90 days (about mid-2031 on our timeline) |
| Registration's cutoff is the day the law is signed; tier A's is January 1 of that year | — |
| No more than 30,000 people detained on average | That detention costs will fall by a set amount: on our estimates it is about +$7 billion over ten years, depending on how many people are in case management |
| Decisions on asylum in about six months; in a surge about 90 days | That surges will not happen |
| Wall money goes to border security that agents recommend | A dollar figure: the unspent balance in mid-2029 is not known |
| Protections against wrong E-Verify flags | That E-Verify catches identity fraud: it misses about half of unauthorized workers it checks [EV-IMM-393] |
