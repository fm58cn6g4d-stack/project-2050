# A new consensus on immigration: design choices

Role: section 3 of the immigration dossier: the real forks, the options weighed with their evidence and costs, a recommendation for each, one decision memo per batch of forks in `memos/`, and a numbered list of narrower yes-or-no points. Status: draft for decision (stage E, board row I3, question 0p); nothing here is decided. Every recommendation is a proposal to Espen, decided memo by memo and point by point (`charter/STANDARDS.md`, "Decision memos"). Sources are named in plain text in the memos; bracketed tags point to `evidence.json` or another dossier's register; "project arithmetic" is shown where it is used; "judgment" marks a working assumption with its basis below (`DEC-2026-11-10-07`). Figures and analysis from the second Trump administration's agencies are its own (`DEC-2026-10-30-01`).

- **Each memo opens with a decision line** in the same form: the recommended option, its cost over FY2030-39 and in FY2040, and its evidence. `memos/README.md` repeats them in one table.
- **Legacy designs are options** (`DEC-2026-10-30-02`): IMM-1A's tiers, the application window, IMM-2, IMM-3, IMM-4A, E-Verify's pairing, the five-year bar, the USCIS line and the one-bill vehicle are each weighed against alternatives. "Inherited assumptions, traced" says which Espen chose; none of the immigration designs is Espen's.
- **Report, don't gate** (`DEC-2026-11-10-05`). Costs are reported for the financing settlement and not trimmed to fit. Memo 6 is partly a money question, because its options reach people without status at different cost.
- **Design lens** (`DEC-2026-11-09-13`). Each option says what it asks of people and what it buys. Memo 2 is mostly about burden; memos 5 and 6 drop verification steps whose burden falls on lawful residents.
- **Time frame** (`DEC-2026-10-29-01`). Recommendations start with the 2029 law; anything earlier is labeled preparation.
- **Working assumption on rule 3 and the child benefit.** Secure floor question 0o is still open. Where rule 3 meets the child benefit, this section takes that memo's recommendation (memo 1, part 2 B, the child's own ID) as the working assumption, and says so in memo 6.

## The baseline in 2029

Section 1 gives it in full ("The January 2029 baseline"). In brief:
- **Status:** about 12-14.5 million people without status (working assumption); TPS for about 274,000 or fewer, terminations unreviewable; DACA for current holders under a Texas-only injunction; parole programs ended; the registry date January 1, 1972 [EV-IMM-022] [EV-IMM-023] [EV-IMM-027] [EV-IMM-590].
- **Queues:** 3.3 million court cases; 12.8 million pending at USCIS; numbers set in 1990 [EV-IMM-100] [EV-IMM-140] [EV-IMM-120].
- **Enforcement money:** the 2025 law and P.L. 119-98 fund ICE and CBP until **September 30, 2029, nine months into the plan**, then lapse together [EV-IMM-035] [EV-IMM-039] [EV-IMM-040]; 800 judges at most from November 1, 2028 [EV-IMM-105].
- **Benefits:** SNAP, Medicaid, Medicare and the premium credits limited to citizens, permanent residents and two groups; the child credit's parent number rule [EV-IMM-046] to [EV-IMM-050].

## IMM-1's score, re-derived

**The legacy figure cannot stand.** The ledger carries the immigration bill at −$0.14 trillion, "S.744 CBO pattern (~$175B first decade), not rescored" [EV-IMM-570]. The $175 billion is widely reported as CBO's preliminary figure for S. 744 as amended by the border surge, not confirmed from CBO's text, which says only that the saving would fall short of the committee bill's $197 billion "by something less than $40 billion" [EV-IMM-207]; CBO's final estimate, as passed, was $158 billion [EV-IMM-205] (`legacy-research.md`, finding 1). Either way it is a 2013 score of a different bill. And the pattern itself does not transfer:
- **S. 744's savings came from its new flows,** about 10 million more people by 2023 [EV-IMM-203]; its on-budget deficits rose even so [EV-IMM-208]. BPC's 2013 reading of the same kind of bill found about $180 billion of first-decade savings [EV-IMM-610]; its step-by-step study found legalization with E-Verify, without the new flows, saved $10 billion in a decade and added about $150 billion over twenty years [EV-IMM-611].
- **The two narrow 2021 bills, the closest guide to memo 1,** raised deficits: H.R. 6 by $35.3 billion over 2021-2031, with on-budget direct spending of $36.5 billion, mostly marketplace credits, Medicaid and refundable credits [EV-IMM-600] [EV-IMM-601] [EV-IMM-602]; the farmworker bill by $4.7 billion [EV-IMM-215]. In both, costs rise each year of the window.
- **Social Security gains either way:** SSA's actuaries put S. 744 at +$284 billion to the trust funds by 2024 and the long-range deficit from 2.72% to 2.51% of payroll [EV-IMM-604] [EV-IMM-605]; unauthorized workers already pay about $12 billion a year more than the benefits on their earnings [EV-IMM-606]. That gain is off-budget and is not general-fund room.
- **Two Dallas Fed economists, Orrenius and Zavodny, writing in the Cato Journal,** judged in 2012 that legalization is likely to widen the gap between revenues and outlays, with states and localities bearing much of the medium-run cost [EV-IMM-616], while another Cato Journal article found large GDP gains [EV-IMM-615]; Penn Wharton finds a larger population and GDP but slightly lower GDP per person by 2050 [EV-IMM-608]. Both sides agree the economy grows; neither finds a large first-decade budget saving without new flows.

**The bound (project arithmetic and judgment).** H.R. 6 reached Dreamers and TPS holders; memo 1 reaches about two to three times as many people in its first decade. Scaling H.R. 6's $35.3 billion by 2-3 and growing it to the 2030s gives about +$0.09-0.14 trillion. Two things lower that: the decided single plan covers every resident from 2031 whatever their status (memo 6), so much of H.R. 6's health cost is not caused by legalization; and memo 3's higher legal numbers, which travel with it, save a little. Two things raise it: green cards on approval for tier B (memo 1) bring costs forward, and memo 6 ends the five-year wait. Netting them gives **about +$0.05 trillion over FY2030-39, range −$0.05 to +$0.15 trillion**, and **about +$10 billion in FY2040**: a small cost, not a saving, and of uncertain sign at the low end.

**What the budget model's IMM-1/E11 row should become** (for a later booking; `models/` is not edited here):
- *Now:* −$0.14 trillion (−0.25 to −0.02), profile "Net saving grows as legalized workers enter the tax base" (`models/data/budget/provisions.csv`, row IMM-1).
- *Proposed, if the recommended set is chosen:* a cost. Either one row at **+$0.13 trillion (range −$0.04 to +$0.31 trillion), +$19 billion in FY2040**, or, better, split so each part can move with its decision: IMM-1 status and delivery (memos 1-2) +$0.05 trillion; the eligibility table (memo 6) +$0.08 trillion; IMM-2 legal immigration (memo 3) −$0.04 trillion; IMM-3 asylum and the court requirements (memo 4) +$0.03 trillion; IMM-4 (memo 5) zero; these rows sum to about +$0.12 trillion, and the remaining +$0.01 trillion is memo 7's USCIS work beyond memos 2 and 4, carried in CAP-7S, whose USCIS share is rebuilt at about $0.02-0.03 trillion in all instead of $46 billion. The profile becomes a cost rising through the decade, as in CBO's 2021 tables, with Social Security's gain reported off-budget.
- *Basis tag:* estimate, bounded by CBO's 2013 and 2021 scores; a real score is requested in 2027-28 (point 13).

## Recommended without a memo: fourteen yes-or-no points

Each is decided on its own. None is adopted by silence. Costs are judgment unless a tag gives them.

1. **Registration's cutoff is the immigration bill's introduction date in 2029,** so debate does not draw arrivals; the ten-year line rolls from then on (memo 1).

   **Recommended: yes.**
2. **The criminal bars are the registry's existing grounds** [EV-IMM-623], with no bar for immigration-only offenses (unlawful entry and reentry) and a waiver for old or minor convictions.
   - *Why:* a bar should screen for danger, not for how a person crossed.

   **Recommended: yes.**
3. **A work permit and protection from removal from the day a person files** (memo 2).
   - *Burden it removes:* months without lawful work while USCIS decides.

   **Recommended: yes.**
4. **Back taxes only as the IRS assesses them on income already earned,** not a reconstruction by the applicant (the legacy rule, `domain-immigration.md`, line 43).

   **Recommended: yes.**
5. **The new statuses carry work-eligible Social Security numbers from registration,** so legalized parents meet the 2025 child credit rule [EV-IMM-050] even if Espen keeps it, and earnings post to their own records.

   **Recommended: yes.**
6. **Officer identification is one statute shared with the executive-power file** (`domain-immigration.md`, line 91; staging 5.11).

   **Recommended: yes.**
7. **The 2029 reconciliation bill rescinds the 2025 law's unobligated wall money** [EV-IMM-034] and funds port screening instead.
   - *Cost:* a saving of whatever is unobligated in mid-2029, unknown now; reported, not booked.

   **Recommended: yes.**
8. **Repeal the asylum charges that grow with delay:** the $100-a-year pending fee and the $550 first work permit charge [EV-IMM-043] [EV-IMM-045].
   - *Cost:* small; part of CBO's $66.5 billion of fee revenue for the 2025 title [EV-IMM-619] (judgment: under $1 billion a year).

   **Recommended: yes.**
9. **Fold the pending $6 billion asylum and refugee reserve** [EV-IMM-578] **into memo 7's bottom-up USCIS line,** rather than adding it on top. This answers the item D9 left pending.

   **Recommended: yes.**
10. **Codify *Trump v. Barbara* in 8 U.S.C. 1401,** in the Court's words, closing the statutory route Justice Kavanaugh left open [EV-IMM-031].

    **Recommended: yes.**
11. **Denaturalization only in a judicial proceeding, on clear and convincing evidence, within a ten-year limit, with no quotas** (legacy, `domain-immigration.md`, line 112).

    **Recommended: yes.**
12. **The civics and English test at the 2008 standard; end neighborhood investigations of naturalization applicants** [EV-IMM-427].

    **Recommended: yes.**
13. **Ask CBO and JCT to score the status title and memo 6's table, and SSA's actuaries the Social Security effect, in 2027-28** (preparation). No score of a design like this exists (section 2, "Evidence still needed").
