# Dossier: A new consensus on immigration (flagship 6)

Role: the immigration flagship (`charter/PLAN.md` 3.1). Status: started, stage E (board rows I1 to I4). Sections 1 and 2 are drafted, with the legacy record restated; section 3's seven memos and fourteen points are **decided** (`DEC-2026-11-12-07`, question 0p): as recommended, except memo 5 (replace ICE with A's conduct rules in statute; mandatory E-Verify phased in at least a year after the status programs run, with B's labor enforcement) and points 1 and 7. Sections 4-6 specify and price the decided set (row I4, PR 1); sections 7-9 follow in PR 2. Level: the decided items are at **L2** (`agenda/agenda.csv`); L3 after sections 7-9 and the milestone review. Headline: about **+$0.12 trillion over FY2030-39 (range about −$0.06 to +$0.33 trillion) and about +$16.5 billion in FY2040** on the general fund, against the booked IMM-1 row's −$0.14 trillion (`6-cost.md`); not booked. The target is L3, with priority pieces at L4. The plan specifies immigration "well before the 2028 cycle" (`charter/PLAN.md` 6, stage E), because "a new consensus can win only if it is presented and defended in full" (3.1).

**What it brings together** (`charter/PLAN.md` 3.1, exactly): "A humane, orderly and lawful system: legal pathways and status for long-settled residents, fair and fast adjudication, enforcement that respects rights, integration and workforce."

**How CRT-5 meets this dossier.** CRT-5, the Article I immigration court (CRT-5A chosen; 5B and 5C alternatives), is designed in `legacy/domains/domain-courts.md` (lines 100-118). It lives with the democratic renewal foundation (`agenda/agenda.csv`, `home_dossier` = `foundations/democratic-renewal`), which is not started. It decides IMM-3's asylum denials and IMM-4's removal cases, and the legacy files ask that the two be scored together (`domain-immigration.md`, line 131; `domain-courts.md`, line 251). This dossier therefore:
- maps the immigration courts' caseload, judges and due-process record as part of the problem (section 1, part C);
- weighs adjudication models, counsel and court structure in section 2;
- in I3, states what the immigration system needs from the court (capacity, timelines, counsel, independence) as a requirement, without designing the court.

The court's form, tenure and Article III fallback stay with the foundation, unless Espen moves CRT-5 here.

- OUT-01, child poverty, which names immigrant families among those who struggle to claim benefits (through rule 3 and the child benefit's number rule);
- OUT-03, care people can count on, through coverage for people without status and the care workforce (rules 3 and 8);

## Forks for I3

I3's memo set puts these to Espen as one batch. It follows the health memo set's format: a forks-at-a-glance table, costs benchmarked against outside scores, bill-text comparisons and plain consequences, with each legacy design weighed as one option among others.
1. **Who gets status, on what path.** IMM-1A against tier A only (1B), status without citizenship (1C, the Dignity Act model) and everyone present before a cutoff (1D, the U.S. Citizenship Act). Also the residence threshold, the cutoff date, and the time to a green card and to citizenship.
2. **How status is delivered (delivery and form, the fork the design lens asks for).** Not how many people, but how they get status:
   - a USCIS application with fees, back taxes, penalties and proof of presence (the legacy and the bills), against a rolling registry date (section 2, part A);
   - proof of residence from records the government already holds (tax filings, Social Security earnings, school and court records), with self-attestation where they run out, against documents gathered by each applicant;
   - one registration window against a standing route;
   - the shape of the criminal bars, and work permits from the day an application is filed (`DEC-2026-11-09-13`).
3. **The status programs.** TPS and DACA holders and parolees in 2029, and whether temporary statuses stay a long-term category or lead to permanence.
4. **Legal immigration and the backlogs.** Recapture, the family caps, the per-country cap, the H-1B design and fee, and the employment categories.
5. **The workforce visas (rule 8).** Agriculture (the Farm Workforce Modernization Act's model), care (portability and the $20 floor) and construction, tied to the decided programs' demand.
6. **Asylum and the border.** Asylum officers deciding merits, a timeline, regional processing, the refugee floor and its size, and what border authority the plan keeps or repeals from 2025-26 and the 2024 bill.
7. **What the system needs from the courts** (with CRT-5, owned by democratic renewal): timelines; counsel for children and people with serious mental illness, or for everyone in removal proceedings; and how far adjudication moves out of the executive.
8. **Interior enforcement and detention.** IMM-4A against the DHS split (4B) and guidance only (4C); the detention cap and its size; alternatives to detention as the default; protected places; officer identification.
9. **Workplace verification.** Mandatory E-Verify phased in after registration (the legacy pairing), against voluntary E-Verify with labor-standards enforcement, on its record and its burden on workers and employers.
10. **Citizenship and naturalization.** Codifying the birthright ruling; denaturalization limits; fees and fee waivers; the test standard.
11. **One eligibility table (rule 3).** For each program, which statuses qualify and when, keeping or replacing the five-year bar; coverage for people without status; the child benefit's number rule; the owner programs; the data firewalls.
12. **USCIS and the vehicle.** Fee-funded or appropriated adjudication; the processing target; the Office of New Americans. Also whether the plan moves one bill or splits it (status, legal immigration, enforcement), what can go in reconciliation (funding, fees), and what to prepare in 2027-28.
