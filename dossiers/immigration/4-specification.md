# A new consensus on immigration: specification

Role: section 4 of the immigration dossier. It writes the decided designs as provisions: what each does, its parameters, who is covered, the vehicle, the agency, how it changes the January 2029 law and the evidence it rests on. Provision IDs and their fields are in `provisions.json`. Status: draft (stage E, board row I4, PR 1). It rests on Espen's decision `DEC-2026-11-12-07` (question 0p) and on three standing rules: `DEC-2026-11-09-13` (guide, don't police), `DEC-2026-11-10-05` (report, don't gate) and `DEC-2026-11-10-07` (what needs an answer now). Costs are in `6-cost.md`, and each provision's `cost_source` points there. Delivery is in `5-delivery.md`.

## How to read this section

- **Status of each provision.**
  - *Decided:* Espen decided it, in a memo option or a yes-or-no point in section 3, cited.
  - *Specified:* a choice that `DEC-2026-11-12-07` left to this section ("section 4 proposes"). There are two: registration's cutoff (IMM-1.04) and the measurable start of the E-Verify mandate (IMM-4.13). Each is written as recommended and goes to Espen as a small question (end of this section).
  - *Working choice:* a parameter this section sets so the draft is complete. It rests on judgment and can change without reopening a decision.
  - *Proposed:* a seam this dossier proposes to another dossier, which decides it.
- **Commitments and working assumptions.** This section commits to:
  - who gets status and on what path;
  - the form of the registry and what applicants pay;
  - the numbers and routes in legal immigration;
  - who decides asylum, and the refugee floor;
  - the conduct rules, the detention cap and the restructure;
  - the E-Verify mandate and its protections;
  - the eligibility table and the firewall;
  - USCIS's funding rule.

  Dates after enactment, numerical ceilings on the new work routes and every cost are working assumptions, with ranges in section 6.
- **Dates.** The plan begins in January 2029 (`DEC-2026-10-29-01`). The working timeline: the 2029 reconciliation act (R1) by about mid-2029; the FY2030 Homeland Security appropriations act by October 1, 2029, when the enforcement money of the 2025 and 2026 laws lapses [EV-IMM-035] [EV-IMM-039] [EV-IMM-040]; the regular-order immigration act signed by about December 2029. Preparation in 2027-28 is labeled as such (`5-delivery.md`, part 3).
- **Evidence.** The last column lists the evidence IDs a provision rests on, in this dossier's register or another's. "Project arithmetic" is `tools/immigration_path.py`.
- **Agenda homes.** The provisions sit under the six agenda items the memos name:
  - IMM-1: status and the registry (memos 1 and 2), with memo 6's table at IMM-1.20 to 1.36, because the legacy design put the benefit bar in IMM-1;
  - IMM-2: legal immigration and the workforce routes (memo 3);
  - IMM-3: asylum, the border and the court requirements (memo 4; point 7);
  - IMM-4: enforcement, the restructure and workplace verification (memo 5);
  - IMM-5: citizenship (points 10-12; memo 7 part 2);
  - IMM-6: USCIS and delivery (memo 7).

**Why this split.**
- *Reconciliation carries money, not rules without a budget effect* (the Byrd rule). The parliamentarian rejected legalization three times in 2021 (news account) [EV-IMM-238], so status goes to regular order. The 2025 and 2026 reconciliation laws carried immigration money and fees [EV-IMM-619] [EV-IMM-039], and the 2025 law's benefit limits were made by reconciliation [EV-IMM-046] to [EV-IMM-050], so their reversal can be too. The data firewall and the single plan's residency rule have no budget effect of their own and go in the immigration act.
- *Appropriations by October 1, 2029* because the reconciliation money for ICE and CBP lapses on September 30, 2029 [EV-IMM-035] [EV-IMM-040]. From then on, detention's size is an appropriations choice. The plan funds the cap and case management, and no third enforcement reconciliation law.
- *The restructure rides the immigration act,* not appropriations: it changes agencies' authorities and needs an organic statute.

## What the decisions changed

- **Memo 5 part 1: replace ICE, with A's rules.** Section 3 recommended A's rules inside ICE. Espen chose to restructure DHS and replace ICE, with no name committed. IMM-4.01 and 4.02 write the restructure; IMM-4.03 to 4.10 keep every rule A carried. Cost: about $3 billion over FY2030-39 for the transition and the inspector (section 6).
- **Memo 5 part 2: mandatory E-Verify, phased by size, at least a year after the status programs run, with B's enforcement.** Section 3 recommended no mandate. IMM-4.12 to 4.18 write the mandate with its start test, error correction and published counts, and keep B's labor enforcement and worker protection. On the general fund it nets to a small gain under CBO and JCT's conventions; Social Security's funds lose (section 6, part 2).
- **Point 1: the cutoff is the signature date or a set period after it,** not the introduction date. IMM-1.04 recommends the signature date.
- **Point 7: wall money is redirected,** not rescinded. IMM-3.07 writes the uses and the conditions. Against CBO's baseline it saves nothing, where the rescission would have saved an unknown amount.
- **Everything else as recommended** (`DEC-2026-11-12-07`).

## Open questions for Espen

Two small questions, each a choice `DEC-2026-11-12-07` left to this section. Both are written as recommended.

1. **Registration's cutoff (IMM-1.04).** **Recommended: the day the act is signed.** A date after signature would tell people a window is open under a known law, which is the draw the cutoff exists to stop; filing opens later anyway, so readiness does not need it. The alternative: a named period, such as 90 days after signature, which covers people already on their way or whose presence on the day leaves no record.
2. **E-Verify's start (IMM-4.13).** **Recommended: a checked test, not a fixed date.** The mandate's first tranche starts the first October 1 at least a year after the Inspector General confirms that registration is open everywhere, work permits are issued and recorded within 30 days, known people have their notices, and registered people clear E-Verify as permanent residents do. On the working timeline that is October 1, 2032. With it, a one-time twelve-month pause before a later tranche if wrong flags for lawful noncitizens pass 1%. The alternative: a fixed date (for example October 1, 2032), which is simpler to promise but could start the mandate before registered workers are in E-Verify's records.
