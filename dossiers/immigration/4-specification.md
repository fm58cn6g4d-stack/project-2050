# A new consensus on immigration: specification

Role: section 4 of the immigration dossier. It writes the decided designs as provisions: what each does, its parameters, who is covered, the vehicle, the agency, how it changes the January 2029 law and the evidence it rests on. Provision IDs and their fields are in `provisions.json`. Status: reviewed (L2; `review-milestone.md`), with the review's fix steps applied; L3 after the re-check. It rests on Espen's decision `DEC-2026-11-12-07` (question 0p) and on three standing rules: `DEC-2026-11-09-13` (guide, don't police), `DEC-2026-11-10-05` (report, don't gate) and `DEC-2026-11-10-07` (what needs an answer now). Costs are in `6-cost.md`, and each provision's `cost_source` points there. Delivery is in `5-delivery.md`.

## How to read this section

- **Status of each provision.**
  - *Decided:* Espen decided it, in a memo option or a yes-or-no point in section 3, cited.
  - *Decided later:* the two choices `DEC-2026-11-12-07` left to this section, registration's cutoff (IMM-1.04) and the measurable start of the E-Verify mandate (IMM-4.13), were put to Espen and decided in `DEC-2026-11-12-12`.
  - *Working choice:* a parameter this section sets so the draft is complete. It rests on judgment and can change without reopening a decision.
  - *Proposed:* a seam this dossier proposes to another dossier, which decides it.
- **Commitments and working assumptions.** This section commits to:
  - who gets status and on what path;
  - the form of the registry and what applicants pay;
  - the numbers and routes in legal immigration;
  - who decides asylum, and the refugee floor;
  - the conduct rules, the detention cap and the restructure;
  - the E-Verify mandate and its protections;
  - the eligibility table and the firewall;
  - USCIS's funding rule.

  Dates after enactment, numerical ceilings on the new work routes and every cost are working assumptions, with ranges in section 6.
- **Dates.** The plan begins in January 2029 (`DEC-2026-10-29-01`). The working timeline: the 2029 reconciliation act (R1) by about mid-2029; the FY2030 Homeland Security appropriations act by October 1, 2029, when the enforcement money of the 2025 and 2026 laws lapses [EV-IMM-035] [EV-IMM-039] [EV-IMM-040]; the regular-order immigration act signed by about December 2029. Preparation in 2027-28 is labeled as such (`5-delivery.md`, part 3).
- **Evidence.** The last column lists the evidence IDs a provision rests on, in this dossier's register or another's. "Project arithmetic" is `tools/immigration_path.py`.
- **Agenda homes.** The provisions sit under the six agenda items the memos name:
  - IMM-1: status and the registry (memos 1 and 2), with memo 6's table at IMM-1.20 to 1.36, because the legacy design put the benefit bar in IMM-1;
  - IMM-2: legal immigration and the workforce routes (memo 3);
  - IMM-3: asylum, the border and the court requirements (memo 4; point 7);
  - IMM-4: enforcement, the restructure and workplace verification (memo 5);
  - IMM-5: citizenship (points 10-12; memo 7 part 2);
  - IMM-6: USCIS and delivery (memo 7).

**Why this split.**
- *Reconciliation carries money, not rules without a budget effect* (the Byrd rule). The parliamentarian rejected legalization three times in 2021 (news account) [EV-IMM-238], so status goes to regular order. The 2025 and 2026 reconciliation laws carried immigration money and fees [EV-IMM-619] [EV-IMM-039], and the 2025 law's benefit limits were made by reconciliation [EV-IMM-046] to [EV-IMM-050], so their reversal can be too. The data firewall and the single plan's residency rule have no budget effect of their own and go in the immigration act.
- *Appropriations by October 1, 2029* because the reconciliation money for ICE and CBP lapses on September 30, 2029 [EV-IMM-035] [EV-IMM-040]. From then on, detention's size is an appropriations choice. The plan funds the cap and case management, and no third enforcement reconciliation law.
- *The restructure rides the immigration act,* not appropriations: it changes agencies' authorities and needs an organic statute.

## What the decisions changed

- **Memo 5 part 1: replace ICE, with A's rules.** Section 3 recommended A's rules inside ICE. Espen chose to restructure DHS and replace ICE, with no name committed. IMM-4.01 and 4.02 write the restructure; IMM-4.03 to 4.10 keep every rule A carried. Cost: about $3 billion over FY2030-39 for the transition and the inspector (section 6).
- **Memo 5 part 2: mandatory E-Verify, phased by size, starting only once the status programs pass the Inspector General's check, with B's enforcement.** Section 3 recommended no mandate. IMM-4.12 to 4.18 write the mandate with its start test, error correction and published counts, and keep B's labor enforcement and worker protection. On the general fund it nets to a small gain under CBO and JCT's conventions; Social Security's funds lose (section 6, part 2).
- **Point 1: the cutoffs** (`DEC-2026-11-12-07`, answered in `DEC-2026-11-12-12`): tier A on January 1 of the year of enactment, registration on the day the act is signed, tier B unchanged (IMM-1.04).
- **The E-Verify start** (`DEC-2026-11-12-12`): no earlier than October 1 of the year after enactment, on a passing Inspector General check repeated every 90 days, with 90-day waits before later tranches while errors for lawful noncitizens exceed 1% (IMM-4.13).
- **Point 7: wall money is redirected,** not rescinded. IMM-3.07 writes the uses and the conditions. Against CBO's baseline it saves nothing, where the rescission would have saved an unknown amount.
- **Everything else as recommended** (`DEC-2026-11-12-07`).

## Design lens

Each requirement the act adds, what it buys and what it costs in burden (`DEC-2026-11-09-13`):

| Requirement | Buys | Burden | Why kept |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ten years' residence for tier B (IMM-1.02) | The answer to "permanent amnesty": a long wait already served | None to show beyond records (IMM-1.06) | Decided (memo 1) |
| Biometrics and the criminal check (IMM-1.08) | Vetting every option shares | One visit; fewer bars than today's for immigration-only offenses | Decided (memo 2, point 2) |
| Statement and one document per gap year (IMM-1.06) | Reach to people with the least paper | One statement and one paper per missing year, only where records run out | Decided (memo 2) |
| The fee above 200% of poverty (IMM-1.09) | Fees at cost for those who can pay | A capped family fee, in instalments | Decided (memo 2) |
| The cutoffs (IMM-1.04) | No new draw once the law is known; the debate year does not reach the green-card-on-approval tier | People who arrive after them register (during the year of enactment) or wait for the registry's ten years | Decided (`DEC-2026-11-12-12`) |
| Tier A's work history (IMM-1.01, group 3) | The work route for farm, care and construction workers | Records first; where they run out, one statement and one document per gap, and a statement by an employer, co-worker, union or care recipient counts as the document | Decided (memo 1, change 3); the evidence rule is a working choice |

| Records of reasons for arrests outside the priorities (IMM-4.03) | Priorities that hold | A written reason, on officers, not on people | Decided (memo 5, A's rules) |
| The detention cap's waiver and report (IMM-4.05) | A cap that can flex in an emergency, in public | A report to Congress | Decided |
| Case management check-ins (IMM-4.06) | Appearance without a cell [EV-IMM-354] | Check-ins and hearings, with help to keep them | Decided |
| **E-Verify for every new hire (IMM-4.12)** | The trade Espen accepted for much wider status; fewer unauthorized hires [EV-IMM-399] [EV-IMM-402] | *On employers:* about 35 million more checks a year at full coverage (project arithmetic: queries were 34.9 million in FY2017 [EV-IMM-191], about half of new hires [EV-IMM-398]). Most checks cost little, but Cato's average with every notice rule followed is $147 a check [EV-IMM-614], up to about $5 billion a year at full coverage, an upper bound. *On workers:* a first error for about 0.2% of citizens and 0.7% of permanent residents at FY2009 rates [EV-IMM-391], 370,000 to 1.2 million native-born workers wrongly flagged at first under a national mandate on Cato's estimate [EV-IMM-613]. *It misses* about half of unauthorized workers it checks, mostly by borrowed identities [EV-IMM-393] | Decided (memo 5 part 2 A). Kept lighter by: new hires only; no fee; the start test (IMM-4.13); the newly legalized loaded first; error correction with a default to confirmation and lost wages paid (IMM-4.14); a 90-day wait before a later tranche while errors exceed 1% |
| The start check and its 90-day repeats (IMM-4.13) | An earliest date anyone can quote, and a mandate that cannot misfire on registrants | An Inspector General check every 90 days until it passes, on agencies only; no employer exemptions or appeals | Decided (`DEC-2026-11-12-12`) |
| Employer attestation for the work routes (IMM-2.12) | Wage floors that hold | One attestation; no tie to one employer | Decided (memo 3) |
| The labor agency's statement for deferred action (IMM-4.17) | Protection for workers who report abuse | One statement, by the agency, not the worker | Decided (memo 5, B's enforcement) |

Beyond these rows, nothing in the act adds a document, a fee, a test or a periodic report for people. It removes several: the 1972 date, the penalty, the document hunt, the asylum-pending fee and its first work permit charge, the five-year wait for the newly legalized, and status questions in the plan's universal programs.
