# Memo 5: Enforcement, detention and workplace verification

Role: decision memo for Espen, forks 8 and 9 of the immigration dossier (`../README.md`, "Forks for I3"; `../3-design.md`). Status: draft for decision (board row I3, question 0p); nothing here is decided. Sources are named in plain text; bracketed tags point to `../evidence.json`. Costs marked "judgment" are working assumptions, with their basis in `../3-design.md`, "What the options cost". Figures from the second Trump administration's agencies are its own data or claims (`DEC-2026-10-30-01`).

**Decision line.** Recommended: **part 1 A, IMM-4A: priorities, enforceable detention standards and a cap of 30,000 on average daily detention in statute, alternatives with case management as the default, protected places and officer identification; part 2 B, no E-Verify mandate: labor-standards enforcement and protection for workers who report abuse, with E-Verify kept voluntary.** Cost: about **zero net over FY2030-39 against CBO's baseline (range −$0.01 to +$0.01 trillion) and about zero in FY2040**: the cap's savings against the base detention appropriation (about $0.6-1.0 billion a year) pay for case management. Against a continuation of 2026 detention levels it saves about $3-5 billion a year, which CBO's baseline does not count. Evidence: official cost data; GAO audits; program evaluations; CBO and JCT's score of a mandate; quasi-experimental studies of state mandates.

**This memo decides how the interior system enforces the law after the plan's status programs (memos 1 and 2) and how workplaces are checked.** Memo 4 covers the border and the courts; memo 6 covers the data firewalls.

## The question

Enforcement money from two reconciliation laws runs until September 30, 2029 and then lapses together: $45 billion for detention and $29.85 billion for ICE operations from the 2025 law, and about $61 billion more for ICE, CBP and investigations from the June 2026 Secure America Act (Public Law 119-98) [EV-IMM-035] [EV-IMM-036] [EV-IMM-039] [EV-IMM-040]. So the plan's first nine months run on the old money, and **from October 1, 2029 the size of ICE and of detention is an appropriations choice**. A continuing resolution at FY2029's regular level would fund only the base: ICE's FY2027 request sustains 41,500 beds from its regular account [EV-IMM-362], against about 65,000-73,000 people held in 2026 [EV-IMM-171] [EV-IMM-172].

The legacy design (IMM-4) puts priorities, standards and a cap in statute and pairs legalization with mandatory E-Verify after registration closes (`legacy/domains/domain-immigration.md`, lines 85-105). Those are working choices, not Espen's decisions (`DEC-2026-10-30-02`).

## Why it matters

- **Most people held have no criminal conviction.** 70.6% of the 65,765 held on July 11, 2026 had none (TRAC, from ICE data) [EV-IMM-171]. Of deportations of people here two years or more, 71% had never been convicted (MPI) [EV-IMM-014].
- **Detention is at a record and so are deaths.** Thirty-seven people died in ICE custody in FY2026, the most on record (news tallies from ICE notices) [EV-IMM-179].
- **The local footprint grew.** 2,506 agreements with local police were signed by September 2026 (news, from ICE) [EV-IMM-180].
- **Workplace checks today.** About 1.39 million employers use E-Verify, and 22 states require it for some employers (SHRM, citing DHS) [EV-IMM-190].

## What the evidence says, in five lines

1. **Alternatives cost a few dollars a day against $137-238 for detention** (official cost data, three sources): ISAP at about $4 a person, family case management at $38.47 a family, family detention at $237.60 [EV-IMM-181] [EV-IMM-355] [EV-IMM-359].
2. **Case management, not monitoring alone, carries the appearance evidence** (official audits and program data): more than 99% of full-service participants with a hearing appeared (GAO) [EV-IMM-354]; families in case management complied 99.3% (advocacy program data) [EV-IMM-356]; but ICE's absconding figures leave groups out (GAO) [EV-IMM-353]. Moderate.
3. **E-Verify confirms about half of unauthorized workers it checks, mostly through borrowed identities** (program evaluation for USCIS) [EV-IMM-393], while its errors fall hardest on lawful noncitizens: 4.2% wrongly flagged against 0.2% of citizens [EV-IMM-391].
4. **A mandate moves work off the books** (official estimate): CBO and JCT put a national mandate at about $30 billion more in deficits over ten years, with payroll revenue down $88 billion [EV-IMM-394] [EV-IMM-395]. Cato, which opposes a mandate, estimates that 370,000 to 1.2 million native-born workers would be wrongly flagged at first under a national mandate [EV-IMM-613].
5. **State mandates cut unauthorized populations; the effect on other workers is contested** (quasi-experimental): fewer likely unauthorized workers in five mandate states (Dallas Fed) [EV-IMM-399]; no gain for competing legal workers in Arizona [EV-IMM-401]; gains for natives in another study [EV-IMM-402]. IRCA's sanctions led about one employer in ten to discriminate (GAO) [EV-IMM-237].

## Part 1. Interior enforcement and detention

**A. IMM-4A, conduct rules in statute inside ICE (the legacy choice; recommended).**
- **What it does.**
  - *Priorities:* national security, public safety and recent unlawful entry first, with a recorded reason for each arrest outside them.
  - *Detention:* enforceable standards that people held can sue on; a cap of 30,000 on average daily detention, with an emergency waiver reported to Congress; release to alternatives with case management as the default for people outside the priorities.
  - *Protected places:* no enforcement at schools, places of worship, hospitals or courthouses without a warrant or exigent circumstances.
  - *Officers:* visible identification and no masked arrests outside documented undercover work, one statute shared with the executive-power file (`domain-immigration.md`, line 91).
- *Where tried:* priorities by memo in 2014 and 2021, reversed each time; never in statute. Case management at scale in the Family Case Management Program (2016-17), ended after 1.5 years of five [EV-IMM-357].
- *For people:* a mother of two citizen children with 16 years here and no record is not arrested at the school gate; if she has a final order, she reports to a case manager, not a cell.
- *Cost:* the cap holds detention below the base account's 41,500 beds [EV-IMM-362]. 11,500 fewer beds at about $150 a day saves about $0.6 billion a year (project arithmetic on [EV-IMM-359]); counted against CBO's baseline, which extends the base account but not the lapsing reconciliation money, that is a small saving. Case management for about 100,000 people at about $20-40 a day costs about $0.7-1.5 billion a year (judgment, between ISAP and family case management [EV-IMM-355]). Net: about zero (range −$0.01 to +$0.01 trillion over FY2030-39).
- *Burden:* on ICE, a recorded reason and a release decision; on people released, check-ins and hearings, with case management to keep them.
- *Political risk:* the cap reads as "releasing people"; the answer is appearance rates under case management and a waiver for emergencies.

**B. IMM-4B, the DHS split.** Customs enforcement stays; interior enforcement is replaced by a Humanitarian Processing Service (Bill Book 15, folded into the legacy file as an option, line 104).
- *What it buys:* a clean break in culture.
- *What it costs:* a reorganization fight, years of transition while the plan's status programs need DHS working, and the hardest vote in the bill. No U.S. evidence shows a reorganization changes conduct more than rules and money do.

**C. IMM-4C, priorities by guidance only.**
- *What it buys:* nothing to pass; the President can issue it on January 20, 2029.
- *What it costs:* it is reversed by the next administration, as both earlier priority memos were. It fails the durability lesson of section 2, part F.

**D. Keep 2026 levels** (no cap; a third reconciliation law or appropriations at about 65,000-73,000 beds).
- *Cost:* about $3-5 billion a year above A (project arithmetic: 35,000-40,000 more beds at about $150-240 a day [EV-IMM-359] [EV-IMM-362]).
- *Evidence against:* most people held have no conviction [EV-IMM-171]; deaths are at a record [EV-IMM-179]; alternatives cost a few percent as much [EV-IMM-181].

**Within A, the cap's size.** 30,000 is the legacy number (estimate, `domain-immigration.md`, line 90). It sits below the base account's 41,500 beds [EV-IMM-362] and below 2026's level of about 65,000 [EV-IMM-171], and near the roughly 40,000 held a year before the 2025 surge [EV-IMM-172]. With legalization (memo 1) removing most long-settled residents from the enforcement pool, and priorities in law, 30,000 holds the priority cases. *What would change it:* a sustained rise in priority arrests above the cap for two years, reported under the waiver.

## Part 2. Workplace verification

**A. Mandatory E-Verify phased in by employer size after registration closes (the legacy pairing).**
- *What it buys:* the moderate wing's answer to "legalization invites the next wave" (`domain-immigration.md`, line 95); fewer unauthorized hires in mandate states [EV-IMM-399].
- *What it costs:* about $30 billion more in deficits over ten years on CBO and JCT's 2013 estimate, as work moves off the books [EV-IMM-394] [EV-IMM-395]; after a legalization the pool it targets is smaller, so the loss would be smaller too (judgment: about $0.01-0.03 trillion over FY2030-39). An average of $147 per check when employers follow every notice rule (Cato) [EV-IMM-614].
- *Burden:* a check on every hire. Its errors fall on lawful noncitizens, including the people the plan just legalized [EV-IMM-391], and it misses borrowed identities [EV-IMM-393].
- *Note:* under memo 2's standing route, "registration closes" no longer happens, so the legacy sequencing would need a date instead.

**B. No mandate: labor-standards enforcement, worker protection and voluntary E-Verify (recommended).**
- **What it does.**
  - Wage and Hour and OSHA inspections targeted at sectors with high rates of violations, funded in the agency rebuild's Wage and Hour line (legacy D9's agency list).
  - Protection from retaliation, including immigration retaliation, for any worker who reports a labor violation, with deferred action and a work permit while the case runs (the U and T visa model, made faster).
  - Employers who knowingly hire people without work authorization to undercut wages are penalized under current law; E-Verify stays voluntary and free, with the 2009 error rates as a reason not to mandate it.
- *What it buys:* it attacks the reason employers hire off the books (cheaper, more pliable labor) rather than the workers. Fewer than one employer in ten discriminating after IRCA's sanctions is the risk it avoids [EV-IMM-237].
- *Cost:* inside the agency rebuild's decided Wage and Hour line; no score against the baseline.
- *Political risk:* it drops the pairing the moderate wing wanted. The answer is memo 1's ten-year line, memo 4's surge rule and part 1's priorities: order is shown at the border and in enforcement, not by checking every hire.

**C. A narrow mandate: federal contractors and employers above a size, after a date, plus B.**
- *What it buys:* a visible pairing at lower burden than A.
- *What it costs:* the same error problem for lawful noncitizens in covered firms; a smaller off-the-books shift.
- *Fallback:* the version to offer if the pairing is the price of passage.

## Recommendation: part 1 A, part 2 B

- **Part 1 A changes conduct in law without a reorganization fight,** at about zero net cost against the baseline, because alternatives cost a few percent of detention [EV-IMM-181].
- **The lapse on September 30, 2029 is the moment.** The plan does not need a vote to cut detention: it needs FY2030 appropriations that fund the cap and case management, and no third enforcement reconciliation law.
- **Part 2 B follows the design lens** (`DEC-2026-11-09-13`): a check on every hire is a verification step whose burden falls on every worker and hardest on the lawful noncitizens the plan just legalized [EV-IMM-391], and its benefit is partial [EV-IMM-393].
- **What it costs in burden:** for part 1, records and release decisions on ICE; for part 2, nothing new on workers or employers. What it buys: fewer people held without cause, and enforcement aimed at abusive employers.

**What would change this:** for part 1, priority arrests exceeding the cap for two years (raise the cap by statute, not waiver); for part 2, a verification method that does not misfire on lawful workers and catches borrowed identities (a biometric or records-based check), or a vote count that needs the pairing (option C).

## If you decide

- **Under part 1 A:** section 4 writes the priorities, the standards and their private right of action, the cap and waiver, protected places, and the officer-identification statute (with the executive-power file); the FY2030 Homeland Security appropriations act funds the cap and case management (memo 7, the vehicle). The data firewall is memo 6's.
- **Under part 2 B:** the work flagship receives the labor-standards and anti-retaliation items (seam); E-Verify's authority, extended year by year in appropriations [EV-IMM-397], is renewed as a voluntary program.
- **Preparation before 2029:** a release and case-management contract ready for October 1, 2029; a census of detention contracts that end with the reconciliation money.
