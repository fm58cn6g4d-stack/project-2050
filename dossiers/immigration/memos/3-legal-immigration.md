# Memo 3: Legal immigration, the backlogs and the workforce visas

Role: decision memo for Espen, forks 4 and 5 of the immigration dossier (`../README.md`, "Forks for I3"; `../3-design.md`). Status: draft for decision (board row I3, question 0p); nothing here is decided. Sources are named in plain text; bracketed tags point to `../evidence.json`. Costs marked "judgment" are working assumptions, with their basis in `../3-design.md`, "What the options cost".

**Decision line.** Recommended: **part 1 B, the legacy IMM-2 (recapture, spouses and children of permanent residents outside the caps, per-country caps lifted over three years, H-1B ranked by wage with a fee in statute) plus the 2024 bipartisan text's higher numbers and protection for children who age out; part 2 B, portable sectoral routes for farm, care and construction work, each with a wage floor and a route to a green card, sized to the decided programs' demand.** Cost: about **a small saving, −$0.02 to −$0.06 trillion over FY2030-39 and about −$5-8 billion in FY2040 (judgment)**, from added workers' taxes; the H-1B fee (about $0.015 trillion) is retained by USCIS and a training fund, not the general fund. Evidence: official data on lost numbers and queues; CBO's scores of added immigration; one bill-scored sectoral route; no evaluation of a care visa.

**This memo decides how many people the legal system admits, how the queues are cleared, and whether the plan adds routes for the sectors its own programs depend on** (shared rule 8). Memo 7 decides who pays for the adjudication.

## The question

The numbers in the law were set in 1990: 140,000 employment green cards a year [EV-IMM-120], 226,000 family preference numbers and a 7% per-country ceiling [EV-IMM-121]. The queues are measured in decades: an Indian applicant in the second employment category waits for numbers from November 2013 [EV-IMM-128], a Mexican sibling of a citizen from April 2001 [EV-IMM-129]. Numbers are also lost: about 62,000 employment numbers went unused in FY2021 because USCIS lacked staff [EV-IMM-123].

At the same time the plan's decided programs need workers. Immigrants are 30% of direct care workers [EV-CAR-614] and 34% of construction trades workers [EV-HOU-055]. Care asked this flagship to keep current care workers able to stay and work, to open a portable care visa paid at least the $20 floor, and to count care in any shortage list (`DEC-2026-11-11-04`, point 8). Housing's supply estimates are conditional on the construction workforce (`dossiers/home/3-design.md`, fork 7).

## Why it matters

- **Recapture without capacity fails.** Numbers made available are lost when USCIS cannot use them [EV-IMM-123]; memo 7 sizes the staff.
- **Five countries take almost half of employment green cards** [EV-IMM-124], so the per-country cap keeps their queues decades long.
- **About 3.8 million people are on the family waiting list** (State, via NFAP) [EV-IMM-127].
- **The last bipartisan Senate text raised the numbers.** The 2024 border bill would have added 32,000 family and 18,000 employment numbers a year for five years [EV-IMM-535]. It failed cloture on other grounds [EV-IMM-532].

## What the evidence says, in five lines

1. **Added workers improve the federal budget** (official estimates): CBO put the 2021-24 surge at $0.9 trillion less deficit over 2024-2034 [EV-IMM-220]; most of S. 744's savings came from its added flows [EV-IMM-203].
2. **Effects on natives' wages are very small over ten years** (consensus review of both literatures) [EV-IMM-223]. This is the best-supported finding in the dossier.
3. **One sectoral route has been scored:** the Farm Workforce Modernization Act's certified worker status, about 300,000 workers and 200,000 dependents, with H-2A reform [EV-IMM-216]; passed the House 247-174 [EV-IMM-531].
4. **Temporary status without a route to permanence builds a lasting underclass** (official review): Australia's 1.8 million temporary workers and its warning against a "permanently temporary" class [EV-IMM-468].
5. **Planned levels by category can be adjusted** (international practice): Canada sets permanent targets three years ahead, 64% economic, and cut them when pressure rose [EV-IMM-450] [EV-IMM-451].

## Part 1. Legal immigration and the backlogs

**A. The legacy IMM-2 as written.**
- **What it does.** Recaptures unused numbers from past years; puts spouses and minor children of permanent residents outside the caps; lifts the employment per-country cap over three years; ranks H-1B petitions by offered wage instead of a lottery, with a statutory fee (about $5,000-10,000 by employer size) retained by USCIS and a training fund (`domain-immigration.md`, lines 61-71; ledger IF1).
- *For people:* a permanent resident's wife waiting since 2024 (F2A [EV-IMM-130]) joins her husband on approval.
- *Cost:* recapture and the cap exemption add people over the window; small saving (judgment, below). The fee: about $0.015 trillion (range $0.010-0.020 trillion), retained (`legacy-research.md`, section 1).
- *Against January 2029:* the $100,000 H-1B proclamation charge lapses in September 2027 and was struck down in one court [EV-IMM-056] [EV-IMM-057]; a fee in statute replaces it with one Congress authorized.

**B. A plus the 2024 bill's increases and an age-out fix (recommended).**
- **What it adds.** The 2024 text's 32,000 family and 18,000 employment numbers a year [EV-IMM-535], made permanent instead of five years; children of long-term visa holders who age out keep their place (the America's CHILDREN Act model [EV-IMM-518]).
- *Cost:* on CBO's surge estimate, roughly $10,000 of deficit reduction a year for each added person in a mostly working-age inflow (project arithmetic: $0.9 trillion over 2024-2034 for about 8.7 million added people, present on average for most of the window [EV-IMM-219] [EV-IMM-220]; spouses and children bring less, so the figure is discounted). Recapture, the cap exemption and the higher numbers together add perhaps 0.3-0.6 million people on average over the decade (judgment), so about −$0.02 to −$0.06 trillion over FY2030-39 and about −$5-8 billion in FY2040 (judgment).
- *Political risk:* "more immigration" is a hard line in 2029. The answer is that the numbers are the 2024 bipartisan text's, and the lost numbers were already authorized once.

**C. Capacity only.** Recapture lost numbers and staff USCIS to use them; leave the caps alone.
- *What it buys:* the cheapest step with the least vote risk.
- *What it costs:* the queues stay decades long; the per-country cap stays.

**D. Planned levels.** Congress sets a three-year plan by category on a commission's advice, as Canada does [EV-IMM-451].
- *What it buys:* room to adjust without new law each time.
- *What it costs:* a new institution and a yearly fight; no causal evaluation of Canada's system is in the register. Worth keeping as a later reform, not the first bill.

## Part 2. The workforce visas (shared rule 8)

**A. The legacy's two sectoral visas:** expanded farm visas with year-round eligibility and a care-worker visa (`domain-immigration.md`, line 69), with details left open.

**B. Portable sectoral routes for farm, care and construction (recommended).**
- **What it does.**
  - *Farm:* the Farm Workforce Modernization Act model: certified worker status for current farmworkers and H-2A reform [EV-IMM-522] [EV-IMM-523].
  - *Care:* a care-worker visa for direct care occupations, paid at least the care wage floor (`DEC-2026-11-11-04`, memo 14: $20 an hour in 2026 dollars, indexed), portable between employers in care, with a green card after three years of care work. Care occupations go on any shortage list.
  - *Construction:* a construction-worker visa on the same terms, paid at least the local prevailing wage, tied to the decided building programs (housing's production and repair, transportation's construction).
  - *Numbers:* set each year by the Labor Department from the decided programs' published hiring needs, with a cap and a review trigger, not by employer petition alone.
  - *Current workers* are covered by memo 1's tier A work-history route (care and construction, as well as farm work), which answers care's first ask.
- *For people:* a home health aide from the Philippines can change agencies when one cuts her hours, and is a permanent resident after three years.
- *Cost:* small saving inside part 1's range (judgment); the wage floors are the care and housing dossiers' decided costs, not added here.
- *Burden:* employer attestation of wage and job; the worker carries no tie to one employer.

**C. No new visas:** legalize current workers (memo 1) and raise general numbers (part 1); let the market sort the rest.
- *What it costs:* the plan's own hiring for care and construction, decided and funded, rests on a shrinking workforce (housing's fork 7 test).

**D. Guest-worker expansion without permanence** (H-2B-style, tied to one employer).
- *Evidence against:* Australia's warning [EV-IMM-468] and the TPS problem in another form; tied visas weaken the wage floors the care dossier decided.

## Recommendation: part 1 B, part 2 B

- **The queues are a capacity and a numbers problem, and both have bipartisan text.** Recapture and the 2024 numbers [EV-IMM-123] [EV-IMM-535].
- **The plan's own programs need the workers.** Care and housing have decided budgets that assume a workforce [EV-CAR-614] [EV-HOU-055]; part 2 B ties the routes to those programs, portable and paid at their floors.
- **It saves a little,** on CBO's estimates of added workers [EV-IMM-220], and does not lower natives' wages over ten years [EV-IMM-223].
- **What it costs in burden:** an employer attestation; the review trigger replaces petition-by-petition caps. What it buys: workers for decided programs and queues measured in years, not decades.

**What would change this:** a sharp fall in the decided programs' hiring needs (smaller part 2 numbers), or a downturn with high unemployment in care or construction (the review trigger pauses new visas).

## If you decide

- **Under B and B:** section 4 writes recapture, the cap exemption, the per-country phase-out, the higher numbers, the age-out rule, the H-1B ranking and fee, and the three sectoral routes with their floors and trigger. The work flagship receives the wage and portability rules (shared rule 8; LAB-8 apprenticeships stay there). Care and housing are told their asks are answered.
- **Preparation before 2029:** USCIS staffing to use recaptured numbers (memo 7); the Labor Department's method for counting the decided programs' hiring needs.
