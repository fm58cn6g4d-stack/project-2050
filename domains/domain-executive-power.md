# Domain file: Executive power (EXE)

Canonical. This file covers staging inventory section 5, which is pointers. It is the domain most changed by 2026: *Trump v. Slaughter* (June 29, 2026) overruled *Humphrey's Executor*, and *Learning Resources v. Trump* (Feb 20, 2026) held that IEEPA does not authorize tariffs. Rows that relied on for-cause removal protection are redesigned with structure or placement.

All figures are ten-year totals for 2030-39 in trillions. Basis tags: **sourced**, **scaled**, **estimate** (mine). Survival tags follow D1: ✔, ◐ (needs D2; F is decided), ✖.

## The two design rules this domain runs on

**1. Post-Slaughter: never rely on for-cause removal.** The Court held that the separation of powers prohibits Congress from requiring cause before the President removes a subordinate who exercises executive power. Every institution in this file is therefore built from one of four tools instead: placement clearly outside the executive branch; structural features (staggered terms, partisan balance, quorum rules); appropriations and reporting conditions; or a cause of action held by someone outside government. Where a row still wants removal protection, it carries an express fallback.

**2. Every constraint on the presidency needs a fast path.** A constraint with no quick route to approval breaks the first time it binds badly — the first genuine emergency with a hostile Congress, and the constraint is either ignored or repealed. Each row below that limits presidential action carries a short fast-track approval procedure with a short sunset and mandatory reporting (Instruments, Part III). This is also the reciprocity answer: these are rules a Democratic president must be willing to govern under from January 2029.

## EXE-1. National Emergencies Act reform

**Design.** Declared emergencies lapse automatically unless Congress affirmatively approves them within a set period, reversing the current default in which a declaration persists until Congress overcomes a veto. The fast path is express and short: privileged floor consideration, a hard clock, and an approval vote that cannot be buried in committee.

**Reciprocity.** This is the first row a Democratic president feels. The design rule above exists for exactly this: the fast path is what makes the constraint survivable rather than a thing the next administration simply ignores.

## EXE-2. War powers

**Design.** War powers restoration with a funding-cutoff default: absent authorization within the statutory window, appropriations for the operation lapse. The funding default is the enforcement mechanism that the 1973 resolution lacks, and it rests on Congress's clearest power rather than on a justiciability theory.

**Vehicle.** Merged into the executive-power bill under ledger cut C10.

## EXE-3. Anti-impoundment

**Design.** A statutory cause of action for the **intended recipient** — a state, grantee or contractor — rather than congressional standing, which *Raines v. Byrd* forecloses (Instruments #8). The provisions:

- Disbursement deadlines with deemed apportionment when they pass.
- Termination only for cause, with stated reasons.
- No rescission except by law.
- District-court jurisdiction with expedited review. **Note the forum problem:** *NIH v. APHA* split grant-termination claims between the Court of Federal Claims (contract-like) and district courts (APA-like). The statute must say which, expressly, or the first case is spent litigating the forum.
- No new grant conditions imposed after award.
- Third-party fiscal agents where the recipient is vulnerable to pressure.

**Second version folded in.** The Thriving Economy map's Chapter 9 "Trump-proofing" provisions are the same policy; they are options inside this row, not a separate item. The House can attach some of them as FY2028-29 appropriations provisions in the groundwork phase (staging 18.9).

**Why it matters most.** Of everything in this file, impoundment is the power whose abuse most directly threatens the rest of the agenda: every mandatory-spending program in the health, family and climate files depends on the money actually going out.

## EXE-4. Insurrection Act reform

**Design.** Defined triggers replacing the current open-ended language, judicial review of the predicate, and hard time limits with the fast path for extension.

## EXE-5. Inspector general independence

**Design.** Notice to Congress and stated reasons before removal, with a waiting period, enforced through appropriations conditions rather than for-cause protection. The old design — for-cause removal only — is Slaughter-exposed and is not available.

**Honest limit.** Notice-and-reasons does not prevent removal; it makes removal visible and costly. That is the most the post-Slaughter design space allows for an officer inside the executive branch, and the row should be described that way rather than as "independence."

## EXE-6. Vacancies Act

**Design.** Close the Federal Vacancies Reform Act workarounds: limit serial acting designations, bar acting service by officials whose nominations have been returned or withdrawn, and attach consequences to actions taken by improperly serving officials.

## EXE-7. OLC opinions and secret law

**Design.** Statutory publication requirement for OLC opinions, with narrow classified carve-outs and a reporting requirement for anything withheld. The executive version sits in EXE-17; the statute is what makes it durable (design rule 2).

## EXE-8. Pardon reform

**Design.** Disclosure requirements for clemency grants and an express bribery carve-out — bribery in exchange for a pardon is prosecutable as bribery, which is a statute about the bribe, not about the pardon.

**Limit.** Clemency is a constitutional power, so statutory limits on its exercise are exposed (my analysis; not drawn from the project's source docs). A self-pardon ban probably needs an amendment and is held at staging 17.7 with the immunity amendment.

## EXE-9. Independent agencies after Slaughter

**Design.** A cross-cutting template for rebuilding bipartisan commissions without removal protection:

1. Staggered terms, so no single president replaces a majority at once.
2. Statutory partisan-balance requirements.
3. Quorum rules that prevent a vacancy strategy from disabling the body.
4. Appropriations and reporting conditions.
5. Quasi-judicial functions moved to Article III or Article I adjudicators (the courts file's CRT-5 and CRT-12 are the same move).

**What it touches.** The FEC (domain-integrity.md INT-7), FERC and the NRC (the climate file treats an independent FERC as fundamental), the FCC (the information file), and the FTC.

**Honest assessment.** This template is weaker than what *Humphrey's Executor* provided. It raises the cost of capture without preventing it. Saying so is better than implying the old architecture can be rebuilt, and it strengthens the case for moving functions out of the executive entirely wherever that is possible.

## EXE-10. Special counsel independence

**Design.** Independence by structure: judicial appointment, or placement outside the executive, plus appropriations and reporting conditions — not for-cause removal, which Slaughter forecloses.

## EXE-11. Politicized-prosecution safeguards

**Design.** Give the DOJ White House contact policy statutory force: logged contacts, limited channels, reporting of deviations.

## EXE-12. Domestic deployment limits (shared statute)

**Design.** Limits on domestic military deployment and a Posse Comitatus clarification. **One statute, two homes:** it is Safe Everywhere plank 2 ("keep soldiers out of routine policing", domain-public-safety.md SAF-2) and this row. The public-safety revenue package does not count deployment savings as channel revenue. The public-safety file is canonical on the substance; this file carries the executive-power framing.

**Evidence note.** Niskanen found the Guard deployment in DC cut petty property crime but not violent crime — worth carrying in the record, because it is the strongest available counter to the deployment argument on its own terms.

## EXE-13. Federal law-enforcement identification

**Design.** Identification requirements for federal law-enforcement officers conducting operations, and a ban on unmarked detentions.

## EXE-14. Tariff authority

**Design.** With IEEPA out after *Learning Resources*, narrow the remaining delegations — Section 232 and Section 301 — with congressional-approval triggers for new or increased tariffs above a threshold.

**Revenue interaction, resolved.** After *Learning Resources*, current Section 232/301 tariffs are already in CBO's baseline, so keeping them raises no new revenue (ledger revenue row T1). Repeal or narrowing would be a new **claim** of roughly $1.5-1.9T (estimate). This row therefore has a fiscal cost that depends entirely on how far the narrowing reaches, and that cost is not in the ledger. **Flagged:** EXE-14 should be scored before it is drafted, or it will surprise the revenue file.

| Option | Design | Fiscal effect | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| EXE-14A (chosen) | Approval triggers for new or increased tariffs; existing tariffs untouched | ~0 | estimate |
| EXE-14B | Approval triggers plus sunset of existing sec. 232/301 actions | A claim of ~$1.5-1.9T | estimate |
| EXE-14C | Procedural requirements only (findings, comment, reporting) | ~0; weakest constraint | estimate |

**Why A.** B is the better separation-of-powers policy and an unfunded $1.5-1.9T hole. The plan's general-fund claims run $4.60T above its base, and accepted revenue and envelope borrowing close that only to about −$0.08T over ten years, so B would leave the plan $1.5-1.9T short again. If B is wanted, it needs a pay-for named at the same time.

## EXE-15. Succession and continuity

**Design.** Presidential Succession Act reform (the current line of succession puts legislative officers in the line, with known constitutional objections), clarified 25th Amendment sec. 4 procedure, and continuity-of-government provisions for mass-casualty events — the piece that is genuinely absent from current law and that no one works on until it is needed.

## EXE-16. Presidential immunity

**Design.** Statutory pieces only: evidentiary procedures, strengthened inspectors general, and criminal statutes that expressly reach presidential conduct. Congress cannot overrule *Trump v. United States* by statute. The amendment (the 2024 "No One Is Above the Law" proposal) is held at staging 17.7, to be used only if the decision proves unworkable in practice.

## EXE-17. Day-one executive bridge

**Design.** Pre-drafted in opposition during 2027-28, signed in week one of 2029:

- Rescind Schedule Policy/Career placements (the rebranded Schedule F; OPM's final rule published Feb 6, 2026, effective March 2026, about 50,000 positions or 2% of the workforce, placed by executive order — CRS LSB11412, Federal Register). The statutory prohibition is in domain-capacity.md (CAP-8).
- Publish OLC opinions (EXE-7's executive version).
- Restore OMB ecosystem-valuation guidance.
- Finalize the OSHA heat rule (the climate file's statutory version).
- Reinstate the BLM Public Lands Rule.

**Why it is a bridge and not a substitute.** Every item here is reversible by the next president by the same instrument. Each one is paired above with the statute that makes it durable, per design rule 2. The bridge's value is the eighteen months between inauguration and enactment, which is the window in which the agenda's early credibility is set.

**Survival.** ✔ at 51 — it needs no votes at all.

## EXE-18. Payment continuity

**Design.** The statutory form of the governing doc's design rule "payments do not stop": **every recurring payment carries standing disbursement authority that does not lapse in a shutdown.**

- **In each program's own statute** (✔, rides the program's reconciliation bill): a permanent, indefinite appropriation for the payment and express authority for Treasury and the paying agency to disburse during a lapse in appropriations, with the staff who make the payments designated as excepted. This covers FAM-1's monthly advance payments (which also need express advance-payment authority), the Climate Dividend (CLI-2, whose dividend account already has this drafting note), EDU-3's meal reimbursements, paid-leave benefits (FAM-3), LAB-9 stipends and SS and Medicare changes (already permanent).
- **A general provision** (◐, rides the EXE bill): any mandatory payment program's administrative staff are excepted in a lapse by operation of law, so that a payment with money cannot stop for want of people to send it.
- **Why.** In the Talarico model, the drafting choice that let payments continue is what made a shutdown fail as leverage. It is also design rule 2 (durable instruments) applied to the plumbing: a program a hostile Congress cannot interrupt without changing the law.
