Include household bills and real delivery constraints; no unscored climate saving or reallocation is adopted. Labor capacity and remedies for failed delivery are part of policy design.

Canonical domain file. It covers clean-energy credits, the delivery side of the carbon dividend, the Clean Energy Standard, fixes to the stipulated 2026-28 permitting law, Clean Air Act restoration, the Thriving Economy reconciliation add-ons, grid and utility rules, and disaster resilience and climate risk. It replaces staging inventory rows 9.7, 10.3 (delivery side only), 10.5 and 14.1-14.10, and ledger rows E1, E3, E4 and E5 on substance. The carbon fee and border adjustment themselves stay in domains/domain-revenue.md (REV-C1, REV-C2) under Dec. D7.

**Decisions that govern this file.** (1) **Carbon (D7):** REV-C1 is $25 a ton in 2030 rising to about $100 by 2037, with gasoline and diesel exempt. The channel is about $1.45T in, $1.16T dividend (equal per adult), $0.29T transition (estimates set from index/climate-carbon-model.md, Oct 21; domain-revenue.md governs). The general fund books a 25% income and payroll tax offset on the carbon fees (CX). Road-fuel convergence (REV-C1D) is an open option. Survival at 51 (Dec. D1): ✔ survives, ◐ needs a D2 majority-precedent path, ✖ needs 60, 67 or Republican votes.

## Where things stand (world state, Oct 2026)

- **Credits.** OBBBA (July 2025) ended 25C and 25D after Dec 31, 2025, 30D, 25E and 45W after Sept 30, 2025, and 30C after June 30, 2026. Wind and solar lose 45Y and 48E unless construction began by July 4, 2026 or the project is in service by Dec 31, 2027. Foreign-entity restrictions apply from 2026 (Novogradac). By January 2029 the household and vehicle credits will have been gone for three years, and new wind and solar starts will have had no credit for about two years.
- **Clean Air Act.** EPA finalized repeal of the 2009 endangerment finding and all federal vehicle greenhouse-gas standards on Feb 12, 2026. Two challenges are pending in the D.C. Circuit (Nos. 26-1037 and 26-1061, the second led by New York, Massachusetts, California and Connecticut with about 40 parties), with reconsideration petitions before EPA (Jones Day, June 2026).
- **Permitting.** Stipulated (Dec. D1): AAJA is enacted in 2026-28. Its Sec. 1401 bars actions that "interfere with" a permitted project's "operation at full capacity," and Secs. 1401-1402 pay damages from the Judgment Fund without appropriation. Its transmission planning arrives around late 2028, so a Democratic FERC approves the first interregional plans in 2029.
- **Emissions path.** After OBBBA, about 26-35% below 2005 by 2035 (Rhodium, as cited in the permitting analysis). The analysis estimates a 2029 climate law reaches about four-fifths of its 2035 potential with AAJA and about half without it; these are illustrative estimates, not model runs. The durability session's sector model lands in the same place: its "no legislated permitting title" case reaches −50% by 2035 against −62% to −64% with it (session estimate).

## CLI-1. Clean-energy credits restored

**Design.** Restore what OBBBA repealed and make every credit reach people who owe little tax:
- **Households:** 25C (efficiency, including heat pumps) and 25D (rooftop solar and batteries), refundable and delivered at the point of sale.
- **Vehicles:** 30D (new EVs) and 25E (used EVs) with transfer to the dealer at sale; 45W for commercial vehicles; 30C for charging.
- **Power:** 45Y and 48E restored for wind and solar on the IRA's schedule; the Clean Electricity Investment Credit extended with domestic-content and labor-standard bonuses; direct pay and transferability kept.
- **Industry:** 48C allocation restored.

Long-dated (through 2039 at least) with no cliffs inside the window, so the credits carry a demand signal through the decade. Foreign-entity rules stay, written against named countries of concern rather than broad "material assistance" tests that block financing.

**Cost.** About $0.9T, range $0.6T to $1.1T.
- **Restoration alone:** CRFB put the House-passed version of OBBBA's credit repeals at $571B for 2025-34; scaled to 2030-39 that is about **$0.70T** (scaled). The enacted law differed from the House version, so this needs a fresh score.
- **Expansions** (the CEIC extension, point-of-sale delivery and refundability) add about $0.2T (estimate).
- Uptake risk runs both ways. The IRA credits came in well above their 2022 scores, which is why the high end is $1.1T.

**Second version folded here.** Staging inventory 14.9 put the transmission ITC and the 45X expansions in this row (ledger E1), and 14.10 put them in the add-ons too (ledger E4). They live once, in CLI-5. CLI-1 covers restoration and the CEIC only. If E1's $0.9T already counted them, CLI-1's midpoint is overstated by up to about $0.2T (estimate). That should be checked when the row is rescored.

## CLI-2. The Climate Dividend (delivery of REV-C1)

**What this file owns.** The fee itself is REV-C1 in domains/domain-revenue.md: $25 a ton in 2030 rising to about $100 by 2037 (D7), upstream on coal, natural gas and non-transport petroleum, with gasoline and diesel exempt under Dec. D7. It raises $1.25-1.60T (mid $1.45T, estimate). The lower starting price makes "paid first" easier: the first year's dividend is large relative to the first year's price effect. This file owns how the 80% comes back.

**Design.**
- **Equal per adult, quarterly, named.** Every adult (18 and over) gets one equal share, whatever their income or carbon use; a joint return carries two. Adult dependents (college students, disabled adult children, elderly parents) are counted. Children get no share: FAM-1's child credit ($3,000-3,600 a child) is the child benefit, roughly ten times what a child share would be, and the dividend's weight goes instead to the adults the credit does not reach (single adults, retirees, childless couples) (Espen, Oct 21). It is called the Climate Dividend in statute, and every payment carries the name (legibility rule).
- **Delivery, on the 2020-21 stimulus-payment model.** Paid through the tax system to filers, automatically to Social Security, SSI, VA and Railroad Retirement beneficiaries from their agencies' payment records, and through a simple non-filer sign-up for everyone else.
- **Paid first.** The first payment goes out in early 2030, before the fee reaches retail prices. People see the check before the cost.
- **Channel.** The dividend is paid by the IRS to filers and by SSA to beneficiaries, with an outreach path for non-filers on the Child Tax Credit model. The same IRS build delivers FAM-1's monthly payments.
- **Transition share (20%, about $0.29T).** This goes to coal and energy communities and to low-income energy help. It co-finances CLI-5's LIHEAP and Weatherization lines (see CLI-5).

**Drafting note (durability session and Talarico scenario; EXE-18).** Both reviews converge on the same instrument detail, which the payment-continuity rule requires. The dividend is paid from a dividend account with a **permanent, indefinite appropriation** (the account passes the trust-fund rule: a permanent benefit with its own revenue) and Treasury's **standing disbursement authority**, so neither a lapse in appropriations nor a hostile Congress can stop a payment without changing the law. In the Talarico model that drafting choice is what made a shutdown fail. The session also pre-funds the first payment and repays it from fee receipts; CLI-2A already pays first, so that is a mechanics note, not a new option.

**Emissions and dividend by year.** index/climate-carbon-model.md runs the structural carbon model on D7 with CLI-1 and CLI-4 layered on: energy CO2 below 2005 by sector to 2050, the dividend per adun by year, and what each companion measure adds.

## CLI-3. Clean Energy Standard and permitting-law fixes

**Design: the standard.** The standard requires 80% clean electricity by 2035 (Bill Book 09's target), counting renewables, nuclear, hydro, geothermal and plants with carbon capture at their captured share. It works through tradable credits retail suppliers must hold. It is written as statute with an express schedule, not delegated to EPA or FERC, so it does not rest on agency discretion after *Loper Bright* and *West Virginia v. EPA*.
- **Alternative compliance payment.** Suppliers can pay a capped amount per missing credit. The cap limits rate shocks, and the payments fund CLI-5's low-income energy lines. The revenue is unsized; it should be its own small channel if it is kept.
- **Starting point.** Carbon-free generation was roughly 40% of U.S. power in 2024 (EIA order of magnitude; not re-checked this session). The durability session puts the 2025 clean share at about 43% (Ember). 80% by 2035 is a steep path from a 2030 start, which is why credits, transmission and the permitting law all matter.

**Design: the permitting-law fixes.** These follow the permitting analysis's recommendations. None is Byrd-eligible, so they need the floor:
1. **Sec. 1401 override.** Emissions limits and compliance obligations under this law or the Clean Air Act are not actions that "amend or alter" a permit. Without this, a CES or CLI-4's power-sector standard invites suits to protect any fossil plant's "operation at full capacity."
2. **Judgment Fund damages cap.** Cap Sec. 1401-1402 damages per project and per year, and require appropriations above the cap. AAJA's uncapped exposure (25-50% of sunk costs; 50-100% of construction cost for a pattern) makes any fossil-permit action after 2029 a contingent general-fund cost. The cap bounds it.
3. **The 401 fix.** Drop the "direct point source discharge" limit for pipelines and transmission and the clear-and-convincing burden, and keep AAJA's deadlines.
4. **Rail fixes.** Extend the ESA right-of-way exemption to FRA programs (49 U.S.C. chapters 229 and 243), clarify that FRA grant agreements count as "predominant control" or are excluded consistently with FTA, and add intercity rail to Sec. 1402's listed project types. This is the one place the climate and transportation files must agree, and the ledger's open conflict row on it closes here.
5. **Option, not chosen:** cap Sec. 2102(b)'s pipeline looping and compression exemption by capacity added, or require an EA above a threshold.

**Cost.** About $0.02T, range $0 to $0.05T (estimate): CES administration at EIA and FERC and the 401 and ESA staff. The damages cap reduces a contingent liability that no row currently counts.

## CLI-4. Clean Air Act restoration

**Design.** Congress makes the endangerment finding itself: greenhouse gases are air pollutants that endanger public health and welfare. It then directs EPA in express terms:
- **Vehicles (sec. 202):** set greenhouse-gas standards for vehicles and engines.
- **Power plants (sec. 111):** set them for power plants, expressly allowing standards based on generation shifting and averaging. That is the delegation *West Virginia v. EPA* (2022) found missing.
- **Override:** the Sec. 1401 override in CLI-3 covers both.

It rides CLI-3's bill and adds no floor days (estimate).

**Why statute.** The D.C. Circuit cases may restore the 2009 finding, but a win sends the question back to an agency a later administration can redirect, and the Supreme Court would likely take the case. A statutory finding ends the cycle. This follows design rule 7 and CRT-8's express-delegation templates (domain-courts.md).

Both add drafting weight to CLI-3's floor bill but no floor days on the ledger's convention (estimate). D also deepens the AAJA problem: Sec. 1401's "operation at full capacity" language makes CLI-3's override a precondition for any power-sector limit, caps included. **Open:** how far Sec. 1401 still constrains statutory caps even with the override (session open question).

## CLI-6. Grid and utility statute

**Design.** This bill covers what AAJA leaves out. Most of the map's grid rules are already in the stipulated law: FERC backstop siting, Order 1920 compliance, 20-year consolidated planning with fixed zonal costs, and the §228 data-center no-subsidy floor for new loads of 20 MW or more.
- **Interregional transfer minimums:** a statutory minimum transfer capability between regions, from NERC's transfer-capability study, with FERC enforcement. AAJA requires common planning assumptions but no fixed horizon and no fallback if regions never file.
- **Performance-based ratemaking:** a PURPA sec. 111(d) standard states must consider, with shared-savings incentives for grid-enhancing technologies. AAJA already requires them where the benefit-cost ratio exceeds one.
- **Utility accountability:** no ratepayer-funded lobbying or advertising; FERC review of whether allowed returns exceed what is needed; stronger discovery for state commissions.
- **RTO governance:** minimum governance and transparency rules, with intervenor funding.
- **Large loads:** extend the §228 floor to *new incremental service* at existing large loads, closing AAJA's grandfather gap. Clarify that §228 reaches ERCOT utilities.
- **Distribution cyber floor:** mandatory standards for distribution utilities, aggregated DERs and inverters, with funding for small co-ops.

**After *Slaughter*.** FERC commissioners are now removable at will (staging 5.16; EXE-9), so a later administration can redirect FERC quickly. This bill therefore writes standards into statute rather than delegating them to FERC's discretion, wherever the standard can be stated in advance.

**Cost.** About zero (estimate); FERC is fee-funded through annual charges.

## CLI-7. Disaster response and flood insurance

**Design.** This takes the map's counteroffer to the FEMA Review Council (final report May 2026):
- **FEMA:** an independent, cabinet-level agency with Senate-confirmed leadership and its own inspector general.
- **Mitigation:** a set-aside of $1 for every $1 of disaster supplemental.
- **Recovery:** estimate-based Public Assistance, a Universal Survivor Application and a permanent CDBG-DR authorization.
- **NFIP:** a multi-year reauthorization. It adds means-tested premium support for low- and moderate-income primary residences (not rate caps), future-conditions flood mapping, and authority to restructure the program's roughly $22.5B debt to Treasury (map).
- **Insurance affordability:** the Climate Insurance and Recovery Affordability Program for low- and moderate-income owners and renters (staging 14.2).

**Sequence.** Try first in 2027-28 at 60 votes; FEMA reform and NFIP are must-pass vehicles there (staging 18.10). Whatever fails returns as a first-Congress floor bill with CLI-8.

**Cost: unscored, and not in any total.**
- **Mitigation set-aside:** its size depends on future supplementals.
- **Premium support:** this is a permanent program, so D4 requires a pay-for.
- **NFIP debt:** writing it off costs about $0.02T once (scaled from the map's $22.5B). Under budget scoring that is probably near zero, since CBO's baseline already expects little repayment (my reading; to check).
- **Rough order of magnitude:** $0.05-0.15T over 2030-39 (estimate), most of it premium support and the set-aside. The durability session sizes adaptation, reinsurance and resilience together at about $0.20T (session estimate), which brackets the high end.

**This row needs a score; until it has one it is in no total.**

**@51.** ◐. The insurance affordability program and premium support are spending and could move to R3 instead (✔). That is option CLI-7B.

**Option CLI-7C (durability session and Talarico scenario): a federal reinsurance backstop tied to codes.** A federal facility reinsuring residential property catastrophe risk above a high attachment point, available only in states that adopt CLI-8's hazard-resistant codes and risk disclosure. Both reviews point here for the same reason: the Talarico model's 2034 insurance collapse (state insurers of last resort insolvent, reinsurers leaving the Gulf, mortgage origination stalling) is what produced a bipartisan adaptation consensus there, "not persuasion; the mortgage market." **The fiscal risk is off-score:** a facility of the Talarico model's size carries about $400B of contingent exposure, and one bad season can draw $150-200B in a year (scenario figures, not estimates of this design). Under D4 that exposure needs a stated treatment before CLI-7C is chosen: premiums priced to expected loss with a Treasury line of credit repaid from later premiums (the FAM-3 pattern), not an open general-fund backstop.

## CLI-8. Climate risk and where federal money builds

**Design.**
- **Floodplain standard:** a forward-looking federal floodplain standard in statute, the Federal Flood Risk Management Standard codified.
- **Disclosure:** climate-risk disclosure at sale or lease, on the lead-paint model.
- **Underwriting:** forward-looking risk in GSE, FHA, VA and USDA underwriting, with anti-redlining guardrails.
- **Codes:** hazard-resistant code baselines as a condition of new federal housing and infrastructure aid.
- **Data:** a national property-insurance data partnership.

**Not chosen as baseline:** the map's extreme-risk ineligibility zones (no new federal funding, permits or backed mortgages in extreme-risk areas). The map itself calls it "politically hard even for Democrats in coastal and Western seats." It is option CLI-8B.

**Option CLI-8C (durability session): electric-ready lending.** All-electric or electric-ready construction as a condition of new Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac and FHA mortgages from about 2032 (roughly 6-7 million homes by 2036, session estimate), plus funding for utilities to electrify whole blocks instead of replacing aging gas pipe. Same lever as CLI-8's underwriting bullet; it is the buildings half of the session's lock-in program.

**Federalism.** Code conditions attach to *new* aid only, so they stay clear of the *NFIB* coercion line (staging 2.6).

**Cost.** About zero (estimate).

## CLI-9 to CLI-13. Option rows from the durability session

Not chosen; not in any total. Each is written so it could be taken up without disturbing a chosen row.

**CLI-9. Contracts for difference and advance purchase commitments.** Federal contracts that pay the gap between a strike price and the market for firm clean power, and advance purchase commitments with volume targets for clean steel, cement and hydrogen, timed to the rebuild dates of the roughly dozen active blast furnaces and kiln replacement cycles. A **reactor fleet order** on France's Messmer model: one federal contract (through TVA, public power or a contract for difference) for 20-30 reactors of one or two proven designs, signed by 2034.
- *Insulation:* fully funded at enactment under contract authority; an unpaid contractor sues and is paid from the Judgment Fund, a permanent appropriation, so a hostile Congress cannot stop payment by inaction. AAJA already uses the same tool to protect permitted fossil projects.
- *Cost:* about $0.15T net in-window (session estimate). *Fiscal treatment (Decision Brief 2):* if taken, the contracts are scored at full expected net cost when obligated, as borrowing-eligible climate capital inside the D4-B envelope, so the Judgment Fund backing adds no new cost. AAJA's fossil-side damages are a disclosed contingent-liability line, bounded by CLI-3's cap.
- *Vehicle:* R2 (budget authority and outlays). ✔

**CLI-10. Public power as builder of last resort.** Large new borrowing authority for TVA, Bonneville and WAPA, and a TVA-model federal transmission corporation that builds where no regional builder exists, using existing staff, balance sheets and eminent domain. This is the climate analogue of the Talarico scenario's housing finding: conditional grants fail in exactly the places that most need the thing built, and an institution that builds is the remedy.
- *Cost:* scored at subsidy cost inside the session's ~$0.10T lending-and-equity line; balance-sheet commitments across CLI-10, CLI-11 and REV-E2 of about $1T or more (session estimate).
- *Vehicle:* floor (new borrowing authority and a new corporation). ◐

**CLI-11. Coal securitization and worker guarantees.** Treasury buys low-rate, ratepayer-backed bonds that refinance remaining coal-plant book value, so every coal unit retires by 2032 (the Colorado and New Mexico securitization models), with guaranteed pensions and health care for coal and power-plant workers and a mandatory energy-communities fund. Retired plants are demolished or converted, often to storage on the existing interconnection, so they cannot be mothballed and restarted. New gas plants are capture-ready with a statutory capture date, or carry a retirement date. **Overlap:** the worker guarantees and energy-communities fund (~$0.08T) serve the same people as CLI-2's 20% transition share (~$0.27T, coal and energy communities plus low-income energy help). If CLI-11 is taken, its worker piece should be drawn from the transition share first, so the two are not both counted as new money; index/options-register.md carries the pair.
- *Cost:* lending at subsidy cost (inside the ~$0.10T line); workers and communities about $0.08T (session estimate), which overlaps CLI-2's transition share. Co-ops and public power need a direct-pay path.
- *Vehicle:* R2. ✔

**CLI-12. State redundancy and payment for results.** Three pieces that keep the transition moving if federal agencies are redirected: codified state authority to exceed federal standards (including vehicles, independent of an EPA waiver); congressional consent in advance to interstate carbon-market compacts; and multi-year formula grants plus payments per ton cut or per MWh added above a baseline, which states and utilities implement without new federal staff.
- *Cost:* about $0.15T for the payments (session estimate). *Vehicle:* authority and consent need the floor (◐); payments can ride R3 (✔).

**CLI-13. Farms, forests and carbon removal.** Mandatory USDA conservation funding for methane and soil carbon; wildfire risk reduction and reforestation; and a federal carbon-removal purchase program by reverse auction, rising to about 300 Mt a year by 2045. **Conditioning (Talarico finding):** agricultural transition payments are conditioned on enteric methane, nitrogen efficiency and manure systems. In the Talarico model, unconditioned agriculture reaches only −22% by 2050 and becomes a third of residual emissions; conditioning gets it to −41% (scenario figures) and is politically costly in farm states, which is why it belongs in a bill with large farm payments rather than alone.
- *Cost:* about $0.08T (overlaps CLI-5's ag line, so not additive in full) plus about $0.03T of removal purchases in-window, growing after 2038 (session estimates).
- *Vehicle:* R3. ✔

## Durability: insulation from a hostile Congress and lock-in before 2037

The durability session's stress scenario is harsher than Dec. D1: a trifecta only in 2029-30, then a lost chamber and no trifecta before 2037. D1 stipulates a Democratic Senate through 2036 and a House regained for 2033-36, so a plan that survives the session's case survives D1's. Its toolkit applies across the project, not only to climate.

**Where the money sits.** Nearly all of the plan's money can be placed beyond a hostile Congress until 2037, because changing enacted law then needs the president's signature.

| Tool | Best used for | Residual risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tax credits in permanent law | CLI-1, CLI-5's credits | Essentially none until 2037 |
| Mandatory spending, available until expended | Grants, climate bank (CLI-5E), state formula funds (CLI-12), energy-communities fund (CLI-11) | Riders in must-pass bills |
| A dividend account with a permanent appropriation | CLI-2 | Very low |
| Contract authority plus the Judgment Fund | CLI-9, long-term transmission capacity | Low |
| Borrowing authority | CLI-10, DOE loans | Low |
| Fee-funded operations | EPA's climate office, compliance registries, FERC and NRC permitting staff | Low; structurally like the CFPB funding the Supreme Court upheld in 2024 |

**What stays exposed, and the cross-domain fixes.** Must-pass leverage (appropriations riders, shutdowns, the debt limit) and Senate confirmations. Proposed for the files that own them, not decided here:
- **Anti-impoundment terms** on every grant program (termination only for cause, deemed apportionment, state and grantee standing to sue): already EXE-3.
- **An automatic continuing resolution at prior-year levels, and repeal or long suspension of the debt limit:** domain-capacity.md CAP-4 (the automatic CR) and domain-executive-power.md EXE-18C (the debt limit), both companions to EXE-18's payment continuity.
- **Confirmations front-loaded in 2029-30**, new judgeships created in 2029, and commissions (FERC, NRC) able to act with reduced quorums: flagged to domain-courts.md (CRT-P1's judgeships) and domain-executive-power.md.

**Final judgments, not just final rules, before 2037.** The Congressional Review Act lookback for a Congress starting January 2037 reaches back to about summer 2036, but the bigger risk is a 2037 administration declining to defend rules still in litigation, as in 2025. So: caps, credit formulas, fleet standards, fee rates and dividend mechanics are self-executing in statute (CLI-4D, CLI-4E); any rule still needed runs on a 12-24 month statutory deadline with a default standard if missed, pre-drafted by transition teams in 2027-28; challenges are channeled under CLI-4F; and no major new rules are started after 2034 except updates the statute requires.

**Lock-in, 2032-36 (a second-trifecta program under D1).** The session's 2032-36 measures raise the 2045 floor under a 2037 repeal from about 75% to about 85% below 2005 (session estimate). Under D1 the 2033-36 trifecta can carry them through reconciliation (FY2033-36 resolutions) without a 2033 regular-order queue:
- 400 GW or more under construction in January 2037 (CLI-1E's start-of-construction rule);
- the reactor fleet order signed by 2034 and industrial contracts timed to furnace rebuilds (CLI-9);
- about a third of homes changing heating systems in 2032-36 through point-of-sale rebates and statutory appliance standards, and electric-ready lending (CLI-8C);
- at least half of households with a direct stake beyond the dividend by 2036 (EV, heat-pump, rooftop, plug-in or community solar owners), plus co-op and community solar, employee ownership in new factories and retail climate savings bonds;
- carbon-club agreements under which REV-C1 counts against the EU's border charge, so repeal would immediately cost U.S. exporters (REV-C2);
- grants rewarding states that write matching targets into state law by 2034 (CLI-12).

The binding risk is execution in 2030-33: nearly every lock-in measure depends on hitting the 2032 milestones, which is why day-one delivery channels, the pre-built pipeline and the workforce program (LAB-8; interstate electrician license reciprocity) matter as much as the headline policies. Under the 2020 DACA decision, an agency rescinding a policy must weigh the reliance interests built on it; every contract, factory, loan and household investment by 2036 strengthens that record.
