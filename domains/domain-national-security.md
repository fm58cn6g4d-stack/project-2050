# Domain file: National security and international affairs (NSC)

Canonical (batch 4). This file covers the defense envelope, alliances, diplomatic and development capacity, sanctions, trade agreements, technology competition and export controls, pandemic preparedness, climate finance, nuclear arms control and veterans. On substance it replaces staging inventory section 16, ledger row F6 (Bill Book 26, which had no domain home) and the national-security half of ledger A2. War powers, emergencies and tariff authority stay in domain-executive-power.md (EXE), where batch 2 put them (ledger DEM8 with DEM9 merged under C10). The USAID successor's architecture stays in domain-capacity.md CAP-10. Both are referenced here, not restated.

The old inventory called this "a second agenda" and kept it thin by design. This file keeps it thin. It sets the envelope, assigns the pieces that touch the domestic agenda, and names what the first Congress must do. Grand strategy is out of scope.

All figures are ten-year totals for 2030-39 in trillions. Basis tags: **sourced**, **scaled**, **estimate** (mine). Survival at 51 (Dec. D1): ✔, ◐, ✖.

## Where things stand (world state, Oct 2026)

- **Defense budget.** The FY2027 request (Apr 3, 2026) asked for **$1.5T**:
  - $1.15T in base discretionary funding, about a 24% real increase over FY2026;
  - $350B in mandatory funding through a reconciliation bill.

  Its own out-year projection has national defense falling about 16% in real terms from FY2027 to FY2028 (CSIS, Apr 10, 2026). The House Armed Services Committee's mark authorized about $1.15T without the $350B. A continuing resolution funds the government through **Dec 11, 2026** (Akin, Sept 2026).
- **Iran.** A war with Iran began in about March 2026 (inferred from reporting of "more than three months of war"). It ended in a June 15, 2026 agreement to end hostilities and reopen the Strait of Hormuz (NPR member stations, June 15, 2026). The Senate repeatedly failed to advance war-powers resolutions during the fighting (ABC, May 2026). This is the live case for EXE's war-powers row.
- **Development.** The State Department formally shut USAID in 2025 and absorbed its remaining functions (ABC; date from my knowledge, July 1, 2025).
- **WHO.** The United States' withdrawal took effect in January 2026, one year after notice (my knowledge; not re-checked).
- **Arms control.** New START expired in February 2026 (my knowledge; not re-checked).
- **NATO.** Allies agreed at The Hague in June 2025 to a 5% of GDP target (3.5% core defense plus 1.5% related spending) by 2035 (my knowledge). The FY2024 NDAA bars withdrawal from NATO without Senate advice and consent or an act of Congress (my knowledge; not re-checked).

## NSC-1. Defense envelope

**Design.** D9 sets a direction: defense "roughly flat in real terms," with any increase paid for inside the defense budget. This file adds the one definition D9 needs.

- **The base is FY2029 enacted national-defense discretionary budget authority (function 050), excluding any one-time reconciliation money.** Flat real means that base grown with the defense deflator through FY2039.
- **Why it matters.**
  - If FY2027 or FY2028 enacts the $350B reconciliation request, that money is mandatory and time-limited. CBO's baseline does not extend it, so it must not be read into "flat."
  - If FY2027's base lands near $1.15T and FY2028 falls as the request projects, a 2029 Congress inherits a base set by two hostile budget fights. The flat-real rule then locks in whatever those fights produced. That is a feature (no new claim) and a risk (no room for a correction).
- **The high case.** $0.30T is the ledger's one-time +3.2% level increase, kept as A2's high bound.

**Options.**

| Option | Design | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NSC-1A (chosen) | Flat real from the FY2029 base, as defined | D9; confirmed in Decision Brief 2 |
| NSC-1B | Flat real from a 2026-28 average | Smooths a volatile base; slightly higher claim if FY2027 is high |
| NSC-1C | Flat share of GDP | Rises with growth; a large claim (estimate: several tenths of a trillion over 2030-39) |

**Domestic deployments.** Ending them frees money inside a flat envelope. domain-public-safety.md flags that its revenue package counts those savings as public-safety revenue. They are only revenue if A2 is cut by the same amount.

## NSC-2. War powers and emergencies

A pointer only. EXE carries statutory notification before strikes, AUMF sunsets, a funding-cutoff default and National Emergencies Act reform. The ledger's DEM9 (War Powers and Iran Accountability) rides DEM8 under C10. The 2026 Iran war is the record those bills should cite. The Bill Book's "Iran architecture" (codifying a ceasefire framework and a nuclear-oversight successor) needs re-scoping against the June 15 agreement before it is drafted (open item 2).

## NSC-3. Alliances

**Design.**

- **NATO:** the FY2024 NDAA's bar on withdrawal is kept and strengthened with an appropriations condition (no funds to implement a withdrawal Congress has not approved).
- **Burden-sharing:** formalized by executive agreement against the Hague target, with an annual report to Congress. This is ✔ as executive action. Treaty-level changes stay ✖ (staging 16.1).

## NSC-4. Diplomatic capacity

**Design.** A statutory floor for Foreign Service and Civil Service staffing at State, career appointees for a minimum share of ambassadorships (the Bill Book's "career diplomats over donor appointees"), and restored language and regional training.

**Money.** The USAID successor is the agency rebuild's $60B line (CAP-7S and CAP-7; CAP-10's architecture). For State's own diplomatic rebuild, **proposed: $8B from the rebuild's batch 4 reserve** (estimate, pending Espen). State joins D9's list if it is accepted.

## NSC-5. Sanctions

**Design.** Sanctions programs default to coordination with allies, carry a sunset and periodic review, and use a standard humanitarian carve-out (staging 16.5). Most of this can ride the NDAA. ◐

## NSC-6. Trade agreements

**Design.**

- **Agreements:** congressional-executive agreements become the working default for trade and other international agreements (staging 16.6). ◐
- **Tariffs:** tariff authority is EXE-14's.
- **WTO:** a successor framework is a treaty-level project (✖) and is held (staging 16.2).

## NSC-7. Technology competition

**Design.**

- **Export controls:** advanced-chip and frontier-model-weight controls set in statute, with a congressional review mechanism for licensing decisions on adversary destinations. This takes the policy out of case-by-case executive bargaining. The project has not sourced the 2026 licensing policy (open item 3).
- **Compute security:** location verification for export-controlled chips (the Chip Security Act lineage, my knowledge).
- **CHIPS:** continuity of CHIPS Act programs.

Most of it can ride the NDAA. ◐ It pairs with domain-ai.md: AI-3's evaluations cover the national-security risks; NSC-7 controls who gets the hardware.

## NSC-8. Pandemic preparedness and biodefense

**Design.**

- **WHO:** rejoin by executive action on day one. ✔
- **Statute:** future withdrawal from WHO or other health treaties requires an act of Congress. ◐
- **Funding lines:** BARDA and CDC preparedness lines get multi-year funding: biosurveillance, stockpile, and state and local preparedness grants.
- **IHR:** reform is a treaty-level project and is held. ✖

**This is the CDC placeholder's work.** The agency rebuild (CAP-7S and CAP-7) holds CDC at a $15B placeholder; NSC-8 is its named work: biosurveillance and preparedness capacity. **Proposed: keep CDC at $15B in the rebuild**, with this named dependency, and about $0.02T (estimate) of program funding here for BARDA and preparedness grants.

## NSC-9. Climate finance

A pointer to domain-climate.md. Domestic statutory commitments (multi-year contributions authorized in statute) are ◐. Treaty commitments are ✖ (staging 16.7).

**October 26 direction (PR #31, review).** Climate finance is organized as three windows: an International Mitigation Results Program counted toward the 2035 benchmark above a 50% domestic floor, a loss-and-damage contribution, and adaptation through the restored development programs, reorganized around climate resilience. The plan is to rejoin Paris and the UNFCCC in 2029. Design and pricing: [climate-centered development finance](../sources/climate-portfolio-international.md). This row changes only when the deltas are integrated.

## NSC-10. Veterans health and capacity

**Design.** Bill Book 26, ledger F6:

- **Clinicians:** a surge of 12,000 VA mental-health clinicians, with retention pay.
- **Claims:** backlog reduction toward a 125-day decision target.
- **Rural care:** community-care expansion where VA facilities are distant.
- **Outcome target:** a published goal on veteran suicide.

**Cost.** $0.07T, range $0.04-0.12T (ledger F6, estimate). It rides R2 as mandatory funding. ✔ It is the most bipartisan bill on the war-game board.

**Why it is in this file.** Bill Book 26 had no domain home after batches 1-3. Veterans policy sits with national security in committee structure and in how the agenda presents itself.

## NSC-11. Nuclear arms control

**Design.** Pursue a successor framework to New START by executive agreement, with Senate approval sought only for a treaty-level instrument. Keep the testing moratorium. Fund NNSA within NSC-1's envelope. ✔ for the executive pieces.
