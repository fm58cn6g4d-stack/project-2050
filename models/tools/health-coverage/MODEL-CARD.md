# Health coverage model: model card

Role: compare the health design options in the care dossier on three things only: coverage (people by source and income band), federal cost (federal subsidies, net of offsets) and household cost (premiums plus out-of-pocket, before any new financing). Status: **exploratory**. Owner: Claude Code (care dossier). First version, 2026-11-10. The results block below is generated by `model.py` on every run; the rest is written by hand and changes only when the model does. Full results are in `results.md` and `results.json`.

Under the stopping rule (`charter/PLAN.md` 3.4) nothing is built that no memo needs; this model exists to put the restore, public option, staged and single-payer options on one footing for flagship 2, with the evidence and the assumptions visible.

## What it answers, and what it cannot

Answers, for one representative year (2032, a column CBO tabulates, after phase-in) and for FY2030-39 by a simple growth path:
- how many people are insured, by source, and how many remain uninsured, under each option;
- the change in national health spending and in federal subsidies (gross new spending, offsets, net);
- premiums plus out-of-pocket per person by coverage source and income band;
- the "amount to be financed" for single payer and staged paths, reported and not allocated (DEC-2026-11-10-05: the financing settlement allocates it).

Cannot answer, by design (see Limits): who pays the new financing; labor-market and macro effects; provider supply over time; state budgets in detail; year-by-year enrollment dynamics; risk selection in the marketplace beyond a premium parameter; individual eligibility or benefit. It never produces an official score.

## Structure

A cell model. The cells are coverage source by income band (under 138, 138-250, 250-400 and 400+ percent of the poverty level), in millions of people, for 2032. Sources: employer (ESI), subsidized marketplace (with the Basic Health Program), unsubsidized nongroup, Medicaid/CHIP, Medicare, other (held fixed), uninsured. Option scenarios add cells (restored Medicaid adults, public option, Medicare at 60-64, 50-59 and for children, the single public plan) so each can be priced on its own.

- **Baseline** (`baseline_2029`): CBO's February 2026 projection for 2032 under current law (P.L. 119-21 in force, enhanced credits expired). Sources are netted so no one is counted twice (CBO counts 19.6 million twice). The income split uses CBO's 2036 coverage by income for people under 65, converted to four bands with labeled assumptions. Federal subsidies by source are CBO's 2032 figures, so baseline federal cost is CBO's $3.1 trillion by construction.
- **Restore**: people move from uninsured to Medicaid and to subsidized marketplace coverage in the amounts of CBO's estimates (Medicaid provisions of the law, marketplace provisions, permanent credits); the credit schedule becomes the enhanced one and reaches above 400 percent of the poverty level.
- **Public option**: restore, plus a public plan paid at a multiple of Medicare (Washington's 160 percent as the central case), auto-enrolling a share of the remaining uninsured, taking a share of marketplace and a few ESI enrollees, and lowering the benchmark premium (which cuts the credits); optional richer subsidies.
- **Staged**: public option plus Medicare at 60-64 (from 2031), at 50-59 (from 2035) and, optionally, children's Medicare. `staged` is the 2032 status (60-64 only); `staged_full` and `staged_full_kids` put every stage in force on the 2032 population. A stylized linear path blends the fully staged option into single payer between 2030 and a target year (2040).
- **Single payer**, two variants. `single_payer_cbo_like` sets CBO's two payment-rate scenarios (EV-CAR-220) at their midpoint with low cost sharing and no long-term care, and two benchmark corners reproduce the ends of CBO's range. `single_payer_project_central` has explicit switches: payment rate (Medicare plus 15 percent), utilization, administration (5 percent), drug prices (15 percent lower), long-term care (on), cost sharing (none) and state maintenance of effort (half of baseline state Medicaid spending). Federal cost = plan spending net of cost sharing, less state and retained flows, less the federal subsidies it replaces (Medicare, Medicaid, credits, the employer tax exclusion).

Mechanics are a few dozen lines per scenario in `model.py`: flows between cells (capped so no cell goes negative), a per-person federal and household amount per cell, and a national-spending change for every flow.

## Mechanisms and their research basis

- **Coverage restoration** follows CBO's own estimates of what the law cut and the credits restored (EV-CAR-025, 029, 038, 049); the model does not re-estimate take-up.
- **Auto-enrollment** raises take-up among eligible uninsured people (EV-CAR-206; CBO's 70 percent take-up for uninsured people newly eligible for Medicare, EV-CAR-208).
- **Medicare at 60** moves uninsured, nongroup and some employer-covered people (EV-CAR-207 to 209); the cost of a mover is the private-plan cost scaled by an age index (EV-CAR-057) and by the Medicare-to-commercial price ratio (EV-CAR-131).
- **Public option premiums** are lower by the provider-rate ratio and the administrative share (EV-CAR-217, 236); only part of the advantage reaches the benchmark (assumption, EV-CAR-243 for direction).
- **Single payer**: payment rates dominate cost (EV-CAR-220), cost sharing and coverage raise demand but supply limits what is delivered (EV-CAR-219, 228), administration falls toward Medicare's (EV-CAR-217).

## Judgment calls

- **Medicare Advantage in single payer**: enrollees are folded into the single plan at fee-for-service-equivalent rates, so the 7 percent MA premium over fee-for-service (EV-CAR-061; MedPAC finds 14 percent, EV-CAR-135) is removed (`sp_ma_to_ffs`, `ma_gap`, `ma_spend_share`).
- **State maintenance of effort**: project central has states keep paying half of baseline state and local Medicaid spending (`sp_state_moe`, range 0 to 0.8); the CBO-like variant uses none, because the register gives no state design. The federal cost moves about $300 billion with it.
- **Long-term care**: included in project central (flagship 2 weighs single payer "in full") at an assumed $200 billion, range 100 to 400 (`sp_ltc_added`).
- **Employer premiums**: reported as a memo (the "amount to be financed" table) and not charged to households; the model does not decide how those payments are recaptured.
- **Medicare for 50-59**: 60-64 take-up evidence scaled by `m50_takeup_scale` (0.6, range 0.3 to 1.0).
- **Medicaid financing changes** (provider taxes, directed payments): restored only if `mcd_financing_restore` is raised (central 0; CBO's $990 billion Medicaid figure includes them).

## Limits

- Not microsimulation: income bands use CBO's three-group figures and shares from assumptions; no household-level eligibility.
- Coverage effects of restore are CBO's, not re-derived; the credits-above-400-percent step uses the unsubsidized marketplace share as a stand-in for who would enroll.
- The marketplace premium is calibrated to CBO's credit spending, so it absorbs CSR load, risk adjustment and related spending; no risk-pool or adverse-selection response (only a benchmark pass-through).
- Uncompensated care and its federal offsets are not modeled; the uninsured's use is a share of insured use (assumption).
- Provider supply and congestion over time are flagged, not modeled (one unmet-demand share). Behavioral labor-market effects, macroeconomic effects and state budgets are out of scope.
- The FY2030-39 window grows each stage's 2032 effect at CBO's net-subsidy growth, scaled by simple phase-in years; transition costs and year-by-year enrollment are not modeled. CBO's tabulation ends in 2036; later years extend its 2032-36 growth rate.
- National spending uses CMS's pre-2026 projection (which does not reflect the 2025 law), while coverage and federal subsidies use CBO's 2026 baseline; they do not reconcile exactly (the implied federal Medicaid share is lower than CBO's two-thirds).
- Single-payer financing (taxes, employer contributions, premiums) is not allocated here.

### Differences explained

- **BM-1a, BM-1b (CBO single-payer options, 2020)**: a miss, reported and not tuned. Both corners exceed CBO's range once it is grown to 2032. The plan carries about 80 percent of national spending, close to the federal share CBO's range implies, so the gap is one of scale. The model's national spending base comes from CMS and its newly insured, long-term care and utilization assumptions are the model's own; the register has only CBO's two rate scenarios (EV-CAR-220), not the five options' cost sharing, long-term care or baseline. The model reproduces CBO's finding that the direction of the national spending change depends on the payment rate: its low corner reduces national spending and its high corner raises it.
- **BM-2, BM-3a**: pass by construction. The restore flows are set from the same CBO figures, so these check phase-in and band arithmetic, not independent accuracy.
- **BM-3b (credits, about $350 billion over ten years)**: a miss; the model's 2032 cost is well above the ten-year average CBO implies. The model has no premium reduction from a healthier pool and no employer-coverage offset, and it adds the cost of removing the 400 percent cliff. Treat the credit cost as an upper estimate until those are modeled.
- **BM-4 (Medicaid provisions)**: passes on the coverage-linked cost alone; CBO's total also includes provider-tax and directed-payment cuts, which the model leaves at zero in the central case.
- **BM-5 (Medicare at 60: 7.3 million)**: the target comes from the model spec; the saved CBO text (SRC-3204) gives shares and the 1.3 million uninsured, not this count. The model's count of people who move is lower, and it does not count people who add Part A while keeping employer coverage.
- **BM-6 (Urban 2019, secondary)**: indicative. Urban's baseline is not in the register, so RAND's federal base stands in, and Urban's bundle is not the same policy as restore plus public option.

## Files and how to run

`model.py` (model), `inputs.csv` (every parameter), `scenarios.csv` (option definitions and phase-in years), `test_model.py` (accounting identities, boundary cases, benchmarks), `results.json` and `results.md` (generated, drift-checked), this card (generated block).

```bash
python3 models/tools/health-coverage/test_model.py
python3 models/tools/health-coverage/model.py [--out DIR]
bash models/tools/run-all.sh [--write]    # step 6f runs both and compares the outputs with the committed copies
```

Python 3.10 standard library only.
