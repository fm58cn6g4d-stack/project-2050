# Immigration model: model card

Role: the model card of `MOD-immigration`, which prices the immigration flagship's decided design (`dossiers/immigration/`, `DEC-2026-11-12-07` and its amendments) on federal fiscal years: the system's capacity (USCIS, the asylum corps, the immigration courts, detention), adjudication, the fiscal effect of status, and, in a separate unbooked module, the labor-market effects (`charter/PLAN.md` 3.4). It serves the dossier's section 6 and the budget model's booking. Status: **working** (set 2026-11-25, step H6: the flagship 6 figures booked in the budget model rest on it, it has tests and a validation table; it stays below "preview" and "released", which wait for memo H1). Owner: the Project 2050 lead session (Claude Code), through GitHub issues on this repository. Version: 0.2.0, see `CHANGELOG.md`; the dossier's first version is from November 12, 2026 (board row I4). License: the line step H2b adds once memo H2 is answered. Every output of this model is analysis of the evidence, not an official score or a benefit determination; its results files carry that line in their header.

## What it answers

For FY2030-40 (federal fiscal years; FY2030-39 is the window, FY2040 the first permanent year), in nominal dollars, a cost positive, against current law as it will stand in January 2029:
- **The general-fund cost of each decided line** (`immigration_model.lines()`): status under memo 1 (from CBO's scaled H.R. 6 score), the records-first registry's waivers (memo 2), asylum, refugees, regional access and counsel (memo 4), the judge teams (memo 4 part 2), the detention cap against case management (memo 5), memo 6's eligibility table (priced on care's own path) and USCIS's other public work (memo 7).
- **Section 6's whole path** (`immigration_path.py`): those lines plus the judgment lines (memo 3, the DHS restructure, E-Verify's operations, the fee repeals), E-Verify's revenue on and off budget, and the H-1B fee's offset, each with section 3's range. Central: **+$159.2 billion over FY2030-39 and +$24.6 billion in FY2040** on the general fund, the figures booked as IMM-1, IMM-EV and IF1T. Off budget, Social Security's funds: E-Verify's loss (+$41.8 billion over FY2030-39, booked as IMM-EV-SS) and status's gain (−$22.2 billion, `status_offbudget`, booked as IMM-1-SS).
- **Capacity:** how many immigration judges clear the court backlog by FY2035 (central about 1,670 in all, 920 added), when it clears at 800 judges or 150 hires a year, the corps by year, the steady-state need after clearance; USCIS's filings (about 9.3 million) and how long its backlog takes to clear.
- **For care's booking** (not counted here): the people with no status that care's base counts as covered (`care_base_correction`).
- **Labor market, not booked** (`immigration_labor.py`): the earnings gain from legal status, people moved into work, the federal tax on income that comes onto the books (off-budget payroll, on-budget HI and income tax, in three layers), and the change in average wages as a range.

Headline results are in `results.json` (keys `scenarios`, `path`, `labor_market`); a dossier quoting a figure cites the run ID in `models/runs.csv`.

## What it cannot answer

- **Behavior and the macroeconomy:** it is conventional. The labor module reads the evidence on earnings and taxes but has no general equilibrium, capital response or price effects; it is never a dynamic score or a pay-for.
- **Status's fiscal effect from the bottom up:** the status line is CBO's 2021 score of H.R. 6 scaled, grown and netted, not a microsimulation of people by status, years here, earnings and program use. It cannot say what any one program pays.
- **Social Security's long-run effect of legalization:** not quantified (the labor module's payroll tax is ten-year revenue only, with no benefits).
- **State and local budgets**, border behavior after enactment, crime, and the next generation's fiscal effect.
- **Who gains or loses** by occupation, place or group, beyond naming the groups the evidence says are most exposed.
- **An individual's eligibility or a case's outcome.** It never produces an official score or an individual benefit determination.

## Mechanisms and their research basis

- **Filings** (`uscis`): the eligible (tiers A and B and registration, about 13.25 million) times take-up (70%, EV-IMM-609), front-loaded as IRCA's were (EV-IMM-635), from FY2031. Waived fees and the records match are the appropriation (memo 2); backlog money runs until the net backlog of 7.7 million (EV-IMM-140) clears (EV-IMM-575's pace).
- **Courts** (`court_path`, `eoir`): a stock-flow model, pending cases plus receipts minus completions, with judges hired at a ramp (EV-IMM-646) toward the smallest corps that clears by FY2035, completions at CRS's average of about 510 a judge (EV-IMM-641), attrition (EV-IMM-646, -643), new judges at half a year's output, filing closing a share of pending cases (IMM-1.10) and officers deciding first cutting receipts (IMM-3.01). It reproduces CRS's own projection (R47637, SRC-4089) when run on CRS's assumptions (BM-CRS-1, -2).
- **Asylum and refugees** (`asylum_refugees`): decisions times DHS's cost a case (EV-IMM-302); the refugee floor against the baseline (EV-IMM-052).
- **Detention** (`detention`): the cap's saving against the base account's 41,500 beds (EV-IMM-362) at ICE's bed-day rate (EV-IMM-628), against case management at $20-40 a day less today's alternatives (EV-IMM-629, -355); the number in case management is a labeled assumption and decides the sign.
- **Status** (`status_fiscal`): CBO's H.R. 6 on-budget deficit increase ($42.48 billion, EV-IMM-669; I1 A, `DEC-2026-11-25-27`) times memo 1's reach (an assumption, 2-3 times; checked against CBO's farm bill score, BM-CBO-3) and window growth, less the health share the single plan pays anyway (EV-IMM-602), on CBO's yearly profile. Its off-budget side (`status_offbudget`): CBO's −$7.15 billion for Social Security (payroll tax less added benefits, EV-IMM-669), scaled the same way with no health netting, booked in Social Security's funds, not on the general fund (`DEC-2026-11-25-28`).
- **The eligibility table** (`table`, `care_cost_of_residual`): lawful residents above care's population base priced on care's own path (`dossiers/care/tools/health_path.py`) at two-thirds of care's average use (EV-IMM-661, -662; `DEC-2026-11-13-05`), plus the five-year wait's programs.
- **Labor market** (`immigration_labor.py`, not booked): (a) the wage gain of legal status, about 6% (Kossoudji and Cobb-Clark on IRCA, EV-IMM-232; range 4-14%), building over five years, less for registered people and current DACA and TPS holders (EV-IMM-016, -022); (b) DACA's effect on work (Pope, EV-IMM-235); (c) federal tax on income that newly comes onto the books, from ITEP's 60% of income taxed before status to 92% after (SRC-4185, EV-IMM-229) at payroll rates and CBO's 9.7% average income tax rate for recent immigrants (SRC-4181); (d) average wages from the added labor force at CBO's short- and long-run S. 744 responses (EV-IMM-203, -204), with the National Academies' finding that the effect is very small and falls most on prior immigrants and native dropouts (EV-IMM-223).
- **Labeled assumptions without research behind them:** the counsel unit costs, the people in case management, memo 1's reach, the health share, the filing shape, the labor module's phase-in years and the registered share of the wage gain.

## Limits

- **The status line's split follows CBO's.** Since version 0.2.0 the general-fund line scales CBO's on-budget figure and Social Security's side its off-budget one (I1 A, `DEC-2026-11-25-27`; before, the line carried the unified $35.33 billion, about $50.5 billion over FY2030-39 against about $60.8 billion now). Both sides take CBO's 2021 yearly profile; CBO's off-budget effect actually peaks mid-window (EV-IMM-669's yearly rows), so IMM-1-SS's timing is approximate, not its total.
- **Ranges are bounds:** `low` and `high` set every input to one end together; they are not probability intervals, and for memo 1 they are not ordered (low 67.1, high 55.0), because take-up and reach move it in opposite directions.
- **Baseline vintages:** CBO's 2021 H.R. 6 score (February 2021 baseline) for status; CBO and JCT's 2013 score for E-Verify; EOIR's FY2026 counts for the courts; ICE's FY2023 bed-day rate. Receipts and staffing have moved a great deal since 2023 (EV-IMM-102, -103), and the second Trump administration's figures are read with the skepticism `DEC-2026-10-30-01` asks.
- **Judgment lines on outside scores:** memo 3, E-Verify, the restructure and the fee repeals in `immigration_path.py` are hand-drawn paths with section 3's ranges, not modeled.
- **Labor module:** the wage gain is treated as productivity, not a transfer from employers or consumers, which overstates the fiscal gain; ITEP's income is per resident and treated as earnings, which overstates payroll tax a little; filers are taken as average, though they are probably more settled and already more on the books, so formalization is overstated; the wage evidence is from 1986-era men; income tax is gross of refundable credits. Its formalization layer is about six times CBO's scaled H.R. 6 off-budget revenue (BM-CBO-4): an upper reading.
- **Shared code:** memo 6's coverage line and care's base correction run care's dossier tool, `dossiers/care/tools/health_path.py`, and through it `MOD-health-coverage`; a change there moves this model's results.

## Known issues

- The model imports care's dossier tool (`dossiers/care/tools/health_path.py`) instead of reading `MOD-health-coverage`'s outputs as data (audit 1.7).
- `uscis()` copies FY2039's filings into FY2040 for people reaching ten years, so cumulating filings to FY2040 overshoots by about 2%; `care_base_correction` and the labor module stop at FY2039.
- `immigration_path.everify()` fixes the post-legalization pool at 0.3 of 2013's and grows wages 4% a year, a third growth convention beside `price_growth` (2.5%) and `window_growth`; it does not move with take-up, as `dossiers/immigration/7-validation.md` says the pool should.
- The court plan is expected to change with democratic renewal's judicial branch plan (Espen, November 12; `7-validation.md` section 2).
- The labor module's ITEP and CBO passages are catalogued sources without register entries yet.

## How to run

From a fresh clone, Python 3.10 or later, standard library only; every input is committed.

```bash
python3 models/tools/immigration/immigration_run.py                    # results.json beside the code (all three scenarios)
python3 models/tools/immigration/immigration_run.py --scenario low --out DIR
python3 models/tools/immigration/immigration_run.py --print            # the plain-text reports of the model, the path and the labor module
python3 models/tools/immigration/immigration_run.py --record --out build/immigration-recorded   # a recorded run
python3 models/tools/immigration/test_immigration.py                   # tests, benchmarks, the card's generated tables
```

The dossier's commands still work: `python3 dossiers/immigration/tools/immigration_model.py` and `immigration_path.py` are wrappers that run this code, and `dossiers/immigration/tools/test_immigration_model.py` is the dossier's own test.

Recorded runs are rows in `models/runs.csv` (`RUN-immigration-<date>-<letter>`), written by `models/runlog.py` when the model runs with `--record`; a dossier quoting a figure cites the run ID.
