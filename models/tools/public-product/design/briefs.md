# A day in 2050: illustration briefs, one per scene

Role: the art brief for each scene of the front door (stage H step H9). Each paragraph starts from the scene's `illustration` field in `models/tools/public-product/day/day.json` (H8, #494). It then adds what the picture must show, what it must avoid, and how each of the three directions in `README.md` would render it. Status: **draft, November 25, 2026. Not published.** C (flat graphic) is now the working direction: Espen said "I think bright and bold might be the way to go" (Espen, chat, November 25, 18:31 UTC). The A and B notes in each scene are kept as archived alternates. The final-art brief for C comes first; the per-scene briefs follow.

Rules for every scene, whichever direction is chosen:
- The page labels every picture "imagined scene", and in direction B "imagined scene, staged". The picture itself carries no words, except C's clock time, which is also in the text.
- No real people or likenesses, no real places a reader could identify, no party colors, flags, logos, slogans or campaign marks, and nothing that imitates an existing campaign's look.
- Cast the people as the composites they are: a range of ages, bodies, skin tones and abilities, ordinary clothes, no celebrities.
- Every picture needs alt text that says what happens in it, written with H8's copy, not as a caption of the art.
- **The three families (O1, `DEC-2026-11-25-23`):** the reader now follows one of three families: a single man in his mid-20s; a couple in their 30s with a child or two and aging parents; and a couple nearing retirement whose adult son, who has struggled with substance use, lives with them because he can't afford a place. H8 decides which family carries each scene. The "featured family" below is a suggestion so the picture can be drawn. Family names in the Figma frames (Jordan; Maya and Sam; Carol, Dev and Eli) are placeholders.

## The final art for direction C: characters and style

This brief is for the illustrator who replaces the placeholders. Espen's note on the first placeholders: "We're definitely going for stylized not anything like realistic but the shapes with the elongated half circle for their body makes them all look like they're wearing strange long robes" (Espen, chat, November 25, 18:31 UTC). The redrawn placeholders in `people/` and `screens/cast.png` show the proportions to start from, not the finished look.

- **Stylized, not realistic.** Flat color, no gradients or textures, a 2.5 to 3 px navy outline (`#14213D`, the C `line` token) at 1440 px, square ends on shapes, round joints. Faces are simple: dot eyes, a short smile line, glasses or a beard where the cast list says so. No photo references traced and no likeness of any real person.
- **Real bodies, not robes.** Every person has a distinct head, a neck, a torso with shoulders, two arms with hands, and two legs with shoes. Clothes follow the body: tops, trousers, skirts, dresses, an apron, a coat. Children are drawn with child proportions (bigger heads, shorter limbs), not as shrunken adults.
- **Varied people.** A range of skin tones, hair (straight, curly, coiled, grey, bald, a bun, a ponytail), heights and body types, including heavier and thinner people, and older people who stand and move like older people (a cane for Grace). Nobody is the default.
- **The cast (placeholders until H8 names them):**
  - Jordan, 26, on his own: a line cook; apron over a teal top.
  - Maya, 34, and Sam, 36, with Leo, 8, and Ana, 4, and Sam's parents Grace, 71, and Walter, 74.
  - Carol, 63, and Dev, 64, nearing retirement, and their son Eli, 31, who gets the keys to his own place. Recovery is not illustrated.
  - Supporting people (the clinic doctor, the child care teacher, the home care aide, the line crew) are not family members and must not read as any real person.
- **Palette.** Only the C tokens: cream `#FFF8E6`, butter `#FFE9A8`, sun `#FFC93C`, navy `#14213D`, brick `#B5371B`, coral `#FF8A6B`, plus a teal `#2A9D8F` and a violet `#6B4E9B` for clothing, and skin and hair tones as needed. Walls and floors use the cream, butter and sun; navy is kept for outlines and the night scenes, so dark clothes don't vanish into a dark floor.
- **Composition.** People stand on a floor line at about 94% of the frame's height; the scene's main moment sits in the middle third so it survives the 390 px phone crop. No words in the picture except the clock time, which is decorative and repeated in the text.
- **Dark mode.** Each picture is delivered as SVG with its fills named after the tokens, so H10 can swap cream and butter for the dark `bg` and `surface` and keep the people's colors.
- **Deliverables.** One SVG per scene at 1440 × 960 and a phone crop at 390 × 520, plus a character sheet (front view, each person at the same scale). Alt text comes from H8's copy, not from the illustrator.

## 1. 6:30 am, a kitchen: "The money comes on the first" (A secure floor)

Early light in a small kitchen. A parent glances at a phone while making breakfast; two children at the table, one still in pajamas. Through the window, a neighbor on her porch holds a newborn. Featured family: the couple in their 30s. Show calm, not celebration: the payment is routine, so nobody is staring at the phone in surprise. The newborn next door carries paid leave, so the neighbor is rested and at home on a weekday. Avoid any on-screen amount or bank branding. Archived A: cool blue dawn turning gold at the window. Archived B: shot from the doorway, steam from a kettle, window light only. C: the phone as a flat rectangle with a check mark, the sun as a disc in the window, "6:30" in the corner.

## 2. 7:45 am, a child care center: "Drop-off, and nothing to pay" (Care: child care and pre-K)

A bright child care room at drop-off. A small girl runs in ahead of her parent toward a teacher kneeling to greet her; cubbies, paintings, a window onto the street. Featured family: the couple in their 30s. The teacher is the second subject: an adult professional at ease, not a harried worker, because the scene is also about her wage. No sign-in desk with a payment terminal. Archived A: busy, warm, paint-box colors in the children's art. Archived B: low camera at child height, motion blur on the running girl. C: the cubbies as a grid of colored squares, the teacher and child as two shapes meeting in the middle.

## 3. 9:00 am, a clinic: "No bill at the desk" (Care: health)

A neighborhood clinic waiting room, unhurried. A man in a cook's apron talks with a doctor at the door of an exam room; a boy sits on a bench swinging his legs beside his mother. No payment window. Featured family: the single man in his mid-20s (the restaurant worker of H8's draft becomes him), with the 30s couple's son on the bench. The key absence is the payment window: draw a reception desk that is clearly a desk, with a person, not a cashier's grille or card reader. Avoid hospital drama: no gurneys, no masks, no worry on faces. Archived A: morning light through blinds across the floor. Archived B: the doctor's back to camera so the patient's face carries the moment. C: as in the Figma frames, the door as a red block, people as shapes, "9:00" large on the floor.

## 4. 10:30 am, a living room: "Help at home, savings intact" (Care: long-term care)

A sunny living room. A home care aide helps an older woman with her cardigan; a photo of her family on the shelf, a calendar with the aide's visits marked. Featured family: the 30s couple's aging parent. Dignity first: the older woman is doing most of the dressing, with a hand from the aide, and the two are talking. The calendar is how the picture says "this is regular"; keep its marks wordless. Archived A: soft late-morning light, house plants. Archived B: medium shot, the photo frame sharp in the foreground. C: the calendar as a grid with colored dots, two figures side by side.

## 5. 12:00 pm, a warehouse break room: "A raise that keeps up" (Work)

A warehouse break room at noon. A worker reads a pay stub at a table; on the bulletin board behind, a union contract summary and a posted schedule. Through a window, a man in work clothes walks into a machine shop across the street. Featured family: the single man in his mid-20s or a coworker; H8 decides. The board's papers must stay unreadable: no union name or logo, no real union's colors. Archived A: noon light flattening the room, vending machine glow. Archived B: fluorescent light, a lunch box, a real-feeling but generic workplace. C: the pay stub as a white rectangle with an up arrow, the board as pinned squares.

## 6. 2:00 pm, an apartment: "A lease of their own" (Housing)

A modest apartment on moving day: boxes half unpacked, keys in hand. Featured family: the couple nearing retirement and their adult son, who gets the keys to a place of his own (the draft in the Figma frames). **The scene's copy is a placeholder; H8 rewrites Eli's housing beat.** H7 (#508) found that Eli does not qualify for the rent awards' first stage in 2030-33: doubled-up households count only with children, and the leaver extension covers prison, jail and foster care, not treatment (`dossiers/home/4-specification.md`, lines 161 and 163). Show the parents helping, then stepping back: their relief is part of the story. Recovery is not illustrated (no meetings, pills or bottles); he is a young man moving in. Archived A: afternoon sun across bare floorboards. Archived B: stairwell or doorway shot, boxes in the foreground. C: as in the Figma frames, boxes as squares, keys as a bright dot.

## 7. 4:00 pm, a front porch as a storm comes in: "Storm season, dividend day" (Clean power)

A retired couple on a porch as dark clouds roll in; porch light on, wind in the trees. In the distance a line crew in a bucket truck works on a new transmission line; a solar field and wind turbines on the far hills. Featured family: the couple nearing retirement. The lit porch light is the point (the power stays on); the storm should look real, not apocalyptic. No utility branding on the truck. Archived A: bruised purple sky, warm porch glow. Archived B: long lens, the crew small but sharp. C: the cloud as one big navy shape, the porch light as a yellow disc, turbines as simple crosses.

## 8. 5:30 pm, the courthouse steps: "Earned, not given" (Immigration)

Late-afternoon light on courthouse steps. A man in a cook's jacket holds up a certificate for a photo; a woman beside him laughs. Further up the steps, a family with a small child talks with a lawyer. Featured family: a neighbor of any of the three; H8 decides. The building must be a generic civic building, not an identifiable courthouse, and the certificate stays wordless (no seal that imitates a real agency's). Archived A: golden hour, long shadows down the steps. Archived B: a phone-camera framing from below, as if the woman is taking the picture. C: the steps as stacked bands, the certificate as a white card held high.

## 9. 7:00 pm, the public library: "A meeting that anyone can join" (Democratic renewal)

An evening town meeting in a public library: folding chairs, a candidate speaking without a podium, a young woman in the second row with a voter card in her hand. Bookshelves and a children's corner behind. Featured family: any; the 30s couple could be in the chairs with a child in the children's corner. The candidate has no party, no campaign sign, no lapel pin and no resemblance to any real politician. Archived A: lamplight, a warm room against a dark window. Archived B: wide shot from the back row. C: chairs as repeated shapes, the voter card in the one accent color.

## 10. 10:00 pm, a night bus (optional scene, O2): "The route that survived" (Transportation)

A lit city bus crossing a newly repaired bridge at night. Inside, a woman in scrubs leans her head against the window; a few other riders on their way to night shifts. Featured family: whichever H8 picks, or a neighbor. Transit is in as an optional scene (O2, `DEC-2026-11-25-24`), so the brief should be finished even if the scene can be turned off. No real transit agency's livery. Archived A: the bus as a lantern on a blue night. Archived B: shot through the bus window from outside, the rider soft. C: the bus as a lit rectangle on a navy band, the bridge as a clean arc.

## 11. Night, the end of the day: "The money, told plainly" (Fair financing)

The town at night from a hill: houses, the clinic, the warehouse, the library, a bus crossing the bridge, a few lit windows. No people in focus. The page ends on the money (O3, `DEC-2026-11-25-25`). The picture is the calm after the day: every place from the earlier scenes is visible, so the reader sees what the money pays for. No charts in the picture; numbers belong to the text. Archived A: a painted panorama, the earlier scenes' palettes as lit windows. Archived B: a dusk long exposure of a generic town (composited or rendered, not a real town). C: a skyline of the earlier scenes' shapes, each window lit in its scene's color.
