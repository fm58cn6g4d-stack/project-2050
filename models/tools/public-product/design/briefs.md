# A day in 2050: illustration briefs, one per scene

Role: the art brief for each scene of the front door (stage H step H9). Each paragraph starts from the scene's `illustration` field in `models/tools/public-product/day/day.json` (H8, #494). It then adds what the picture must show, what it must avoid, and how each of the three directions in `README.md` would render it. Status: **draft proposal, November 25, 2026. Not published.** Once Espen picks a direction, these briefs go to the illustrator or photographer with that direction's notes only.

Rules for every scene, whichever direction is chosen:
- The page labels every picture "imagined scene", and in direction B "imagined scene, staged". The picture itself carries no words, except C's clock time, which is also in the text.
- No real people or likenesses, no real places a reader could identify, no party colors, flags, logos, slogans or campaign marks, and nothing that imitates an existing campaign's look.
- Cast the people as the composites they are: a range of ages, bodies, skin tones and abilities, ordinary clothes, no celebrities.
- Every picture needs alt text that says what happens in it, written with H8's copy, not as a caption of the art.
- **The three families (Espen's answer to O1, relayed by the lead on November 25; the lead records its `DEC-` entry):** the reader now follows one of three families: a single man in his mid-20s; a couple in their 30s with a child or two and aging parents; and a couple nearing retirement whose adult son, who has struggled with substance use, lives with them because he can't afford a place. H8 decides which family carries each scene. The "featured family" below is a suggestion so the picture can be drawn. Family names in the Figma frames (Jordan; Maya and Sam; Carol, Dev and Eli) are placeholders.

## 1. 6:30 am, a kitchen: "The money comes on the first" (A secure floor)

Early light in a small kitchen. A parent glances at a phone while making breakfast; two children at the table, one still in pajamas. Through the window, a neighbor on her porch holds a newborn. Featured family: the couple in their 30s. Show calm, not celebration: the payment is routine, so nobody is staring at the phone in surprise. The newborn next door carries paid leave, so the neighbor is rested and at home on a weekday. Avoid any on-screen amount or bank branding. A: cool blue dawn turning gold at the window. B: shot from the doorway, steam from a kettle, window light only. C: the phone as a flat rectangle with a check mark, the sun as a disc in the window, "6:30" in the corner.

## 2. 7:45 am, a child care center: "Drop-off, and nothing to pay" (Care: child care and pre-K)

A bright child care room at drop-off. A small girl runs in ahead of her parent toward a teacher kneeling to greet her; cubbies, paintings, a window onto the street. Featured family: the couple in their 30s. The teacher is the second subject: an adult professional at ease, not a harried worker, because the scene is also about her wage. No sign-in desk with a payment terminal. A: busy, warm, paint-box colors in the children's art. B: low camera at child height, motion blur on the running girl. C: the cubbies as a grid of colored squares, the teacher and child as two shapes meeting in the middle.

## 3. 9:00 am, a clinic: "No bill at the desk" (Care: health)

A neighborhood clinic waiting room, unhurried. A man in a cook's apron talks with a doctor at the door of an exam room; a boy sits on a bench swinging his legs beside his mother. No payment window. Featured family: the single man in his mid-20s (the restaurant worker of H8's draft becomes him), with the 30s couple's son on the bench. The key absence is the payment window: draw a reception desk that is clearly a desk, with a person, not a cashier's grille or card reader. Avoid hospital drama: no gurneys, no masks, no worry on faces. A: morning light through blinds across the floor. B: the doctor's back to camera so the patient's face carries the moment. C: as in the Figma frames, the door as a red block, people as shapes, "9:00" large on the floor.

## 4. 10:30 am, a living room: "Help at home, savings intact" (Care: long-term care)

A sunny living room. A home care aide helps an older woman with her cardigan; a photo of her family on the shelf, a calendar with the aide's visits marked. Featured family: the 30s couple's aging parent. Dignity first: the older woman is doing most of the dressing, with a hand from the aide, and the two are talking. The calendar is how the picture says "this is regular"; keep its marks wordless. A: soft late-morning light, house plants. B: medium shot, the photo frame sharp in the foreground. C: the calendar as a grid with colored dots, two figures side by side.

## 5. 12:00 pm, a warehouse break room: "A raise that keeps up" (Work)

A warehouse break room at noon. A worker reads a pay stub at a table; on the bulletin board behind, a union contract summary and a posted schedule. Through a window, a man in work clothes walks into a machine shop across the street. Featured family: the single man in his mid-20s or a coworker; H8 decides. The board's papers must stay unreadable: no union name or logo, no real union's colors. A: noon light flattening the room, vending machine glow. B: fluorescent light, a lunch box, a real-feeling but generic workplace. C: the pay stub as a white rectangle with an up arrow, the board as pinned squares.

## 6. 2:00 pm, an apartment: "A lease of their own" (Housing)

A modest apartment on moving day: boxes half unpacked, keys in hand. Featured family: the couple nearing retirement and their adult son, who gets the keys to a place of his own (the draft in the Figma frames). H8 must confirm he fits the rent awards' first-priority groups (the awards go "first for people with no home of their own", `day.json` claim `awards`) before this pairing is used; if he doesn't, the scene returns to H8's original family and the emergency-fund neighbor. Show the parents helping, then stepping back: their relief is part of the story. Recovery is not illustrated (no meetings, pills or bottles); he is a young man moving in. A: afternoon sun across bare floorboards. B: stairwell or doorway shot, boxes in the foreground. C: as in the Figma frames, boxes as squares, keys as a bright dot.

## 7. 4:00 pm, a front porch as a storm comes in: "Storm season, dividend day" (Clean power)

A retired couple on a porch as dark clouds roll in; porch light on, wind in the trees. In the distance a line crew in a bucket truck works on a new transmission line; a solar field and wind turbines on the far hills. Featured family: the couple nearing retirement. The lit porch light is the point (the power stays on); the storm should look real, not apocalyptic. No utility branding on the truck. A: bruised purple sky, warm porch glow. B: long lens, the crew small but sharp. C: the cloud as one big navy shape, the porch light as a yellow disc, turbines as simple crosses.

## 8. 5:30 pm, the courthouse steps: "Earned, not given" (Immigration)

Late-afternoon light on courthouse steps. A man in a cook's jacket holds up a certificate for a photo; a woman beside him laughs. Further up the steps, a family with a small child talks with a lawyer. Featured family: a neighbor of any of the three; H8 decides. The building must be a generic civic building, not an identifiable courthouse, and the certificate stays wordless (no seal that imitates a real agency's). A: golden hour, long shadows down the steps. B: a phone-camera framing from below, as if the woman is taking the picture. C: the steps as stacked bands, the certificate as a white card held high.

## 9. 7:00 pm, the public library: "A meeting that anyone can join" (Democratic renewal)

An evening town meeting in a public library: folding chairs, a candidate speaking without a podium, a young woman in the second row with a voter card in her hand. Bookshelves and a children's corner behind. Featured family: any; the 30s couple could be in the chairs with a child in the children's corner. The candidate has no party, no campaign sign, no lapel pin and no resemblance to any real politician. A: lamplight, a warm room against a dark window. B: wide shot from the back row. C: chairs as repeated shapes, the voter card in the one accent color.

## 10. 10:00 pm, a night bus (optional scene, O2): "The route that survived" (Transportation)

A lit city bus crossing a newly repaired bridge at night. Inside, a woman in scrubs leans her head against the window; a few other riders on their way to night shifts. Featured family: whichever H8 picks, or a neighbor. Transit is in as an optional scene (O2), so the brief should be finished even if the scene can be turned off. No real transit agency's livery. A: the bus as a lantern on a blue night. B: shot through the bus window from outside, the rider soft. C: the bus as a lit rectangle on a navy band, the bridge as a clean arc.

## 11. Night, the end of the day: "The money, told plainly" (Fair financing)

The town at night from a hill: houses, the clinic, the warehouse, the library, a bus crossing the bridge, a few lit windows. No people in focus. The page ends on the money (O3). The picture is the calm after the day: every place from the earlier scenes is visible, so the reader sees what the money pays for. No charts in the picture; numbers belong to the text. A: a painted panorama, the earlier scenes' palettes as lit windows. B: a dusk long exposure of a generic town (composited or rendered, not a real town). C: a skyline of the earlier scenes' shapes, each window lit in its scene's color.
