# Provision specs: how the ledger's money is enacted

Role: a summary of `models/data/budget/provisions.csv`, the provision spec for every ledger row (piece B3 of `models/tools/budget/WORKFLOW.md`). It shows where the model's picture of how money is enacted is still thin. Status: generated by `models/tools/budget/provisions_report.py` from the specs and the current ledger; do not edit by hand. The method is in `models/tools/budget/provisions.py` and the format in `models/data/budget/README.md`, "Provision specs".

**What this changes.** Nothing in any figure. For every row the fiscal model scores, the library produces the same path, year by year, as `models/tools/fiscal-model.py`, the same floating-point number in every year from FY2026 to FY2056; each row's FY2030-39 total equals its ledger midpoint; and `models/data/fiscal-paths.csv` is unchanged. `models/tools/budget/test_provisions.py` checks all three on every run. What B3 adds is the budget-concept reading: kind, vehicle, appropriation type and budget authority.

## The rows

The ledger has 165 rows: 72 bills, 61 revenue options, 23 gap-closing options and 9 dynamic-feedback rows. One bill, HOU-1, is split into 32 components, each with its own spec. The fiscal model scores 110 of the rows; the other 55 are on the revenue menu, outside the general fund, or are borrowing lines whose spending sits in other rows.

**What the discretionary spend-out shows.** For the 21 rows with discretionary budget authority, the library estimates that budget authority (the appropriations) behind the model's outlays: each year's appropriation is the outlays it pays for over B2's four-year spend-out. The shares are B2's, calibrated to CBO (`spendout-discretionary-cbo.csv`): OMB's history gives their timing, with ground transportation taken out of nondefense because its outlays run on contract authority (see below), and one scale per group makes CBO's projected budget authority give CBO's projected outlays. Rebuilt that way, CBO's FY2029-36 discretionary outlays come back within 0.9% for defense and 1.4% for nondefense, once the nondefense accounts with no discretionary budget authority are carried on their own. Over FY2030-39 that is $1.25 trillion of budget authority for $1.20 trillion of outlays. Another $0.09 trillion would have to be appropriated before FY2030, in FY2027-29: at these rates only 25% of new nondefense budget authority is spent in its first year, so outlays that start at full speed in FY2030, as most profiles have them, need money enacted earlier. The scoring window does not count it (CONVENTIONS.md, choice 3).

Spending that budget authority out at the same rates does not give back the model's outlays exactly, because the model's profiles start and stop faster than B2's rates allow. The largest FY2030-39 gaps, in billions: MOV-31 -10.1 in FY2036; MOV-29 -7.9 in FY2039; CAP-7 -7.8 in FY2032; MOV-30 -2.8 in FY2039; DEM-17 -1.8 in FY2036. The model's outlays stay canonical. The budget-authority figures are a diagnostic reading, not validated for spending-cap or official scoring use (Codex's review of #108); they are rough, because the shares' timing is OMB history's, not CBO's account-by-account rates (B2 README, "Spend-out set by CBO's projections").

**Contract authority.** Highway and transit programs paid from the Highway Trust Fund get mandatory budget authority (contract authority) from their authorizing law, while annual appropriations acts cap how much may be obligated each year, so their outlays count as discretionary. CBO records them that way: in its February 2026 baseline, FY2026-2036, the trust fund's highway and transit accounts carry about $80 billion a year of mandatory contract authority (the FY2026 level, which CBO's baseline extends) and $78-89 billion a year of discretionary nondefense outlays (`models/data/budget/cbo-contract-authority.csv`). Those outlays have no discretionary budget authority to spend out, so the rebuild of CBO's outlays above carries them on their own. No ledger row is contract authority. Moving America's renewal top-ups (MOV-29, MOV-30) are general-fund appropriations to programs, made in addition to the trust fund's contract authority, which the bill keeps within each account's receipts (Draft 2 sec. 110; decision A-14). So they are scored as discretionary appropriations like the rest of those rows, with the conflict between their reconciliation rail and their discretionary category listed below.

## Where the files disagree

These are reported, not fixed: changing a category or a profile changes adopted inputs, which is Espen's decision (tier 3). B6 (item 3) sorted them: open design choices kept as they are, and findings that would move figures, proposed to Espen. None is a plain booking error.

### Open design choices: vehicle and category (board 0d)

Espen, November 8, 2026: how each of these is enacted is not a decision to take now. They stay legacy working choices, weighed provision by provision as each dossier reaches them, and many programs will end up with both a mandatory or advance-appropriation part in reconciliation and a regular-appropriations part. The library can now score that: a provision with method `split` has parts, each with its own share, kind, category and vehicle (`provision-splits.csv`; no row is split yet). Until a dossier decides, each row stays booked as it is. Where an existing document already says how the row is enacted, it is cited here.

- **EDU-4**: On a reconciliation bill but booked as discretionary. No document says mandatory or appropriated; the rail is R2 (legacy/domains/domain-education.md:22), and its apprenticeship part runs through LAB-8.
- **LAB-8**: On a reconciliation bill but booked as discretionary. Legacy/domains/domain-labor.md:134 says mandatory grants through R2.
- **DEM-11**: Vehicle and category to be settled. Legacy/domains/domain-democracy.md:222 prefers mandatory funding if a reconciliation committee can carry it, otherwise annual appropriations, as the ledger records.
- **DEM-17**: On a reconciliation bill but booked as discretionary. Legacy/domains/domain-democracy.md:269 prefers capital grants as direct spending in R3 (Byrd unchecked), with appropriations as the fallback.
- **CAP-7S**: Vehicle and category to be settled. Legacy/domains/domain-capacity.md:157 targets R1 direct multi-year appropriations, pending Byrd and jurisdiction checks.
- **NSC-10**: On a reconciliation bill but booked as discretionary. Legacy/domains/domain-national-security.md:126 says it rides R2 as mandatory funding.
- **SAF-10**: On a reconciliation bill but booked as discretionary. Legacy/domains/domain-public-safety.md:130 chose mandatory formula funding in R3 (SAF-10A); SAF-10C (line 132) is a split, a mandatory core with the rest appropriated, if titles fail the Byrd check.
