# Provision specs: how the ledger's money is enacted

Role: a summary of `models/data/budget/provisions.csv`, the provision spec for every ledger row (piece B3 of `models/tools/budget/WORKFLOW.md`). It shows where the model's picture of how money is enacted is still thin. Status: generated by `models/tools/budget/provisions_report.py` from the specs and the current ledger; do not edit by hand. The method is in `models/tools/budget/provisions.py` and the format in `models/data/budget/README.md`, "Provision specs".

**What this changes.** Nothing in any figure. For every row the fiscal model scores, the library produces the same path, year by year, as `models/tools/fiscal-model.py`, the same floating-point number in every year from FY2026 to FY2056; each row's FY2030-39 total equals its ledger midpoint; and `models/data/fiscal-paths.csv` is unchanged. `models/tools/budget/test_provisions.py` checks all three on every run. What B3 adds is the budget-concept reading: kind, vehicle, appropriation type and budget authority.

## The rows

The ledger has 165 rows: 72 bills, 61 revenue options, 23 gap-closing options and 9 dynamic-feedback rows. One bill, HOU-1, is split into 2 components, each with its own spec. The fiscal model scores 110 of the rows; the other 55 are on the revenue menu, outside the general fund, or are borrowing lines whose spending sits in other rows.

**What the discretionary spend-out shows.** For the 21 discretionary rows with spending, the library estimates the budget authority (the appropriations) behind the model's outlays: each year's appropriation is the outlays it pays for over B2's four-year spend-out. Over FY2030-39 that is $1.26 trillion of budget authority for $1.20 trillion of outlays. Another $0.10 trillion would have to be appropriated before FY2030, in FY2027-29: at B2's rates only 24% of new nondefense budget authority is spent in its first year, so outlays that start at full speed in FY2030, as most profiles have them, need money enacted earlier. The scoring window does not count it (CONVENTIONS.md, choice 3).

Spending that budget authority out at the same rates does not give back the model's outlays exactly, because the model's profiles start and stop faster than B2's rates allow. The largest FY2030-39 gaps, in billions: MOV-31 -10.3 in FY2036; CAP-7 -8.2 in FY2032; MOV-29 -8.1 in FY2039; MOV-30 -2.9 in FY2039; DEM-17 -1.8 in FY2036. The model's outlays stay canonical; the budget-authority figures are rough until CBO's account-level spend-out replaces B2's estimate (B2 README, "Spend-out").

### Model assumptions with no statement in the ledger

The model always has a growth index and an end year (or none) for a scored row; that is how it spreads the ten-year amount. Whether the law itself is indexed, or expires, is stated in the ledger's text for only some rows (`indexation_basis` and `expiry_basis` = `stated`, quoted in the row's notes). For the rest the model's value is its own assumption (`model`).

## Where the files disagree

These are reported, not fixed: changing a category or a profile changes adopted inputs, which is Espen's decision (tier 3), and B6's work.

- **EDU-4**: Conflict: a reconciliation rail with a discretionary category; reconciliation carries only direct spending and revenue (CONVENTIONS.md section 3).
- **LAB-8**: Conflict: a reconciliation rail with a discretionary category; reconciliation carries only direct spending and revenue (CONVENTIONS.md section 3).
- **DEM-11**: Conflict: the ledger says mandatory if Byrd-eligible (unchecked), but the rail is annual appropriations and the category nondefense discretionary; the ledger also says indexed while the profile grows it with GDP.
- **DEM-17**: Conflict: a reconciliation rail with a discretionary category; reconciliation carries only direct spending and revenue (CONVENTIONS.md section 3).
- **CAP-7S**: Conflict: the profile calls it a direct multi-year appropriation and puts it in other mandatory; votes-bills.csv says its R1 routing assumes eligibility and does not certify it.
- **NSC-10**: Conflict: a reconciliation rail with a discretionary category; reconciliation carries only direct spending and revenue (CONVENTIONS.md section 3).
- **SAF-10**: Conflict: a reconciliation rail with a discretionary category; reconciliation carries only direct spending and revenue (CONVENTIONS.md section 3).
- **SAF-10**: Conflict: the ledger says the funding title is mandatory spending (SAF-10A), but the profile's category is nondefense discretionary.
- **MOV-29**: Conflict: a reconciliation rail with a discretionary category; reconciliation carries only direct spending and revenue (CONVENTIONS.md section 3).
- **MOV-29**: Text and model differ: the ledger names FY2032-35 renewal top-ups; the profile has no end year and continues 30% after 2039.
- **MOV-30**: Conflict: a reconciliation rail with a discretionary category; reconciliation carries only direct spending and revenue (CONVENTIONS.md section 3).
- **MOV-30**: Text and model differ: the ledger names FY2035-36 top-ups; the profile has no end year and continues 30% after 2039.
