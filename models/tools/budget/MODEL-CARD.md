# Federal budget model: model card

Role: what the federal budget model is for, what it isn't for, what goes into it, how well it matches published figures, and where it is known to be wrong. Status: current (piece B4 of `models/tools/budget/WORKFLOW.md`). The results block below is generated by `models/tools/budget/test_benchmarks.py` on every run; the rest is written by hand and changes only when the model does. Full results, with each benchmark's source and reasoning, are in `benchmarks.md` (and `benchmarks.json` for other tools).

## What the model is

The budget model projects the whole federal budget, year by year from FY2026 to FY2056: revenue, spending, deficits, debt held by the public and net interest. It starts from CBO's February 2026 current-law projections, adds the plan's bills and revenue as changes to them, and works out the interest on the extra borrowing. It is several pieces that share one baseline:

| Piece | File | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fiscal model | `models/tools/fiscal-model.py` | The plan's rows as yearly changes to the baseline; interest; debt; the ten scenarios; the D4 tests |
| Data layer | `models/data/budget/`, `models/tools/budget/data.py` | CBO's and OMB's series, price indexes, and the one way to convert to real dollars or share of GDP |
| Provision library | `models/data/budget/provisions.csv`, `models/tools/budget/provisions.py` | How each row's money is enacted: kind, vehicle, budget authority, timing |
| Dedicated-funds engine | `models/tools/funds-model.py` | Social Security, Medicare Hospital Insurance and the plan's other self-funded programs, on 75 years |
| Counting rules and assumptions | `models/tools/budget/CONVENTIONS.md`, `models/data/budget/assumptions.csv` | Every rule and assumption, with an `ASM-` ID |

## What it is for

- **Checking that the plan pays for itself the way Espen decided** (D4-B): the permanent programs paid for by FY2040, total debt and interest below the baseline, and the room left for time-limited investment.
- **Seeing how a change moves the fiscal path**: add, drop or resize a row and read the effect on deficits, debt and interest in nominal dollars, real dollars or share of GDP.
- **Showing spending the way Congress enacts it**: discretionary or mandatory, which bill carries it, and (roughly) the budget authority behind the outlays.
- **Comparing the plan with the current-law path** on the same baseline CBO uses.

## What it is not for

- **Not a cost estimator.** The model doesn't work out what a bill costs. Each row's ten-year cost comes from the ledger (`models/data/inputs/`), where it is sourced; the model spreads that total over the years and carries it forward. A benchmark that "reproduces" a score tests the spreading and the interest, not the cost (PS-1, PS-2).
- **Not a forecast.** Like CBO's baseline, it shows what current law plus the plan would do, not what Congress will do.
- **Not dynamic.** The economy doesn't respond to the plan, and interest rates don't respond to borrowing. The model can run a growth stress on CBO's rules of thumb (SE-2a, SE-2b), but the plan doesn't change growth in any scenario. Dynamic effects are a separate, labeled scenario (DEC-DYN).
- **Not a substitute for an official score.** CBO and JCT will score the plan's bills against their own baseline of the day. The model is built to land near them, and the benchmarks show how near.
- **Not yet a source for budget authority figures.** Budget authority for discretionary rows is rough (`ASM-SPENDOUT`; PS-3b).

**What the groups test.**
- **Baseline (BL):** that the model's starting point is CBO's, exactly. B2's test does this; B4 reports it.
- **Accounting identities (ID):** that the arithmetic holds in every year and scenario: deficits, debt, interest and unit conversions. These must always pass, and the test run fails if one doesn't.
- **Published scores (PS):** that the model's methods land near CBO's and JCT's own numbers for the same inputs: the timing of a tax provision, the interest on added deficits, and the conversion of appropriations into spending.
- **Sensitivity (SE):** that the model responds to higher rates and slower growth as CBO's rules of thumb say (CBO's April 2026 report on the February 2026 baseline).
- **Trust funds (TF):** that the dedicated-funds engine reproduces the 2026 Trustees and SSA's actuaries.

A miss is a result. Nothing in the model or the tolerances is changed to make a benchmark pass; a fix to a method is its own change, reviewed on its own.

## What is not yet validated

The workflow says nothing new feeds a public or decision figure until B4's benchmarks pass. They don't all pass yet (the milestone status in the block above), and Codex's milestone review asked for changes on that ground (PR #115). Until each open benchmark is resolved, or Espen decides the scope explicitly, these readings are **diagnostic only** and must not be used in a decision memo, a dossier's cost section or a public page:

| Reading | Open benchmark | What resolves it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Medicare Hospital Insurance's absolute depletion year and 75-year balance (the program's gain to HI is the reading the engine supports) | TF-2 (fail) | A fix in `funds-model.py` by its owner, or a year-by-year Trustees HI table |
| The year-by-year timing of any single discretionary row's spending (and its budget authority) | PS-4a, PS-4b (fail) | The library uses one average spend-out per group (PS-3 tests the average). A single act can spend far faster or slower, as CBO's 2024 supplemental estimate shows. Per-program spend-out rates, from CBO's account-level data, would be the fix (B3, B6) |
| The size of any growth stress reading | SE-2a, SE-2b (pass, but in-sample) | The link is CBO's 2026 table itself, so the pass tests only the bookkeeping and the debt service. CBO's own rules of thumb moved 20-33% between the 2025 and 2026 editions; read growth figures with that range until a later edition tests the link out of sample |

Two passing benchmarks are weaker than their status suggests, and Codex's review says so too:
- **PS-1 and PS-2 check methods, not costs.** The cost total goes in from JCT; the benchmarks check how the library spreads it over the years and what interest the model adds. They do not show that the model can estimate a cost.
- **PS-2 is a near match.** It is about 13% from CBO against a 15% tolerance, now on Table 2's actual years; the remaining difference is mostly the two interest-rate vintages.
- **PS-3a and PS-3b are partly in-sample.** B2 set the spend-out shares' overall level to match CBO's outlays over the same years the benchmark checks, so the ten-year total is fitted. Only the year-by-year timing, taken from OMB's history, is tested.

What the benchmarks do support now: the baseline (BL-1), the accounting identities (ID-1 to ID-5), the year-by-year timing of the discretionary spend-out (PS-3a, PS-3b), Social Security's trust-fund engine (TF-1, TF-3), the size and timing of the interest-rate stress case (SE-1, since B6 phased it in), and the arithmetic behind the D4 readings. The D4 readings also rest on the ledger's midpoints, which no benchmark tests (`CONVENTIONS.md`, choice 2). B4 changes none of those figures.

## Known limits and errors

**Found by the benchmarks.**
- **Nondefense appropriations used to turn into too little spending (PS-3b, now fixed).** In the first run, the library's spend-out fell about 11% short of CBO's nondefense outlays. The library had rescaled B2's spend-out shares down to one, dropping the highway, transit and airport spending that counts as discretionary outlays though its budget authority is mandatory. B3's fix (PR #118) uses B2's CBO-calibrated shares and carries that spending on its own; PS-3a and PS-3b now pass. Budget-authority figures stay rough (`ASM-SPENDOUT`): the shares' timing is history's, not CBO's account-by-account rates.
- **The default timing for a tax provision can grow too fast (PS-1).** Revenue rows grow with their tax base by default (`ASM-PROF-REVENUE`). JCT's estimate for the four household deductions grows much more slowly; the yearly figures pass, but the growth rates differ by about two points a year.
- **The interest stress case used to reprice all debt at once (SE-1, now fixed).** A one-point rise applied to the whole debt from its first year, where CBO's rules of thumb phase it in as debt is refinanced: on CBO's scenario the model added $502 billion of interest over FY2027-36 against CBO's $383 billion, and $32 billion against $14 billion in FY2027. B6 (item 1) phases the rate in on Treasury's maturity structure (`ASM-RATE-ROLLOVER`); SE-1 now passes, within 1% of CBO's ten-year total. The stress cases DEC-R1 and BASE-R1 are lower as a result: the baseline's 2056 debt under the stress is 217% of GDP, not 224%.
- **The model had no response to slower growth (SE-2, now built).** CBO's baseline was taken in dollars, so slower growth changed nothing, where CBO's rules of thumb add $166-317 billion to deficits over ten years for a 0.1-point slowdown. B6 (item 2) adds a growth link from CBO's 2026 rules of thumb (`ASM-GROWTH-LINK`). It is simple: CBO's effects as shares of GDP by year, scaled to the size of the change, continued on a straight line after 2036; the plan's own rows don't shrink with GDP. SE-2a and SE-2b pass, in-sample.
- **One average spend-out for every discretionary row (PS-4).** Run on a single act, CBO's estimate of the 2024 Israel security supplemental, the library's shares misplace the timing badly: too fast for the defense title and far too slow for the State title, which CBO spends almost entirely in its first year. The ten-year totals are right; the years are not, so row-level outlays and budget authority by year stay rough.
- **Medicare Hospital Insurance is a year off the Trustees (TF-2).** The engine works from the Trustees' published rates at intervals, interpolated, so it lands near but not on their depletion year and balance. The engine's own page says to read the program's gain, not the absolute balance. The Trustees are the right reference, since the engine runs on their rates. CBO's own February 2026 projection puts HI's exhaustion much later, in FY2040, so the two forecasters differ by far more than the engine's one-year miss.

**Found by B3 (`provisions.md`, "Where the files disagree"; reported there, not fixed, since fixing them changes adopted inputs).** B6 (item 3) sorted them. The seven reconciliation rows, and DEM-11 and CAP-7S, are open design choices (board 0d), each now citing what the dossier documents already say; the library can score a provision split across vehicles, each part in its own category. None is a plain booking error: C33 is a net trim whose faithful booking is a split. The timing and growth findings (c) and (d) go to Espen as proposals.
- **(a)** Seven rows on a reconciliation bill sit in a discretionary category: EDU-4, LAB-8, NSC-10, MOV-29, DEM-17, SAF-10, MOV-30. Reconciliation carries only mandatory spending and revenue.
- **(b)** Four rows' categories don't match the ledger's text: SAF-10, DEM-11, CAP-7S and C33.
- **(c)** MOV-29 and MOV-30 have no end year in the model, but the ledger names top-up years.
- **(d)** C35: the ledger says the exemption is frozen in dollars, so its revenue would grow faster than GDP; the model grows it with GDP.

**Unknown fields.** Where the repository doesn't say how a row is enacted (its vehicle, appropriation type or budget authority), B3 marks it `unknown` rather than guess. The counts are in the results block above; the rows are listed in `provisions.md`. For most rows, the growth index and end year are the model's assumption, not something the law states.

**What the model doesn't count yet.**
- **FY2029 is unpriced.** Every total runs FY2030-39, but the plan starts in January 2029. Anything spent or raised in FY2029 (CAP-7S's FY2029 cash is the known case) is outside the totals, the tests and the debt path. It is "unpriced, not zero" (`ASM-WINDOW`; `CONVENTIONS.md`, choice 3).
- **The cautious readings are not computed.** The tests use each row's midpoint. The ledger's high-cost and low-revenue ends never reach the model, so nobody yet knows whether the plan passes at the cautious end (`ASM-CALIB-MID`; choice 2).
- **Interest rates don't respond to the plan's borrowing** (`ASM-RATE-NOFEEDBACK`).
- **Rows CBO's baseline already contains read $0.** Defense is the visible case: NSC-1A reads $0 against CBO's baseline (`CONVENTIONS.md`, section 1; the defense memo).

**Carried from earlier pieces.**
- OMB's and CBO's historical outlays differ by up to 0.6% a year in FY2020-25 (B2's README, "Which administration"). B4 doesn't explain the gap; neither series is used for those years in any benchmark here.
- The largest dynamic-feedback row, DY1, rests on a JCT figure recorded "from memory" (`CONVENTIONS.md`, section 8). It enters only scenario DEC-DYN.

## How to rerun

From the repository root:

```
bash models/tools/run-all.sh            # rebuilds everything into build/ and runs every test, including the benchmarks (step 6e)
bash models/tools/run-all.sh --write    # the same, then copies the outputs into place, including this card's results block
python3 models/tools/budget/test_benchmarks.py   # the benchmarks alone, after a run-all
```

`bash .github/scripts/run-checks.sh` runs the full PR checks: the rebuild, the drift check (it fails if `benchmarks.md`, `benchmarks.json` or this card no longer match the code and data) and the other test suites.

**When a source arrives.** The documents listed at the end of `benchmarks.md` turn pending benchmarks into pass or fail. Save the upload into the library, record its figures in `test_benchmarks.py` beside the benchmark with the `SRC-` ID, set the tolerance with its reason, and rerun.
