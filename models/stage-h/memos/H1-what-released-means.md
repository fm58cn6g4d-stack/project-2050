# Memo H1: What "released" means for a model

Role: decision memo for Espen, stage H step 1 (`../1-audit.md`, part 1). Status: **provisional, drafted November 25, 2026. Not decided.** Decided on its own, not as a package.

## The question

PLAN 3.4 gives each model a status: exploratory, working or released. Nothing says what earns "released". The audit finds three models near the standard (household, health coverage, energy), three with real gaps (budget, immigration, transportation) and one missing (housing). Staff and researchers will read "released" as a promise of quality. What should the label promise?

## The options

**A. Released means every part of the standard is met, checked and reviewed (recommended).**
- The card has all seven elements; every published benchmark is run, with differences explained (a miss is allowed if it is explained); one command runs from a fresh clone with public or documented data; the tests pass in the project's checks; the run log is machine-written; the project's reviewer has tried to break it. The release is a version number and a tag.
- *For:* a clear bar the project can meet with its own sessions; honest about misses rather than hiding them.
- *Against:* no outside eye. A model can be "released" and still wrong in a way no agent spotted.
- *What it means now:* household, health coverage and energy can reach it in stage H; the budget model after H4; immigration after H6; transportation after H5. Health coverage's three benchmark misses stay visible on its card.

**B. A, plus a review by an outside expert before "released".**
- *For:* the strongest claim of quality, and what a congressional office would trust most.
- *Against:* agents never contact other people for Espen, so Espen would have to find and ask reviewers; it can't be scheduled from here. Models would sit at "working" until then.

**C. Ship every model at whatever status it has, labeled.**
- *For:* everything is available at once; the label carries the warning.
- *Against:* a toolkit with "exploratory" pieces in it reads as unfinished, and readers skip labels. A wrong number from an exploratory model would be quoted as the project's.

## Recommendation

**A**, with B as a later upgrade Espen can pursue when the plan goes public in 2028. Under any option, every page and card says the toolkit never produces official scores or individual benefit determinations (PLAN 3.4).

## Consequences

- **A:** stage H ends with four to six models released and the rest labeled "working" with their gaps listed. The reviewer's milestone review (H11) is the release gate.
- **B:** stage H ends with models at "working"; release waits on Espen's outreach.
- **C:** no gate; the cards' status lines carry all the weight.
