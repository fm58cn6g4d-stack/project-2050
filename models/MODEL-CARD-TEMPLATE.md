# Model card template

Role: the template every core model's card follows (`charter/PLAN.md` 3.4, "The toolkit standard"; stage H step H2a). Status: current; the release criteria it serves are provisional until Espen answers memo H1 (`models/stage-h/memos/H1-what-released-means.md`). Copy everything below the line into `models/tools/<model>/MODEL-CARD.md`, keep the `##` headings exactly (`models/registry/toolkit_check.py` checks them), and replace the guidance in *italics*. The conventions behind the generated blocks are in `models/schema/README.md`.

The standard's seven elements and where each sits: (1) the question the model answers and (2) the ones it cannot, under their own headings; (3) the evidence map, generated from the parameter file; (4) the mechanisms and their research basis; (5) the limits; (6) the status and (7) the owner, in the opening paragraph. The other headings carry release criteria 5, 6, 8 and 10 (`models/release-criteria.csv`).

# *Model name*: model card

Role: the model card of `MOD-<name>`, *one sentence on what the model measures and whom it serves* (`charter/PLAN.md` 3.4). Status: **exploratory** *(or working, preview, released: the register's value in `models/toolkit.csv`; "released" and "preview" wait for memo H1)*. Owner: *a role a reader can reach, not a session label: "the Project 2050 lead session (Claude Code), through GitHub issues on this repository"*. Version: *X.Y.Z*, see `CHANGELOG.md`. License: *the line step H2b adds once memo H2 is answered*. *Every output of this model is analysis of the evidence, not an official score or a benefit determination; its results files carry that line in their header.*

## What it answers

*The questions, in plain words, with the year or window and the units: "for FY2030-39 and FY2040, the federal cost of ...". A reader should be able to tell from this list whether the model is the right tool.*

## What it cannot answer

*What it does not do and must not be quoted for, each with the reason: behavioral or macroeconomic effects not modeled, populations not covered, individual eligibility. End with: "It never produces an official score or an individual benefit determination."*

## Mechanisms and their research basis

*Each mechanism (a flow, a response, a rule) in one or two sentences, with the evidence IDs of the research it rests on. A mechanism without research behind it is a labeled assumption, said so here.*

## Limits

*What the model simplifies and how that could bias a result, with the direction where it is known. Include the baseline's vintage and anything that does not reconcile.*

## Validation

*Generated by the model's test code (`models/schema/validation.py`): every benchmark with its target, source and vintage, the model's figure, the difference, the tolerance fixed in advance, pass or miss, and the explanation. Add "Differences explained" after the table for each miss and each in-sample check.*

## Known issues

*Defects and gaps a user should know before relying on a figure, each with where it is tracked (the board, a PR, an issue). "None known" is an answer only after the reviewer's release review.*

## How to run

*One command from a fresh clone, the data it needs and where it comes from (committed, or a fetch script with a hash), its arguments and scenarios, and its outputs. Then the test command.*

```bash
python3 models/tools/<model>/run.py [--out DIR] [--record]
python3 models/tools/<model>/test_<model>.py
```

## Run log

*Recorded runs are rows in `models/runs.csv` (`RUN-<model>-<date>-<letter>`), written by `models/runlog.py` when the model runs with `--record`; a dossier quoting a figure cites the run ID. Don't keep a hand-written table here: link the rows instead.*
